# UX Guidelines for Recommended Content

## Summary

This article presents evidence-based design guidelines for presenting personalized recommendation content based on recent user research. The findings show that users appreciate personalized suggestions that help avoid information overload, particularly when recommendations are prioritized over generic content. Users want to understand the sources behind recommendations, enjoy seeing separate recommendation categories, and appreciate the ability to fine-tune or dismiss suggestions. The article provides specific, actionable recommendations for maximizing the effectiveness of recommendation systems through proper presentation and user control.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prioritize personalized recommendations above generic content** — Individualized suggestions are more valued than generic promotions, so placement higher on the page significantly increases discoverability and use compared to lower positioning.

- **Clearly state the data sources behind recommendations** — Users appreciate understanding what data or behaviors drove each suggestion; specific source attribution builds credibility and helps users judge whether they want to explore the recommendations.

- **Separate recommendations into specific categories** — Rather than grouping all recommendations together, breaking them into genre-based or behavior-based categories makes them more useful and less overwhelming when users have diverse interests.

- **Allow users to fine-tune and provide feedback** — Enabling rating of suggestions, editing of browsing history, and other feedback mechanisms empowers users to improve future recommendations, increasing engagement especially for services where accuracy matters.

- **Update recommendations quickly and frequently** — Users expect their feedback to be reflected immediately in recommendations; fast updates demonstrate that the system is learning and encourage continued interaction.

- **Avoid vague data-source descriptions** — Stating "and more" as a source is confusing; be specific about whether recommendations come from viewing history, purchases, or other sources to set proper expectations.
