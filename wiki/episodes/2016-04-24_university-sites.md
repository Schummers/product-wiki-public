# University Websites: Top 10 Design Guidelines

## Summary

University websites rank far below usability expectations of modern web, yet represent critical touchpoints for prospective students and families making education decisions. Based on usability testing with 33 users across 57 university sites, this article provides 10 evidence-based design guidelines. Key findings show that 48% of users did not realize the university offered their target program because they could not find it on the site. Prospective students (even young adults) have poor advanced research skills and are frustrated by insider language and poor information architecture. Universities that prioritize user experience and clear communication see better outcomes in enrollment decisions and student satisfaction.

## Key Takeaways

- **Identify university on every page** — Display full university name prominently on every page, not just the footer; this is critical for subsites, department pages, and pages reached via search to prevent confusion about institutional affiliation.
- **Use authentic photos** — Select images that reflect institutional values and diversity; users prefer authentic photos of actual students and activities over generic stock photos, and perceive stock images as indicating lack of effort.
- **Strengthen About Us pages** — Lead with informative summaries in plain language, include scannable fact lists and statistics, and provide videos conveying school character; this is a top destination for prospective students making fit decisions.
- **Emphasize strengths and rankings** — Make institutional statistics, rankings, and awards highly visible (above fold); place information in scannable format rather than dense paragraphs, as users scan rather than read.
- **Provide major/program lists** — Offer a discoverable list of all majors and programs; grouping by field or school helps, but students often need to see the complete program name and may not realize programs exist if they can't find them easily.
- **Include job placement data** — Provide employment outcomes with sources and numbers; link this from Alumni, About Us, and Admissions sections, as families want to know return on educational investment.
- **Make application deadlines prominent** — Display deadlines clearly on Admissions or Apply pages; consider a dedicated Deadlines page if multiple or complex deadlines exist, and use tables for easy scanning.
- **Follow the user journey** — Identify top user tasks (prospective student evaluation, application, financial information) and trace the required path; identify where users must leave the main site or bounce between offices.
- **Avoid trying to be "cool"** — Clever labels, flashy interactions, and trendy videos are perceived as superficial and insulting by young adults; concentrate on clear, age-appropriate content; student-generated content is more authentic and appealing.
- **Account for external research** — Users will look to external sites (college aggregators, review sites, Google) if they can't find information; provide accurate, up-to-date information to improve external representation and implement robust internal search.
