# User-Feedback Requests: 5 Guidelines

## Summary

Collecting user feedback through voice-of-customer (VOC) programs helps UX teams make informed design decisions and improve products. However, poor feedback-request timing and methodology can bias results and frustrate users through excessive contact. This article presents five guidelines for effective feedback collection: task-then-ask (timing feedback requests after users complete tasks rather than before), easing eager emails (avoiding feedback request overload by using contextually relevant channels), keeping surveys short (respecting user attention), offering flexible formats (letting users choose feedback methods), and appreciating participants. The author illustrates mistakes and solutions through real-world examples, emphasizing that the timing, channel, and framing of feedback requests significantly impact both response quality and user experience.

## Key Takeaways

- **Task-then-ask timing prevents bias** — asking for feedback before users interact skews results; asking after task completion when experience is fresh produces meaningful, relevant feedback.
- **Email fatigue is real** — users receive excessive feedback-request emails; instead of defaulting to email, request feedback through the channel where interaction occurred (push notification, in-app message, SMS).
- **Intrusiveness matters** — poorly-timed popups that appear before tasks or interrupt workflows reduce honest feedback; subtle, dismissible requests after tasks provide better data.
- **Flexible feedback formats increase response** — offering choices (email, push notification, SMS, call, social media) respects user preferences and increases participation compared to single-channel approaches.
- **Context makes requests work** — feedback requests specific to the completed task ("Tell us about your cancellation experience") are more relevant than generic satisfaction questions.
- **Emotional state affects feedback** — cheerful feedback requests misaligned with user emotional state (e.g., during tax filing) feel tone-deaf and reduce trust in the product.

## Quotes

> "Wait until after users complete a real task to ask for feedback. Ensure the request is subtle and does not obstruct important information they need to reference. Include easily accessible, on-demand features, such as a small *Feedback* tab on the right side of the page, so users can provide feedback when they're ready or even if they dismissed the initial request. **Remember: task, then ask.**"

> "Don't flood users with intrusive [popups](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/popups/) asking for feedback as soon as they land on a page. How can they give feedback about their experience if they haven't done anything?"

> "**Even better is to allow people to choose how to give feedback.** Some users may find that a push notification is less intrusive and avoids cluttering email inboxes and texts, while others may find clearing out app notifications to be just as cumbersome."

## Concepts

- [[User Feedback]] — effective user feedback collection requires timing requests after task completion, using contextually relevant channels, and offering flexible feedback formats.
- [[Survey Design]] — feedback surveys should be short, task-specific, and delivered through channels matching user interaction channels to maximize response quality and quantity.
- [[Voice of Customer]] — VOC programs are most effective when they respect user time and attention, offer channel choices, and request feedback in moments aligned with user emotions and task completion.
