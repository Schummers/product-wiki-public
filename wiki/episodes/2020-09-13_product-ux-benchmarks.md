# 7 Steps to Benchmark Your Product's UX

## Summary

UX benchmarking evaluates a product or service's user experience using metrics to gauge relative performance against meaningful standards. A seven-step benchmarking process guides organizations through initial setup (choosing metrics, deciding measurement methods, establishing baseline) and ongoing cycles (redesigning, collecting new measurements, interpreting findings, calculating ROI). Benchmarking is typically a program rather than a one-time activity, with organizations collecting metrics repeatedly across design iterations. Effective benchmarking keeps teams accountable and documents progress measurably.

## Key Takeaways

- **Step 1: Choose metrics** — Focus on key metrics reflecting UX quality and translating to organizational goals. Define context (product, user group, tasks). Identify top tasks (5-10 most important), then select 2-4 metrics across different aspects using Google's HEART framework (Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task effectiveness).
- **Step 2: Decide methodology** — Consider time, cost, skill, and tools. Review existing organizational data. Three primary methods work well: quantitative usability testing, analytics data, and surveys. Ideal approach pairs survey data (self-reported) with behavioral observation (quantitative testing or analytics).
- **Step 3: Collect baseline** — Conduct pilot study first to validate methodology. Gather first measurement while considering external factors affecting data. Compare to competitors, industry benchmarks, or stakeholder-determined goals.
- **Step 4: Redesign product** — Outside scope of this article but essential. Apply 10 usability heuristics during redesign process.
- **Step 5: Collect additional measurement** — Measure again after redesign launch. Allow users time to adapt to changes (2-3 weeks for daily products, 4-5 weeks for weekly products) before measuring. Document external influences.
- **Step 6: Interpret findings** — Use statistical methods to determine whether visible differences are real or random noise. Interpretation is highly contextual to product and metrics chosen.
- **Step 7: Calculate ROI** — Connect UX metrics to organizational KPIs (profit, cost, productivity, satisfaction) to demonstrate value of UX efforts.

## Quotes

> Focus on the key metrics that best reflect the quality of the user experience you're interested in evaluating.

> Don't do something if you don't have the right skills, since bad numbers are worse than no numbers.

> Pick metrics that will matter for the long haul, since ideally, you'll be collecting these metrics repeatedly over many years.

## Concepts

- [[UX Metrics]] — Effective benchmarking selects 2-4 metrics across different aspects of user experience (happiness, engagement, adoption, retention, task effectiveness) that align with organizational goals and can be tracked repeatedly.
- [[Benchmarking]] — Benchmarking is a program for evaluating overall product performance using meaningful comparisons to competitors, industry standards, or organizational goals, enabling teams to track progress measurably over time.
- [[Research Methods]] — Combining quantitative usability testing or analytics (behavioral data) with surveys (self-reported data) provides holistic understanding of user experience and accounts for contextual factors affecting metrics.
