# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 9. Growing Your Impact

## Summary

Part IV of the book shifts from planning to implementation. This chapter restates that the main predictor of a designer's seniority is the impact they have which benefits the organization, and cites Dropbox, where impact is broken into three factors: consistency (how often you make an impact), velocity (how big that impact is), and accountability (taking responsibility and ownership). It argues that at mature companies impact is looked at individually rather than in relation to the team, giving Intercom as the example, where a designer with high individual impact in a less successful team is evaluated more highly than one with smaller impact in a very successful team; this gives designers significant control over their own growth. The core method for increasing impact is finding and shifting toward high-leverage activities. The author teaches a three-step framework: define what an activity impacts (product, work, or people), identify the level of impact, and upgrade to a higher impact level. The chapter also positions career development as a business investment, using return on investment logic to justify spending on professional development and education. Finally, it discusses learning faster through selective reading, multi-idea books, and podcasts, and introduces the remaining chapters of the book as concrete competency-by-competency guidance with action items and further learning resources.

## Key Takeaways

- **Impact formula** — At Dropbox, impact is described as consistency (how often you're making an impact), velocity (how big that impact is), and accountability (taking responsibility and ownership for it). At mature companies, the chapter says, impact is assessed individually and not in relation to the team, and it cites Intercom as an example; this gives you control over your growth.
- **Leverage through activity redesign** — Instead of working more hours, find high-return-on-investment activities. Use a three-step method: identify what an activity impacts (product, work, or people), determine the level of impact (task to product line for product, learning to managing for work, self to industry for people), then upgrade to the next level.
- **Career as business investment** — Frame your career as your largest financial asset with the highest ROI potential. Professional investments like books, courses, and consultants are cheap compared to the lifetime income increase from one promotion. The chapter's example: a $200 consultation with an interview preparation coach, assuming it improves your chances of getting the job by 10%.
- **Kaizen and continuous improvement** — Apply the Japanese concept of continuous small improvements to your career. Improving 1% per week compounds to 170% improvement over two years, creating significant advantage through consistency rather than dramatic change.
- **Learning strategy** — Read selectively by topic rather than cover-to-cover; prefer multi-idea books over single-idea ones; use podcasts to learn niche knowledge and research companies before joining them. Apply learnings immediately to maximize retention and return on time investment.

## Quotes

## Concepts

- [[Business Impact]] — The chapter defines seniority primarily through impact on the organization (consistency, velocity, and accountability) and teaches how to increase impact by identifying high-leverage activities and advancing them to higher levels of scope.
- [[Design Ownership]] — Accountability for outcomes and delivering on them is one of the three pillars of impact; the chapter emphasizes ownership of results across product, work, and people dimensions.
- [[Design Leadership]] — Leadership emerges from expanding your impact: from self-improvement to team mentoring to organizational influence, and from individual tasks to full product lines. The chapter sketches how to recognize leverage opportunities at each level.
- [[Productivity]] — Improving productivity (and thus consistency and velocity) is not about working longer hours but about shifting toward higher-leverage activities through the three-step redesign framework.
- [[UX Career]] — Understanding impact as the core measure of seniority reframes career progression; designers have control over this through intentional leverage-seeking, continuous learning, and strategic investments in their development.
- [[Career Growth Plan]] — The chapter sets up the framework that the remaining chapters will fill with competency-specific guidance and action items to put into a growth plan.
