# Startup Studio Playbook: A preview on financial modeling

## Summary

Chapter 19 provides a framework for modeling the financial viability of a venture studio, breaking the analysis into two levels: individual startup cash flow and the aggregated studio funnel. The author emphasizes that meaningful financial modeling requires prior decisions on studio focus, the typical startup profile, regional wage expectations, and the financial agreements between studio and portfolio companies about cost tracking and resource charging.

The chapter walks through key variables to estimate: time and resources spent on idea generation and validation, the pass-through rates at each stage of the funnel (from fresh ideas to validated ideas to initially built products), and funding strategy decisions (whether the studio or external investors finance seed rounds). The goal is to calculate the runway needed to sustain the core team until the first major exit, which can take years.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two-Level Model — Build separate spreadsheets** first for one startup's cash flow, then aggregate them into a studio funnel model. This separation lets you understand per-venture economics before scaling.

- **Startup-Level Inputs — Estimate resources required** for idea generation, idea validation (landing pages, market research), and initial build including core team and infrastructure costs. These are the building blocks of per-startup burn.

- **Funnel Rates Drive Studio Economics — The critical variables** are the pass-through rates at each stage: how many ideas become validated ideas, how many validated ideas become built products, and how many get to seed funding. Small changes in these rates dramatically affect overall cash flow.

- **Valuation and Cap Table Projections — Use success rates** and pass-through rates to model portfolio value over time, cap table progression, equity dilution across rounds, and typical ESOP sizes to understand long-term returns.

- **Runway Calculation — Determine monthly cash burn** for the core team and infrastructure, then multiply by the time expected before first major exit. This reveals how much capital the studio needs to raise or self-fund before reaching positive contribution.

- **Charging Models for Startups — If studios bill startups** for resources, the rate must be low enough that it is cheaper for a startup to use the studio than hire full-time employees, yet high enough for the studio to sustain itself and investors to see no overcharging.

## Quotes

> This is a short guide on how to estimate and model the costs, revenues, valuations and returns of a studio.

> With all this you want to figure out how much money you need on a monthly basis to keep the core team engaged and building ventures for as long as you expect your first startup success to bring home a big return. This can take years.

> Because in many cases it's more cost efficient to use and on-demand resource from the studio than to hire a full time employee.

## Concepts

- [[Studio Economics]] — The financial structure of a studio, including core team payroll, infrastructure, and the costs and revenues of producing multiple startups, along with techniques for forecasting monthly burn and runway needed to sustain operations until exits or revenue-generating milestones.
- [[Studio Funnel]] — the sequential stages from idea generation through validation to initial build to seed funding, each with pass-through rates that determine studio output.
- [[Valuation]] — how to project portfolio value over time using pass-through rates, typical investment round sizes, and equity dilution patterns.
- [[Team Compensation]] — different strategies a studio founder can use to fund operations: self-fund from personal wealth, raise from VCs, take agency revenue to subsidize ventures, or require portfolio companies to raise seed before studio exits.
