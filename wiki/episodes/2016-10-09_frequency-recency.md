# Frequency & Recency of Site Visits: 2 Metrics for User Engagement

## Summary

Frequency and recency metrics measure how sticky websites and apps are—do users return regularly? Frequency indicates total visits per user (count-of-session report); recency measures days since last visit. Combined, they reveal whether returning users form a loyal segment and which pages appeal to frequent visitors versus first-time visitors. Segmenting by conversion events shows how many visits precede conversion, informing content strategy, email timing, and design priorities. Analyzing frequency and recency data enables comparison with content update frequency, revelation of seasonal patterns, and optimization of resource investment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Frequency reveals loyalty and familiarity levels** — Counting visits per user (not just new vs. returning) shows what percentage are highly familiar (6+ visits) versus relatively fresh. Histograms of visit counts inform whether design should optimize for novices or experts.
- **Recency indicates how regularly people return** — Days since last visit reveal whether users visit daily, weekly, or monthly, and whether this pattern aligns with content update frequency. A spike at recency = 6 days often correlates with weekly email newsletters.
- **Analyze frequency and recency together** — Segmenting by both metrics reveals patterns: do daily visitors convert more than weekly visitors? Are frequent visitors attracted by particular content or features? Do certain marketing efforts trigger repeat visits?
- **Segment conversion data by visit frequency** — Knowing that conversions typically occur after 5–6 visits enables targeted strategies: email reminders to users with 3–4 visits, design features to ease context recovery, or user research to identify conversion barriers.
- **Compare patterns to business activities** — Notable frequency spikes, recency patterns, and seasonal variations indicate which marketing efforts, content updates, or events drive return visits, helping allocate resources effectively.
- **Frequency data in isolation is less revealing** — Raw histograms typically show decreasing numbers as visit count increases. Real insights emerge from filtering by conversion events or analyzing segments (daily visitors vs. weekly vs. one-time).

## Quotes

> For most websites and apps, visitors go through a long dating process, not a one-night stand: they rarely convert after the first visit.

> To better support our users, we need to understand the actual dating process for our site visitors. Do people use our site every day or even multiple times per day? How many visits precede that final conversion?
