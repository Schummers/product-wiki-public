# Triangulation: Get Better Research Results by Using Multiple UX Methods

## Summary

Triangulation is the practice of using multiple sources of data or multiple research methods to enhance research credibility. Borrowed from geometry (locating an object's position using two known points), triangulation in social research means seeking alternative viewpoints to verify results. All research methods have limitations: qualitative methods lack statistical proof, quantitative methods lack context. Triangulation mitigates one method's limitations using data from another source. The more significant the decision, the more triangulation investment is warranted. Small, reversible decisions need less triangulation; expensive, hard-to-reverse decisions warrant robust triangulation mixing qualitative, quantitative, and external analysis.

## Key Takeaways

- **Triangulation enhances research credibility** — using multiple sources or methods to examine the same question provides verification and reveals aspects not visible from a single viewpoint.
- **All methods have inherent limitations** — qualitative studies lack statistical proof and small sample sizes; quantitative methods lack context and meaning; triangulation balances these tradeoffs.
- **Risk justifies triangulation investment** — more significant decisions warrant more robust triangulation; simple, reversible decisions need only minimal investment (a few hours checking existing data).
- **Triangulation can mix methods or metrics** — combining qualitative usability testing with quantitative analytics, or examining multiple metrics (satisfaction, time spent, revenue) for the same activity.
- **Examples of triangulation are everyday** — checking survey data against analytics, doing qualitative research after quantitative findings, consulting support records when analytics show high error rates.
- **Diverse UX skills enable rapid triangulation** — teams with experience in multiple methods can implement triangulation quickly without slowing decision-making and development pace.
