# Expandable Menus: Pull-Down, Square, or Pie?

## Summary

Menus are lists of options in a GUI, either visible (menu bars) or expandable. Expandable menus present two design problems: making the menu handle discoverable with good information scent, and minimizing selection time (visual search time plus movement time). Fitts' Law quantifies movement time based on target size and distance. Linear menus (pull-down) have the shortest movement time to first items and longest to last items. Rectangular/megamenus reduce average distance and movement time compared to linear menus. Pie/radial menus place all options at equal distance but suffer from lack of familiarity. Marking menus offer expert-mode shortcuts by moving toward targets without displaying the menu. Touch interfaces require different considerations since finger position differs from mouse position after menu opens.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two core design problems** — Make menu handles discoverable (looking clickable with good information scent) and minimize selection time (visual search and movement time).
- **Fitts' Law governs movement** — Selection time depends on target size (smaller = longer) and distance to target (farther = longer); menu designs optimize by reducing distance to options.
- **Linear/pull-down menus** — Movement time is shortest for first item, longest for last item; avoid overly long menus; aligning the handle with the middle item reduces average distance.
- **Rectangular/megamenus** — Placing options in a rectangle reduces average distance and movement time compared to linear arrangement; now hugely popular on the web.
- **Pie/radial menus** — All options at equal distance from handle (on a circle) optimize movement time, but users lack familiarity with them, losing the theoretical advantage; adoption improving on touchscreens.
- **Marking menus** — Pie-menu variant allowing expert mode (moving without displaying menu) and normal mode; well-suited for touch and contextual use; users can move toward targets without traveling full route.
- **Familiarity moderates design** — Initial learning time can easily outweigh movement-time savings unless users will use the menu many times; choose novel menu types for high-frequency use or wait until familiarity increases.
- **Touch considerations** — After menu opens, finger position may no longer be on the handle (unlike mouse), requiring menu placement optimization for hand reach; marking menus' normal mode works well for touch.

## Quotes

> Movement time in an expandable menu is governed by Fitts' law. Linear menus are best when the number of menu options is small; as the number increases, rectangular menus (or megamenus) are more appropriate.

> Marking menus are well suited for touchscreens, and, like pie menus, optimize the reach time to individual options, but are only now starting to become more familiar due to their popularity on touchscreen devices.
