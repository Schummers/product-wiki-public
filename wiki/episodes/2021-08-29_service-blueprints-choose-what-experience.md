# Service Blueprints: How to Choose What Experience to Visualize

## Summary

Selecting which experience to visualize in a service blueprint is one of the most challenging aspects of getting started. Service blueprints operate at three scope levels: small scope (2 touchpoints or fewer), medium scope (5 touchpoints or fewer), and large scope (end-to-end experience). Small-to-medium scopes are best for most situations because they provide specific insights around strengths and weaknesses without requiring the extensive research and cross-functional effort that large-scope blueprints demand. When choosing an experience to blueprint, teams should consider which experiences are problematic, which they control, which have planned redesigns, and which require minimal research. Prioritization matrices help weight factors and select the best starting point.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three Scope Levels** — Small scope targets 2 touchpoints (focused problem areas), medium scope includes 5 touchpoints (focused experiences), and large scope captures end-to-end customer journeys; scope determines research effort and time investment.
- **Small-to-Medium Best for Most** — Most situations benefit from small-to-medium scoped blueprints that reveal specific strengths and weaknesses; large-scope blueprints provide high-level overview but may only identify problems for later investigation.
- **Research and Timeline** — Small-to-medium scoped blueprints typically require less than one month each for research and creation; large-scope blueprints requiring cross-departmental involvement take significantly longer and need substantial team effort.
- **Selection Criteria** — Choose experiences based on: existing data showing they are problematic, touchpoints you directly control, upcoming redesigns on the roadmap, and minimal research requirements to minimize effort and maximize impact.
- **Prioritization Framework** — Document candidate experiences, filter for those you control, assess research data availability, and prioritize based on timeline and influence; this structured approach prevents arbitrary selection.

## Concepts

- [[Service Blueprinting]] — A service blueprint visualizes the relationship between service components (people, processes, technology) tied to customer journey touchpoints; it maps customer actions alongside internal activities to reveal dependencies and improvement opportunities.
- [[Scope Definition]] — Scope refers to which experience or journey segment to visualize; defining scope requires choosing a level (small, medium, large) that matches research capacity and goals, and selecting a specific experience based on business priorities and research needs.
- [[Journey Mapping]] — Journey mapping visualizes the end-to-end customer experience; service blueprints extend journey mapping by showing the internal service delivery systems that enable each customer touchpoint.
