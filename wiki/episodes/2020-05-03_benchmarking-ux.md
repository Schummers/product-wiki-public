# Benchmarking UX: Tracking Metrics

## Summary

UX benchmarking is the practice of evaluating a product or service's user experience using metrics to gauge its performance against a meaningful standard. A single UX metric is meaningless without comparison; benchmarking provides four possible reference points: an earlier version of the product, a competitor, an industry standard, or a stakeholder-determined goal. This is a summative evaluation that occurs between design cycles rather than formative evaluation that guides design decisions. Benchmarking can be conducted using quantitative methodology, analytics, surveys, or customer-service data, and helps teams track improvements over time while demonstrating design impact to stakeholders and calculating ROI.

## Key Takeaways

- **A single metric requires a reference point** — comparing UX metrics against earlier versions, competitors, industry standards, or internal goals is essential; without comparison, a metric is meaningless.
- **Benchmarking is summative, not formative** — it happens at the end of one design cycle (like after an evaluate phase) and before the next begins, capturing a snapshot of a specific product version's performance.
- **Three methods collect benchmarking metrics best** — analytics, surveys, and quantitative usability testing are the most practical approaches for gathering UX data, though customer-service data is also valuable.
- **Baseline metrics become future comparisons** — the first time you measure (the baseline) becomes the reference point for future redesigns, and getting it right early matters for long-term tracking.
- **Frequency depends on your methodology** — quantitative usability testing may warrant annual benchmarking due to cost, while analytics data allows more frequent measurement after each redesign.
- **ROI calculations prove value to stakeholders** — demonstrating concrete metrics showing how much faster, easier, or more enjoyable the user experience has become provides unambiguous evidence of design's impact.

## Quotes

> UX benchmarking: Refers to evaluating a product or service's user experience by using metrics to gauge its relative performance against a meaningful standard.

> Benchmarking's real power comes in when you show those results externally, for example, to stakeholders or clients. You can demonstrate the impact of your UX work in a concrete, unambiguous way.

> You can also use customer-service data (for example, the number of support emails about a specific task).
