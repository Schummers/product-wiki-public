# Augmented Reality for Ecommerce: Is It Useful Yet?

## Summary

This article reports findings from a usability study of augmented reality features in ecommerce mobile apps and websites. While AR technology has potential to help shoppers preview items in their space or on themselves, current implementations have significant usability challenges. The study found that AR is most useful for items where aesthetics and size matter, accuracy and realism determine helpfulness, and past negative experiences damage trust in the technology. Despite usability issues, users remain optimistic about AR's future.

## Key Takeaways

- **Most useful for aesthetic items** — AR benefits home décor, furniture, wearable items, and makeup where appearance and fit matter, but provides less value for functional items like appliances where specs are primary.
- **Accuracy and realism critical** — Unrealistic or inaccurately placed AR renderings diminish perceived usefulness; users are forgiving of minor imperfections but reject cartoony or misaligned presentations.
- **Calibration is difficult** — AR tools require difficult, unfamiliar calibration processes; face-based AR (virtual try-on) calibrates more easily than room-based AR due to facial detection technology being more reliable.
- **Past experiences drive expectations** — Negative prior experiences with AR hurt trust; brands perceived as high-end are expected to have better AR tools; users often avoid trying AR again after poor experiences.
- **Supplementary not primary** — Currently, users view AR as supplementary information to product photos rather than the primary tool for purchase decisions, using it to gain confidence but not weighing it heavily.

## Quotes

> AR tools seemed particularly useful for **bridging the gap of not seeing an item in real life** before making a purchase decision.

> How realistic an item appeared and whether it seemed accurately placed in the environment had a huge impact on the overall perception of the tool's helpfulness.

> I think **the only way to do it well is to make it feel very realistic**. [When they don't appear realistic,] it's very neutral and probably, when you're spending that much money on having somebody create that, it's not worth it.

## Concepts

- [[Augmented Reality]] — emerging technology that superimposes digital items over real-world camera views, with growing ecommerce use but significant usability challenges that currently limit utility.
- [[E-Commerce]] — online shopping contexts where AR features help users preview items in their spaces or on themselves to reduce purchase uncertainty and return rates.
- [[Emerging Technology]] — new tools like AR that have potential but require significant UX refinement before they become truly useful; negative early experiences damage adoption and future trust.
