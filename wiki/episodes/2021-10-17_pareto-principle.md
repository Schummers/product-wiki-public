# Prioritize Quantitative Data with the Pareto Principle

## Summary

The Pareto principle (the "80/20 rule") describes a statistical pattern where roughly 80 percent of results come from 20 percent of causes. Originally observed by economist Vilfredo Pareto in the 19th century and popularized by management consultant Joseph Juran, this principle appears across diverse contexts from web traffic to software bugs. The article explains how UX professionals can apply the Pareto principle to focus research and design efforts on high-impact areas. Rather than assuming data will follow exactly 80/20, the principle signals that inputs and outputs are often unevenly distributed, helping practitioners identify which areas deserve attention.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pareto principle patterns are ubiquitous** — Examples include 20% of websites capturing 80% of traffic, 20% of customers generating 80% of revenue, and 20% of software bugs causing 80% of crashes.
- **Create a Pareto chart to visualize impact** — A Pareto chart combines a bar plot (showing metric by category) with a line plot (showing cumulative percentage). This dual visualization quickly reveals the highest-impact areas.
- **Use Pareto to filter analytics overload** — When analytics tools overwhelm teams with metrics, the Pareto principle helps identify the vital few areas worth improvement over the trivial many.
- **Don't ignore the 80%** — Focusing exclusively on the top 20% risks stagnation and overoptimization of narrow metrics. Reserve bandwidth for studying other parts of the user experience that may have aggregated impact.
- **Minor improvements yield disproportionate results** — Even small improvements to the vital areas (the top 20%) can generate disproportionately powerful business and user-experience results.

## Quotes

> The Pareto principle (also described as the "80/20 Rule") states that 80% of the results come from 20% of the causes.

> The scope and robustness of analytics tools can mire any design team with information overload and analysis paralysis.

> Leverage the Pareto principle by first confirming your organization's goals, the metrics that measure them, and the user data aligned to those goals. Then focus UX research and design efforts on those areas contributing significantly to those business metrics.
