# Solving Product Design Exercises: What is a design exercise?

## Summary

This introductory chapter of *Solving Product Design Exercises* defines design exercises and the three main formats used in hiring. Design exercises test a candidate's ability to solve design challenges under interview conditions, varying in format from live whiteboarding to take-home deliverables depending on the skills companies want to assess.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three main formats** — live whiteboarding (15-40 minutes, on-site), on-site exercise (about one hour to solve, 10-15 minutes to present), and take-home exercise (8 hours to one week), each with different deliverables and some companies compensating for the time
- **Exercise type reflects skill priorities** — testing product thinking typically uses conceptual tasks like designing an ATM for kids, while testing visual skills often uses take-home exercises focused on UI design
- **Multiple assessment dimensions** — exercises evaluate communication effectiveness, critical thinking and questioning, handling feedback, performance under pressure, and whether the candidate is someone the team wants to collaborate with daily
- **No standardized process** — interview processes vary significantly across companies and even within the same company over time; one designer interviewed at Google, Palantir, Facebook, Apple and Kayak experienced five completely different processes
- **Task categories** — most exercises fall into building new products or improving existing ones, with rarer conceptual and business metric-oriented variants like "improve retention" or "create a new revenue driver"
