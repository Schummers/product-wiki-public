# Unmoderated User Tests: How and Why to Do Them

## Summary

Unmoderated remote usability testing allows researchers to gather feedback from dozens or hundreds of users simultaneously without direct researcher-participant interaction. While faster and cheaper than moderated testing, unmoderated studies have important limitations: they are difficult for early-stage prototypes and participants tend to be less engaged in tasks requiring imagination or emotional investment. This article provides a six-step framework for planning and executing unmoderated studies, from defining clear goals through participant recruitment and results analysis, with emphasis on meticulous planning since no moderator can adapt procedures on the fly.

## Key Takeaways

- **Speed and scale advantage** — unmoderated testing eliminates scheduling individual meetings, allowing studies to launch and receive results within hours; enables collecting feedback from dozens or hundreds of simultaneous participants.
- **Limitations of unmoderated approach** — early-prototype testing is difficult without moderator explanation and error recovery; participants less engaged in tasks requiring imagination, decision-making, or emotional response behave less realistically.
- **Best suited for live products** — unmoderated studies work well for evaluating live websites, apps, or highly functional prototypes and activities not requiring significant imagination or emotion.
- **Meticulous task design essential** — task instructions must be exceptionally clear since participants cannot ask for clarification; must be specific and actionable without hints, and must explicitly state when to stop.
- **Pilot testing critical** — essential to discover problems with wording, task sequence, prototype functionality, and technical limitations before launching; run with real participants using their own equipment.

## Quotes

> Because there's no need to schedule an individual meeting with each participant, unmoderated testing is usually much faster than a moderated study. It may be possible to launch a study and receive results within just a few hours.

> Unmoderated research requires even more meticulous planning that a moderated study, since you can't rely on human judgment to adapt the study procedures on the fly.

> Writing tasks is where most researchers fail in getting the results they need from their studies.
