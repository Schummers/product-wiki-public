# Supercharge UX Research by Automating Workflows and Repetitive Tasks

## Summary

User research is time- and resource-intensive, involving administrative overhead in recruiting, scheduling, study management, and data preparation. ResearchOps focuses on increasing productivity and efficiency in research processes. The article highlights practical automation techniques using tools like Zapier, webhooks, and local machine scripts to streamline repetitive tasks, reduce manual work, and enable researchers to focus on higher-value analysis and insight generation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Recruiting and scheduling automation** — Webhook-based scripts can automate sending personalized recruitment emails, screening responses, and scheduling participants across multiple platforms, reducing manual coordination overhead while working in batches to maintain quality control.
- **Reminder and consent workflows** — Automated systems can send confirmation emails, internal announcements, reminder notifications, and consent forms at appropriate times, reducing researcher burden and improving participant engagement.
- **Diary study tracking** — Automated scripts monitoring email or survey submissions can track participant progress in diary studies, updating logs when entries are received and prompting reminders to participants who fall behind.
- **Incentive delivery automation** — Integration with platforms like BHN Rewards enables automatic distribution of incentives to participants upon study completion, eliminating manual payment processing.
- **Data preparation pipeline** — Automated uploads of completed survey responses directly into analysis tools like Dovetail enable real-time tracking of study progress, coding, and analysis without manual data transfer.
- **Custom webhooks and local automation** — Beyond commercial tools like Zapier, researchers can write custom webhooks, Google Apps scripts, or use tools like Apple Automator to automate file preparation, participant folder setup, and desktop environment configuration.

## Quotes

> "By setting up these automations, researchers can schedule study sessions without running through the overhead of emailing, coordinating with potential participants, and keeping track of the recruitment progress."

> "Look for manual and repetitive activities such as the need to move data from one tool to another and the need to manually track and document the progress of a study."
