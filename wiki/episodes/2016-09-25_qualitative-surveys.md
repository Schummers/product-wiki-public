# 28 Tips for Creating Great Qualitative Surveys

## Summary

Qualitative surveys ask open-ended questions to gather rich feedback and discover what questions to ask before conducting quantitative surveys. Key strategies include iterative testing with 4+ rounds (recruiting from target audience), randomizing questions to avoid order bias, using conditional logic to avoid irrelevant questions, keeping surveys short to maximize completion, placing required/optional indicators after each question, and front-loading important questions. Data analysis requires realistic acknowledgment that results represent respondents' opinions, not population-wide statistics. Coding responses to find trends and using visualization make findings actionable.

## Key Takeaways

- **Test surveys iteratively with target users** — Conduct at least 4 think-aloud test rounds with one participant per round, using real target-audience members rather than colleagues. Test on paper first, then in the survey platform, checking output format for analyzability.
- **Keep surveys short to maximize completion** — Every extra question reduces response rate and decreases validity. Twenty questions are too many unless participants are highly motivated. One-question surveys achieve highest participation. Segment audiences and conduct multiple short surveys rather than one long survey.
- **Mark required and optional clearly** — Red asterisks and top-level instructions don't work well. Add "(Required)" or "(Optional)" after each question so users understand without relying on subtle visual cues.
- **Use conditional questions to avoid frustration** — Show users only questions relevant to their previous answers. This keeps surveys short for each respondent and prevents users from encountering unanswerable questions that bias results.
- **Randomize question and answer order to prevent bias** — Long alphabetical lists and fixed orderings bias responses. Randomly vary the order of sections and questions so people quitting partway through don't skew overall data, and first-position options don't attract disproportionate attention.
- **Open-ended questions reveal what to ask** — Qualitative surveys using open-ended questions help discover appropriate answer categories for later quantitative surveys, preventing the mistake of going straight to closed-ended questions designed from conference-room assumptions.

## Quotes

> Improve surveys through iterative testing with open-ended feedback. Test surveys on paper first to save time-consuming rework in the testing platform.

> Better to administer 2 short surveys to 2 different subsamples of your audience than to lump everything you want to know into a long survey that won't be completed by the average customer.

> Qualitative survey metrics are rarely representative for the whole target audience; instead, they represent the opinions of the respondents.
