# User-Experience Quiz: 2018 UX Year in Review

## Summary

This article presents a 10-question quiz reviewing key UX concepts published by Nielsen Norman in 2018. The quiz covers topics including the authority principle, web reading patterns, user behavior patterns (the Vortex), inverted-pyramid writing, research methodologies (between-subjects design), UX visualization methods (empathy maps), questionnaire types (post-test versus post-task), design review methods, memory types, and mobile carousel usability. The quiz serves as a self-assessment tool for UX practitioners to test their knowledge while reinforcing central concepts from the year's published content.

## Key Takeaways

- **Authority principle reflects compliance tendency** — The correct answer defines the authority principle as the tendency to comply with those in positions of authority, grounded in research showing that people often defer to authority figures even when they should question instructions.

- **F-shaped web-reading pattern is not absolute** — The F-pattern describes common scanning behavior but does not always occur; scanning patterns vary with content and user goals. The pattern itself is neither inherently positive nor negative; design can either support or interfere with natural scanning.

- **Vortex describes unplanned interaction spirals** — The Vortex behavior pattern describes rapid, frequent switching among windows or tabs, distinct from deliberate tab-opening for saved reference items or simple window switching.

- **Inverted-pyramid writing benefits web audiences** — Inverted-pyramid style (leading with most important information) suits web reading and doesn't negatively affect comprehension; it differs from academic writing structures.

- **Between-subjects study design assigns different participants to different versions** — In between-subjects designs, different people test each design version (each exposed to only one), contrasting with within-subjects designs where the same person tests multiple versions.

## Concepts

- [[Authority Principle]] — A cognitive principle describing the tendency to comply with those in positions of authority, applicable to UX through trustworthiness signals and expert positioning.

- [[F-Pattern]] — A common web-reading scanning pattern forming an F shape, though not universal; design can support or interfere with this natural behavior.

- [[User Behavior]] — Observable patterns in how users interact with digital systems, including the Vortex (rapid tab-switching), tab-hoarding (opening multiple tabs for later), and others.
