# Less Chat, More Answer: Site AI Chatbots Need to Get to the Point

## Summary

Users treat site chatbots like search bars: they type minimal queries, expect quick responses, and prefer scannable answers. They are not seeking conversation. Users type incomplete sentences and typos, grow impatient with long responses, and abandon chatbots that waste time. Effective site chatbots eliminate pleasantries, organize responses for scanning (bullets, headers, white space), follow the truncated-pyramid rule (essential answer first, detail on demand), and avoid overwhelming users. When a chatbot cannot help, it should say so plainly without padding or lengthy explanations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Transactional, not conversational** — Users type minimally, don't say "hello" or "please," and grow increasingly terse as trust builds; they want answers, not relationships.
- **Cut the pleasantries** — Remove "great question" and sycophantic responses; they only lengthen replies without adding value.
- **Scannable formatting critical** — Keep sentences short, paragraphs to 2-3 sentences, use lists, bold text, headers, and white space to break up dense text.
- **Truncated pyramid rule** — Give only the essential answer first, offer relevant follow-up prompts so users can pull detail on demand; avoid overwhelming.
- **Specific beats generic** — Provide ranges or estimates when exact data unavailable; generic responses that account for multiple scenarios are longer and less useful.
- **Plain refusals** — When chatbot cannot help, say so clearly without lengthy explanations or padding with irrelevant advice.
