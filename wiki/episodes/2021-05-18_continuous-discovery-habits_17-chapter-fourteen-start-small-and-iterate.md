# Continuous Discovery Habits: Chapter Fourteen: Start Small, and Iterate

## Summary

Part 3 of *Continuous Discovery Habits*, this chapter addresses the practical challenge that most product teams work in organizations that don't yet support modern discovery practices. Torres draws on her own career starting at HighWire Press in 1999, where she lacked organizational support for user-centered design but found ways to advocate for customers by starting small and iterating. The chapter teaches that teams have more agency than they think—you don't need permission or top-down support to build discovery practices; you can start with one conversation, one prototype, one trio member, and gradually expand your capabilities.

## Key Takeaways

- **Build your cross-functional trio, even if informally** — if a designer or engineer isn't interested in the full discovery partnership, start by asking them to weigh in on one small decision, then iterate.
- **Continuous interviewing is the keystone habit** — once a team talks to customers weekly, they naturally adopt other habits like rapid prototyping, assumption testing, and connecting research to decisions; this is where to start if you can only start one place.
- **Work backward when given a solution** — ask "If customers had this solution, what would it do for them?" and "What value would this feature create for the business?" to uncover the implied opportunity and outcome.
- **Use retrospectives to improve discovery** — regularly ask "What surprised us?" and "How could we have learned that sooner?" to identify gaps in your discovery process and close them iteratively.
- **Focus on what you control, not what you can't** — don't wait for permission or organizational transformation; make your own work better by starting small and iterating; next week should look better than last week.
- **Instrument releases to measure impact** — document expected impact for features, measure actual impact post-release, and use gaps as opportunities to show stakeholders the value of discovery work.

## Quotes

> Keystone habits start a process that, over time, transforms everything.

> So, I encourage you to consider what you can do and let go of the "That would never work here" mentality that is so easy to fall into.

## Concepts

- [[Continuous Interviewing]] — continuous interviewing, particularly talking to customers weekly, serves as the foundational, highest-leverage habit in any organizational context, requiring no special permissions and enabling adoption of other discovery practices like rapid testing and opportunity mapping.
- [[Product Trio]] — even in organizations without formal trio structures, individuals can build informal cross-functional partnerships by starting with small collaborative decisions.
- [[Change Management]] — the chapter emphasizes that individuals have more power to shape how they work than they typically recognize; organizational constraints don't eliminate opportunities for user-centered practice.
- [[Continuous Improvement]] — regular team reflection on what was learned and how to learn better sooner is a continuous-improvement mechanism for discovery practices.
