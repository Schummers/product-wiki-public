# A Model for Conducting UX Workshops and Exercises

## Summary

Effective UX workshops use a three-step model: Explain, Execute, and Examine. The Explain phase introduces the activity and establishes neutral, unbiased foundations without stating predetermined outcomes. The Execute phase lets participants carry out the activity while facilitators observe and remain out of focus. The Examine phase includes three parts: presenting outcomes, reflecting on the experience, and connecting outputs to broader learning. This model enables teams to collaborate effectively, share understanding, and produce actionable insights while avoiding bias and enabling diverse participation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Explain clearly without bias** — Introduce activities by describing steps, defining roles, explaining context, and answering questions. Avoid stating what will be learned or using biased framing that influences participants.
- **Facilitate rather than contribute** — Step back during execution; avoid playing both facilitator and participant roles since facilitators hold authority and their contributions receive disproportionate weight.
- **Create space for reflection** — Use silence intentionally, ask reflective questions about what participants experienced, and have team members explain from their own perspectives and roles.
- **Connect to broader learning** — After the activity, synthesize what was learned and connect outputs to previous work and future activities to ensure continuity.
- **Iterate your facilitation approach** — Document what went well and where difficulties arose, observe other facilitators to learn different styles, and continuously improve your facilitation practice.

## Quotes

> Effective groupwork facilitation means that participants have enough guidance to move quickly and effectively through activities, while having the agency and freedom to produce actionable new insights and ideas to move projects forward.

> Don't be afraid of silence. Silence is often needed so people can think and formulate their comments or questions.

> A skilled facilitator has a framework that he or she feels confident adapting to any exercise.
