# Employees as Usability-Test Participants

## Summary

Using employees as usability-test participants saves time and money but introduces systematic biases that compromise research validity. Employees possess prior knowledge about the company and project, higher motivation to engage, differing perspectives on feasibility and resources, and fear of disappointing teams. Research confirms that employee behavior differs significantly from actual customer behavior—they are more critical of competitors and less critical of their own company. Testing with employees is appropriate only for intranets (where employees are the target audience), pilot tests to refine methodology, or as a skunkworks technique in organizations resistant to UX investment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Employee testing introduces systematic bias** — Employees' prior knowledge of company jargon, business goals, and the project itself leads them to behave differently than external users. Yahoo! research showed employees rated their company's site 2% higher than external users rated it, while external users rated it 15% lower than competitors.
- **Prior knowledge distorts task performance and attitudes** — Employees spend less time on competitor sites, are more critical of competitors, feel obligated to complete tasks, and may fear disappointing designers or insulting their work—all skewing results toward the company's interests.
- **Use employees only in specific limited cases** — Legitimate uses include testing intranets (employees are the target audience), conducting pilot tests to identify methodology flaws, or demonstrating UX value in organizations lacking UX maturity. Find employees with minimal project knowledge when possible.
- **External recruitment is the only reliable method** — 30 years of usability research conclusively shows external participants provide more trustworthy and generalizable data than employees, despite employees potentially enjoying higher credibility within the company.
- **Data quality beats accessibility** — While internal recruitment is convenient and cheap, acting on biased employee data risks wasting resources without serving actual customer needs. Inconclusive results may gain false credibility simply because they reflect views of trusted colleagues.

## Quotes

> Both preference and performance data were skewed, and the research would have been misleading if conducted purely with employees.

> Don't fall into that trap: by taking employees studies ad litteram and acting upon their findings, you risk wasting a lot of time and money without fulfilling the needs of your actual audience.

> Recruiting employees as test participants is not a replacement for recruiting externally. Limit its use to these few scenarios because it can prevent your organization from recognizing the benefits of recruiting externally.
