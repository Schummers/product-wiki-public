# Statistical Significance Isn't the Same as Practical Significance

## Summary

Statistical significance—whether a result is unlikely to be random—is not the same as practical significance—whether the result is big enough to matter in real life. In UX research, a statistically significant finding may represent a trivial effect that does not justify design investment, while a small-sample study with no statistical significance may reveal patterns worth investigating. Sample size complicates the picture: large datasets make tiny differences appear significant, while small samples may hide meaningful patterns. Researchers must evaluate both dimensions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Statistical vs practical** — A result can be statistically significant while having no meaningful impact on user experience; conversely, meaningful patterns may lack statistical significance in small samples.
- **Large sample risk** — With massive datasets, even microscopic differences (0.03% reduction) can achieve statistical significance without affecting real-world user perception.
- **Small sample insight** — Small-sample studies can reveal practical significance when patterns are large and consistent; a 10-of-12 versus 1-of-12 task failure rate shows meaningful difference without p-values.
- **Context-dependent value** — Practical significance depends on scale and context; a $2 improvement per conversion becomes $150,000 annually at 2 million annual conversions.
- **Three evaluation lenses** — Assess practical significance by asking: Would users notice? Does it support business outcomes? What does effect size tell us?

## Quotes

> "Reliability and impact are not the same thing. In this example, the completion rate for design B is higher than design A by just 0.2%. We also need to ask whether the effect is big enough to matter."

> "Many impactful UX insights come from qualitative studies: watching users struggle, listening to their reasoning, or noticing patterns in behavior that don't require large samples or statistical tests to be compelling."
