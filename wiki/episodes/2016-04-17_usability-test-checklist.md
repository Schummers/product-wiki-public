# Checklist for Planning Usability Studies

## Summary

Effective usability studies require thorough preparation despite the fundamental methods being straightforward. The article provides a nine-point checklist for planning usability studies, covering goal definition, format selection, participant recruitment, task design, pilot testing, metrics, test plans, and team coordination. Proper planning directly impacts research ROI; the checklist ensures teams avoid common pitfalls and allocate time and resources effectively. The guide distinguishes between qualitative studies (which benefit from 5 participants) and quantitative studies (which require 20-30+ participants), and emphasizes that clear goals, representative participants, and realistic task scenarios are prerequisites for valid findings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Define focused goals** — Meet with stakeholders to identify specific questions and concerns; avoid overcommitting to too many goals, as additional questions degrade insight quality on primary research goals.
- **Choose appropriate format** — Consider in-lab vs. field, moderated vs. unmoderated, and in-person vs. remote based on constraints and the criticality of observing actual contexts.
- **Recruit representative participants** — Get real users who match target demographics and behavioral characteristics; using proxy users or those who must imagine scenarios produces invalid results, especially for specialized or content-rich sites.
- **Write tasks matching study goals** — Tasks should be concrete, free of clues that prime behavior, and match research goals; distinguish between exploratory (open-ended) and specific (defined endpoint) tasks.
- **Run a pilot study** — Test task wording, timing, recruitment criteria, and setup with 1-2 participants before formal sessions to catch problems early.
- **Document in a test plan** — Communicate approach to team members and create a record; the plan should cover product name, goals, logistics, participant profiles, tasks, metrics, and system description.

## Quotes

> Even though the basic methods are easy enough, if you jump into a study without proper preparation, you won't get nearly as high a return on your research investment.

> For traditional qualitative studies, we generally recommend 5 participants for the best return on investment.

> A foundational rule in conducting user testing is to get representative participants. The greatest insights are derived from gathering feedback from real users.
