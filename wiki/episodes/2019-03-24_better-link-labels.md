# Better Link Labels: 4Ss for Encouraging Clicks

## Summary

Link text is one of the most critical elements of web navigation and accessibility. Users rely on link labels to understand where they will go and whether content is worth clicking. The article outlines four essential qualities that make links effective: they should be Specific about the destination, Sincere in setting accurate expectations, Substantial enough to stand alone, and Succinct while remaining informative.

## Key Takeaways

- **Specific — what users will find** — Vague links like "Learn more" fail to communicate what content awaits, forcing users to guess about the destination and reducing click likelihood.

- **Sincere — expectations met immediately** — Link labels must set expectations that are instantly fulfilled when clicked; delayed results erode user trust in the site and the organization.

- **Substantial — stand alone without context** — Links must be readable and meaningful on their own, since users scan pages and often read only the links, not surrounding text.

- **Succinct — concise yet complete** — Brevity is important but must not compromise the other three qualities; links can be long if necessary to meet user needs.

- **First words matter most** — Users scan link text by reading the first few words, so frontload information-carrying language to maximize comprehension during rapid scanning.

## Quotes

> "These Learn more links… It's just not very specific. When I click *Learn more*, what is that going to tell me? I want specifics. […] I have no idea what to expect, that's why I never click those."

> "Users allocate a minuscule click budget to spend on your site, so don't force them to spend those clicks in vain."

> "Especially since high-payoff clicks (to content that users want) can lead people to increase their click budget."

## Concepts

- [[Link Labels]] — The four Ss provide a framework for writing link text that is discoverable and trustworthy.

- [[Information Scent]] — Link labels must communicate what content lies beyond so users can anticipate whether to click.

- [[Navigation Design]] — Clear link labels are fundamental to helping users understand site structure and move through content.

- [[Scannability]] — Since users scan rather than read, links must be visually distinct and meaningful when read quickly.

- [[Information Architecture]] — Specific, substantial links help users locate the information they need without wasted clicks.
