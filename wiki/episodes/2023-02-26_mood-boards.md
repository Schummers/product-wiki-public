# Mood Boards in UX: How and Why to Use Them

## Summary

Mood boards are collaborative collages of images, colors, patterns, and text that convey the emotional feeling and visual direction a product should create. While commonly used in traditional design fields, mood boards are increasingly valuable in UX and UI design for aligning teams on aesthetic and emotional direction before prototyping begins. This article explains when to create mood boards (during define or ideate phases), how to create them (review existing materials, select tools, choose moods, collect visuals, arrange hierarchically, share for feedback), and how they support design goals beyond aesthetics, including tone-of-voice definition and interaction-design patterns. The article emphasizes that mood boards help teams generate ideas, quickly convey product tone, and align on visual direction.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mood boards align teams visually** — before detailed design work begins, mood boards help teams establish shared understanding of the product's visual and emotional direction, reducing future miscommunications.
- **Mood boards work throughout design** — they can be created early in new product design or during substantial revisions, and can incorporate tone-of-voice words, interaction patterns, and even persona characteristics.
- **Digital tools enable remote collaboration** — platforms like FigJam, Miro, or Mural allow distributed teams to build mood boards together asynchronously, eliminating physical-board limitations.
- **Unity requires thematic coherence** — all collected visuals should fall under one theme and convey the intended mood; contradicting aesthetics signal separate mood boards are needed.
- **Visual hierarchy focuses impact** — prioritizing key sources through size and central placement, and grouping similar items, makes mood boards more effective and easier to navigate.
- **Screenshots risk feature focus** — using too many competitive product screenshots shifts mood boards toward feature comparison rather than emotional and visual direction.

## Quotes

> "A **mood board** is a collage of images, video frames, patterns, or text that convey a certain feeling at a glance. Mood boards are widely used in various applications of visual art including branding, graphics, fashion, cinema, industrial and interior design."

> "In UX design, mood boards are used to **visually show the feelings or values that the digital product should create**. Mood boards are collages that are approachable and easy to understand — even for people with little design experience."

> "Although a mood board is a melting pot of diverse formats, they should all fall under a certain theme and convey the moods you've chosen. Avoid making a messy board with contradicting images."
