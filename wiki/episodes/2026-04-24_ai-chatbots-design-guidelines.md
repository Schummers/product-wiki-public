# 10 Guidelines for Designing Your Site's AI Chatbots

## Summary

Small design decisions have large impacts on whether users find site chatbots helpful. A study of 9 users with 8 chatbots across multiple sites identified 10 practical design guidelines: consolidate multiple chats, keep chatbots accessible across pages, clarify capabilities through opening messages and suggested prompts, offer suggested questions as clickable buttons, include images in recommendations, use progressive disclosure to keep chats short, avoid autoscrolling users, allow window resizing, enable saving/sharing, and consider voice input. These guidelines help users discover the chatbot, understand its scope, and use it efficiently.

## Key Takeaways

- **Single entry point** — Consolidate AI and human chat into one; multiple chatbots confuse users about which to use for what.
- **Persistent across pages** — Users expect chatbots to follow as they browse; disappearing chatbots are abandoned.
- **Clarify in opening message** — Tell users upfront what the chatbot can do; avoid vague greetings like "ask me anything."
- **Context-aware suggestions** — Tailor suggested prompts to the current page; show the chatbot knows what the user is viewing.
- **Clickable prompts** — Present suggested questions as buttons, not text; reduces friction and supports multiple suggestions without walls of text.
- **Images in responses** — Product images, diagrams, and visuals make recommendations easier to evaluate than text-only.
- **Progressive disclosure** — Let users expand/collapse details rather than appending to chat; keeps conversation shorter.
- **No autoscroll** — Keep scroll position at message start; users need to read from beginning.
- **Resizable windows** — Support larger windows for rich content like maps and product comparisons.
- **Save/share capability** — Enable users to save recipes, guides, and comparisons for future reference.
