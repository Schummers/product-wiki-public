# Designing for Kids: Cognitive Considerations

## Summary

This article examines how children's cognitive development stages (preoperational ages 2–7 and concrete operational ages 7–11) affect web and app design. Children's cognitive abilities including theory of mind, cognitive flexibility, and executive function are still developing. The article presents five design recommendations grounded in cognitive psychology, demonstrating how understanding these developmental differences enables designers to create usable and appealing experiences for younger audiences.

## Key Takeaways

- **State goals and operations explicitly** — Clear, specific visual and audio instructions about game objectives and how to achieve them are essential. Children cannot infer unstated goals; they need explicit guidance on what to accomplish and the steps required.

- **Tailor instructions to comprehension level** — Instructions must match children's cognitive development stage. Young children under 6 cannot reliably interpret subtle feedback or infer others' emotions (theory of mind limitations), requiring exaggerated cues and explicit language.

- **Leverage existing mental models** — Skeuomorphic design and real-world metaphors (coloring books, cooking) leverage children's existing knowledge, reducing cognitive load. Children naturally knew how to make pasta in an app designed like a real kitchen.

- **Reduce cognitive load through design** — Young children have smaller working memory capacity than adults. Self-explanatory interfaces, error prevention, and autocorrect functionality relieve cognitive burden. Search autocorrection helps children compensate for spelling difficulties.

- **Balance clarity with flexibility** — Instructions should be clear and specific but avoid being overly prescriptive. Specifying which input device (mouse versus trackpad) can frustrate children who prefer different tools and limits their ability to complete tasks.

## Concepts

- [[Cognitive Development]] — Children's reasoning, inference, and connection-making abilities develop through stages (preoperational, concrete operational), affecting their ability to understand interfaces and tasks.

- [[Young Users]] — Designing for users aged 3–12 requires understanding developmental stages, reducing cognitive load, leveraging mental models, and providing explicit instructions tailored to comprehension level.

- [[Mental Model]] — Children apply existing knowledge of the real world to interfaces, meaning skeuomorphic designs and familiar metaphors help them learn quickly.
