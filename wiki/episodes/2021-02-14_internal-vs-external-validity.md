# Internal vs. External Validity of UX Studies

## Summary

Research validity has two dimensions: internal validity (study does not bias participants toward particular responses) and external validity (participants and setup represent the real-world situation). Both are distinct from reliability (reproducibility of results). Internal validity errors occur when study design favors certain responses through confounding variables, task order bias, or facilitator effects. External validity errors occur when participants or study setup are not representative of real-world use. Neither type of error is unavoidable, but both require deliberate attention to study design and conscious tradeoffs when perfect validity is impossible.

## Key Takeaways

- **Internal validity prevents design bias** — a study has internal validity if it does not favor particular participant responses; task order, facilitator style, and study protocols must be randomized or controlled.
- **Confounding variables hide real effects** — hidden factors like time of day, facilitator differences, or study protocol changes can produce results that don't reflect reality.
- **External validity requires representative participants** — participants must match target audience in demographics, goals, and motivation; proxy users (those pretending to be in a situation) lack authenticity.
- **Study setup should replicate real conditions** — testing mobile designs on desktop, testing in lab rather than field environments, or other deviations from natural use contexts reduce external validity.
- **Randomization and counterbalancing ensure internal validity** — random task order, warmup tasks, and randomized condition order (which site participants see first) prevent biasing results.
- **Sacrificing validity requires interpretation caution** — when external validity must be sacrificed (e.g., paper prototyping due to cost), results must be interpreted as best-case scenarios or laboratory findings that need field retesting.

## Quotes

> "A study has internal validity if it does not favor or encourage any particular participant response or behavior."

> "A study has external validity if the participants and the study setup are representative for the real-world situation in which the design is used."

> "reliability is no good without validity: a study with high reliability and low validity is one where you get a really good measurement of the wrong thing."
