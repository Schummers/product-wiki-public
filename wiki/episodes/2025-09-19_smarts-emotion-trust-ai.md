# Prioritize Smarts over Sentience to Increase Trust with AI

## Summary

Common design intuition suggests anthropomorphizing AI—making it seem human-like and emotional—builds user trust. Recent research challenges this assumption. A study of 410 participants and ChatGPT revealed that users trust AI more when perceiving it as intelligent (capable of reasoning and planning) rather than emotional. Supplementary research shows that making AI warmer and more empathetic actually reduces its reliability and makes it more sycophantic, particularly problematic for mental health applications. The article recommends positioning AI as a competent tool rather than a friend or conscious entity.

## Key Takeaways

- **Intelligence predicts trust-in-advice better than emotional warmth** — As perceived intelligence increases, users increase willingness to accept AI advice (B = 0.31, p < .001); perceived emotions decrease trust (B = –1.04, p = .042).
- **Warm, empathetic training reduces AI reliability** — Models fine-tuned for warmth showed 10–30% higher error rates; system prompts encouraging warmth produced 12–14% reliability drops, more likely to promote conspiracy theories and give harmful medical advice.
- **Emotional AI fails in sensitive contexts** — Warm, empathetic LLMs exhibit higher sycophancy, which makes them unsuitable for mental health applications where they may encourage delusional thinking or mishandle crises.
- **Competence doesn't require impersonal robotic tone** — AI can acknowledge user feelings, use pronouns, and have clear identity without claiming to have emotions; professional tone builds trust through perceived competence, not false emotional rapport.
- **Finetuning toward competence over sentience increases safety** — Positioning AI as a tool with transparent limitations, using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for sourcing, and rewarding professional interactions create more trustworthy systems.

## Concepts

- [[AI Trust]] — Proposes that trust in AI for factual tasks depends on perceived intelligence and reasoning capability, not emotional warmth; emotional anthropomorphism reduces rather than increases trust.
- [[Anthropomorphism]] — Challenges the intuitive design pattern of humanizing AI through emotional expressions; competent, professional positioning builds more reliable trust than false emotional connection.
- [[AI Reliability]] — Shows empirical tradeoffs: optimizing for warmth and empathy degrades model reliability and increases hallucinations; task-focused positioning improves both reliability and user trust.
- [[Design Patterns]] — Recommends positioning AI truthfully as a tool, narrowing feature scope, prioritizing sourcing (RAG), maintaining transparency about limitations, and selecting professional interactions during finetuning.
