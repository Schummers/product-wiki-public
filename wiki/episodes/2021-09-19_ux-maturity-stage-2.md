# UX-Maturity Stage 2: Limited

## Summary

Stage 2 organizations make small, inconsistent UX efforts driven by a few advocates rather than organization-wide practice. UX has low priority and lacks official recognition, dedicated roles, or appropriate budget. While some employees understand UX's value, that knowledge doesn't spread across the organization. UX maturity comprises four factors: strategy, culture, process, and outcomes. At stage 2, all four areas show limited development, with scattered UX activities often replacing rigorous research and design methods.

## Key Takeaways

- **Limited and erratic effort** — UX work is driven by a few passionate individuals rather than systematic practice. Small pockets of UX activity exist but lack organization-wide support or consistency.
- **Poor method execution** — Organizations substitute proper UX methods with shortcuts like informal checklists instead of user research, or focus groups instead of usability testing. Research conducted by inexperienced people often yields poor or inaccurate results.
- **Design inconsistency** — Without shared tools or design systems, design inconsistencies are rampant. This fragmentation results from lack of UX coordination across teams.
- **Misuse of metrics** — Organizations may collect quantitative data but misuse it, relying on vanity metrics from analytics that feel useful without being actionable.
- **Progression requires culture and process** — Moving to stage 3 requires building organizational understanding of UX's value and investing in proper method execution. This often involves recruiting more UX advocates and expanding the UX toolkit.

## Quotes

> Stage-2 organizations might reference users in their vision, but users are not their primary focus. User needs and behaviors are not core to the vision.

> Typically, stage-2 organizations do have some vague awareness of what UX is — most employees have at least heard of it. However, that understanding is often incorrect, inconsistent, or limited across the organization.

> To move up from stage 2 to stage 3, focus on two key maturity factors: culture and process.
