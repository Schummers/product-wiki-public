# Remote Ideation: Synchronous vs. Asynchronous

## Summary

Remote ideation can be as effective as in-person brainstorming and offers flexibility in structure. Synchronous ideation occurs at a specific time when all participants meet (via video, screen-sharing, or conference calls) to discuss and contribute ideas directly. Asynchronous ideation occurs over time in a shared digital space (document, message thread, or tool) where participants contribute whenever convenient. Both approaches have benefits and drawbacks. Synchronous sessions enable fast idea exchange, focused attention within time limits, and team building; disadvantages include scheduling difficulty across time zones and workloads, remote-meeting awkwardness (simultaneous talking, long silences), and difficulty finding tools supporting multiple concurrent idea sketches. Asynchronous ideation avoids scheduling conflicts and allows time for ideas to incubate; disadvantages include fewer team-building opportunities, reduced attention over time, and time spent catching up on prior contributions. Neither is inherently better; choose based on deadline, team timezone spread, and complexity; hybrid approaches (kickoff meeting followed by asynchronous contribution) combine benefits of both.

## Key Takeaways

- **Synchronous Ideation Is Fast and Focused** — real-time meetings enable rapid back-and-forth, focused attention within set time limits, and team bonding; best for quick decisions, complex problems requiring discussion, and time-sensitive projects.
- **Asynchronous Ideation Suits Distributed Teams** — asynchronous contribution allows participation across time zones and busy schedules; enables incubation time for deeper thinking; best for longer timelines and teams that are geographically or schedually dispersed.
- **Synchronous Challenges Include Scheduling and Awkwardness** — finding common time zones is difficult; remote meetings suffer from simultaneous talking, awkward silences, and group-dynamics issues that slow participation and idea flow.
- **Asynchronous Challenges Include Attention and Catch-Up** — interest can fade over days or weeks; participants must spend time re-reading prior contributions and getting up to speed each visit.

## Quotes

> Remote ideation can be just as effective, and opens up more options for how to structure and run the idea-generation process.

> Synchronous sessions work best when you need to generate and choose an idea quickly, in order to move forward in the design process. They also work well when the situation is complex and requires back-and-forth discussion, as well as the ability to talk through potential solutions together.

> You can always blend the two methods! You could conduct a synchronous ideation kickoff meeting to talk through the issue at hand and perhaps capture some of the team's initial ideas, then open up a shared document or thread for people to contribute additional ideas asynchronously for a designated period of time after that meeting.

## Concepts

- [[Ideation]] — Real-time collaborative brainstorming via video, calls, or screen-sharing enables fast idea exchange, focused attention, and team bonding suited for quick decisions and complex problems, while distributed ideation via shared documents, message threads, or tools allowing contribution over time enables schedule flexibility across time zones and idea incubation suited for longer timelines.
- [[Remote Work]] — remote teams can combine synchronous and asynchronous ideation: kickoff meeting to frame the problem, then asynchronous contribution for expanded idea generation, then synchronous convergence on final choices.
