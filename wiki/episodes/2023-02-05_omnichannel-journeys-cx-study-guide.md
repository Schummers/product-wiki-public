# Omnichannel Journeys and Customer Experience: Study Guide

## Summary

A comprehensive study guide curating Nielsen Norman Group resources on customer journey design and omnichannel experience. The guide covers customer journey and CX fundamentals, research and mapping methods, designing effective omnichannel experiences (consistency, optimization, seamlessness, orchestration, collaboration), and journey management from an organizational perspective. Resources are organized in recommended reading order, progressing from foundational concepts through specialized topics, helping readers understand journey-level experience design beyond single interactions.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX exists at multiple levels** — Beyond individual interaction design, UX operates at journey level (interactions over time and across devices) and relationship level (ongoing customer-organization relationships).
- **Omnichannel components matter** — Effective omnichannel experiences require five elements: Consistency (cohesive experience across channels), Optimization (designs for device and context), Seamlessness (effortless transitions), Orchestration (proactive personalized guidance), and Collaboration (multiple channels solving problems together).
- **Research methods for journeys differ** — Context-research methods (field studies, diary studies) better capture longitudinal experiences; journey maps and service blueprints visualize findings and serve as strategic ideation tools.
- **Cross-functional collaboration essential** — Journey management requires organizational change; teams from different functions (product, marketing, support) must collaborate to design cohesive experiences across touchpoints.
- **Journey management as operational practice** — Mature organizations adopt journey-centric approaches requiring continuous practice of three competencies: collecting insights, analyzing for strategy, and orchestrating experiences.
- **Organizational fluidity needed** — Traditional organizational silos prevent effective journey design; adopting journey-centric approaches requires structural and operational adaptations.
