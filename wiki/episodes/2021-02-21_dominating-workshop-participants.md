# How to Handle Dominating Participants in UX Workshops: 3 Tactics

## Summary

Dominating workshop participants monopolize conversations, drown out diverse perspectives, and limit group discussion richness. Three participant types commonly dominate: helpful HIPPOs (high-paid persons intimidating others unintentionally), eager enthusiasts (overpassionate advocates), and enlightened experts (defensive about their authority). Facilitators can prevent dominance through preparatory measures (partial attendance, balanced team breakdowns, ground rules) and intervene with three ascending tactics: redirect (refocus on shared goals), balance (invite others' contributions), and silence (remove verbal contributions through structured activities like round-robin, quota, silent posting, or brainwriting).

## Key Takeaways

- **Three common dominator types have different motivations** — HIPPOs dominate by intimidating others unintentionally, enthusiasts dominate through passion, and experts dominate defensively or from belief in their special knowledge.
- **Preparation prevents dominance** — inviting known dominators only to segments, balancing team composition, establishing ground rules, and clarifying shared goals reduce dominance before intervention.
- **Redirect refocuses dominating participants** — using a visible group-purpose statement or parking-lot technique channels off-topic contributions while signaling that contributions are valued but not immediate.
- **Balance encourages quieter participants** — round-robin structures ensure everyone speaks in turn; quota systems request specific numbers of contributions from each participant.
- **Silence equalizes participation through structure** — silent postup (sticky-note activity) and brainwriting (written idea rounds) remove the power of louder voices and ensure introverts' ideas are heard.
- **Facilitators must assume good intent** — most dominators are not malicious; they may be unaware of their impact and appreciate the chance to contribute productively.

## Quotes

> "Dominating workshop participants are those who, for a variety of reasons, seek to take control of the workshop by monopolizing conversations or activities, so that their own ideas are at the center of the group's focus."

> "The role of a facilitator is to hear and capture the consensus of the group — not the perspective of the loudest person."

> "Don't write off the dominator as a nuisance with no value to add."
