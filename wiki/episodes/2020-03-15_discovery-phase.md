# Discovery: Definition

## Summary

This article defines and contextualizes the discovery phase as a preliminary UX design stage focused on researching the problem space, framing problems, and gathering evidence to guide future work. Discovery ensures that solutions are desirable to users, viable for the organization, and feasible with available technology. The article outlines common discovery activities, people involved, instigators for discovery, and typical outcomes, positioning discovery as essential to directing projects toward solving the right problems.

## Key Takeaways

- **Definition and Scope** — discovery is a preliminary phase involving research of the problem space, framing of problems, and gathering evidence to determine what to do next; it covers both the Discover and Define stages of the double-diamond model.
- **Four Key Outcomes** — successful discovery results in understanding of users and their needs, understanding of problems and opportunities, identification of existing constraints, and shared vision across the team about goals and measures of success.
- **Discovery is Needed** — when many unknowns prevent moving forward, when the team is not aligned on goals, or when facing new market opportunities, acquisitions, policy changes, organizational strategy shifts, or chronic problems.
- **Common Activities** — exploratory research (user interviews, diary studies, field studies), stakeholder interviews, workshops (kickoff, assumption-mapping, research-question generation, affinity diagramming, mapping, ideation), and follow-up exploration.
- **Team Composition** — ideally 3-7 dedicated team members including someone who can research, a facilitator or team leader, a sponsor or owner with domain expertise and organizational influence, and someone technical.
- **Deliverables** — typical discovery outputs include problem statements, finalized maps, user-need statements, personas, and high-level concepts or wireframes for exploration in the develop phase.

## Quotes

> **Discovery** is a preliminary phase in the UX-design process that involves researching the problem space, framing the problem(s) to be solved, and gathering enough evidence and initial direction on what to do next.

> Well-done discoveries ensure that any solutions proposed later are desirable to users, viable for the organization, and feasible with the available technology.

> A discovery starts broad and requires team members to investigate the context of the problem.
