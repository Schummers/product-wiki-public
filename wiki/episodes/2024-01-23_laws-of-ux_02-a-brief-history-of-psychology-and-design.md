# Laws of UX: A Brief History of Psychology and Design

## Summary

This chapter traces the intellectual history of how psychology shaped design and human-computer interaction. Beginning with Gestalt psychology in the early 20th century, which established that humans perceive wholes rather than isolated elements, the chapter traces the discipline through human factors engineering, which emerged during World War II to optimize equipment interfaces for human limitations. It progresses through the Cold War era when psychology combined with computer science to improve human-computer interaction, highlighting pivotal contributions from Xerox PARC, and finally to the emergence of user experience design as a discipline. Key figures like Donald Norman and Jane Fulton Suri established the importance of designing systems that align with human cognition rather than forcing users to adapt to technology.

## Key Takeaways

- **Gestalt psychology foundation** — The principles of Gestalt psychology (figure-ground, similarity, proximity, closure) established that humans perceive visual information as organized patterns rather than isolated sensory elements, principles still influential in design today.
- **Human factors engineering from WWII** — Psychologists like Paul Fitts and Alphonse Chapanis recognized that humans are error-prone, especially under stress, and that machines should be designed to align with human capabilities rather than requiring users to adapt.
- **Xerox PARC's psychological foundations** — The publication of *The Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction* (1983) by Card, Moran, and Newell established cognitive psychology as central to personal computing design; Smalltalk's metaphorical desktop mimicked the way people organize physical documents on a desk and became the mental model for later operating systems.
- **Engelbart and Kay** — Douglas Engelbart's work at the Stanford Research Institute produced the computer mouse, hypertext, networked computers and precursors to graphical user interfaces. Alan Kay, central to Smalltalk, drew on educational psychology: Seymour Papert's Logo project (Papert had spent five years working with Jean Piaget) convinced him programming languages should be built at a level children could use, and psychologist Jerome Bruner's study of learning mentalities in children was a further influence.
- **User experience as discipline** — Donald Norman coined the term "user experience" to encompass all aspects of the user's interaction with a system, while Jane Fulton Suri pioneered ethnographic research and human-centered design at IDEO, establishing psychology as the lens through which design decisions are made.

## Quotes

> It was psychologists who established human factors engineering, a discipline that focused on designing tools, machines, and systems that take into account human capabilities, limitations, and characteristics.

> Psychology has also played a fundamental role in shaping the development of computers and how we interact with them. The combination of psychology, systems analysis, and computer science became a defining characteristic of research institutes during the Cold War era, much of which was linked to the defense establishment and large mainframe computers.

> Psychology continues to inform how we can design systems that align with how people actually are, rather than how we want them to be. While technology and our interactions with it will keep evolving, psychology offers a lens to decipher the fundamental "blueprint" of how we perceive and process the world.

## Concepts

- [[Gestalt Principles]] — Visual perception principles from Gestalt psychology (figure-ground, similarity, proximity, closure) that continue to influence design by recognizing how humans organize sensory information into meaningful wholes.
- [[Human Factors Engineering]] — A discipline established by psychologists during World War II to design tools and systems that account for human capabilities and limitations, reducing errors by aligning machines with human nature rather than expecting adaptation.
- [[Interaction Design]] — The chapter traces the lineage the discipline came from: psychology, systems analysis and computer science combined in Cold War research institutes into the study of human–computer interaction, out of which UX design later emerged alongside the web and the need for better interaction design.
- [[User Experience]] — A discipline that emerged from HCI alongside the web, emphasizing design that encompasses all aspects of the user's interaction with a system; pioneered by Donald Norman and Jane Fulton Suri to center on human needs and psychological insights.
- [[Cognitive Psychology]] — The psychological study of how humans think, learn, and perceive, which has continuously informed the development of interfaces and systems from early computing through contemporary design.
