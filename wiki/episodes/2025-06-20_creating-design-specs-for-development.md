# Creating Design Specs for Development

## Summary

Design specifications are documents that align design and development teams on how to implement designs. A complete spec includes two components: a design file (e.g., Figma) containing visual and interaction details, and a development issue with strategic context, requirements, scope, and risks. The specification serves as a "contract" between teams, defining what the developer is responsible for delivering.

Effective specs are created only after substantial design work—discovery, ideation, prototyping, and testing—ensuring designs have been validated before implementation investment. Smaller, digestible specs are easier to create and implement than large, monolithic documents. Clear communication between design and development, including early involvement before designs are finalized, improves spec quality and reduces implementation surprises.

## Key Takeaways

- **Specs align teams on reality** — design files show what to build; development issues explain why, what the context is, what's in and out of scope, and what success looks like.
- **Design files specify all details** — interaction flows, visual design including color and typography, layout and breakpoints, components with states, real content, and accessibility needs.
- **Development issues provide context** — goals, use cases, functional and nonfunctional requirements, scope boundaries, assumptions, success metrics, and potential risks guide implementation decisions.
- **Smaller specs are easier to maintain** — when design becomes too large to organize, break it into smaller, digestible pieces defined in collaboration with developers.
- **Timing matters** — specs are created in the design phase after discovery, ideation, prototyping, and testing, not before validating designs are worth building.

## Quotes

> "Design specifications are a way to align on and efficiently communicate designs to a software-development team."

> "Small specs are much easier to create and implement than one very large spec. If a design spec is getting too large and difficult to organize and maintain, it might be a sign that it needs to be broken down into smaller, more digestible pieces."

> "Design specs allow the design and development teams to have productive conversations and align on user needs, design feasibility, and design and technical requirements."

## Concepts

- [[Documentation]] — comprehensive written and visual communication of design decisions to guide implementation and support team alignment.
- [[Developer Communication]] — practices and formats that enable designers to convey design intent, context, and requirements to development teams effectively.
- [[Design Specifications]] — formal documents combining visual design files and strategic context to define what will be built and why.
- [[Design-Development Collaboration]] — productive working relationships between design and development teams built on clear communication and mutual respect for each discipline's expertise.
