# Design Taste vs. Technical Skills in the Era of AI

## Summary

As generative AI tools democratize technical design capabilities, the distinction between who can create and who should create is shifting dramatically. While AI can produce visuals, copy, and interfaces quickly, the authors argue that technical ability was only ever one aspect of what designers bring to the table. The real differentiator—design taste, discernment, and strategic judgment—remains uniquely human and increasingly valuable as the barrier to creation disappears.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI enables non-designers to create** — generative tools eliminate traditional barriers to technical production, meaning anyone can now generate illustrations, website layouts, or copy without formal training.
- **Technical capability does not equal creative ability** — just because someone can produce something technically does not mean it will be high-quality, meet business goals, or resonate with users.
- **Taste is the new differentiator** — as technical skills become commoditized, design taste (discernment in making intentional choices that align with a central vision) becomes the primary distinction between professional designers and AI-assisted amateurs.
- **Taste requires training and context** — developing good design taste takes time, intentional practice, exposure to art history, and strategic thinking about how individual decisions serve larger business and user goals.
- **Photography as precedent** — the authors use smartphone cameras as a historical example: technical capability to take photos is now universal, but photographers with trained eyes still command value through composition, perspective, and artistic vision.
- **Design taste combines strategy and aesthetics** — effective designers judge not just how something looks in isolation but how hundreds of small decisions orchestrate around a vision that serves both user needs and business goals.

## Quotes

> "Taste is what enables designers to navigate the vast sea of possibilities that technology and global connectivity afford, and to then select and combine these elements in ways that, ideally, result in interesting, unique work."

> "While AI can produce all kinds of outputs, the quality of those outputs is not guaranteed. **Technical capability does not equal creative ability.** The perfect analogy came from Oisin Hurst on LinkedIn: *"* AI is to creativity what microwaves are to cooking."

> "Designers exhibit taste by being intentional in the choices they make. All details should work to expand and enhance a single organizing thought, idea, or vision."

## Concepts

- [[Design Taste]] — the authors redefine design taste as the intentional judgment required to navigate vast possibilities and orchestrate details around a central vision, making it essential in an AI-enabled world.
- [[AI in Design]] — AI tools democratize technical production but do not replace the strategic and aesthetic decision-making that distinguishes professional design work.
- [[Technical Skills]] — the article argues that technical skills, once a primary barrier to entry and a core designer skill, are increasingly commoditized by AI, reducing their value as a professional differentiator.
- [[Creative Ability]] — distinguished from technical capability, creative ability encompasses the vision, discernment, and strategic thinking that guides design decisions.
- [[Professional Differentiation]] — as technical barriers fall, designers must compete on taste, vision, and strategic insight rather than on ability to execute technically.
