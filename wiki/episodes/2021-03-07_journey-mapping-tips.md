# Getting Started with Journey Mapping: 27 Tips from Practitioners

## Summary

Journey mapping is a powerful but challenging practice for visualizing the complete customer experience. Based on surveys and interviews with over 300 UX professionals, this article consolidates 27 practical tips from practitioners who have successfully led journey-mapping initiatives. The tips address the full lifecycle of journey mapping: learning the fundamentals, defining clear goals and scope, gathering research, collaborating effectively, and visualizing the results. The article emphasizes that journey mapping is iterative, requires crossfunctional collaboration, should start with a small focused scope, and must be grounded in actual user research to be effective.

## Key Takeaways

- **Start small with focused scope** — Rather than attempting to map complex end-to-end journeys, practitioners recommend beginning with a smaller journey that has known pain points and achievable quick wins that your team has authority to address.
- **Use mixed research methods** — Combine qualitative methods like user interviews to understand what users think and feel, with quantitative data like analytics to show the scale of insights and validate findings.
- **Prioritize collaboration over perfection** — Journey-mapping workshops bring together crossfunctional teams to create maps collaboratively, building buy-in, but asynchronous approaches work when synchronous collaboration isn't feasible.
- **Keep it low-fidelity initially** — Start with sticky notes and paper-based approaches to make iteration easy; don't invest in high-fidelity designs until content and structure are validated.
- **Focus on actionable insights, not visualization** — The goal isn't just to create a nice-looking artifact; the real value comes from using the map to inform decisions and drive improvements to the experience.
- **Involve stakeholders early and often** — Share progress regularly with stakeholders and involve influential team members from the start to build alignment and overcome organizational silos.

## Quotes

> Leading a journey-mapping initiative is no small challenge. It takes product knowledge and research savvy, along with project- and stakeholder-management skills.

> Start small and scale. Accept that you don't map or `save' everything.

> Have a plan for what you are going to do with the information to make it actionable. It doesn't do any good to hang the map above the copy machine and expect everyone to know how that impacts their job/function.
