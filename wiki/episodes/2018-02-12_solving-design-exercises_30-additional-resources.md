# Solving Product Design Exercises: Additional Resources

## Summary

The final chapter of Solving Product Design Exercises is an annotated guide to external resources organized by topic: the UX interview process, learning product design and UX, career advice, hiring designers, and design's impact on society. Rather than making original claims, this chapter curates and recommends publicly available books, articles, videos, podcasts, and websites that Artiom Dashinsky found valuable during his research and practice, with brief explanations of what each resource offers and why it matters to designers and hiring managers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design interview questions require preparation** — Many design interview questions are behavioral and not unique to design roles; resources exist to help designers prepare, including guides on product critiques and lists of common UX interview questions from senior practitioners.
- **Design career paths are documented through interviews** — Books like "How They Got There" and "Design Founders" feature interviews with influential designers about their careers, providing real examples of diverse and non-linear career trajectories and decisions.
- **Multiple platforms offer design education and leadership resources** — High-Resolution features video interviews with design leaders at major tech companies and agencies; Case Study Club, DesignBetter.Co, and Nielsen Norman Group provide case studies, free books, and research on design thinking and design systems.
- **Design hiring is becoming more transparent and intentional** — Companies like BuzzFeed publish their hiring processes and designer leveling; resources from Khosla Ventures and Paul Adams provide frameworks for evaluating design skills and writing effective job descriptions.
- **Design has broader societal impact beyond product outcomes** — Resources like Offscreen Magazine, SustainableUX, Tragic Design, and Joe Macleod's book on product endings encourage designers to consider how their work affects society, the environment, and marginalized users.

## Quotes

> Many designers I spoke with during my research for this book said that they are intimidated by behavioural questions, like "What is your greatest strength?" or "Describe a stressful situation at work and how you handled it".

> Luckily, these questions aren't unique to design interviews, so there is a lot of content available on how to handle them well.

> It explores how poorly designed products can anger, sadden, exclude, and even kill people who use them, even though the designers responsible certainly didn't intend any harm.
