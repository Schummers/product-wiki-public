# Shopping Cart or Wishlist? Saving Products for Later in Ecommerce

## Summary

Shopping carts serve dual purposes: holding items for immediate purchase and storing items for future consideration. Users often employ carts as external memory tools and comparison tables rather than purely as purchase vehicles. The article examines how wishlists, favorites, and "save for later" features compete with carts for saving products, finding that users have misconceptions about wishlists (associating them with gift-sharing) and often prefer using carts instead. Effective save-for-later features must have high discoverability, clear labeling, low interaction cost, and should not require registration.

## Key Takeaways

- **Shopping carts function as external memory and consideration tools** — Users leverage carts to store and compare items across sessions, using them as sandboxes for decision-making rather than solely as vehicles for immediate purchase.

- **Wishlist labels carry greedy connotations that discourage use** — The term "wishlist" implies sharing gift ideas, making users reluctant to use it for personal saving; labels like "Favorites" or "My List" carry fewer negative associations.

- **Users perceive list savings as higher commitment than cart additions** — Mental models treat cart additions as "might want" and list additions as "definitely want," causing users to add items to carts first even when intending long-term saving.

- **Save for Later features must be highly discoverable and easy to use** — Hidden behind swipes, dropdowns, or low on pages, save features go unused; they must be visible, clearly labeled with strong information scent, and low-effort.

- **Never require registration for Save for Later functionality** — Forcing users to create accounts or log in to save items derails checkout and deters use; most users already provide necessary information during checkout.

- **Item preservation is critical for abandoned cart recovery** — Users want to avoid redoing work of finding products; providing easy save-for-later options prevents cart abandonment and maintains customer interest for future purchases.

## Quotes

> The shopping cart is not only a place to store items until purchase: it is a comparison table, a reference, a scrapbook for ideas.

> Users often wish to purchase only some of their cart's contents right away, but don't want to lose all the work that went into finding other products of interest.

> An item left in the shopping cart may actually be leading to a purchase later on.
