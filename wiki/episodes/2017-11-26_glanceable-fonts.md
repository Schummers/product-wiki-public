# Typography for Glanceable Reading: Bigger Is Better

## Summary

Glancing — quickly reading 1 to 2 words in isolation — requires different typography than scanning longer passages. MIT research on lexical decision tasks shows that larger text, regular width, and uppercase all outperformed smaller, condensed, and lowercase text for glanceable reading. Lowercase required 26% more time, condensed required 11.2% more. However, these findings apply to isolated words, not sentences; all-caps text remains problematic for longer passages. Designing for glanceable contexts (notifications, smartwatch displays, AR/VR text) should prioritize size and clarity over trends toward slim, small, light fonts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Bigger always better for glancing** — larger font sizes, regular width (not condensed), and uppercase all improved speed of recognizing isolated words at statistically significant levels.
- **Lowercase penalty** — required 26% more time for accurate recognition than uppercase; effect is exacerbated with small font sizes.
- **Condensed font cost** — condensed or thin typefaces required 11.2% more time than regular width.
- **Context matters greatly** — findings apply only to isolated words; all-caps text remains problematic for longer passages where lowercase ascenders/descenders aid legibility.
- **Design applications** — use larger fonts for anything requiring quick glance reading (GPS directions, notifications, smartwatch displays); avoid all-lowercase and condensed fonts in these contexts.

## Quotes

> Glancing refers to quickly reading short text (often just 1 or 2 words long).

> Bigger text is better, irrespective of the dimension being increased

> for readability at a glance, bigger is better

- [[Mobile Design]] — addresses font choices for micro-sessions and glanceable content on phones, smartwatches, and AR/VR devices.
