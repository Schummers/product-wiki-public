# Vanity Metrics: Add Context to Add Meaning

## Summary

Vanity metrics appear impressive but lack actionable insight because they lack context or relate to no key performance indicator. Ever-growing numbers (total users, downloads, page views) are inherently misleading without context. Meaningful metrics require rates or ratios: tracking new users per week instead of cumulative users, conversion rates per visit, or engagement ratios (DAU/MAU) that remain relatively stable over time. Changes in stable metrics reliably signal design changes or issues rather than random fluctuations. Selecting the right time frame and normalizing metrics per user, per visit, or relative to other metrics transforms vanity metrics into actionable insights.

## Key Takeaways

- **Vanity metrics always grow, making them poor indicators of change** — Total users, downloads, page views, and shares always increase with time; without context, they reveal nothing about user experience quality or business health.

- **Add context through rates or ratios normalized by time or population** — Instead of app downloads, track downloads-to-store-page-visits ratio or ratio of downloads leading to active use; rate metrics should remain relatively stable, with changes signaling true improvements.

- **Choose time frames that balance stability and responsiveness** — Too-short frames (per-minute tracking) produce random noise; too-long frames (yearly) delay finding problems; weekly or monthly rates usually hit the sweet spot.

- **Per-user and per-visit rates reveal behavior better than aggregate counts** — Page views per session is more meaningful than total page views; cohort analysis (comparing users who joined the same week) shows whether experience improvements actually stick.

- **Ratios between metrics provide comparative insight** — Unique page views/total page views reveals pogosticking; DAU/MAU stickiness ratio shows what proportion of monthly active users engage daily; comparing metrics adds context.

- **Actionable metrics should change when design changes occur** — A metric that never changes or always moves in one direction (ever-growing) cannot tell you whether your design changes helped or hurt.

## Quotes

> A vanity metric appears impressive but doesn't give insight into the true performance of a digital property (for example, because it lacks the context needed create a noteworthy comparison or because it measures an aspect of the system not related to any KPI).

> Remember that all tracked metrics should help you gauge your system's design performance and prompt you to take action if needed.

> Rates and ratios that stay mostly stable are ideal, because any change in the metric is likely because of a true change in the system — either a design change or a bug! — and not a random fluctuation.

## Concepts

- [[Analytics]] — Metrics require careful selection; raw counts without context mislead; meaningful metrics include rate, time frame, and relationship to design goals.

- [[KPIs]] — Key performance indicators must be actionable: tied to business or UX goals, responsive to design changes, and stable enough to distinguish signal from noise.

- [[UX Metrics]] — Choosing what to measure requires understanding user tasks, business goals, and what changes in the metric actually mean; aggregates without context waste analysis effort.

- [[Data Analysis]] — Time-based normalization, cohort analysis, and ratio-based comparisons transform raw data into actionable insights; analysis methodology determines insight quality.
