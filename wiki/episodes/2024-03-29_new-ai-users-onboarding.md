# New Users Need Support with Generative-AI Tools

## Summary

This article examines onboarding challenges for users new to generative AI tools through a study of Chinese AI chatbots. Users with low AI literacy lack understanding of how these tools work and what they can accomplish, leading to confusion when tools assume prior knowledge. Effective onboarding should leverage tool names to indicate functionality, provide brief contextual help rather than long tutorials, and offer general task examples instead of niche ones. The fundamental principles of interaction design remain constant regardless of technology novelty, emphasizing user-centric approaches over feature-focused design.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI literacy gap** — users with no prior AI experience cannot differentiate between AI-generated images and conversational chatbots; they assume tools know their own capabilities and ask the chatbot directly rather than consulting documentation.
- **Tool naming matters** — tool names should clearly communicate function; unclear names (like Chinese terms from classical language) frustrate users while names like "ChatGPT" effectively indicate a chat interface.
- **Onboarding simplicity** — brief, focused tutorials addressing "What does this do?" and "How does it work?" outperform long tutorials; information placed too early in the journey is skipped as users don't yet see its relevance.
- **Contextual help** — provide detailed guidance at the right moment (e.g., voice-interaction tips when users are about to use voice, not during initial setup).
- **General examples work** — broad prompts like "Generate text" are more effective than niche examples; general examples invite exploration while overly specific examples require user adaptation.

## Quotes

> Users with no previous experience with generative AI may have a basic understanding that AI tools can create images and videos. However, they have no idea about how the tools work and what they can be used for.

> Users tend to skip information that doesn't seem immediately relevant.

> Our role as designers is to close the gap between a tool's capabilities and the user's proficiency in harnessing its full potential.
