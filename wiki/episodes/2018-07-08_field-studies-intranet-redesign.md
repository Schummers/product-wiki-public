# Field Studies Should Inform Intranet Redesign

## Summary

Field studies provide observational research of users in their actual work environments, revealing realistic context and workflow patterns that lab studies cannot capture. While surveys and lab-based usability testing are helpful, they often paint incomplete or inaccurate pictures of real workflows. Field studies uncover how employees use multiple information sources, develop workarounds to system limitations, navigate organizational dynamics, and integrate digital and physical tools. These observations reveal the true pain points and needs that should drive intranet redesign.

## Key Takeaways

- **See realistic context and user data** — Field studies reveal how users work with actual data, physical or contextual limitations, workarounds they've developed, and environmental factors that lab settings cannot simulate.
- **Understand actual user journeys** — Field observations show what the journey actually is, how employees transition between channels, what information sources they rely on, and where they struggle and why.
- **Correct inaccurate theories** — Workflow mismatches discovered in field studies often differ dramatically from team assumptions. Users' renditions of problems may be incomplete or misdiagnose the underlying system issues.
- **Build empathy for users** — Direct observation of users in their workplace helps teams understand motivations behind behaviors and move from blaming users to blaming system design.
- **Identify workflow impacts across roles** — Field studies reveal how workflow issues affect different user types and organizational functions, not just the primary user group being studied.

## Quotes

> Lab studies isolate participants in artificial settings. Despite the researchers' best efforts to simulate real-world conditions, sometimes that is not possible.

> Field studies may ultimately build a more accurate representation of workflows within the organization.

> If you solve the wrong problem, it doesn't matter how well you solve it. A great design of the wrong thing? It'll still be the wrong thing.
