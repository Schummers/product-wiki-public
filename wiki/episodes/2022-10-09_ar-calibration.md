# Augmented-Reality Calibration in Mobile Apps: 10 Guidelines

## Summary

Calibration is a critical part of the AR experience that allows virtual elements to be accurately aligned with the real-life environment. Unlike onboarding, which happens once, calibration usually occurs every session when users access an AR feature. Based on a usability study with 11 participants testing various AR apps, this article presents 10 guidelines addressing the two main components of calibration: instructions that tell users what to do, and feedback that informs users whether they are on the right track.

The study found that AR calibration presents significant usability challenges stemming from users' low familiarity with AR technology. Calibration patterns vary (scanning surfaces, absolute positioning, relative positioning, or no calibration) and each requires clear communication. Key issues include: instructions that are too complex, presented all at once, or lack visual clarity; vague feedback signals that users cannot interpret; and misalignment between visual demonstrations and actual requirements. Effective calibration requires low-granularity, descriptive, visually salient instructions presented one at a time, paired with clear feedback that keeps users informed of system status and how to recover from errors.

## Key Takeaways

- **Provide low-granularity instructions one at a time** — breaking instructions into small steps prevents confusion and helps users focus on one action before moving to the next.
- **Instructions must be descriptive and unambiguous** — vague phrases like "scan a textured surface" confuse users; concrete examples and visual guidance clarify what surface types are required.
- **Make instructions visually salient** — enclose text in solid-colored boxes with high contrast against variable backgrounds to ensure visibility across different environments and lighting conditions.
- **Use clear, standard signifiers** — scattered dots and vague visual cues confuse users; explicitly communicate what the system is doing and what users should do next.
- **Feedback must inform users of system status** — users need to know whether the system is processing, stuck, or ready; without feedback, they become frustrated and uncertain.
- **Provide guidance when AR objects appear outside the field of view** — users should initially see the AR object, but if it moves, visual cues or arrows help them locate it.

## Concepts

- [[Augmented Reality]] — calibration instructions must be broken into small, sequential steps; users need clear examples, visuals, and explicit feedback about system status and recovery actions.
- [[Feedback Design]] — keeping users informed of what the system is doing (processing, stuck, successful) is essential; vague visual signifiers frustrate and disorient users.
- [[Visual Communication]] — instructions and UI elements must be high-contrast and highly salient to be visible against variable real-world backgrounds; text placed on solid backgrounds ensures readability.
- [[Onboarding]] — effective calibration combines text, visual examples, and sometimes audio instructions to accommodate users holding devices at distances or in contexts where reading text is difficult.
