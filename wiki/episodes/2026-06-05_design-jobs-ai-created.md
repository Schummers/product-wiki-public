# The Four Design Jobs AI Created (So Far)

## Summary

The term "AI design" has fragmented into four distinct orientations, each involving different work, skills, organizational structures, and definitions of success. Using a car metaphor, these are: designing with AI (the driver using AI tools), designing AI products (building the car), designing for AI agents (engineering infrastructure for agents), and designing the AI itself (tuning the engine). Most designers work in the first two orientations; very few design for agents or shape model behavior. However, demand for the latter two is growing much faster than the supply of designers who understand them. Designers who build depth in underserved areas now will have significant competitive advantage as organizations' relationships with AI become more complex.

## Key Takeaways

- **Designing with AI** — Using AI as a tool to ideate, prototype, generate copy, and critique work; this is where most designers work today and is transformative for workflow efficiency.
- **Designing AI Products** — Building interfaces and experiences that deliver AI capabilities; includes both AI-native products (entirely AI experience) and AI features in existing products (integrating into established patterns).
- **Designing for AI Agents** — Structuring data, content, and interactions so AI agents can parse and act on them; these "roads" are mostly being ignored by design teams but profoundly shape end-user experience.
- **Designing the AI** — Shaping model behavior, evaluation criteria, and principles; requires collaboration with engineers but involves design decisions about what the model should do, refuse, and how it should weigh competing instructions.
- **Depth Over Breadth** — The market is moving fast and few designers have deep experience across all four; building depth in one orientation now creates significant advantage over staying broadly "AI-adjacent."

## Quotes
