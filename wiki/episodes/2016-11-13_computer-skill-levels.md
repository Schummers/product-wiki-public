# The Distribution of Users' Computer Skills: Worse Than You Think

## Summary

An OECD study of 215,942 adults across 33 countries quantifies the skill gap between design teams and the general population. Design teams represent the top 5-8% of computer skills globally, but the majority of users fall into lower skill categories: 26% cannot use computers at all, 14% are below level 1, 29% are at level 1, and 26% are at level 2. Only 5% in the United States reach level 3 (the expert category). This massive gap means designers routinely assume simplicity that is inaccessible to the majority of the population.

The digital divide is serious and persistent: one quarter of the adult population cannot use computers at all, representing a major barrier to digital access. This gap exists even in the wealthiest nations and carries economic consequences for both individuals and organizations.

## Key Takeaways

- **You are not your user** — Designers typically have IQ, literacy, and technical skills far above average users; the skill gap is the most damaging to design intuition because it's invisible.
- **The 5-8% reality** — Only 5% of Americans, 6% of UK/Australia, and 7% of Canada/Northern Europe have level-3 computer skills; 95% cannot handle complex interfaces designed by level-3 experts.
- **Four skill levels** — Below level 1 cannot use a computer for any meaningful task; level 1 handles basic, familiar applications with explicit criteria; level 2 uses multiple applications with navigation and monitoring; level 3 handles implicit goals, unexpected outcomes, and high monitoring demands.
- **26% cannot use computers** — One quarter of the population across OECD countries cannot use computers at all; many websites still ignore this fundamental digital divide.
- **Keep designs extremely simple** — If targeting a broad consumer audience, assume level-1 skills; anything more complicated serves only 31% of the population or fewer depending on country.
- **Level-1 capabilities define the floor** — Level-1 users require little navigation, few steps, explicit criteria only, simple reasoning, and minimal information integration; exceeding this raises accessibility barriers.

## Concepts

- [[User Research]] — Large-scale research studies reveal actual skill distributions across populations, correcting designers' assumptions about user capabilities.
- [[Accessibility]] — Computer skill gaps are part of the broader digital divide; many designs are inherently inaccessible to people without advanced technical skills.
- [[Design Simplicity]] — Targeting consumers requires designing for level-1 skills (few steps, little navigation, explicit criteria, simple reasoning) to reach 70% of the population.
- [[Digital Divide]] — One quarter of adults cannot use computers at all, representing a serious and persistent barrier to digital access; designing for broad audiences requires acknowledging this gap rather than assuming universal capability.
