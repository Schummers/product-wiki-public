# Average UX Improvements Are Shrinking Over Time

## Summary

This article analyzes UX metrics data collected over 14 years, comparing improvement scores from 2006-2008 to those from 2020. The data shows that average UX improvement scores have decreased substantially from 247% to 75%, a statistically significant decline. The article argues this decline reflects not declining UX quality but rather industry maturation: the most egregious problems have been solved, user expectations have risen, and even small improvements are now valuable as the field focuses on refinement rather than foundational fixes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Substantial improvement decline** — Average UX improvement scores decreased 69% from 2006-2008 (247%) to 2020 (75%), a statistically significant change indicating diminishing returns on UX interventions.
- **Industry maturation explanation** — Rather than indicating poor UX work, declining improvement scores reflect that the field has solved major problems, best practices are widespread, and user expectations are higher.
- **Wide variability in outcomes** — Individual projects show improvement scores ranging from 8% to 104% depending on existing experience quality, team expertise, quantity of changes, measurement sensitivity, and user resistance to behavior change.
- **Small improvements remain valuable** — Even though magnitude decreased, small improvements are no less important because user expectations rose in lockstep, and competitors are also improving, creating a UX arms race.
- **Shift to refinement phase** — The industry is moving from fixing foundational problems to fine-tuning experiences, with smaller individual changes accumulating to create sophisticated, enjoyable interfaces.

## Quotes

> At the beginning of the human factors and usability movement, just about every product had substantial room for improvement.

> "In these early years, design was truly abominable — think splash screens, search that couldn't find anything, bloated graphics everywhere. The only good thing about these early designs was that they were so bad that it was easy for usability people to be heroes: just run the smallest study and you would inevitably find several immense opportunities for improvement."

> Because experiences overall are getting better, user expectations have gotten much, much higher.

## Concepts

- [[ROI and Metrics]] — measuring UX improvements through before-and-after metrics and improvement scores to quantify design impact and justify UX investment.
- [[UX Maturity]] — how the field of UX has matured over 14 years, with early years focused on solving major problems and current work focused on refinement and incremental improvements.
- [[Design Increments]] — small individual design changes that together accumulate to produce more sophisticated experiences, with each change being valuable despite lower individual improvement scores than foundational fixes.
