# Effective Resumes for UX Students and Graduates

## Summary

This article provides comprehensive guidance for UX students and recent graduates creating resumes for industry positions. Drawing on research about hiring manager expectations and application review processes, the author addresses common mistakes that can significantly reduce interview chances, including poor resume design, vague descriptions, and missing information. The guide covers essential sections (contact information, education, experience, extracurriculars) with specific formatting recommendations and content strategies, emphasizing that students and graduates cannot afford resume errors due to limited prior experience.

## Key Takeaways

- **Resume errors have outsized impact on graduates** — research shows that just two errors on a graduate resume reduces interview chances by 7.3%, with women penalized more than men for the same mistakes.
- **Multiple formats serve different purposes** — Word format for applicant tracking systems and PDF format for human review are both necessary; students and graduates should provide both.
- **Coursework descriptions matter** — describing relevant courses, skills, and tools in 2–3 sentences receives more favorable ratings than omitting coursework details.
- **Work experience accumulates value** — even non-UX jobs should be included on graduate resumes; research shows more work experience improves job search success.
- **Contact information must be complete** — include full university location, country, program type, graduation date, and portfolio links; hiring managers should not have to search for basic information.
- **Achievements require structure** — use the formula [verb] [quantitative outcome] [context] [skill/tool] to make accomplishments clear and impactful.
