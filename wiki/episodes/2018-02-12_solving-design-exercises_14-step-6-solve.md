# Solving Product Design Exercises: Step 6: Solve

## Summary

The solve step is where candidates demonstrate their core UI/UX skills by creating the visual and interaction design of their chosen solution. Dashinsky emphasizes this is the only step where candidates cannot fail, though poor execution significantly reduces hiring chances. The chapter presents three complementary techniques—storyboarding, defining tasks, and speedy sketching—to help designers map out interactions and flows. The author recommends focusing on one or two major user flows given time constraints, and describes how deliverables differ between on-site exercises (wireframes, sketches, journeys) and take-home exercises (high-fidelity designs).

## Key Takeaways

- **Storyboarding maps customer journey** — Sketch the steps customers take to use the product, considering context before, during, and after interaction; this reveals which interactions the product must support and can identify opportunities beyond the core user need.
- **Defining tasks breaks flows into checklist** — List the specific tasks customers must complete to successfully use the product; this technique is faster than storyboarding and suitable for whiteboarding sessions with limited time.
- **Speedy sketching generates alternatives** — Fold paper in four sections and sketch one interface concept per section in one minute or less; the goal is to challenge the designer to move beyond the first obvious solution and combine ideas.
- **Deliverables differ by exercise format** — On-site exercises require wireframe flows, user journeys, ideas lists, and sketches; take-home exercises call for high-fidelity designs, but the same foundational techniques apply.
- **Allocate majority of time to this step** — Given the 30-minute allocation in a one-hour exercise, focus on solving the most important flows rather than attempting comprehensive coverage.

## Quotes

> Map out the customer's journey to get a picture of what interactions your product needs to support.

> Make a list of tasks the customer needs to be able to complete to use your product successfully.

> The goal of this technique is to challenge your mind to come up with more than just the first and most obvious solution and to generate a range of ideas that you can pick from or combine.

## Concepts

- [[Design Exercise]] — The practical phase of a design interview exercise where candidates create wireframes, flows, and sketches to demonstrate their ability to translate a chosen idea into an interactive experience.
- [[Storyboarding]] — A narrative visualization technique that maps out the sequence of steps a customer takes through their journey with a product, revealing interaction requirements and potential value-add opportunities.
- [[UX Metrics]] — While not the focus of this chapter, the author frames task lists in terms of task completion as a measurable outcome, which ties to later evaluation of solution success.
