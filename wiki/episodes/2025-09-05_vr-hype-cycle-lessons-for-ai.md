# The VR Hype Cycle: Lessons for the Age of AI

## Summary

This article examines how virtual reality exemplifies the hype cycle phenomenon, where expectations and messaging outpace technological capability. Drawing parallels between VR's trajectory and current AI hype, the author argues that AI faces similar risks of disillusionment as companies overpromise capabilities. The article provides practical advice for UX practitioners to navigate emerging technology adoption without overcommitting to immature platforms.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hype cycles create expectation-reality rifts** — When messaging outpaces technological capability, a gap develops between promised and actual user experiences, leading to eventual disillusionment.
- **VR adoption remained limited despite massive investment** — Despite billions invested by Apple and Meta, VR failed to achieve mainstream adoption due to technical limitations, cost, ergonomics, and performance constraints.
- **Niche applications succeed where mainstream adoption fails** — VR found viable markets in entertainment, medical visualization, hazard training, and flight simulation where benefits clearly outweigh drawbacks.
- **Avoid platform lock-in during hype** — Focus learning on transferable skills and platform-agnostic tools rather than proprietary platforms that may not win market adoption.
- **Develop personal evaluation separate from marketing claims** — Direct experience with emerging technology provides better judgment than relying on marketing narratives or press releases.
- **Identify tradeoff points for technology adoption** — Understanding your users' specific pain points helps determine when a technology becomes worth adopting versus when alternatives remain superior.

## Quotes
