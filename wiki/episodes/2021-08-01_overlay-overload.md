# Overlay Overload: Competing Popups Are an Increasing Menace

## Summary

Popups have been hated by users since the 1990s, and the problem has worsened despite decades of UX guidance against them. Modern users encounter multiple overlays in a single session from different sources: privacy notifications, marketing requests, browser plugins, and app download prompts. These overlays often stack, overlap, and conflict with each other, creating frustration. Some overlays persist as "stalking" overlays that follow users around after dismissal. The cumulative experience across multiple sites exhausts users and erodes brand trust, yet many designers continue using disruptive modal overlays to optimize for newsletter signups and promotions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Persistent User Hatred** — Users have consistently hated popups for three decades; surveys show popups among the most hated advertising techniques, and this sentiment has not changed despite repeated UX guidance.
- **Multiple Overlay Types** — Modern users face privacy popups (cookies, location, notifications), marketing overlays (newsletters, promotions), and browser-initiated overlays (plugins, deal finders) all in single sessions; individually manageable, cumulatively overwhelming.
- **Stacking and Conflict** — Multiple overlays frequently appear stacked or overlapping, especially on small screens; they often malfunction and work against each other, creating frustration without delivering intended value.
- **Stalking Behavior** — Some sites display a modal overlay on first visit, and after users dismiss it, the same overlay shrinks and follows users around the site as a persistent floating box, rejecting user intent.
- **Cumulative Exhaustion** — Users visiting multiple sites in a session face constant popup battles; this exhausting pattern across sites poisons the well for all websites and degrades brand perception more than any individual popup.

## Quotes

> Users have hated popups since the 1990s, and they still hate them today.

> When users face a barrage of multiple modals in different forms, sometimes even overlapping and stalking them, the experience is extremely frustrating.

> The cumulative effect is exhausting.

## Concepts

- [[Overlay]] — Overlays are content boxes displayed on top of another page; they range from non-modal (can be ignored) to modal (require explicit interaction); while effective at capturing attention, they are frequently perceived as disruptive and annoying.
- [[Modal Dialog]] — A modal overlay disables underlying page content until users explicitly interact with the modal; modals are the most disruptive and hated version of overlays because they interrupt user workflows and force interaction.
- [[User Interruption]] — Design patterns that interrupt users to request actions (newsletters, location access, promotions) degrade user experience and trust; when multiple interruptions stack in single sessions, the cumulative effect exhausts users.
