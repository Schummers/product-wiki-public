# 6 Tips for Successful Personalization

## Summary

Personalization—identifying users and delivering appropriate content and functionality—requires careful implementation. Six guidelines ensure success: assign roles conservatively (past behavior may not predict future actions), restrict access sparingly (prefer adding/reshuffling content over removing access), create only roles you can support (maintenance and content creation limits force prioritization), personalize functionality as well as content (autofill, remembered selections, progress saving), provide an "out" via view-as or universal access options, and review roles regularly. Successful personalization combines assumptions about user needs based on data analysis with explicit user control to prevent frustrating misalignment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Past behavior does not always predict future actions** — Personalization is a best guess, and mis-tagging a user creates frustration at every visit. A shopper who bought a responsive-design book might have done so for a course, colleague, or gift, not because they're interested in technology generally. Tagging too narrowly creates inaccurate and restrictive experiences.
- **Restrict access sparingly** — Most successful personalization adds or reshuffles content rather than removing access. Amazon promotes products matching interests without restricting access to others. Remove access only when necessary (minors/mature content, role-based security, regional policy compliance) and recognize that users have different needs at different times.
- **Create only as many roles as you can maintain** — Intranet teams report getting carried away with user segmentation, only to discover maintenance is impossible or overwhelming. Personalize by broader characteristics (location, department, level) rather than fine-grained, nuanced profiles to keep content creation manageable.
- **Personalize functionality beyond just content** — Autofill known information (with editing capability), remember frequent selections and searches, save progress in long workflows, and provide seamless cross-device continuation to streamline experiences based on known patterns.
- **Provide view-as functionality to avoid troubleshooting problems** — Personalization creates different experiences for different users, complicating support. A "swap role" or "view as" feature lets managers and support staff see what users see without requiring detailed change requests.
- **Review personalization regularly** — Monitor traffic to personalized content, track complaints related to personalization, verify data source accuracy, and adjust roles based on actual usage patterns and user feedback.

## Quotes

> Personalization can result in a frustrating experience that might annoy the user at every visit, or worse, keep them from using the system altogether.

> Users rarely have any control over these assumptions. As we move into a world with more and more "smart" devices, we need to make sure that users can override these personalized settings if they go against their needs.
