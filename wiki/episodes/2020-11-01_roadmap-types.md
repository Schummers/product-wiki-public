# 3 Types of Roadmaps in UX and Product Design

## Summary

This article describes three types of roadmaps that UX teams can create, each serving different purposes and audiences. Product roadmaps represent problems to be solved across all departments, field roadmaps represent UX-specific problems across multiple areas, and specialty roadmaps focus on problems within one UX area. Each type has different benefits, ownership structures, and challenges, and teams should select a roadmap type based on their primary goal.

## Key Takeaways

- **Product roadmaps** — The broadest type, created collaboratively across departments, that communicate strategic vision of the entire product including UX, engineering, and marketing problems to solve.
- **Field roadmaps** — UX-specific roadmaps covering research, design, and content problems across multiple products, created by UX directors to bridge different UX areas and educate stakeholders on the design process.
- **Specialty roadmaps** — Focused on one UX area like user research or design, created by individual practitioners, useful for communicating bandwidth allocation and balancing workload within a specific discipline.
- **Match roadmap to goal** — Teams should select roadmap scope based on primary objective: product roadmaps for shared vision, field roadmaps for UX education, specialty roadmaps for resource allocation.
- **Ownership varies by type** — Product managers own product roadmaps, UX directors own field roadmaps, and individual practitioners own specialty roadmaps, each collaborating with appropriate stakeholders.

## Quotes

> A [roadmap](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-roadmaps/) is a strategic, living artifact that prioritizes, and communicates a team's future work and problems to solve. It is meant to act as a single source of truth representing your UX team's North Star.

> **Force crossfunctional collaboration**. Product roadmaps are collaboratively created by product management and crossfunctional leads. This process facilitates important discussions (for example, necessary tradeoffs) and creates coownership over the vision created.

> **Easy to create.** Due to the scoped nature of specialty roadmaps, they tend to be the easiest to the create (especially if the creator is the primary owner of each theme).

## Concepts

- [[Roadmapping]] — creating strategic, living artifacts that prioritize and communicate teams' future work and problems to solve, with three scope levels appropriate for different contexts.
- [[UX Strategy]] — aligning teams around a shared vision of future work, with different roadmap types serving different strategic purposes from product-wide vision to discipline-specific planning.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — using roadmaps to align across departments and roles, with field and product roadmaps enabling collaboration while specialty roadmaps support individual contributor planning.
