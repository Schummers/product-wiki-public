# Emojis in Email Subject Lines: Advantage or Impediment?

## Summary

This article presents empirical research on how emojis in email subject lines affect user attitudes and behavior. Two studies explored whether emojis increase email open rates and if they influence user perceptions. The findings show that while emojis attract visual attention in mixed inboxes (compared to non-emoji emails), they also increase negative sentiment by 26%, reduce perceived value, and don't improve the likelihood that users will want to open that specific email. Emojis shift user focus to visual characteristics rather than message meaning, which can be detrimental unless the emoji adds legitimate context or emotional value to the subject line.

## Key Takeaways

- **Emojis increase negative sentiment by 26%** — Users rate emoji-containing emails more negatively than identical emails without emojis. Terms like "dull" and "boring" were selected twice as often for emoji emails, while "straightforward" was selected more for non-emoji versions.
- **Adding emojis does not increase open intent for that email** — All else being equal, adding an emoji to a subject line does not make users more likely to want to open that specific email. The benefit does not translate to improved email engagement.
- **Emojis attract attention through visual salience, not message appeal** — In mixed inboxes with both emoji and non-emoji emails, emoji emails were considered more often (33% vs. 9% of selections). However, users attributed this to visual characteristics (the emoji itself) rather than the email's meaning or value proposition.
- **Emojis reduce perceived value** — Emails without emojis were rated as more valuable, while trustworthiness ratings showed no difference. Emojis may make emails appear less professional or feel manipulative.
- **Only use emojis to reinforce meaning** — Emojis used purely to grab attention are perceived as gimmicky. Reserve emojis for instances where they genuinely add context or relevant emotional value to the subject line. Use them sparingly to preserve their attention-drawing benefit.

## Quotes

> Emojis are small visual representations of an object or a concept.

> On average, an email with emojis received 2.01 negative words and .99 positive words, whereas an email without emojis received 1.97 negative words and 1.16 positive words.

> Do not add emojis in email subject lines purely for the purpose of jazzing them up to draw users' attention.
