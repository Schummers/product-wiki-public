# Continuous Discovery Habits: Chapter Two: A Common Framework for Continuous Discovery

## Summary

Torres introduces the Opportunity Solution Tree (OST), a framework for structuring continuous discovery work. She explains how this framework helps product trios resolve the tension between business and customer needs by starting with a clear outcome, mapping the opportunity space (customer needs, pain points, and desires), exploring the solution space, and testing assumptions. The chapter uses the Wells Fargo scandal as an example of outcomes gone wrong and demonstrates how proper problem framing prevents such failures. OSTs help teams understand what to do next, build shared understanding, maintain a continuous mindset, make better decisions, accelerate learning cycles, and simplify stakeholder communication.

## Key Takeaways

- **Ill-structured problems need framing** — Reaching a desired outcome is an "ill-structured problem" with many possible solutions; how teams frame the problem determines the quality of solutions they consider.
- **Opportunity space matters most** — The two most critical steps are mapping and structuring the opportunity space and selecting which opportunities to pursue; many teams skip this and jump directly to solutions.
- **OST structure** — The tree has four levels: the desired outcome (root), the opportunity space (customer needs/pain points/desires), the solution space (potential solutions), and assumption tests (validation approaches).
- **Benefits of visualization** — OSTs help teams resolve business-vs.-customer tensions, build shared understanding, maintain continuous mindset, improve decisions, accelerate learning, build confidence, and simplify stakeholder management.
- **Compare and contrast mindset** — Teams should avoid "whether or not" decisions and instead adopt "compare and contrast" thinking, visualizing options on the OST to avoid false certainty.
- **Problem and solution space evolve together** — The best teams work both top-down and bottom-up, using assumption tests to refine understanding of the opportunity space while generating better solutions.

## Quotes

> "Managers must convert society's needs into opportunities for profitable business." — Peter Drucker

> "If I had an hour to solve a problem, I'd spend 55 minutes thinking about the problem and 5 minutes thinking about solutions." — Albert Einstein

> "A well-constructed opportunity solution tree does exactly this. When sharing your discovery work with stakeholders, you can use your tree to first remind them of your desired outcome. Next, you can share what you've learned about your customer, by walking them through the opportunity space."

## Concepts

- [[Opportunity Solution Tree]] — Torres introduces this visual framework as the core tool for structuring discovery, with clear levels representing outcome, opportunity space, solutions, and assumption tests.
- [[Product Outcome]] — The root of the tree is a clear business outcome that frames the scope of discovery and gives the team latitude to find customer-centric solutions.
- [[Product Trio]] — The framework is designed for product trios to build shared understanding and make collaborative decisions by visualizing their options.
- [[Decision Making]] — The OST helps teams overcome decision-making villains like narrow framing, confirmation bias, emotional attachment to ideas, and overconfidence.
- [[Prioritization]] — Teams use the opportunity space to decide which customer needs to address, moving from "whether or not" decisions to "which is most important" comparisons.
