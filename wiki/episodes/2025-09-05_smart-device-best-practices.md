# Smart-Device Apps: 7 Best Practices to Make Devices Truly Smart

## Summary

Smart-device companion apps serve as control hubs that unlock the full potential of smart devices by enabling advanced settings, automations, multi-device coordination, and information-rich dashboards. Designing these apps presents unique challenges: users often operate devices remotely or with delayed feedback, making clarity and immediate status feedback essential for building user confidence. The article presents seven best practices: allow device categorization and naming, make device status instantly clear through overview screens, combine color, iconography, and text for unambiguous status, make progress toward goals visible during long tasks, reduce repetition through shortcuts and reusable configurations, provide immediate and informative feedback about command execution, and allow setup and configuration of other control channels (voice, on-device buttons). These practices address the core challenge that users cannot always observe device status in person.

## Key Takeaways

- **Device Status Must be Instantly Clear** — Overview screens should show status of every connected device without navigation; users need to immediately see essential information (on/off, locked/unlocked) and confirm commands were received.
- **Combine Multiple Cues for Clarity** — Use color, icons, and text together to ensure status is unambiguous; this approach supports accessibility and ensures clarity in any context.
- **Show Progress for Long Tasks** — When devices perform time-consuming tasks, display time remaining, completion percentage, or status animations so users understand progress and trust the device is working.
- **Reduce Friction with Shortcuts** — Smart-device interactions often involve repetitive actions; offer reusable configurations, bulk-editing for multiple devices, and quick-action shortcuts to save time and reduce cognitive load.
- **Immediate Feedback Builds Trust** — Because users control devices remotely and cannot always see status changes, immediate in-app feedback confirming command receipt and execution is essential for preventing repeated actions and building confidence.
- **Support Multiple Control Modalities** — Apps should function as central hubs for configuring voice assistants, on-device controls, and app controls; ensure consistency across all control channels so users trust unified control.
- **Naming Increases Delight** — Allowing custom device names introduces playfulness and fosters anthropomorphism, increasing user delight while also improving organization and access speed.
