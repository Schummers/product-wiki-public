# Usability Testing With Older Adults

## Summary

Older adults (65+) represent a significant portion of internet users, yet many websites fail to accommodate their needs. Conducting usability tests with this demographic requires special consideration due to age-related changes in vision, hearing, motor control, and cognition. Successful testing depends on creating a safe, supportive environment, understanding the diversity of abilities within the older-adult population, and building trust with participants through transparency and respect for their health and well-being.

## Key Takeaways

- **Don't assume homogeneity** — Aging affects everyone differently; recruit a diverse group with varied abilities rather than stereotyping older adults as uniformly frail or technologically inexperienced.
- **Meet participants in familiar locations** — Testing at participants' homes or nearby residential communities removes navigation and mobility barriers, increases comfort, and provides insight into how users actually interact with their devices and assistive technologies.
- **Accommodate assistive technologies** — Ensure testing equipment is flexible and compatible with screen readers, magnifiers, keyguards, head wands, and voice-recognition software; remind participants to bring glasses or contacts and provide live transcriptions when needed.
- **Use realistic, memorable tasks** — Write task instructions that are relevant to participants' lives, provide printed or shared references for easy recall, and limit session length to prevent cognitive fatigue.
- **Emphasize that the design—not the user—is being tested** — Older adults may feel less confident with technology; explicitly state that the website is being evaluated, not their ability to use it, to reduce anxiety and improve candor.
- **Reserve time for relationship-building** — Begin sessions with setup time for assistive technology, answer participant questions, and dedicate post-session time for conversation, as participants often value engagement and learning opportunities.
