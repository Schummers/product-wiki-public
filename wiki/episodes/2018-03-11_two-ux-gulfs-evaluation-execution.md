# The Two UX Gulfs: Evaluation and Execution

## Summary

The gulf of evaluation and gulf of execution describe two fundamental challenges users face when interacting with technology. The gulf of evaluation is the difficulty of understanding system status; the gulf of execution is the difficulty of taking action to accomplish goals. Both gulfs are composed of subtasks: evaluation requires perceiving and interpreting signals, while execution requires planning and manipulating controls. Designers can bridge these gulfs by creating designs with clear visual similarities to familiar mental models so users can correctly interpret system state and predict outcomes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Gulf of evaluation** — users struggle to understand the current state of a system, not because indicators are invisible but because their meaning is ambiguous; a Bluetooth switch labeled "Off" may appear to work opposite to how users expect.
- **Gulf of execution** — users struggle to plan and execute actions, requiring them to understand how controls work and what outcomes will result.
- **Interdependence** — successful execution depends on correct evaluation; if users misunderstand system state, their action plans will fail regardless of how intuitive the controls are.
- **Mental models** — designers can leverage familiar mental models by creating visual and functional similarities to designs users already understand, like checkbox controls that match paper form conventions.
- **Granular analysis** — breaking evaluation and execution into subtasks helps identify specific design failures; problems may stem from visibility, interpretation, planning, or manipulation issues.

## Quotes

> The terms gulf of evaluation and gulf of execution were invented in 1986 by Ed Hutchins, Jim Hollan, and Don Norman, when they wrote about the virtues of direct manipulation in helping users bridge these gulfs.

> To save time, most people rely on their past experiences to quickly build mental models for new systems.

> Don Norman first described his model of interaction decades ago, but difficulties with evaluation and execution still plague today's interfaces. Understanding these gulfs and incorporating them into UX design is important for two reasons.

## Concepts

- [[Interaction Design]] — Interaction design requires both understanding the fundamental challenges users face in perceiving system state and planning effective actions, and designing controls and feedback that bridge these gulfs through clear mental models.
- [[Mental Model]] — using familiar design patterns to help users build accurate mental models of system behavior.
