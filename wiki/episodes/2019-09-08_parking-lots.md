# Parking Lots in UX Meetings and Workshops

## Summary

A parking lot is a collaborative artifact used in workshops and meetings to capture off-topic comments, questions, and ideas without derailing the current discussion. Items are deferred for later discussion while remaining visible and documented. When used effectively, parking lots keep conversations focused, document important questions for follow-up research, and create action items with owners and timelines. However, parking lots can become graveyards of abandoned ideas if not properly managed; five best practices ensure they drive accountability and follow-up.

## Key Takeaways

- **Parking lots defer without dismissing** — A visible, shared artifact captures off-topic items, allowing discussions to stay on track while preserving ideas and questions for later consideration.

- **Clarity and explicit rules prevent friction** — Briefly introduce the parking lot concept, purpose, and ground rules at the start; swift identification of off-scope items and time-boxing borderline discussions maintains focus.

- **Organize as you go using affinity diagramming** — Cluster similar items by topic as they're added; this enables efficient discussion later and allows teams to assign clusters to appropriate subgroups if time runs out.

- **Assign owners, decisions, and timelines to each item** — Apply the "W.W.W." framework (who does what, when) to each parking lot item; clear ownership and deadlines ensure follow-up, not abandonment.

- **Include parking lot items in meeting notes** — Document all parking lot items and action items in written follow-ups; public accountability and visibility ensure participants take responsibility for next steps.

- **Respect contributed ideas through documented follow-up** — Dismissing ideas to a parking lot without follow-up insults the contributor and damages team dynamics; dedicated time or clear action plans demonstrate that contributions matter.

## Quotes

> A parking lot is a place to capture comments, topics, or questions that are not related to the meeting agenda. The parking lot keeps the focus on the immediate discussion while deferring (i.e., "parking") other topics for later.

> When used correctly, parking lots can do more than just document out-of-scope ideas and questions. They are a tangible artifact that ultimately helps build common ground, increases productivity within a workshop, and contributes towards shared accountability amongst a team.

> If something is put in the parking lot, participants will know that there is already dedicated time to discuss it.

## Concepts

- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — Parking lots are a facilitation tool that maintains focus and psychological safety by managing scope creep and off-topic discussions; the technique requires clear introduction, active management, documented follow-up, and visible alternatives that capture ideas without derailing, keeping discussions productive.


- [[Team Collaboration]] — Collaborative parking lots (visible, updatable by all) build shared ownership; clustering and assignment of action items distribute responsibility fairly across the team.

- [[Design Process]] — Parking lots help design teams capture research questions and assumptions that emerge during workshops, informing future research plans and preventing rabbit-hole discussions.
