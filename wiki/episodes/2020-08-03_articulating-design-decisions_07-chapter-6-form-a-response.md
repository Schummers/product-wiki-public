# Articulating Design Decisions: Chapter 6. Form a Response

## Summary

Chapter 6 addresses how to respond to stakeholder feedback in design meetings by establishing a clear strategy and set of tactics. Part of Tom Greever's three-step communication framework (after defining problems and anticipating reactions), this chapter provides a formula for crafting compelling responses that connect design decisions to business goals, user needs, and demonstrated effectiveness. The chapter distinguishes between strategy (what to say) and tactics (how to deliver it), offering five specific techniques for making the case when stakeholders question or suggest changes to designs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Appeal to a nobler motive** — connect every design decision to an agreed-upon business goal, metric, or problem the organization is solving; this grounds discussions in shared priorities and keeps them from devolving into subjective preferences.
- **Represent the user** — move beyond generic references to "the user" and instead ground feedback responses in real observations; tell a story about a specific person whose need your design addresses to create empathy and motivate action.
- **Demonstrate effectiveness** — show (don't just talk about) why your solution is better by using visuals, prototypes, or side-by-side comparisons; images communicate design differences far more persuasively than words alone.
- **Five core tactics** — show a comparison, propose an alternative, give a choice (highlighting what will be lost), ask others to weigh in, or postpone the decision; each tactic serves different situations and can be used alone or in combination.
- **Redirect without derailing** — when feedback addresses issues outside the current project scope, reframe it as a future backlog item to keep the meeting focused on the stated goal rather than allowing scope creep.
- **Practice creates fluency** — responding well requires memorizing your strategy (answers to the Big Three questions) and practicing tactics on the fly; the more you practice, the more natural and conversational your responses become.

## Quotes

> "Responding to stakeholder feedback is a matter of forming your words in a way that will yield the best response by staying focused on the goal of the meeting: to get support from them and agreement to move forward."

> "We are the representatives of our users at this meeting."

> "Our job is to be so aware of these connections that we can respond, \"Yes, I love red! I want that side to be red too, but when I turn that side, it affects the other sides too and I have to solve the whole cube.\""

## Concepts

- [[Design Rationale]] — the chapter's three core questions (what problem does it solve, how does it affect the user, why is it better) form the strategic basis for any response; Greever frames these as the foundation for every communication with stakeholders.
- [[Design Presentation]] — the five tactics (comparison, alternative, choice, consensus, postponement) are the delivery mechanisms; choosing which to use depends on the situation and what will create the strongest case for your design.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — responding to feedback requires staying focused on objectives while addressing concerns; the goal is to move from general agreement to specific buy-in, which demands direct communication and intentional framing.
- [[Design Critique]] — the chapter treats design discussion as a structured dialogue rather than debate; it emphasizes understanding stakeholder motivations and finding win-win framing that preserves user experience while honoring business constraints.
