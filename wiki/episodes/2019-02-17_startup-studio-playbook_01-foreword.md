# Startup Studio Playbook: Foreword

## Summary

This foreword, written by Michael Jones from Science Inc., introduces the concept of technology studios as a framework for creating startups with greater speed and agility than traditional venture models. The book presents studios as structural models that combine ideation, investment, and resource redistribution to generate successful ventures. Jones contextualizes studios within his experience at Science Inc., which achieved three exits (Dollar Shave Club, HelloSociety, FameBit) by operating at the intersection of content and commerce.

## Key Takeaways

- **Studio as an innovation structure** — Studios borrow the term from filmmaking to describe organizations where artists, operators, and producers collaborate to create new ventures, bringing focus to innovation and entrepreneurship.
- **Ternary mission** — The studio model operates on three core activities: originating ideas and concepts, investing and advising, and acquiring properties to redistribute resources and scale them.
- **Speed and agility advantage** — The studio approach enables faster venture creation and iteration compared to traditional models, with the ability to learn from failure and scale proven ideas.
- **Providing infrastructure for entrepreneurship** — Beyond capital, studios offer team support, engineers, marketing, and a fertile environment where founders can focus on creation rather than operations.
- **Future expansion of studios** — The studio model is expected to expand beyond software and commerce into other growth areas, with the responsibility to identify trends and equip entrepreneurs with tools to create technology.

## Quotes

> The definition of studio – a structure where artists, operators and producers work - is much more potent when applied to innovation.

> Our responsibility is to identify trends and provide people the tools and space to create world-changing technology.

> The studio's mission is to provide team support, engineers, marketing encouragement, a fertile environment and become the experts in understanding how to leverage data and insights to move quickly, learn from failure, and iterate for value.
