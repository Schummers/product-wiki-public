# Remote UX Work: The NN/g Case Study

## Summary

Nielsen Norman Group operates as a fully remote, 100% distributed team across the United States and has done so for over 20 years. This case study details challenges and solutions for remote UX work: ideation and collaboration require webcam video alongside collaborative tools (Zoom, Miro, Google Docs); research need not be fully remote if team members are distributed geographically, allowing cost-effective travel to study locations. Developing new talent in a remote environment requires mentorship assignments, shadowing on projects, and team-building efforts (Slack channels, company retreats, monthly in-person gatherings). Employee evaluation is based on output and impact, not observable presence. The article recommends choosing the right team structure (full remote, hybrid, or split-time) and enforces an "equal communication rule" for hybrid teams: if any team member is remote, all should join remotely to prevent remote members from being sidelined.

## Key Takeaways

- **Video and Collaborative Tools Are Essential** — share webcams alongside screen-sharing in synchronous sessions (Zoom, GoToMeeting) paired with digital-ideation tools (Google Docs, Miro, Mural) to enable effective remote ideation and collaboration.
- **Research Can Be Distributed** — remote teams do not require fully remote research; team members can travel to field-study locations, making research feasible and even geographically broader than co-located teams.
- **New Talent Needs Structured Support** — remote onboarding requires assigned mentors (beyond formal managers), shadowing on real projects, welcome gifts, company swag, and periodic in-person team gatherings to build connection.
- **Equal Communication Rule for Hybrid Teams** — if any team member is remote, all should join video calls remotely (even co-located members) to prevent remote participants from being talked over and excluded.
- **Evaluate by Output and Impact** — remote work does not require visible presence; employee performance is assessed by tangible contributions and impact, which can be determined as effectively remotely as in-person.

## Quotes

> At NN/g we often use multiple tools to achieve a smooth remote ideation workshop. For example, we might share webcam feeds and audio using Zoom or GoToMeeting, while we share ideas visually using a collaborative product like Google Docs, Miro, or Mural.

> If one person in a meeting is remote, everyone in the meeting should join remotely, even if some are actually in the same building. This might seem counter-intuitive, but it ensures that you won't end up with that one remote person being talked over and left out.

## Concepts

- [[Remote Work]] — remote UX teams require intentional tool selection, structured mentorship, and periodic in-person connection; 20+ years of NN/g experience demonstrates remote work is viable when communication practices and team building are prioritized.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — remote collaboration succeeds with paired video conferencing and digital-ideation tools; synchronous sessions paired with asynchronous contribution spaces (documents, message threads) enable effective brainstorming and decision-making.
- [[Distributed Teams]] — distributed teams can access broader talent pools and geographies; research conducted by locally distributed team members is cost-effective and supports field studies across diverse locations.
