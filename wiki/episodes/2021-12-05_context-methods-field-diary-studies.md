# When to Use Context Methods: Field and Diary Studies

## Summary

Context methods are user-research approaches involving observation of users in their real-life environments. They differ from hypothetical scenarios (usability tests) and self-reported past behavior (interviews). Three popular context methods are diary studies, field studies, and contextual inquiry. Diary studies have participants document experiences over days or weeks; field studies involve researchers observing in the participant's environment; contextual inquiry combines observation with interview questions. These methods ensure research has high ecological validity — that findings reflect how users actually behave outside laboratory settings. They are particularly valuable during discovery phases and for developing customer-journey maps.

## Key Takeaways

- **Context methods reveal real-world complexity** — They uncover discrepancies between what users believe they do and what they actually do, workarounds they employ, tools they use, interruptions they face, and edge cases.
- **Diary studies capture behavior over time** — Participants self-document experiences over days, weeks, or longer. Researchers provide prompts and follow-up questions. This method reveals habits, routines, and patterns that emerge over extended periods.
- **Field studies observe without interruption** — Researchers observe participants in their natural environment, acting as a "fly on the wall." They document behavior and context for 1-2 hours typically, capturing the full ecosystem users operate within.
- **Contextual inquiry combines observation and interview** — Researchers observe participants while asking clarification questions to augment and validate their understanding, unlike traditional field studies that emphasize observation without interaction.
- **Choose based on research questions** — Use diary studies to understand user experience over time. Use field studies or contextual inquiry to see how users complete tasks in their actual environment with their tools, people, and constraints.

## Quotes

> Context methods are user-research methods that involve studying users in their real-life environments. The goal of context methods is to learn about users' environments, workflows, tools, pain points, and habits.

> Context methods ensure that our research has high ecological validity(which is similar to external validity) — in other words, that we have an accurate understanding of how users behave naturally in their real lives.

> Context studies use a very different approach. They involve observing users' behaviors in their real-life context.

- [[Field Studies]] — Field studies and contextual inquiry are ethnographic methods involving direct observation in the user's natural environment, capturing real tools, interruptions, and interpersonal dynamics while emphasizing understanding of culture and context.
- [[Diary Studies]] — Diary studies track user experiences over extended time periods through self-documentation, revealing patterns and habits.
- [[Ecological Validity]] — Context methods ensure findings reflect authentic behaviors rather than laboratory or hypothetical scenarios.
