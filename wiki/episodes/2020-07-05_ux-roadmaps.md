# UX Roadmaps: Definition and Components

## Summary

A UX roadmap is a strategic, living artifact that aligns, prioritizes, and communicates a UX team's future work and problems to solve. Unlike release plans or project-management artifacts, roadmaps communicate vision, not implementation. They organize work by context (scope and time horizons: Now, Next, Future) and theme. Each theme includes three components: the beneficiary and their need, the business outcome, and ownership/work type. Successful roadmaps are based on user research, user-focused rather than feature-focused, contextually appropriate to the organization, and collaborative. They differ fundamentally from release plans (execution), Kanban boards (task tracking), product backlogs (detailed tasks), and customer-journey maps (user understanding).

## Key Takeaways

- **Roadmaps communicate strategy, not execution** — A UX roadmap answers "What problems should we solve?" not "How will we build it?" It presents high-level user problems and outcomes, not discrete features or tasks. Do not confuse roadmaps with release plans, project backlogs, or Kanban boards.
- **Structure roadmaps around time horizons and themes** — Organize into Now (well-defined, in-progress work), Next (near-future work), and Future (6+ months, more ambiguous). Themes combine beneficiary/need, business outcome, and ownership. This structure scales to any product complexity.
- **Theme components include beneficiary, business outcome, and ownership** — Each theme identifies who benefits (users, employees, etc.), what problem is solved, the business impact, and which team or person will do the work with what type of effort (research, design, strategy, etc.).
- **Base roadmaps on user research, not feature wishlists** — Roadmaps should reflect user problems discovered through research, not accumulated feature requests. If you're transcribing a feature list into a timeline, you're building a release plan, not a roadmap.
- **Add secondary components for context** — Optionally include product areas (for complex products), subthemes (specific aspects), confidence estimates (high/low based on research validation), or disclaimers (risks or requirements). Tailor these to your audience and context.

## Quotes

> UX roadmap: A strategic, living artifact that aligns, prioritizes, and communicates a UX team's future work and problems to solve.

> Roadmaps are successful when they make realistic promises, value functionality over pretty visuals, or are strategic documents instead of feature-specific release plans.

> A UX roadmap is not a release plan, it is a strategic document. It should act as a single source of truth amongst designers, researchers, developers, and stakeholders to define, organize, prioritize, and then communicate future work towards your UX team's vision.
