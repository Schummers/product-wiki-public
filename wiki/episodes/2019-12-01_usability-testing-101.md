# Usability (User) Testing 101

## Summary

Usability testing is a popular UX research methodology in which a facilitator observes participants performing tasks with user interfaces to identify design problems, discover improvements, and learn about user behavior. Testing includes core elements of facilitator, tasks, and participants, each with specific roles and considerations. Testing can be qualitative or quantitative, remote or in-person, moderated or unmoderated. Costs vary widely from inexpensive discount studies (a few hundred dollars, three days) to elaborate studies (hundreds of thousands) depending on scope, geographic needs, and user groups tested.

## Key Takeaways

- **Core elements** — facilitator guides the test and observes without influencing behavior; tasks are realistic activities matching research questions; participants should be realistic users of the product or similar background.
- **Think-aloud method** — participants narrate actions and thoughts while performing tasks; helps researchers understand behaviors, goals, motivations, and reasoning; commonly used but requires training from facilitators.
- **Qualitative vs. quantitative** — qualitative focuses on insights and discovery of problems (five participants uncover most common issues); quantitative focuses on metrics like task success and time-on-task for benchmarking.
- **Remote vs. in-person** — remote moderated tests preserve facilitator interaction via screen-sharing; remote unmoderated tests use software to administer tasks and collect recordings, faster and cheaper but less interactive.
- **Discount usability** — simple studies feasible in conference rooms with three days effort (plan, test five users, analyze) and few hundred dollars for incentives; still valuable for iterative improvement.
- **Advanced studies cost more** — multi-design comparison, international testing, multiple user groups, quantitative approaches, eyetracking, formal lab settings, and detailed analysis reports increase costs into hundreds of thousands.

## Quotes

> In a usability-testing session, a researcher (called a "facilitator" or a "moderator") asks a participant to perform tasks, usually using one or more specific user interfaces. While the participant completes each task, the researcher observes the participant's behavior and listens for feedback.

> The only way to get UX design right is to test it.

> There are many variables in designing a modern user interface and there are even more variables in the human brain. The total number of combinations is huge. The only way to get UX design right is to test it.
