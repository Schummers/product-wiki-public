# Creating Engaging Reports & Asynchronous Presentations

## Summary

Research data alone does not convince others without compelling presentation. This article addresses how to make asynchronous research deliverables (reports and presentation decks) engaging when the author cannot provide live storytelling. The article recommends structuring reports for three audience types (headline readers, theme skimmers, detail explorers), using anecdotes to create emotional connection, finding analogies to make abstract numbers tangible, breaking up text-heavy content, and even exploring nonlinear formats like choose-your-own-adventure presentations. The goal is to help teams and stakeholders care about users so they adopt research-driven recommendations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Structure reports for three reading levels** — headlines for executives, big themes for stakeholders, detailed data for specialists — so each audience finds relevant information.
- **Anecdotes persuade more than numbers alone** by creating emotional connection, though they should support rather than replace quantitative evidence.
- **Use relatable analogies** to make abstract statistics tangible (e.g., comparing abandonment rate to an airplane's economy class walking off).
- **Break up text-heavy content** with varied formatting (checklists, charts, quotes, images) to increase engagement and scannability.
- **Avoid assuming shared findings reach everyone** — lay out all facts does not guarantee agreement; narrative and polish matter.
- **Choose-your-own-adventure formats** can serve multiple audiences by allowing different paths through findings.

## Quotes

> There is a misconception in the UX world that research data speaks for itself and shouldn't need the addition of a narrative or extra polish to convince others.

> People can depersonalize large numbers and forget that such numbers stand for real people.

> When our team members and stakeholders care about users, it's less of an uphill battle to sell them on our ideas and recommendations.
