# Icon IQ: Test Your Digital Icon Knowledge with a Quiz

## Summary

This article presents findings from Kate Kaplan's research on digital icon design, which formed the basis of the NN/g book "Digital Icons That Work." Through quantitative and qualitative research, Kaplan explored how users notice, recognize, and interpret icons. The article frames the problem of icon effectiveness in UX design, where bad icons create confusion and drag down usability, while effective icons enhance clarity, findability, and visual appeal.

## Key Takeaways

- **Icons require testing** — don't assume that simple icons will be universally understood; user recognition and interpretation vary greatly even for straightforward symbols.
- **Universal icons are rare** — while some icons like location pins are more consistently recognized, many icons have ambiguous interpretations across different user populations.
- **Research-driven icon design matters** — data-driven approaches to icon creation, based on rigorous research, lead to icons that users actually understand and can use effectively in interfaces.
- **Icon psychology involves two variables** — both recognition (can users identify the shape) and interpretation (what users think the icon means) are critical to icon usability.

## Quotes

> Effective icons help our designs achieve a host of benefits that optimize usability, such as enhanced clarity, increased findability, and greater visual appeal. Bad icons, however, drag down UIs by creating confusion and clutter.

> Are there universal icons? Which ones (if any) are generally "safe" to use? And, most importantly, are there icons that users consistently understand?
