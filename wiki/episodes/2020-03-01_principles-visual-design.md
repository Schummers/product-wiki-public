# 5 Principles of Visual Design in UX

## Summary

Five fundamental principles guide effective visual design in UX: scale uses relative size to signal importance; visual hierarchy guides the eye through elements in order of importance; balance distributes visual weight across an imaginary axis; contrast juxtaposes dissimilar elements to emphasize differences; and Gestalt principles explain how humans organize complex images into coherent wholes. Good visual design increases usability by making layouts easy to understand, provokes positive emotion and delight, and strengthens brand perception and trust. Visual design is not decoration; it directly impacts task success, user engagement, and emotional response.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scale signals importance** — The principle of scale uses relative size to indicate importance and rank. Bigger elements are noticed first. Effective designs use no more than three different sizes to create variety while establishing clear hierarchy.

- **Visual hierarchy guides attention** — A layout with good visual hierarchy is easily understood. Hierarchy is created through variations in scale, value, color, spacing, and placement. Users should immediately understand what to focus on and in what order.

- **Balance creates satisfaction** — Balance occurs when visual weight is equally distributed on both sides of an imaginary axis, but not necessarily symmetrically. Symmetry is static and quiet, asymmetry is dynamic and engaging. Balance can be symmetrical, asymmetrical, or radial.

- **Contrast emphasizes difference** — Contrast juxtaposes visually dissimilar elements to show they are different categories or functions. High-contrast colors (like red for delete actions) signal distinctness. But avoid reducing text contrast for deemphasis—this compromises accessibility.

- **Gestalt principles explain perception** — Proximity means visually close items are perceived as groups. Similarity, continuation, closure, common region, figure/ground, and symmetry explain how humans organize elements into organized systems rather than seeing separate pieces.

- **Visual design affects outcomes** — Beyond aesthetics, visual design increases usability, provokes positive emotion (aesthetic-usability effect), and strengthens brand perception and trust.

## Quotes

> In other words, when this principle is used properly, the most important elements in a design are bigger than the ones that are less important. The reason behind this principle is simple: when something is big, it's more likely to be noticed.

Having a range of differently sized elements will not only create variety within your layout, but it will also establish a visual hierarchy (see next principle) on the page. Be sure to emphasize the most important aspect of your design by making them biggest.
