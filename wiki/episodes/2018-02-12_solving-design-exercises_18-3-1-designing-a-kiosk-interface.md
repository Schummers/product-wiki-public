# Solving Product Design Exercises: 3.1. Designing a kiosk interface

## Summary

Chapter 3.1 is a worked example demonstrating the author's seven-step design exercise framework applied to a kiosk interface for refilling soap and shampoo. The candidate designs a retail kiosk with sustainability and value propositions, defines the eco-conscious and budget-conscious audience, analyzes retail context and constraints, and designs the interface prioritizing efficiency and single-handed operation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two of the seven steps are skipped, deliberately** — the chapter runs goal, audience, context, solve and measure, and states outright that steps 4 and 5 (list ideas, prioritise) are unnecessary here because the product to build is already given by the brief
- **Value propositions drive decisions** — the candidate identifies two main propositions (sustainability through eliminating plastic waste, and value through competitive pricing), which inform every subsequent design choice
- **Context constraints shape design** — the retail environment creates specific requirements: queues form, customers carry shopping bags (one-handed operation needed), lighting is adequate, and hygiene motivation is unsophisticated
- **Efficiency reduces queues and increases sales** — a clear interface minimizing operation time directly impacts business metrics; queue perception alone can frighten away customers
- **Multiple screens and interactions** — the design includes a promotional default screen, product and flavour selection with quantity controls, payment instructions, filling process with countdown, and a final impact-awareness screen

## Quotes

> To start mapping out the interface, I'll take a use-case of a customer purchasing 200ml of soap and 400ml of shampoo.

> A more efficient interface that requires less time to operate would mean fewer queues and more sales.

> On the final screen, after the transaction ends, we could display information about the resources the customer saved by choosing our products over bottled ones.
