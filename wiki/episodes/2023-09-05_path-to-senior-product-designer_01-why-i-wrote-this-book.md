# The Path to Senior Product Designer: Why I Wrote This Book

## Summary

This introductory chapter establishes the book's premise by examining why product designers struggle with career advancement despite their talent and roles at prestigious companies. Dashinsky explains his own departure from WeWork in 2017 due to unclear career development, validating his premise with McKinsey research showing that lack of career advancement is the number one reason employees leave jobs. The chapter identifies five interconnected systemic challenges designers face: the scarcity of formalized career progression systems (71% of companies lack them), limited advancement options for individual contributors who resist management, insufficient design leadership capacity, role confusion from overlapping competencies between designers and product managers, and the persistent legacy of design being perceived as visual work rather than strategic contribution.

## Key Takeaways

- **Lack of Career Paths** — Citing the 2019 Design Career Index report, 71% of companies lack any formalized career progression, and the smaller the company the lower the chance it has a career ladder: a company of more than 100 employees is twice as likely to have one as a company of 25 to 49.
- **Limited Individual Contributor Advancement** — Designers seeking senior roles while remaining individual contributors face three problematic options: leave for larger companies with staff/principal roles, remain stuck as senior designers, or reluctantly move into management where design work gradually disappears.
- **Design Leadership Shortage** — Many organizations lack strong design leaders capable of creating career development plans, perpetuating a cycle where forced-manager designers lack motivation to build paths for others, and non-designer managers lack knowledge to guide designers.
- **PM-Designer Competency Overlap** — Shared responsibilities between product managers and designers can minimize design's strategic role to visual work alone, reducing designer influence and limiting growth opportunities.
- **Visual Design Legacy** — Years of emphasis on aesthetics (the "dribbblization of design") left designers without the business communication, stakeholder management, ownership, and strategic thinking skills needed to prove broader value.
- **Organizational Cost of Turnover** — Citing Gallup, the chapter puts the cost of replacing an individual employee at between 50% and 200% of that employee's annual salary depending on seniority; applied to the Glassdoor salary range for product designers, it estimates a loss of $37,000 to $234,000 per departing designer.

## Quotes

> According to a McKinsey report, lack of career development and advancement is the number one reason that people quit their jobs. Forty-one percent of people surveyed named it as the main reason they left; far more than any other reasons like inadequate compensation (36%), uncaring/uninspiring leaders (34%), lack of meaningful work (31%), unsustainable work expectations (29%), or lack of workspace flexibility (26%).

> A lack of career paths creates unmotivated managers – As I said in the previous point, when designers have no other choice but to become design managers in order to grow within the organization, it creates leaders whose real desire is to focus on products and not on people.

> Ironically, because we've been focused on improving our UI skills, we've neglected those skills that will help us change that perception and prove our value to businesses: communication, stakeholder management, ownership, strategic thinking, and hiring.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — The central problem the chapter establishes: designers lack clear career development systems and advancement guidance from their organizations.
- [[Career Ladder]] — The chapter reports, from the 2019 Design Career Index, that 71% of companies have no formalized career progression, and that company size correlates with having one.
- [[Design Leadership]] — Identified as critically scarce; weak design leaders cannot develop reports because they were forced into management themselves or lack design expertise.
- [[Career Growth Plan]] — Proposed as the solution to designer advancement challenges; the book aims to provide knowledge and tools to create these plans.
- [[Design Competencies]] — The skills designers need to develop beyond visual design, including communication, stakeholder management, ownership, and strategic thinking to prove business value.
- [[Business Impact]] — Framed as essential for designer growth; designers must move from being perceived as UI-focused to demonstrating value in fixing business problems.
