# Flat-Design Best Practices

## Summary

Flat design has become ubiquitous in digital products since around 2012, adopted by major companies like Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon. While it offers aesthetic benefits such as conveying luxury or trendiness and providing lightweight UIs that scale well across devices, flat design carries a significant usability problem: it often creates click uncertainty by removing visual signifiers that tell users where they can interact. This article provides strategies for implementing flat design without sacrificing usability, including using semiflat or "flat 2.0" designs that incorporate subtle depth rather than ultra-flat aesthetics.

## Key Takeaways

- **Not always appropriate** — Totally flat designs work best only for simple sites with low interactivity, high returning-visitor rates, and tech-expert users; most digital products benefit from a semiflat or "flat 2.0" approach instead.
- **Click uncertainty is a major risk** — Flat design's removal of traditional signifiers (shadows, highlights, dimensional cues) often leads users to be uncertain about what elements are clickable, reducing user efficiency and causing missed interactions.
- **Communicate clickability explicitly** — Never use the same visual treatment for static and interactive text; use color, button-like appearance, recognizable icons with labels, and consistent styling throughout to signal what is clickable.
- **Leverage standard layouts** — Using traditional, expected layouts and UI patterns helps users understand element purpose even without strong visual signifiers, especially when combined with clean design and ample white space.
- **Prioritize contrast and legibility** — Light grey on dark grey and text over background images are problematic in flat design; ensure text and interactive elements have sufficient contrast to be clearly noticeable.
- **Add subtle depth strategically** — Incorporate shadows, highlights, and layering effects to clarify relationships between elements and support user mental models, rather than using them purely for aesthetics.

## Quotes

> Flat design has a major flaw: it often leads to click uncertainty and decreased user efficiency. When designers flatten the UI, they tend to remove many signifiers that normally tell users where to click.
