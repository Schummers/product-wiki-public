# 6 Types of Useful Smartwatch Interactions

## Summary

Analysis of over 200 smartwatch interactions from 11 participants identified six main types of interactions that users find valuable: receiving, referencing, recording, controlling, communicating, and guiding. Users prefer brief and simple interactions on smartwatches due to tiny screens and limited input methods. Each interaction type serves distinct user needs and requires specific design considerations to be effective. Some interaction types common in other contexts, like consuming and browsing, are rarely useful on smartwatches.

## Key Takeaways

- **Receiving interactions** (notifications, reminders, feedback, suggestions) are most common and valuable when informative, glanceable, personalized, and timely, though irrelevant notifications are highly intrusive on personal devices.
- **Referencing interactions** work best when information is simple, visual, accessible in flat hierarchies, and personally meaningful, allowing users to quickly check status without deep navigation.
- **Recording interactions** are most helpful when contextually prompted by the watch detecting activity, perceived as accurate, informative with real-time data, and easy to initiate and access.
- **Controlling interactions** are valued for allowing remote control of devices without retrieving phones, requiring functions to be prioritized, synchronized across devices, and accessible when hands are busy.
- **Communicating interactions** rely on comprehensive awareness of incoming messages with enough content displayed, though typing remains tedious and dictation error-prone, making message content critical for smartwatch notifications.
- **Guiding interactions** must be independent without requiring the phone, use haptic feedback to alert users, and remain focused on the next step rather than overwhelming with comprehensive information.

## Quotes

> The number of users who wear smartwatches is on the rise. As of 2020, Pew Research Center estimated that 1 in 5 Americans had a smartwatch or fitness tracker of some kind.

> Users seem to think about the smartwatch as a filter: they are less likely to tolerate irrelevant information coming to the watch than to any other device. It's like the difference between a salesperson trying to stop you on a busy street (a promotional email) and a salesperson approaching you while you are at a restaurant (a promotional notification on the smartwatch). Both are annoying, but the second feels much more intrusive.

> One of the most valuable things that the smartwatch affords is comprehensive awareness of all incoming communications. Smartwatch users seem to rely on the assumption that, if they are wearing their watch, they will not miss incoming communications.

## Concepts

- [[Smart Devices]] — the article identifies interaction types best suited to smartwatch constraints and advocates for designing interfaces that respect the device's limitations.
- [[Microinteractions]] — smartwatch interactions are typically small, focused moments (notifications, quick checks, one-tap controls) rather than extended engagement.
- [[Mobile Design]] — smartwatch design shares many principles with mobile design but with tighter constraints around screen size and input methods.
- [[Contextual Design]] — receiving and recording interactions are most effective when triggered by context (time, location, activity) rather than generic user requests.
- [[Notification Design]] — notifications are the most common smartwatch interaction and must be informative, timely, and personalized to avoid being intrusive.
