# Minimize the Need for Customer Service to Improve the Omnichannel UX

## Summary

Diary-study analysis of 45 customer journeys found that 64% required at least one customer service contact, meaning only 33% of tasks could be completed online without support. Customers contact organizations for service problems (failed transactions), roadblocks (unsupported tasks or errors), missing or confusing information, or perceived complexity. Minimizing service contacts requires understanding the full customer journey, designing consistently across channels, supporting smooth channel transitions, and providing multiple accessible contact options when self-service fails.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two-thirds of journeys require customer service** — Of 45 customer journeys analyzed, 64% included at least one user-initiated contact with the organization, indicating widespread failure of digital channels to meet user needs without human intervention.
- **Four primary reasons trigger customer contact** — Service problems (broken products, failed transactions), roadblocks (unsupported tasks, errors), missing or confusing information (lack of comparison detail, policy ambiguity), and perceived complexity drive the majority of contacts.
- **Missing information is the top driver** — 38% of service contacts resulted from ambiguous or absent content that left users unable to interpret policies or make confident decisions without reassurance from a representative.
- **Understand and design for the full journey** — Successful omnichannel experience requires mapping customer expectations at each stage, identifying likely channel transitions, and removing friction where users must switch channels mid-task.
- **Multiple contact channels improve outcomes** — Phone remains most common, but email, chat, and social channels serve different urgencies and contexts. Offering options prevents frustration and allows users to select the channel best suited to their situation.
- **Never eliminate customer service, but eliminate the need for it** — Do not hide contact information. Instead, redesign to prevent problems and roadblocks; when users still need support, make it easily accessible and seamless to reach.

## Quotes

> Only a third of customer journeys could be completed online without requiring the customer to contact the organization for support. This means a 2/3 failure rate for a self-service goal such as decreasing call-center costs or improving customer satisfaction.

> Contacting customer service reflects a failure in the user experience.

> Interruptions and channel switching (from digital to human, or otherwise) degrade the customer experience. Over time, they negatively impact key metrics like customer satisfaction and loyalty, and ultimately your business' bottom line.

## Concepts

- [[Omnichannel Experience]] — Defines consistency, information continuity, and seamless channel transitions as essential to reducing service contact and improving self-service completion rates.
- [[Customer Journey]] — Emphasizes mapping full journeys including all stages, channel transitions, and information needs, not just individual touchpoints, to identify and eliminate roadblocks.
- [[Self-Service UX]] — Documents barriers to self-service completion (missing information, errors, unsupported tasks) and how addressing them directly reduces support costs and improves customer satisfaction.
- [[Information Design]] — Shows the critical role of clear, detailed, and accessible content in enabling users to make decisions without support, particularly around policies and product comparisons.
- [[Roadblock Elimination]] — Identifies errors, unsupported functionality, and missing information as roadblocks that force channel switching and service contact, and emphasizes proactive elimination as key to seamless experience.
- [[Service Integration]] — Illustrates how inconsistent backend systems fail to share data across channels, requiring customers to repeat information and reducing confidence in the organization's capabilities.
