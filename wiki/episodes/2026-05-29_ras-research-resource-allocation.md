# Using RAS to Guide UX Research Resource Allocation and Strategy

## Summary

The Recommendation-Adoption Score (RAS) measures what proportion of research recommendations actually reach users through implementation. RAS forces conversations away from research activity (studies completed) toward impact (did the user experience improve). By combining RAS with velocity (recommendations per month), research leaders can make defensible allocation decisions on which product teams to invest in. The matrix signals stop, caution, or continue based on whether recommendations are being produced and adopted. This framework also helps match researcher seniority to product-team maturity: putting junior researchers on low-adoption teams sets them up for burnout, not growth.

## Key Takeaways

- **RAS Measures Impact, Not Output** — Busy research teams may complete many studies but have low adoption; RAS reveals which teams actually improve user experience versus which teams generate documents that get ignored.
- **Velocity Captures Momentum** — Velocity is the average number of recommendations per month; 0-1/month signals stop, 2-3/month signals caution, 3+/month signals continue; thresholds should be calibrated to organizational context.
- **RAS Trend Matters Over Time** — Track the six-month RAS rolling average; negative trends are red flags even on strong baselines; a positive trend is the signal to continue investment regardless of baseline level.
- **The Investment Decision Matrix** — Combining velocity and RAS trend creates a two-by-two matrix that signals whether to stop, proceed with caution, or continue research support for a specific product team.
- **Match Researcher to Team Maturity** — Early-career researchers assigned to low-adoption products burn out; successful teams are opportunities to develop junior researchers and reward senior ones; the team's RAS shapes the assignment.

## Quotes

> RAS tells you whether recommendations are being adopted. That is useful, but it is only one side of the coin.

> With RAS, you can see which team is actually making an impact on the user experience.

> Putting an early-career researcher on a low-adoption product is not a development opportunity. It is a setup for burnout that leadership will eventually misread as a performance problem.

## Concepts

- [[Research Metrics]] — Measuring research impact requires tracking recommendation adoption rather than study volume; RAS combines adoption with value weighting to reveal true research impact.
- [[Resource Allocation]] — Defensible allocation decisions require both productivity (velocity) and effectiveness (RAS trend); combining these creates clear signals for where to invest or divest research resources.
- [[Recommendation Adoption]] — Adoption depends on multiple factors beyond research quality; RAS coaching conversations help determine whether low adoption reflects research quality, stakeholder issues, or organizational culture.
