# Status Trackers and Progress Updates: 16 Design Guidelines

## Summary

This article outlines best practices for designing status trackers and progress updates that reduce customer anxiety by providing visibility into order delivery or service application progress. Status trackers are pull-based features users check at their convenience, while progress updates are push notifications sent when status changes. The guidelines cover both tracker design and notification strategy, emphasizing clarity, scannability, and user control while reducing support inquiries through improved transparency.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prioritize the latest update** — Status trackers should present the most recent update prominently and clearly, avoiding clutter that might bury critical information in longer text sections.
- **Use plain language without jargon** — Backend codes and internal terminology like "fulfilled" or "label created" confuse users; all status messages should be written in language users understand.
- **Make information scannable** — Present updates in a structured format with adequate spacing and visual distinction between entries so users can quickly find the information they need.
- **Allow users to update contact preferences** — Within the status tracker, users should be able to add or change contact information and modify the frequency of notifications they receive.
- **Provide regular updates for long processes** — When delays are inherent to the process, provide updates of lower granularity to reassure users that progress is being made and maintain trust.
- **Ensure accuracy and consistency** — Status information must be reliable and identical across all channels (web tracker, phone support, email); inconsistency erodes user trust.

## Quotes

> Good service design doesn't just leave users in the lurch. Instead, status trackers and timely progress updates can reduce their anxiety, by making the status of the delivery of that product or service visible.

> However, when there are long periods with no update, users start to think perhaps something went wrong, they lose trust in the status tracker, and begin to consider contacting the organization or company.

> It's a good idea to offer both a status tracker feature and progress updates. Having only a status tracker may induce anxiety, as users end up obsessively checking the tracker to see if there has been a change since they last checked.
