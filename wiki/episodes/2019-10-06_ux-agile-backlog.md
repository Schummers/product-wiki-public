# Incorporating UX Work into Your Agile Backlog

## Summary

Agile teams face a dilemma: how to include UX work in the backlog without it being deprioritized in favor of features. Three models exist: unified backlog with explicit UX items (visible but at risk of deprioritization), task-based unified backlog breaking items into role-specific subtasks (good when design and development fit one sprint), and multiple backlogs separating UX work from development. Each has tradeoffs. The best approach depends on team dynamics, stakeholder understanding of UX value, domain complexity, and whether UX can complete work in a single sprint or must work ahead of development.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three backlog structures offer different tradeoffs** — Unified with explicit UX items makes UX visible but risks deprioritization; task-based unified works best for well-understood domains fitting one sprint; separate backlogs give UX autonomy but lose velocity visibility.

- **Explicit UX items in unified backlogs are visible but vulnerable** — When UX work is tagged and prioritized alongside features, stakeholders see it clearly, but may consistently deprioritize research and design if they undervalue UX.

- **Task-based unified backlogs require strong estimation skills** — When multiple roles contribute to one item with different estimation approaches (story points for development, t-shirt sizes for UX), coordination and dependency tracking become complex.

- **Separate UX backlogs suit large teams or those working ahead** — Multiple UX specialties, multiple engineering teams (iOS, Android, web), or work that must precede development benefit from UX autonomy; requires discipline and communication to coordinate timing.

- **Dependencies must be explicitly tracked and managed** — In any model, UX work that blocks development must be visible and prioritized accordingly; poor dependency tracking risks stalling development sprints.

- **Backlog structure should match team context and evolve** — No single model suits all situations; teams should experiment and adapt as team dynamics, product maturity, and stakeholder understanding change.

## Quotes

> There is no definitive rulebook when it comes to Agile backlogs — every team may structure its backlog differently.

> The key is to understand how your team works together and how much influence your stakeholders have on the backlog.

> Your backlog structure may change from team to team and project to project. The great thing about backlogs is that you're able and required to be flexible.

## Concepts

- [[Agile Development]] — Agile flexibility enables different backlog structures; UX integration requires intentional choices about visibility, prioritization, and team structure.

- [[UX Process Integration]] — Incorporating UX into Agile requires addressing when UX work happens (concurrent with development or ahead), how visible it is, and how it's estimated and prioritized.

- [[Team Structure]] — Whether UX is integrated into feature teams or separate affects backlog structure; large teams with specialties may benefit from separate UX backlogs.

- [[Backlog Management]] — Managing UX work visibility, preventing deprioritization, tracking dependencies, and enabling look-ahead work all depend on backlog structure choices and team communication.
