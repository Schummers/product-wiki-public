# When to Use Empathy Maps: 3 Options

## Summary

Empathy maps are collaborative visualization tools that capture understanding of users across four quadrants: what users say, think, do, and feel. While often seen as post-research artifacts for communicating findings, this article demonstrates that empathy maps are flexible tools valuable at three distinct phases of the design process. Teams can use them before research to plan studies, during research to capture observations, or after research to communicate findings and build team buy-in. The structured format makes empathy maps accessible even to team members without research experience, making them effective for engaging diverse stakeholders.

## Key Takeaways

- **Empathy maps work at multiple stages** — they can be used before research to identify knowledge gaps, during research to capture observations, or after research to communicate findings to stakeholders.
- **Pre-research empathy maps identify gaps** — teams can document current knowledge with confidence ratings and use gaps to inform research questions, making future studies more focused.
- **Collaborative creation builds buy-in** — involving the wider team in creating empathy maps from research data creates ownership and ensures all stakeholders understand user needs.
- **Accessible format enables participation** — the simple four-quadrant structure is approachable for non-researchers, making empathy maps effective tools for team-wide engagement.
- **Sticky notes and clustering accelerate insights** — using color-coded sticky notes during observation or after research, followed by affinity diagramming, helps teams organize and discuss findings.

## Quotes

> An empathy map is a visualization that captures our understanding of a set of users. It is a simple yet powerful tool for making sense of user research and communicating what we know about our users to design-team partners and stakeholders so that we can discuss and prioritize user needs collaboratively.

> Not only does this approach create an artifact that communicates research findings in an approachable way, it also builds buy-in and ownership of the research because it invites the wider team to engage with the findings and collaboratively discuss and make sense of them.

> ## Conclusion

Empathy mapping can be used before, during, or after research to engage teams and communicate insights.
