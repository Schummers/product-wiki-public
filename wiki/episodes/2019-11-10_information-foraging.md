# Information Foraging: A Theory of How People Navigate on the Web

## Summary

Information foraging theory, developed at PARC by Peter Pirolli and Stuart Card, explains how people navigate the web to satisfy information needs. Users assess information value relative to the cost of obtaining it, attempting to maximize their rate of gain of relevant information over time. The theory draws from animal foraging behavior and is based on bounded rationality, where users employ satisficing and imperfect heuristics rather than perfectly rational decisions. Information scent, a key concept, describes how users judge the relevance of pages and links based on visible cues before committing time to exploring them.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rate of gain optimization** — users decide which information source to explore based on maximizing the ratio of information value to cost of obtaining that information; get as much relevant information as possible in minimum time.
- **Bounded rationality principle** — humans cannot precisely estimate benefit and cost, so use satisficing and imperfect heuristics rather than achieving theoretical optimal choices; overall effect still aims for highest rate of gain.
- **Information scent concept** — users judge webpage relevance by visible cues (titles, images, above-fold content, link text); high information scent signals the page contains what users need; varies depending on individual information need.
- **Two types of costs** — actual time and effort extracting information, and opportunity costs from foregoing other documents; users quickly extract gists without deep reading because moving to different pages may be more profitable.
- **Enrichments minimize time** — behavioral enrichments (learned habits like page parking, F-pattern scanning) and interaction enrichments (setting filters, keywords) help users minimize between-patch and within-patch time costs.
- **Good design eliminates enrichment needs** — well-designed pages with scanning-friendly formatting, bolded keywords, descriptive headlines maximize efficiency; optimization focuses on top tasks served by the page.

## Quotes

> When people look for information on the web, they attempt to maximize the rate of information gain over time: they want to get as much information as possible in the minimum amount of time.

> In layman terms, information foraging explains why people don't scroll mindlessly or click on every single link on the page: because they attempt to maximize the rate of gain and get as much relevant information in as little time as possible.

> A good user experience involves web pages that are designed so that the user can get the maximum relevant information in the minimum amount of time.
