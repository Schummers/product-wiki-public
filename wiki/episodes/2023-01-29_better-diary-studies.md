# 6 Tips for Better Participant Engagement in Diary Studies

## Summary

Diary studies are longitudinal research methods where participants report thoughts, feelings, and behaviors over time. They are valuable for understanding experiences that unfold over extended periods but face challenges including participant forgetfulness, waning interest, fatigue, and dropout. The article presents 6 evidence-based tips to maintain participant engagement throughout diary studies: recruit participants likely to stay engaged, choose appropriate tools, onboard participants properly, keep entries short, maintain contact, and structure incentives creatively.

## Key Takeaways

- **Recruit wisely with commitment screening** — Clearly explain time commitments in recruitment materials and use screener questions to ensure participants understand and accept the required effort; include open-ended questions to gauge commitment and writing comfort.
- **Tool choice affects engagement** — Minimize interaction cost by choosing tools that match participants' primary device and comfort level (e.g., mobile apps for video/voice, desktop tools for computer-based activities); pilot test tools before launching.
- **Onboarding prevents dropout** — Schedule pre-study briefs covering tool walkthrough, study procedures, what constitutes helpful entries, incentive structure, and conditions for study withdrawal; pre-study briefs build rapport and prevent technical confusion.
- **Keep entries brief (under 15 minutes)** — Minimize questions and prompts to only those necessary for research questions; longer entry requirements increase dropout risk and fatigue.
- **Maintain contact and reminders** — Check in regularly, thank participants, prompt quiet participants to re-engage, and schedule reminders for timed entries; however, avoid overwhelming participants with too many messages.
- **Creative incentive structures** — Provide appropriate compensation for time invested; interval payments reduce dropout risk compared to single end-of-study payment; per-entry or tiered payment encourages consistent contributions; bonus payments reward consistency.

## Concepts

- [[Diary Studies]] — Effective diary studies require careful participant selection, tool choice, onboarding, brief entry templates, regular contact, and thoughtful incentive structures to maintain engagement over time.
- [[Participant Recruitment]] — Recruiting well-matched participants with clear commitment expectations and realistic time estimates is the foundation for successful diary study completion, with incentive timing and structure directly influencing engagement; interval payments and tiered or per-entry compensation models outperform single end-of-study payments for longer studies.
- [[Study Design]] — Diary study design significantly impacts completion rates; entry length, contact frequency, and incentive timing are critical design variables affecting participant engagement.
