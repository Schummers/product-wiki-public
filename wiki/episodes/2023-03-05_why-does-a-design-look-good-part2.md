# The Anatomy of a Good Design: An Analysis of 4 Sites

## Summary

This article analyzes four well-designed websites to identify the specific visual-design principles that make them aesthetically pleasing and functional. Through detailed examination, the authors demonstrate how alignment to grids, strategic use of type, spacing, color, imagery, and scale create designs that are both beautiful and usable. The analysis covers Dovetail's data-analysis platform (grid alignment and typography), MoMA's website (modular grids and asymmetrical balance), Aesop's luxury skincare site (monochromatic palettes and symmetry), and Superhuman's email client (scale and subtle gradients). The article shows that good design doesn't require complexity or decoration — simplicity enhanced by thoughtful application of fundamental design principles is most effective.

## Key Takeaways

- **Grids create visual order** — alignment to consistent grids, whether three-column or modular, gives designs polish and makes elements appear intentionally organized rather than arbitrary.
- **Typography carries hierarchy** — varying text size (30–50% larger for important elements), weight, and color using limited type families creates visual hierarchy without decoration.
- **Spacing enables scannability** — generous leading (line spacing), appropriate line length, and white space between elements make designs readable and comfortable rather than dense or overwhelming.
- **Color systems need harmony** — whether monochromatic (3–4 colors) or complex, colors must work together intentionally; monochromatic palettes are easiest to execute successfully.
- **Balance can be asymmetrical** — asymmetrical balance (weighting opposite sides differently) creates energy and engagement, while symmetrical balance feels calm and static.
- **Imagery must be meaningful** — images should add information or context rather than serve as pure decoration; cropping matters, especially across different screen sizes.

## Concepts

- [[Visual Design]] — visual design excellence comes from consistent application of principles like grids, typography, color harmony, and balance rather than decoration or complexity.
- [[Design Principles]] — understanding grid alignment, type hierarchy, color theory, symmetry, and scale helps designers create aesthetically pleasing interfaces that support usability.
- [[User Interface]] — good interface design leverages whitespace, consistent iconography, meaningful imagery, and subtle visual signals (shadows, gradients) to guide users without distraction.
