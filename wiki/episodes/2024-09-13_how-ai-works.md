# How Do Generative AI Systems Work?

## Summary

This article provides a simplified, nontechnical explanation of how large language models (LLMs) and generative AI systems function. Using metaphors and accessible language, the author explains the core principles: LLMs are probabilistic systems that predict word sequences based on patterns in training data, built on neural network architectures with multiple layers of interconnected "neurons." The explanation covers word embeddings (how words are represented mathematically), neural networks (computational systems inspired by brains), and transformers (the architecture that enables modern LLMs to handle long passages of text and understand context).

## Key Takeaways

- **LLMs are sequence prediction machines** — they complete sequences by predicting the most likely next word, similar to an advanced version of Mad Libs, not magic or knowledge retrieval.
- **Neural networks learn through training** — networks adjust their internal weights and biases (parameters) when exposed to training data to improve their ability to predict correct outputs.
- **Word embeddings enable semantic reasoning** — words are represented as points in multidimensional space, where similar words are positioned near each other, allowing the AI to reason about relationships between concepts.
- **Transformers revolutionized AI through parallel processing and self-attention** — the transformer architecture allows faster training by processing words in parallel and enables context awareness by letting each word "know about" all other words in a passage.
- **AI remains partially unexplainable** — while the mechanisms are understood mathematically, it is not precisely known what each dimension of word embeddings represents, contributing to AI being called a "black box."

## Quotes

> Large language models are probabilistic systems that attempt to predict word sequences. That's what generative AI systems (genAI) do — they are making word-by-word predictions in the context of your prompt.

> Transformers are relatively recent (introduced in 2017 by several Google researchers in a famous paper called "Attention Is All You Need") and have two big advantages that led to the dramatic rise in AI capabilities: speed and contextualization.

> With a self-attention mechanism,**each word "knows about" all the other words in the passage and how they are related.**

## Concepts

- [[Large Language Model]] — probabilistic systems trained on vast text corpora that predict word sequences and generate coherent responses based on statistical patterns in language.
- [[Neural Networks]] — computational systems with layers of interconnected nodes that learn patterns from training data by adjusting internal weights and biases.
- [[Transformer Architecture]] — a neural network design that processes inputs in parallel and uses self-attention mechanisms to understand relationships between all words in a passage, enabling modern LLM capabilities.
