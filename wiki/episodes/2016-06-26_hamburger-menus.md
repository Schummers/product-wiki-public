# Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation Hurt UX Metrics

## Summary

Quantitative usability testing of hidden menus (such as hamburger icons) reveals that hidden navigation is significantly less discoverable than visible or partially visible navigation across multiple device types. Hidden navigation provides worse user experience than visible alternatives on both mobile phones and desktop user interfaces, as measured across multiple UX metrics including users' assessment of task difficulty, time spent on task, and task success. This finding particularly holds on desktops, where hiding navigation degrades the experience more than on phones. The article concludes that mobile-first design should not become mobile-only design, and different design approaches should be optimized for each platform's strengths and constraints.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hidden navigation is less discoverable** — On desktop, users employed hidden navigation in only 27% of cases versus 48% for visible and 50% for combo navigation; on mobile, hidden navigation was used in 57% versus 86% for combo navigation.
- **Hidden navigation takes longer to use** — Users took 5-7 seconds more to access hidden navigation on desktop compared to visible or combo navigation, and 2 seconds more on mobile.
- **Content discoverability drops significantly** — Sites with hidden navigation showed a 20% drop in content discoverability compared to sites with visible or combo navigation.
- **Task difficulty increases** — Users rated tasks 21% more difficult on sites with hidden navigation compared to visible navigation, and 11% more difficult compared to combo navigation.
- **Desktop users are significantly slower** — On desktop, users took at least 39% longer to complete tasks with hidden navigation; on mobile, they were 15% slower.

## Quotes

> On desktops, hiding navigation degrades the experience and the navigation discoverability more than it does on the phones.

> Hiding the navigation mostly affects content that is not directly accessible through an in-page link.

## Concepts

- [[Navigation Design]] — Hidden navigation patterns like hamburger menus significantly impair navigation performance; visible navigation is substantially more discoverable and leads to better task completion.
- [[Mobile Design]] — While hamburger menus may be necessary on mobile due to space constraints, they should not be ported to desktop designs where screen space allows for visible navigation.
- [[Discoverability]] — Visual prominence is key to navigation discoverability; hidden menus suffer from low salience, low information scent, extra work to access, and low familiarity.
- [[User Experience]] — Users take longer and encounter higher difficulty with hidden navigation, and hidden navigation mostly affects content not directly accessible through in-page links.
- [[Design Patterns]] — Designers should not uncritically adopt mobile patterns on desktop; instead, each platform should leverage its unique capabilities to create optimized user experiences.
