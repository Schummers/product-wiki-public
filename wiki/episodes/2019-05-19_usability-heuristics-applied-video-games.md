# 10 Usability Heuristics Applied to Video Games

## Summary

Jakob Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics, originally developed for user interfaces, apply equally to video games. Although games aim to entertain rather than accomplish practical tasks, the principles of visibility, matching system language, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition, efficiency, minimalism, error recovery, and help remain relevant. The article demonstrates each heuristic with examples from popular games, showing how good design principles transfer across domains.

## Key Takeaways

- **Visibility of system status** — Health meters, score displays, and status indicators keep players informed; real-time feedback helps players decide next actions and adjust strategy.

- **Match system and real world** — Using real-world terminology and conventions (actual weapon names, familiar keyboard layouts) helps players apply existing mental models and reduces cognitive load.

- **User control and freedom** — Undo and redo functions, ability to exit unwanted states, and save points let players recover from mistakes and experiment without permanent consequences.

- **Consistency and standards** — Games that follow established control conventions (like A button for jump) are easier to learn; breaking standards increases cognitive load and frustration.

- **Error prevention** — Confirmation dialogs before quitting, clear mode indicators, and preventing impossible actions (disabling unavailable options) help players avoid irreversible mistakes.

- **Recognition over recall** — Contextual controls displayed on screen, maps, and waypoints reduce memory load; players should not need to memorize button combinations or navigate from memory.

- **Flexibility and efficiency** — Hotkeys and customizable controls serve both novice and expert players; power users can create setups matching their playstyle.

- **Aesthetic and minimalist design** — Overlay information in corners, progressive disclosure, and focused gameplay screens prevent cognitive overload while maintaining visual appeal.

## Quotes

> "Video games may seem very different from websites or apps: they don't usually have a practical goal (such as to inform people or help them create an artifact), but instead they aim to entertain. Yet, Jakob Nielsen's 10 heuristics for user-interface design are applicable in both cases."

> "When controls are not used in standard ways, players must spend time and effort to (1) learn how to perform each action; and (2) actively stop themselves from making the mistake of pressing the wrong button out of habit."

> "As designers, whether we're focused on complex workplace tools or mobile apps, we all need to stay informed about related fields."

## Concepts

- [[Usability Heuristics]] — Nielsen's 10 heuristics are universal principles applicable to games, applications, and websites; they enhance user experience across domains.

- [[Game Design]] — Game design shares core usability principles with interface design; good design principles that reduce friction and confusion apply whether users are working or playing.

- [[Interaction Design]] — Game controls and interfaces must follow platform conventions and provide clear feedback; violations increase cognitive load and learning time.

- [[User Interface]] — Games demonstrate that minimalist design, progressive disclosure, and contextual information reduce overwhelm while keeping interfaces powerful.

- [[User Experience]] — Entertainment products and productivity applications both benefit from clarity, control, consistency, and error prevention; usability improves experience in both domains.
