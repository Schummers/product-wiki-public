# When Should We Trust AI? Magic-8-Ball Thinking

## Summary

This article examines when and how generative AI can be reliably used by defining "magic-8-ball thinking" as uncritically accepting AI-generated information. The author explains how large language models work probabilistically, without built-in understanding of truth, and therefore produce hallucinations at rates between 13.5% and 33%. The article provides frameworks for deciding when AI output requires verification and when it can be used without fact-checking, along with recommendations for designers building AI features into products.

## Key Takeaways

- **LLMs predict text, not truth** — large language models select the most likely next word based on training data; they have no built-in understanding of accuracy or the world, making hallucinations inevitable.
- **Hallucinations are creative gap-filling** — mistakes arise from the probabilistic, generative structure of LLMs; terms like "hallucination" mask the reality that models are confabulating without understanding.
- **Magic-8-ball thinking occurs when users stop verifying** — users are most likely to engage in magic-8-ball thinking when using AI outside their expertise, assuming higher capability than realistic, or becoming complacent after good results.
- **Not all outputs require verification** — generating text that looks right, placeholder text, and ideation do not require fact-checking since utility does not depend on accuracy.
- **Expert users can verify effectively** — users with relevant expertise and time to check outputs can use AI more effectively because they can catch errors and recognize whether AI chose appropriate methods.

## Quotes

> GenAI has practical applications for UX practitioners. It can accelerate research planning, crossfunctional communication, ideation, and other tasks.

> It falls to the users of genAI products to evaluate and judge the accuracy and validity of each output, lest users act on a piece of confabulated information that "looked like the right answer."
