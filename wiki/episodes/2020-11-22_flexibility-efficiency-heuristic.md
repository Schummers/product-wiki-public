# Flexibility and Efficiency of Use (Usability Heuristic #7)

## Summary

This article explores the seventh usability heuristic: flexibility and efficiency of use. The principle requires systems to accommodate users with varying levels of expertise by offering multiple ways to accomplish tasks and providing shortcuts that remain hidden from novice users but speed up expert interaction. The article discusses the tension between learnability and efficiency, strategies for supporting multiple methods, and techniques like accelerators and customization that allow users to work according to their preferences.

## Key Takeaways

- **Novice-expert tradeoff** — Systems must balance learnability for new users against efficiency for repeat users; the solution is providing multiple methods and unobtrusive accelerators rather than optimizing for one group.
- **Multiple task methods** — Allow open-ended approaches to tasks; for example, email could be typed individually, copy-pasted, sent as mail merge, or integrated with automation tools depending on use case and expertise.
- **Accelerators hidden from novices** — Keyboard shortcuts, gestures, and other accelerators should be unobtrusive but discoverable through hints (like showing shortcuts next to menu items) so experts can adopt them over time.
- **Customization support** — Allow expert users to customize interfaces (like screen arrangements or workspace configurations) to suit their unique needs, though note that most users won't customize without strong motivation.
- **Avoid duplication** — While offering flexibility, avoid duplicating functionality in multiple places, as this forces users to learn differences that may not exist, making systems harder to learn.

## Quotes

> Like in the kitchen, in UX we must accommodate people with a variety of competencies. Our systems should be flexible enough to allow users to complete a given task using a variety of methods.

> The 7th of the [10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) says that we should prioritize flexibility and efficiency of use through the use of shortcuts and accelerators — unseen by the novice user — which speed up the interaction for expert users.

> Accelerators are *secondary* ways of accomplishing the *same* task that function as faster (but typically less obvious) methods.

## Concepts

- [[Accelerators]] — secondary, faster methods for accomplishing tasks that remain unobtrusive for novice users but available for expert users to discover and employ, such as keyboard shortcuts or gestures.
- [[Novice to Expert]] — designing systems that support users across all expertise levels by providing clear, guided methods for beginners while offering efficient shortcuts and customization for experienced users.
- [[Customization]] — allowing expert users to customize interface arrangements, settings, and workflows to match their unique needs and work habits, though designing defaults that work well for most users.
