# Top 10 UX Articles of 2025

## Summary

The NN/g audience's top 10 most-read UX articles of 2025 reveal dominant industry trends and challenges. AI emerges as a central concern across multiple dimensions: its impact on search behavior, design skills, service design evolution, and the ethics of synthetic user research. Career and skill trajectories are shifting as companies recognize the value of generalist designers who understand multiple disciplines alongside AI capabilities. UX scope is expanding beyond product-level optimization toward holistic customer lifetime experience. Design patterns both new and familiar dominate the conversation, from Neobrutalism and iOS 26's "Liquid Glass" visual language to enduring patterns like hamburger menus and button states. Additionally, the top five articles carried over from 2024 emphasize foundational AI literacy: understanding how LLMs work, crafting effective prompts, recognizing AI hallucinations and sycophancy, and using AI strategically in research rather than replacing real users with synthetic ones.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX generalists are returning as AI reshapes the industry** — AI advances reverse the decade-long trend toward specialization; understanding multiple disciplines (research, design, strategy, emerging technologies) is increasingly valuable as companies need designers who can navigate the evolving AI landscape.
- **UX scope is expanding from product to customer lifetime experience** — The "UX is Dead" conversation signals a shift away from narrow, feature-level product optimization toward holistic customer journeys; businesses must design experiences that span the entire customer lifecycle, not just individual products.
- **AI literacy is foundational to contemporary UX work** — Top articles across 2025 and 2024 emphasize that designers must understand how generative AI works, its limitations (hallucinations, sycophancy), and how to use it strategically in research and design rather than as a replacement for user research or critical thinking.
- **Visual design patterns remain essential despite (and because of) AI** — Neobrutalism, button states, hamburger menus, and iOS 26's design language tradeoffs demonstrate that solid pattern knowledge and visual hierarchy principles are non-negotiable; new design styles must be evaluated for usability, not novelty.
- **AI is reshaping user behavior and service models** — Users' search habits, information-seeking patterns, and expectations are changing with AI availability; service design itself must evolve to account for AI agents as new actors in the system, shifting success metrics and user dynamics.

## Quotes
