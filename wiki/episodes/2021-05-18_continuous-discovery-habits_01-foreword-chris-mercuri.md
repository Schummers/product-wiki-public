# Continuous Discovery Habits: Foreword: Chris Mercuri

## Summary

Chris Mercuri, a product manager, introduces the Continuous Discovery Habits coaching program that underlies this book. He describes how his team shifted from an ad-hoc discovery approach to a structured process by applying these habits, gaining confidence and delivering more consistent results.

## Key Takeaways

- **Structured discovery process** — Mercuri's team moved from constantly changing their approach to having clear expectations and delivering more consistent results through structured discovery habits.
- **Deeper customer understanding** — The team shifted away from relying on heavy, infrequent research to developing deeper understanding through regular contact and lightweight research methods.
- **Outcome focus** — They moved away from maintaining long lists of metrics and outputs, instead focusing on fewer goals with greater clarity and customer problem-solving that drives business value.
- **Ideation and testing** — Rather than falling in love with a single idea and building it, the team learned to generate many ideas and test them faster through smaller simulations.
- **Team collaboration** — Discovery became a whole-team responsibility, with most team members involved in customer interviews, journey mapping, ideation, and discussing results.
- **Stakeholder communication** — Leaders now see the team's thinking earlier through better tools and conversations rather than waiting for big reports and presentations.

## Quotes

> "By applying the Continuous Discovery Habits, we improved customer and business outcomes, but the reward for the team was even bigger—confidence."

> "With our leaders, we no longer wait to show them big reports and presentations. Now we have the tools to show them our thinking earlier and have better conversations about where to go next."

> "If you study this book and practice the habits, product work will still be hard, and you will still make plenty of mistakes. But you will make better product decisions, and the chances that you will succeed will dramatically increase."

## Concepts

- [[Product Discovery]] — Mercuri's team shifted from an unstructured, ad-hoc approach to a systematic, continuous discovery process with consistent results.
- [[Product Trio]] — The continuous discovery habits require the entire product trio (product manager, designer, engineer) to be involved in customer research and decision-making.
- [[Decision Making]] — Structured discovery habits gave the team confidence in their decision-making by grounding choices in customer research rather than intuition.
