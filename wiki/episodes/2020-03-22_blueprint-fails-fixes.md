# Service Blueprinting: Fails and Fixes

## Summary

Based on a survey of 97 UX practitioners, this article identifies common frustrations and barriers in service-blueprinting initiatives. Problems range from obtaining organizational buy-in to gathering research, facilitating workshops, and maintaining living documents. For each major problem category, the article provides evidence-based strategies and solutions drawn from practitioner experience.

## Key Takeaways

- **Organizational Buy-In Requires Allies** — establish a small core team of 2-4 influential allies from key business units to advocate for the initiative; align early on purpose and goals by explaining benefits like uncovering service weaknesses or bridging cross-departmental efforts.
- **Scope Strategically** — focus on services you control to enable confidence building and quick wins; target 8-12 customer actions for appropriate granularity; create consistent criteria for comparing and prioritizing different potential blueprints.
- **Research Responsibility Sharing** — delegate research gathering with your core team; start with existing research to build comfort before expanding; consider creating hypothesis blueprints to identify knowledge gaps and guide research planning.
- **Facilitation Requires Role Clarity** — divide facilitator responsibilities between two people if possible; bring in neutral external facilitators to maintain objectivity; work with small groups (2-4 people) for first-time efforts.
- **Artifact Simplicity Over Fidelity** — skip high-fidelity visualization if your goal is team alignment; test artifacts like interfaces to ensure comprehension; share blueprints engagingly with key takeaways highlighted.
- **Living Document Maintenance** — update blueprints after significant service changes; include version numbers and update dates on artifacts; schedule regular reviews (such as six months post-creation) to maintain relevance and reliability.
- **Lack of Understanding Underlying** — most frustrations stem from lack of organizational understanding and support; building crossdisciplinary team support early is critical to address research, communication, and maintenance challenges.

## Quotes

> Lack of organizational understanding and support is at the core of many of these frustrations.

> Sharing the blueprint deliverable was probably the most difficult because they get pretty complicated. Most people consuming the blueprint did not want to read all the individual touchpoints or support system details.

> Before your next blueprinting initiative, build a crossdisciplinary team that will be there for support. This highly underutilized step will make it easier for you to gather internal research, foster trust and stronger communication on tough decisions, and provide you with a sounding board as you visualize the blueprint.
