# Calculating ROI for Design Projects in 4 Steps

## Summary

Demonstrating design value through ROI calculations helps secure funding and organizational support for UX work. ROI calculation involves four steps: collecting a UX metric through benchmarking studies before and after design changes; choosing a key performance indicator the organization cares about; converting the UX metric to the KPI through a conversion ratio (often multiplication); and reporting responsibly with transparency about data sources and assumptions. ROI calculations are strategic exercises using estimates, not financial projections. However, they effectively show design impact relative to project costs and justify continued investment through multiple-year projections.

## Key Takeaways

- **Benchmark before and after** — Collect UX metrics through benchmarking studies conducted before and after design changes. Metrics come from surveys, analytics, quantitative usability testing, or customer support data. Choose metrics that demonstrate real improvement in the experience.

- **Choose metrics the organization values** — Select a KPI that stakeholders and executives care about. Most KPIs relate to money (profit, cost savings, customer lifetime value, employee productivity) but can include non-monetary measures depending on organizational culture.

- **Calculate conversion ratio** — ROI calculation is unit conversion. Find the conversion ratio between your UX metric and KPI, then multiply. For example, if each support ticket costs $6 and design reduces tickets by 21,900, the savings is $131,400 annually.

- **Report transparently** — Be clear about data sources, assumptions, and methodology. Remember ROI calculations are estimates to compare with project costs, not guaranteed outcomes. Include project costs and project multiple-year returns to show real value.

- **Don't overstate results** — ROI calculations are strategic thinking tools, not financial forecasts. Present them as estimates with clear methodologies so stakeholders understand confidence levels and limitations.

- **Design improvements are cumulative** — Benefits persist across years. A design improvement yielding $300,000 benefit one year likely provides similar benefit the following year. Show 2-5 year projections to demonstrate sustained value.

## Quotes

> Essentially, you need to show how your design changes impact the bottom line — revenue, cost savings, or another key performance indicator (KPI).

> ROI calculations are strategic exercises to help you conceptualize the relative value of design projects. They are not financial projections.

> Once you acquire this skill, you can apply it beyond the more obvious scenarios and use it to calculate and demonstrate improvement for any project.
