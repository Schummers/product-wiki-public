# The Funnel Technique in Qualitative User Research

## Summary

The funnel technique is a foundational qualitative research method that starts with broad open-ended questions or tasks before gradually introducing narrower, more specific ones. This approach prevents researcher bias and discovers unbiased information before focusing on specific elements. The technique applies to interview questions, followup questions, usability test tasks, and multimethod study organization. By starting broad and narrowing, researchers see how users behave naturally and gather more authentic feedback than if specific questions primed participants early.

## Key Takeaways

- **Broad before specific** — Start with open-ended questions or exploratory tasks to learn how users naturally think and behave; only introduce specific questions or tasks after participants have had chance to volunteer information.
- **Avoid priming bias** — Early specific questions risk priming participants on how to behave; starting broad allows researchers to learn preferences and behaviors uninfluenced by researcher cues.
- **Interview guide structure** — Prepare 5-8 broad open-ended main questions followed by open-ended probing questions and closed questions; repeat the funnel process for each main question.
- **Probing questions deepen understanding** — Follow-up questions like "Can you expand on that?" or "Why do you think that?" allow researchers to dig deeper without leading participants.
- **Task ordering matters** — Give broad exploratory tasks first (see if users find something on their own), then directed tasks (ask them to find something specific); this reveals natural behavior before user guidance.
- **Stepped tasks provide flexibility** — Stepped tasks allow researchers to start broad and provide more specific instructions only if needed, revealing both natural behavior and interaction issues.
- **Followup questions show unsolicited feedback** — Starting with broad followup questions about the task, then narrowing to specific UI elements, ensures participants volunteer honest opinions before potentially making up responses.

## Quotes

> This technique involves asking broad open-ended questions before gradually introducing more narrowly-scoped open-ended questions, as well as closed questions.

> The purpose of the funnel technique is to avoid influencing user behavior or perceptions as much as possible. When we ask specific questions or give specific tasks too early in a research session, we risk introducing bias and missing important data.

> A funnel starts broad and gradually gets narrower. In qualitative interviews and usability tests, starting with broad, exploratory questions or tasks can generate lots of unbiased information about users' natural thoughts and behaviors.
