# Why So Many Info Tips Are Bad (and How to Make Them Better)

## Summary

Info tips—icons triggering contextual information—are meant to clarify jargon and explain why data is requested, but they're often misused as band-aids to hide poor design. The article distinguishes between useful info tips (supplemental, concise, accessible) and harmful ones (hiding essential content, interrupting flow, displaying verbose explanations). The encircled "i" icon signals optional information; the "?" signals help and support. Effective info tips should assume most users never activate them and should deliver clear, just-in-time guidance that directly supports immediate tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Info Tip Icons Have Distinct Meanings** — The "i" icon signals optional, helpful information (definitions, details); the "?" signals help and support (FAQs, contact info, contextual help).
- **Pitfall #1: Wasting User Time** — Redundant, obvious, or marketing-fluff tips waste the interaction cost; they should clarify jargon, explain why data is requested, or guide users to information.
- **Pitfall #2: Hiding Critical Information** — Essential instructions, constraints, form limits, and legal disclaimers should never hide in info tips; hiding them turns guidance into a game of hide-and-seek.
- **Pitfall #3: Interrupting Task Flow** — Modals, overlays, or full-page takeovers hijack focus; keep info tips inline or adjacent, allowing users to reference them while maintaining context.
- **Assume Users Won't Activate Tips** — Design for the motivated minority who seek them out, confused or stuck; deliver clear, in-the-moment guidance that supports their immediate task.
- **Info Tips Are Not Decluttering Solutions** — They're not catch-all tools for sweeping content into a hidden layer; poor labeling or dense layouts require redesign, not tips.

## Quotes

> Info tips are not a catch-all solution for decluttering the interface by sweeping essential content into a hidden layer.

> A good rule of thumb is to assume that most users will never see the info tip. Those who do have extra motivation for seeking them out are confused, stuck, or need clarification or reassurance.

> When thoughtfully implemented, info tips reduce confusion and increase user confidence. But they should always serve the user's goals, not the designer's desire to declutter at all costs.

## Concepts

- [[Design Patterns]] — Info tips are a specific UI pattern with established best practices; misuse undermines both their effectiveness and user trust.
- [[Information Architecture]] — Info tips reflect broader IA decisions; deciding what to hide and what to surface is fundamental to information structure.
- [[Progressive Disclosure]] — Info tips exemplify progressive disclosure when done well: surfacing essential info upfront, revealing supplemental details on demand.
- [[UX Copywriting]] — Effective info tips require concise, clear writing that answers the user's immediate question without jargon or unnecessary explanation.
- [[Interaction Cost]] — Every info-tip activation incurs a small cost; that cost is only justified when the tip delivers genuine value, not repetition or obvious information.
