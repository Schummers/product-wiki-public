# How Well Discovery Phases Are Performed in UX Projects

## Summary

A survey of 436 UX practitioners revealed that 75% of respondents' organizations conduct discovery phases, though only 20% do so for every project; most perform one discovery per 2-4 projects. Time pressure to deliver is the top barrier to discovery (35%), followed by lack of management buy-in (28%). Projects with discovery phases report 83% success rates compared to 52% for projects without discovery, suggesting that discovery cuts failure risk by 75%. However, most discoveries are short (58% last two weeks or less), run by small teams (average 4 people), and involve primarily user and stakeholder interviews (62% and 60%, respectively). Key challenges include limited time and budget, difficulty proving ROI, access to users (especially B2B), and lack of leadership buy-in.

## Key Takeaways

- **Discovery dramatically improves project success** — 83% of projects with discovery phases report success compared to 52% without, with the most striking impact being risk reduction (4% failure vs. 16% failure).
- **Time and budget constraints undermine discovery** — the top reason organizations skip discovery is time pressure to deliver, despite evidence that time invested upfront prevents costlier failures later.
- **Most discoveries are too short** — 58% last two weeks or less, which practitioners report is insufficient unless users are easily accessible and the problem space is small.
- **Discoveries are often treated as idea confirmation** — some teams use discoveries to validate predetermined solutions rather than genuinely exploring the problem space with research.
- **Small teams run most discoveries** — the average discovery team is 4 people, typically including designers (74%), researchers (60%), and product managers (52%), with only 36% including a product owner.
- **Management buy-in and ROI communication are critical barriers** — practitioners struggle to convince stakeholders of discovery value, particularly when leadership believes they already know the problem.
