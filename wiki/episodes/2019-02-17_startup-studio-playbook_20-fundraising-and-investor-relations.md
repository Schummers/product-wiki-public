# Startup Studio Playbook: Fundraising and investor relations

## Summary

Chapter 20 addresses the critical challenge of funding a venture studio, exploring five primary funding sources: founder capital, venture capital from VCs, corporate funding, family offices and angels, and pre-seed or studio-specific funds. The chapter emphasizes that credibility of the founder and early traction are the highest barriers to raising institutional capital, as studios are a new and unproven asset class compared to traditional venture capital.

The second half of the chapter focuses on investor relations and deal packaging. Key strategies include centralizing investor outreach at the studio level rather than per-venture, educating investors on the studio model and its advantages, and building trust through portfolio performance. The chapter concludes with a minimal fundraising deck structure covering vision, financial model, team, portfolio, and roadmap.

## Key Takeaways

- **Founder Capital — Most early successful studios** were founded by established entrepreneurs or institutional founders with deep pockets. If you have capital to self-fund the first 5-10 ventures, that removes external fundraising friction and lets you build traction before raising outside money.

- **VC Funding to the Studio — Possible but difficult;** requires founder credibility and demonstrated traction. The key is conveying that the studio reduces per-venture risk through operational expertise, diversification, and shared resources.

- **Corporate Funding — Increasing trend;** corporations dedicate funds to studios to align venture production with strategic goals. Often structured so the corporation takes majority returns while the studio retains operational autonomy.

- **Family Offices and Angels — Growing interest** in studios as a new asset class. The pitch: diversified portfolio of new startups with lower failure risk at portfolio level, better equity upside than single angel investments.

- **Studio-Specific Pre-Seed Funds — A emerging model** where a dedicated pre-seed fund finances studio venture creation, making cost structures transparent and separating studio runway from portfolio funding.

- **Bootstrapping with Agency Revenue — A hybrid model:** operate an agency (software development or marketing) in parallel, use profitable agency work to fund internal ventures. The challenge is balancing short-term revenue with long-term venture commitment.

- **Investor Mindset Education — Approach investors** centrally as a studio, not per-venture. Educate them on the model, explain portfolio diversification, show early traction, and build trust by delivering quality companies. Once one investment closes, subsequent deals come faster.

- **Cap Table and Motivation Concerns — Investors worry** that if the studio takes majority equity in early stages, the CEO and team lack skin in the game. Address this by showing post-seed cap table adjustments that raise team ownership, and let CEOs speak directly to investors.
