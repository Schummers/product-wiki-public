# Paper Prototyping: A Cutout Kit

## Summary

Paper prototyping is a fast and cheap method for testing designs early in the design process when changes are easy to make. The technique involves sketching out potential concepts, flows, or ideas on paper and then testing those sketches with users through a structured process. A testing session includes a facilitator, a participant, and optionally a "computer helper" who manipulates the prototype screens to simulate user actions and system responses. Nielsen Norman Group provides a practical cutout kit that includes templates for framing screens (phone and browser cutouts), an under-construction page placeholder, a loading indicator, and blank paper for on-the-fly design changes discovered during testing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Paper prototyping enables fast design iteration** — Before investing in digital prototypes, sketching ideas on paper and running quick usability tests allows teams to discover and fix fundamental design problems rapidly.
- **Consistency in screen fidelity matters** — All prototype screens should maintain consistent fidelity levels; mixing high-fidelity and low-fidelity screens causes participants to focus disproportionately on the detailed screens and provide inadequate feedback on low-fidelity ones.
- **Simulate interactions carefully** — Scrolling is simulated using long sheets of paper pulled through frame cutouts; dropdowns and overlays use separate paper layers placed on top of the current screen.
- **The "computer" role keeps testing flowing** — A dedicated person playing the computer (device) who is familiar with screens and their intended order prevents excessive wait times and maintains participant momentum during testing.
- **Quick iteration between sessions improves solutions** — When a usability problem appears repeatedly during testing, teams can use blank paper to create new screens between sessions, enabling rapid iterative design discovery.
- **Standard usability testing practices still apply** — Best practices for usability testing research remain essential when testing paper prototypes, including proper participant recruitment and clear task articulation.

## Concepts

- [[Prototyping]] — A low-fidelity testing method where sketched concepts are tested with users using simple materials like paper, frames, and sticky elements to simulate interaction; maintaining consistent visual fidelity and avoiding high-fidelity elements ensures participants give balanced feedback across all screens.
- [[Usability Testing]] — Paper prototyping leverages standard usability testing practices with a facilitator, participant, and optional "computer" helper to evaluate interface learnability.
- [[Iterative Design]] — Testing sessions reveal problems that can be fixed between sessions using blank paper, enabling rapid cycles of design discovery and refinement.
