# Just-Right Personas: How to Choose the Scope of Your Personas

## Summary

The scope of personas—whether broad or narrow—directly impacts the richness and usefulness of the insights they provide. Broad-scope personas offer shallow, general data useful for high-level decisions across multiple products, while narrow-scope personas provide richer, detailed data suited for specific design challenges within a single product or context. Personas should be created with a clear purpose aligned to specific design challenges, and the same user segments will not cascade down a hierarchy of organizational levels. Educating stakeholders about these tradeoffs is essential for successful persona adoption.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scope determines data richness** — The broader the scope of personas, the shallower the supporting data. Broad personas across many business areas must use only general attributes to find common patterns, while narrow personas for specific products can capture rich, contextual details.

- **Context drives segmentation** — User segments are determined by the context and goal of the personas. The same user may be a different persona depending on the specific scenario or product being designed. Different research questions yield different segments.

- **Personas should not cascade** — It is unrealistic to create one universal set of personas and apply them across all organizational levels. Segmentation changes at different scopes because research questions and contexts differ at each level.

- **Breadth versus depth tradeoff** — Broad personas are useful for shallow coverage of many topics and high-level decisions like marketing tone, while narrow personas enable targeted, specific design decisions within focused product contexts.

- **Stakeholder alignment is critical** — Before creating personas, clarify the intended use and scope with stakeholders. Without clear purpose, teams often attempt to create one overarching set of personas that works for everything, which ultimately fails to influence specific design decisions.

- **Educational responsibility** — Persona practitioners must educate stakeholders about why different scopes serve different purposes, explain the relevant tradeoffs, deliver contextual information about the personas, and help teams identify tangible applications.

## Quotes

> The first question you should ask yourself is: what are these personas for? Only after you've answered this question, can you determine the appropriate scope for your personas.

> The broader the scope, the shallower the data supporting those personas. It's not realistic to find segments of customers with similar motivations, goals, information needs, attitudes, and behaviors across a broad array of situations and scenarios.

> To achieve the best results personas should be one-to-one: they should be created specifically for the context of your design challenge.
