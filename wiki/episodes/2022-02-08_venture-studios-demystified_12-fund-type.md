# Venture Studios Demystified: Fund Type

## Summary

Part 3b of Venture Studios Demystified addresses the choice between open-ended (evergreen) and closed-ended fund structures for venture studios. The chapter documents that most studios select closed-ended funds, driven by their acceptance in the traditional VC community and the clarity they provide to investors about expected portfolio size and time horizon. However, studios using evergreen structures (AlleyCorp, Polymath, Founders, Rule 1) emphasize the flexibility these vehicles provide for venture building operations.

The chapter reveals a fundamental tension: evergreen funds offer studio leaders flexibility in capital allocation and venture timing but are harder to raise from LPs unfamiliar with this structure, while closed-ended funds align with investor expectations but require larger subsequent funds to maintain ownership stakes through follow-on investments. Studio fundraising also operates on faster cycles (every 3-4 years) compared to traditional VC, using unrealized valuations and early exits to justify successive fund raises.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fund Structure Prevalence** — Most studios interviewed selected closed-ended funds, primarily due to acceptance within the VC community. A minority chose evergreen (open-ended) structures, typically when studio founders had access to exceptionally patient LPs or pre-existing venture success.
- **Evergreen Flexibility** — Evergreen funds provide studios maximum flexibility: allowing LPs to join and exit as the studio matures, permitting operating budget modifications based on venture needs, and enabling strategy pivots without LP constraints. Some evergreen studios offer guaranteed dividends (5-20%) on preferred shares over 5-10 years instead of traditional carry structures.
- **Evergreen Fundraising Difficulty** — Raising capital for evergreen funds is significantly harder than closed-ended funds. LPs more readily understand and trust traditional VC economics (2% management fee, 20% carry) than the reinvestment model of evergreen structures, making patient capital a prerequisite.
- **Closed-Ended Fund Progression** — Studios with closed-ended funds typically raise small initial funds ($10M-20M), followed by larger funds ($50M-100M), with third funds reaching $100M-200M to support follow-on investments. This progression protects founder equity through later investment rounds.
- **Follow-On Investment Challenges** — Closed-ended fund size directly constrains how far studios can follow portfolio companies through funding rounds. Studios unable to participate in follow-ons due to fund limitations often syndicate their pro-rata rights to LPs, aligning studios more closely with traditional VC models.
- **Rapid Fund Cycles** — Studios raise funds every 3-4 years, significantly shorter than traditional VC cycles, using unrealized valuations and early exits to justify successive fundraises to LPs, creating pressure to demonstrate portfolio success quickly.
