# How Information-Seeking Behavior Has Changed in 22 Years

## Summary

This 2019 replication of a 1997 Xerox PARC study found significant shifts in how users seek information online. Understand activities have grown to comprise 40% of critical internet use (from 24% in 1997), while Compare/Choose activities dropped from 51% to 36%. Passive information acquisition through exploration and notifications has increased. Critical internet use is now predominantly mobile (42% on smartphones) and involves social interaction in 28% of cases. Content types requiring online research have expanded to include entertainment, hobbies, home and family, and pets. The research demonstrates that sites must provide comprehensive educational content, enable device-switching, and support mobile use to meet evolving user needs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Shift toward understanding activities** — Today's critical internet use emphasizes learning and understanding topics (40% of activities) more than comparing and choosing products (36%). Users increasingly use the internet as a primary source for gaining knowledge.

- **Passive information acquisition growing** — The proportion of passive information discovery (through exploration and notifications) has increased from 4% in 1997 to 14% in 2019. Notifications now represent a new category of passive information acquisition.

- **Mobile dominance in critical tasks** — Smartphones account for 42% of critical internet incidents, exceeding desktop use. This is surprising given research suggesting mobile is used for less important tasks, likely because smartphones are always available and memorable.

- **Social interaction is common** — 28% of respondents reported social interaction (inquire, informed, share) while making decisions based on online information, reflecting the ubiquity of smartphones and connected people.

- **Expanded content categories** — New content categories have emerged (entertainment, hobbies, home and families, pets) alongside existing ones. Product information and health content remain dominant, comprising almost half of all critical incidents.

- **Content is why users visit sites** — Beyond appealing design, sites must provide comprehensive educational content that clearly explains topics and product benefits. Content educates users and increases brand awareness.

## Quotes

> A larger proportion of current critical internet activities involved finding answers and gathering information in order to better understand a topic.

> The increase in Understand responses may be due to the fact that online content has become comprehensive and easy to access due to search improvements. Back in 1997, if you wanted to learn about a topic, you would probably have gone to the library, but today most people would just look it up online.

> If you only go by pure statistics, you may end up designing to support people's less important needs and provide poor (or nonexistent) support for their most crucial needs.
