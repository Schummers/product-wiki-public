# The Context CUEs Framework in Field Studies

## Summary

The Context CUEs framework helps researchers organize observations in field studies by categorizing data into three areas: Culture (shared behaviors, beliefs, rules), the Unspoken (tacit knowledge and assumptions), and Environment (material, social, online surroundings). Grounded in distributed cognition theory, the framework recognizes that thinking is not confined to individual minds but distributed across people, artifacts, and environments. Applied to UX, this means designing products that help users offload cognitive tasks onto external tools and scaffolding. The framework guides consistent observation by prompting researchers to look for cultural practices, unspoken norms, and environmental cues that enable or hinder task completion. Examples include sticky notes used for memory support, maps and landmarks for navigation, and written or observed behavioral patterns that help newcomers learn how to function in new settings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Distributed cognition theory** — Thinking is distributed across people, artifacts, and environments; products should support external memory and scaffolding rather than requiring memorization.
- **Culture observations** — What and how do people do something, when they reach out to others, why they do it, and how they explain it; reveals shared practices and group behaviors.
- **Unspoken observations** — Tacit knowledge like group conflicts and their resolution, language norms, questions new members ask, impact of member departures, and access to necessary information.
- **Environment observations** — Physical, social, and online surroundings; where activities happen, how space looks and feels, whether this is the only place where activities occur, and accessibility of locations.
- **Cues lead to opportunities** — Noticing that people always consult address books to enter information suggests designing online directories; observing business-card exchanges suggests QR code connection tools like LinkedIn uses.
- **Reduce cognitive load** — Good designs minimize cognitive load by helping users offload demands onto external processes and artifacts; context observations reveal where this scaffolding is missing or needed.

## Quotes

> The critical point of that theory is that thinking is not confined to the limits of one person and their brain but occurs when people interact with each other and with material artifacts.

> Field studies provide rich data that might seem overwhelming and hard to capture consistently. One way to facilitate observations is to use the context CUEs framework, which is rooted in the DCog approach.

> Good designs minimize cognitive load to maximize usability. As UX professionals, we want to create products that allow users to offload some of the task demands on external processes and artifacts — that is, products that facilitate and scaffold external thinking.

## Concepts

- [[Field Studies]] — A research method combining observation and interview in natural environments, revealing how users actually work; the context CUEs framework provides structure for consistent observation across complex settings, organizing observations into three categories that capture culture, unspoken norms, environmental cues, and relevant context while preventing data from becoming overwhelming.
- [[Distributed Cognition]] — Thinking is distributed across people, tools, and environments; design should support external memory and scaffolding rather than assuming users will memorize or figure things out internally.
- [[Interaction Design]] — Products that minimize cognitive load by providing scaffolding and external memory support better usability; context observations reveal where these supports are missing.
