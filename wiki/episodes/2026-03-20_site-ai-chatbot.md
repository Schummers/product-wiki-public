# What Is Your Site's AI Chatbot for? Users Can't Tell

## Summary

Most users do not notice, rarely use, or struggle to see value in site-specific AI chatbots. In a study of 9 users interacting with 8 chatbots, participants often didn't discover the features, held skepticism from past poor experiences, and couldn't articulate what the chatbot offered beyond existing tools. Chatbots succeed when they answer context-specific questions or provide tailored guidance on product pages, but fail when they replicate search, compete with existing features, or feel like sales channels. The core problem is misaligned design: chatbots are added without understanding the user problems they solve.

## Key Takeaways

- **Discoverability gap** — Chatbot icons are small, lack labels, or blend into busy backgrounds; users skip them even when regular customers on the site.
- **Past poor experiences** — Users have been burned by unhelpful chatbots from years past; skepticism is high until the bot proves itself.
- **Vague messaging** — Chatbots don't communicate their capabilities clearly; users won't experiment to discover value if benefits aren't obvious immediately.
- **Misaligned value** — Some chatbots offer capabilities (recipes, DIY help) that don't match why users visit the site (to buy products).
- **Efficiency problem** — Chatbots often slower and more effortful than established features like search, filters, and navigation that users already master.
- **Context-specific advantage** — Chatbots add value when answering product-specific questions, clarifying complex information, or handling personalized, multivariate questions.
