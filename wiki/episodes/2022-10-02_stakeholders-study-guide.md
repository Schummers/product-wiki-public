# UX Stakeholders: Study Guide

## Summary

This study guide curates Nielsen Norman Group's most useful resources for understanding and working with stakeholders throughout the UX process. Stakeholders are anyone with interest in or influence over a project—including executives, marketing directors, account managers, and internal team members. The guide organizes resources into four core areas: understanding stakeholders, involving them in UX research, facilitating stakeholder workshops, and communicating research findings to drive buy-in.

The guide emphasizes that successful UX work requires early and continuous stakeholder engagement. Involving stakeholders in user testing, workshops, and research increases their empathy for users, buy-in for design decisions, and overall support for the project. The resources cover practical techniques for interviewing stakeholders to uncover business goals and context, strategies for presenting UX work in business-relevant language, and methods for securing stakeholder participation in collaborative design activities.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stakeholders include anyone with interest or power over the project** — they may be internal or external and span executive, marketing, account management, and team roles.
- **Early stakeholder interviews gather critical context and align on vision** — conducting stakeholder interviews at project kickoff uncovers history, business objectives, constraints, and prevents friction later.
- **Involving stakeholders in observation increases empathy and buy-in** — getting stakeholders to observe usability testing helps them understand users directly and builds support for user-centered recommendations.
- **Stakeholder workshops benefit from careful facilitation** — techniques like group sketching and collaborative ideation maximize positive contributions while minimizing disruptive behavior.
- **Translate UX into business value** — presenting research findings in terms of business impact, ROI, and metrics relevant to stakeholders is more persuasive than technical UX language.

## Quotes

> "A stakeholder is anyone who has interest in your project or with whom you need to work with in some way to complete the project."

> "If you're not sure who your stakeholders are, start by asking yourself who is interested in your project and who has power, influence, or control over it."

## Concepts

- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — successful projects require identifying stakeholders early and creating processes that make them feel heard, increase buy-in, and establish open communication channels.
- [[User Interviews]] — structured interviews with stakeholders gather context, history, business goals, and constraints; best conducted early in projects to prevent friction.
- [[UX Communication]] — translating UX work into business language and metrics increases stakeholder understanding and buy-in; video evidence and UX stories build empathy more effectively than reports alone.
- [[Project Management]] — stakeholder workshops and collaborative design sessions require careful facilitation to maximize positive contributions and manage group dynamics.
