# Using Imagery in Visual Design

## Summary

Imagery serves critical functions in user interface design: engaging users, establishing brand identity, communicating complex concepts, and creating emotional connections. The article distinguishes four types of imagery—photographs, illustrations, iconography, and data visualizations—each with distinct purposes and applications. When used thoughtfully, imagery enhances user experience; when used carelessly, it can detract from usability and introduce accessibility barriers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Choose consistent imagery** — When multiple images are used, match their characteristics such as exposure, brightness, contrast, and style to maintain visual cohesion and prevent individual images from drawing excessive attention.
- **Match icon stroke weight with typography** — Icon sets should share consistent stroke weight attributes, and this stroke weight should align with the approximate weight of accompanying typefaces for a uniform visual hierarchy.
- **Balance file size with quality** — Optimize images for the web by targeting a total page weight of 1–2MB, testing across devices, and using appropriate file formats like WEBP or PNG to avoid degraded quality while maintaining performance.
- **Prioritize information-carrying images** — Showcase product photos and infographics over decorative stock images, as decorative imagery offers little value and users quickly overlook familiar free stock photos.
- **Balance text and imagery** — Complex layouts with heavy text benefit from detailed imagery with texture and multiple colors, while minimalist designs with white space call for simpler imagery.
- **Use high data-ink ratios** — In infographics and data visualizations, prioritize conveying information without excessive decorative elements like shadows, grid lines, or 3D effects.

## Quotes

> Relevant, appealing, and informative images — whether they are icons, illustrations, photographs, or data visualizations — engage users and help them accomplish their tasks.

> Decorative images, such as stock photos, take up space and bring little additional value to the site content.

> When users come to your site for the first time, the images will likely catch their attention before the text.
