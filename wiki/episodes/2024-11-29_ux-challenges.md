# The Biggest Challenges Practitioners Encounter Working in UX

## Summary

Based on survey responses from 126 UX practitioners, the article identifies five interconnected challenges facing the UX field: inaccurate perception of UX, difficulty demonstrating measurable impact, lack of stakeholder buy-in, insufficient resources, and a depleted job market. All five challenges appear to stem from one core problem: the inaccurate perception of UX as primarily focused on aesthetics rather than as an insight-driven approach to increasing product viability and business outcomes. These problems have persisted since UX emerged as a field in the 1990s but have been exacerbated by recent economic downturns.

## Key Takeaways

- **Inaccurate perception is the root cause** — Many organizational leaders still see design as focused on aesthetic appearance rather than on insight-driven approaches to business outcomes, which devalues UX and limits its impact.
- **UX maturity and perception are linked** — Organizations stuck at stages 2 (limited) and 3 (emergent) of UX maturity often still see UX as "nice to have," which prevents investment in research and operationalization necessary for genuine impact.
- **Measuring UX ROI is challenging** — Unlike marketing or sales, UX's business impact is difficult to measure and demonstrate, making it harder to secure stakeholder buy-in and resources.
- **Lack of buy-in perpetuates deprioritization** — Without stakeholder support, UX becomes deprioritized when business pressures mount, which prevents designing the research and process improvements necessary to demonstrate value.
- **Limited resources force compromises** — Insufficient time and budget mean teams must prioritize features over UX, perpetuating the cycle of low perception and low maturity.
- **Job market instability reflects broader issues** — Corrections following overinvestment, AI uncertainty, and economic challenges have reduced UX hiring, with junior professionals particularly affected.

## Quotes

> Organizational leaders know that design is necessary for product development. However, many still see design as a practice focused on aesthetic appearance rather than an insight-driven approach to increasing their products' functional viability and ability to convert and retain customers.

> Perception can be improved by improving UX maturity. But improving maturity can be difficult when perception is a barrier.

> The core problem in which many of the others are rooted is the inaccurate perception of what UX is and of the value it brings.
