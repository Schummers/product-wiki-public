# "Get Started" Stops Users

## Summary

The phrase "Get Started" has become a widespread call-to-action on homepages, but it often misleads users about what will happen when they click. Naïve visitors expecting to learn about a company instead find themselves pushed into complex signup flows, quizzes, or sales funnels without the foundational information they need. This mismatch between user expectations and actual outcomes damages trust and causes users to abandon the site. The vague phrase works like a magnet, preventing users from scrolling or exploring other content that might better serve their actual needs.

## Key Takeaways

- **"Get Started" is ambiguous and implies premature action** — users interpret it as a path to learn more information or begin exploring, not as an entry point to a signup or sales funnel.
- **New visitors need foundational information first** — people arriving via marketing campaigns often lack enough context to commit to a complex onboarding flow and need basic information about what the company does.
- **Vague CTAs create the illusion of completeness** — users see a prominent "Get Started" button and assume it's the main path, stopping them from scrolling to find information below the fold.
- **The reciprocity principle matters** — users are more willing to provide personal information and commit time after they've received value from the organization, not before.
- **Specific CTA labels increase information scent** — replacing "Get Started" with descriptive text like "Take our style quiz" or "Switch your cell plan" accurately sets expectations and improves user experience.

## Quotes

> Don't let ego dictate the design.

> The generic label gives strong information scent for almost any customer activity, leading unsuspecting users down the wrong path.

> A link is a promise, and if you set the wrong expectation, you may reach superficial conversion goals but you won't keep customers happy or loyal in the end.

## Concepts

- [[Call to Action]] — ambiguous CTA labels like "Get Started" mislead users about the action they're taking; specific, descriptive labels better support user goals.
- [[Information Scent]] — users judge whether a link or button will lead them toward their goal based on the visual and textual cues present; misleading labels create false information scent.
- [[Onboarding]] — signup and orientation flows should be reserved for users who have decided to commit, not forced on new visitors before they understand what they're joining.
