# PM and UX Have Markedly Different Views of Their Job Responsibilities

## Summary

Nielsen Norman Group surveyed 372 product management and UX professionals to understand how these roles view their responsibilities relative to one another. The research revealed significant misalignment across research, design, project management, and strategic activities. PMs and UXers disagreed on who should conduct user research, define information architecture, manage design work, make design decisions, and lead product strategy. The article argues that without explicit role alignment at the organizational level, this confusion leads to duplicated work, confusion, and professional dissatisfaction.

## Key Takeaways

- **Role Misalignment** — PMs and UXers disagreed significantly on responsibility for research tasks (discoveries: 73% of UXers vs. 19% of PMs), design work, and product strategy, with nearly all responsibility areas showing statistically significant differences.
- **Research Responsibility Confusion** — The biggest disagreement centered on who should conduct discoveries; only 46% of PMs considered user testing a UX activity, despite it being traditionally a UX role.
- **Design Work Complexity** — More than three-quarters of UX respondents but only about a third of PM respondents assigned ideation and prioritizing user needs in design to UX, indicating fundamental disagreement on design ownership.
- **IA Definition Debate** — 75% of UXers thought defining information architecture should be UX's job, but 50% of PMs believed development should own it, despite IA being a core UX discipline.
- **Communication and Advocacy Gap** — PM and UX disagreed sharply on who should explain designs and get stakeholder buy-in; most PMs thought this was PM's job, while most UXers believed UX should do it.
- **Explicit Role Definition Required** — Organizations must clearly define task ownership and align teams on responsibilities to mitigate duplicated work, reduce confusion, and increase productivity and job satisfaction.

## Quotes

> The biggest disagreement was related to discoveries, but even for traditional UX tasks such as user testing there were significant differences, with only 46% of the PMs considering this activity as a UX activity.
