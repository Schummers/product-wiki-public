# A 100-Year View of User Experience

## Summary

This article takes a long-term perspective on the UX field's development from 1950 to 2050, arguing that current progress should be understood as just the beginning. The field originated in the 1950s at Bell Labs when John Karlin (a psychologist) designed the touchtone telephone keypad. Growth occurred in three distinct periods: 1950-1983 (100x growth from 10 to 1,000 UX professionals, driven by mainframe and early computer use), 1983-2017 (1,000x growth from 1,000 to 1 million professionals, driven by the PC and web revolutions), and a projected 2017-2050 (100x growth from 1 million to 100 million professionals). The projected growth reflects that UX is becoming a key economic driver and that the field's future lies in augmenting human cognition, increasing productivity, and addressing market expansion beyond the current IT-focused domain. Currently, UX still represents less than 10% of the quality it should achieve, indicating massive opportunity ahead.

## Key Takeaways

- **Exponential Growth Driven by Business Pressure** — UX growth accelerated when the PC revolution (user = buyer) and web revolution (experience = gatekeeper to revenue) created business incentives for quality design; these structural changes, not just cultural recognition, drove adoption.
- **Three Distinct Growth Rates** — 1950-1983: 100x growth (mainframe era, limited incentive), 1983-2017: 1,000x growth (PC + web pressure), 2017-2050: projected 100x growth (market expansion and productivity focus).
- **UX Professionalization Across Industries** — Early UX was concentrated in IT and specific countries; now every business sector needs UX, and the field is truly global with UX professionals working in 74+ countries.
- **Currently Only 10% of Potential** — Jakob Nielsen estimates that overall user experience quality is less than 10% of what it should be; this indicates vast opportunity and explains why further growth is realistic.
- **UX as Productivity Multiplier** — Future UX growth will focus on augmenting human cognition and overcoming productivity challenges in knowledge economies; this shift expands the addressable market far beyond consumer software.
- **1% of Global Population Will Be UX Practitioners by 2050** — This projection reflects that UX will become as fundamental to product development as building and selling; the remaining 99% of workers will benefit from better-designed systems.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — Professional roles focused on user experience; experiencing rapid growth and professionalization; expanding beyond traditional IT roles into every industry sector.
- [[UX Field Growth]] — The expansion of user experience as a discipline, with factors including business incentives, company adoption, professional expansion to new countries, and market opportunities.
- [[UX Maturity]] — The sophistication level of an organization's UX practice; ranging from no formal UX to integrated, strategic UX; organizations gradually increase UX maturity as they recognize design's impact on success.
- [[User-Centered Design]] — An approach to product development where user needs and research inform all decisions; requires specialized professionals and organizational commitment; increasingly recognized as business-critical.
- [[Business Impact]] — The quantifiable impact of UX on business outcomes; UX influences revenue through conversion rates, customer satisfaction, brand perception, and employee productivity; recognition of this value drives investment and growth.
