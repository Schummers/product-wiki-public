# Venture Studios Demystified: Equity Ownership

## Summary

This chapter examines how much equity studios take in the companies they launch, a critical driver of studio returns and founder attraction. Historically, studios took majority ownership, but this approach deterred top talent and confused traditional investors, forcing a market-wide shift toward lower equity stakes. The chapter details the equity range studios now target (25–40%), factors that influence studio ownership stakes on a per-venture basis, and the tension between maximizing studio returns and attracting high-quality founders.

## Key Takeaways

- **Equity Range Shift** — the typical studio equity stake is now 25–40%, down from the historical majority ownership (>50%) that most studios once pursued; only 4 of 19 studios interviewed take majority equity.
- **Founder Attraction Trade-off** — high initial studio equity stakes prevented studios from recruiting top founding talent and confused potential VC investors with non-traditional cap tables, forcing studios to lower stakes to levels founders and investors would accept.
- **Maintenance Through Rounds** — studios with large fund sizes can target 20–35% ownership maintained through Series A and B, but most studios lack capital for full participation in subsequent rounds and see ownership diluted over time.
- **Variable Structures** — few studios maintain standard equity splits across their portfolio; stakes vary based on variables including whether the studio generated the idea, founder experience, founder capital contribution, salary arrangements, and the founder's role.
- **Extreme Range** — real-world equity splits within portfolios can span from 5–50%, making standard founder compensation rare and negotiations complex.
