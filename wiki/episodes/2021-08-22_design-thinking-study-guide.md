# Design Thinking: Study Guide

## Summary

Design thinking is a problem-solving framework based on hands-on, user-centric approaches to innovation. The process comprises six distinct phases: Empathize (conduct research on user behavior and needs), Define (synthesize findings into problem statements), Ideate (generate solutions for unmet needs), Prototype (build representations of ideas), Test (evaluate prototypes with users), and Implement (deploy solutions). This study guide compiles NN/g resources organized by phase, providing articles, videos, and activities to guide teams through the design thinking process. Design thinking applies broadly across industries and project types, helping teams uncover diverse perspectives, promote mutual understanding, and foster inclusive decision-making.

## Key Takeaways

- **Six Iterative Phases** — Design thinking progresses through empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test, and implement phases; these phases are often iterative, with testing revealing refinement needs that loop back to earlier stages.
- **Empathize and Define Foundations** — The first two phases focus on understanding users and their needs through research, creating visualizations (empathy maps, journey maps), and articulating problem statements that guide the rest of the process.
- **Ideate Through Convergence** — Ideation involves generating diverse ideas (divergence) then collaboratively narrowing to agreed outcomes (convergence); techniques include "how might we" questions, brainstorming, sketching, and scenario mapping with personas.
- **Prototype and Test Cycle** — Prototypes range from low-fidelity sketches to high-fidelity interactive representations; testing with users reveals whether solutions work, creating feedback loops that drive refinement and iteration.
- **Collaborative Problem-Solving** — Design thinking workshops enable hands-on problem-solving and inclusive decision-making in ways standard meetings cannot; structured activities and facilitation guide diverse team members to contribute meaningfully.

## Quotes

> The design thinking framework is based on the philosophy that a hands-on, user-centric approach to problem solving promotes innovation; in turn, innovation can lead to differentiation and a competitive advantage.


> These stages are often iterative — you might test a prototype and realize that it doesn't work as well as you thought and that it needs to be refined.

## Concepts

- [[Design Thinking]] — Design thinking is a structured problem-solving framework emphasizing empathetic user research, problem definition, ideation, rapid prototyping, and iterative testing; it promotes innovation by placing users at the center of decision-making.
- [[User-Centered Design]] — This refers to design methodologies that place user needs, research, and feedback at the core; design thinking is one instantiation of this philosophy, applying it across six phases with specific activities and tools.
- [[Innovation Process]] — Design thinking frames innovation as a structured, repeatable process rather than inspiration-driven; it enables organizations to systematically generate, test, and refine ideas to solve user problems.
