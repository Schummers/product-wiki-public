# Alternatives to Pagination on Product-Listing Pages

## Summary

Many ecommerce sites are moving away from traditional pagination to reduce interaction cost and improve user experience. Traditional pagination splits product lists into numbered pages users must navigate sequentially. However, for sites with small- or medium-size product selections, infinite loading or "Show More" buttons can work better than traditional pagination controls. Infinite loading automatically appends products as users scroll; "Show More" buttons require explicit user action. Nielsen Norman Group's research reveals the trade-offs and best practices for each approach, including the importance of displaying product counts, supporting pogo-sticking (back-button behavior), and saving users' scroll position.

## Key Takeaways

- **Traditional pagination suits mega-retailers with huge inventories** — Amazon and large product catalogs need pagination for precise navigation; however, sites with small/medium product selections can use alternatives with lower interaction cost.
- **Infinite loading works for limited product sets** — Infinite scroll succeeds when sites have fewer than 40 products per page, good filters, and clear item counts displayed; users accept it when loading is fast.
- **Infinite scroll creates footer navigation problems** — Automatically loading new items pushes footer navigation out of reach; users cannot access return policies, shipping information, or other footer content without scrolling for extended periods.
- **Show More buttons offer user control** — Users must explicitly click Show More to load additional products; this gives mobile users (on limited data plans) choice about loading, and allows footer access between product batches.
- **Always display total product count and progress** — Whether using infinite scroll or Show More, clearly show the total number of items in the category, how many have been loaded, and how many remain; Ann Taylor's animated progress indicator exemplifies best practice.
- **Support pogo-sticking by saving scroll position** — Users frequently open product links, assess items, and press Back to return to listings; ensure listing pages remember and restore users' scroll position so they don't have to refind their place.

## Quotes

> "As long as it loads, it's fine. Like if I got down here and it took another 30 seconds to load, I'd lose interest. But this pace is fine."

> "I like the layout where you can just scroll through everything. Some places make it pages after pages. Where here it's a really easy website to get through."

> "Show More buttons are likely to continue to grow in popularity, particularly because Google began using them in place of pagination on its mobile results pages."

## Concepts

- [[Pagination]] — Traditional product-listing pagination uses numbered pages; appropriate for mega-retailers with large inventories but creates higher interaction cost than alternatives for small/medium product sets.
- [[E-Commerce]] — Ecommerce listing-page design should balance user goals (browsing products) with footer access, mobility considerations, and clear navigation; different approaches suit different inventory sizes.
- [[Infinite Scroll]] — Automatically loading products as users scroll reduces interaction cost but can trap users (footer unreachable) and disorient them on large lists; works best for under 40 items per page with clear item counts.
- [[Interaction Cost]] — Show More buttons and infinite loading reduce interaction cost compared to pagination but require careful implementation (displaying counts, supporting pogo-sticking, considering mobile data concerns).
