# Liquid Glass Is Cracked, and Usability Suffers in iOS 26

## Summary

iOS 26 introduces Liquid Glass, Apple's glassmorphic visual language with translucent, rippling surfaces. At first glance it looks fluid and modern; in use, it creates significant usability problems. Text placed on images, translucent controls obscuring content, excessive animation, crowded tap targets, constantly changing interfaces, broken conventions, hidden navigation, and truncated information all degrade the experience. These changes prioritize spectacle over usability and represent a departure from Apple's reputation for building thoughtful, learnable interfaces that support rather than distract from content.

## Key Takeaways

- **Transparency obscures content, harming readability** — Text on images, icons blending into backgrounds, and translucent controls make content invisible; one of the oldest usability findings is that anything layered on top of something becomes harder to see.
- **Motion without meaningful purpose becomes distraction** — Animated buttons, morphing controls, and shimmering elements grab attention repeatedly but provide no benefit; delightful animation on first use becomes noise and fatigue on the hundredth use.
- **Smaller, crowded tap targets break established guidelines** — Touch targets shrink below the 1cm² guideline; navigation feels cramped and fragmented with a floating search button separating controls, breaking the sense of unified control areas.
- **Changing interfaces destroy predictability and learnability** — Controls that appear, vanish, collapse, and expand depending on context require rescanning every time; the forward button appearing only when needed disrupts the learned location of targets.
- **Abandoned conventions create friction for long-time users** — Moving search from top to bottom of screen, changing back-button behavior, and adopting Android-style design breaks habits; even if new patterns prove better eventually, relearning costs productivity and frustration.
- **Hidden controls reduce discoverability** — Breadcrumbs removed from back buttons, tabs hidden in overflow menus, and truncated URLs make navigation harder and require extra steps, violating best practices for essential actions.

## Quotes

> "At first glance, it does look cool. But problems arise as soon as you start using your phone."

> "It's like the interface is shouting "look at me" when it should quietly step aside and let the real star — the content — take the spotlight."

> "Instead of building reliable habits, users now have to play hide-and-seek with the navigation controls."
