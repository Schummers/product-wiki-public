# Screening Participants for User-Research Studies

## Summary

A research screener is a set of questions used to establish whether a prospective participant is a good fit for a research project. The article emphasizes that finding the right test participants — actual users or representative users who mimic the demographic and behavioral characteristics of target users — is critical to conducting valid user research. Without proper screening, researchers risk wasting time and resources on unsuitable participants whose data would be invalid or of poor quality.

## Key Takeaways

- **Purpose of a screener** — A screener ensures you are conducting research with the right users, which in turn ensures that the findings are valid and relevant to the product in question.
- **Benefits over time** — Screeners ensure data quality by weeding out participants who provide poor-quality responses, save time and money by preventing unsuitable participants from advancing to sessions, and reduce bias by avoiding reliance on volunteer research panels that skew toward IT professionals and other web-savvy individuals.
- **Define inclusion and exclusion criteria** — Focus on behavioral and demographic characteristics most critical to your research questions rather than attempting to find a perfect participant who matches every characteristic, which typically results in an overly restrictive screener.
- **Focus on past behaviors** — Ask about relevant past behaviors rather than hypothetical future behaviors, since users are notoriously unreliable at predicting whether they would use a product that doesn't yet exist.
- **Avoid yes/no questions** — Yes/no questions are easy for participants to game; instead use multi-option questions or multiple questions that make it harder for participants to guess the intent and manipulate their answers.
- **Pilot your screener** — Test your screener with a few individuals before launching to catch issues like typos, confusing wording, and problems with skip logic.

## Quotes

> By "right test participants," I mean *actual* users of your product or *representative* users who mimic the demographic and behavioral characteristics of your users.

> A **screener** is a set of questions, typically delivered verbally or via written questionnaire, used to establish that a prospective participant is a good fit for a given research project.

> Users are notoriously unreliable at predicting their future behaviors. They may be inaccurate for a number of reasons, including a genuine misestimation, an attempt to flatter the researcher, or an attempt to lie their way into a paid research opportunity.
