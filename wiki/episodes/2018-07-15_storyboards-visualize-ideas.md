# Storyboards Help Visualize UX Ideas

## Summary

Storyboards communicate stories through sequences of images that chronologically map a story's main events. In UX, storyboards provide additional context to teams and stakeholders using images to make stories quick to understand at first glance and easy to remember. Storyboards consist of three elements: a specific scenario, visuals, and captions. They can be low-fidelity or high-fidelity depending on audience and purpose, and can be used for research communication, journey enrichment, prioritization, ideation, or design communication.

## Key Takeaways

- **Use low-fidelity when starting** — Simple sketches or sticky notes are sufficient and allow easy iteration; don't spend time creating polished visuals unless presenting to clients.
- **Differentiate from journey maps** — Unlike journey maps, storyboards focus on imagery over text and usually depict a fragment of a journey rather than a complete overview; use together for complementary benefits.
- **Apply in multiple UX phases** — Use storyboards for communicating usability test results, enriching journey maps with visual context, establishing team understanding of user scenarios, and supporting ideation.
- **Follow the six-step creation process** — Gather data, choose fidelity level, define basics (persona and scenario), plan steps with emotional states, create visuals and captions, then distribute and iterate.
- **Maintain one path per storyboard** — For complex scenarios with multiple paths, create separate storyboards for each path to keep the story clear and focused.

## Quotes
