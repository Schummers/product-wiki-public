# UX Deliverables: Glossary

## Summary

This glossary defines over 70 UX deliverables that practitioners produce throughout a project lifecycle. Each entry includes a concise definition and links to related articles, videos, and templates. Deliverables span research (personas, scenarios, affinity diagrams), strategy (roadmaps, user flows, journey maps), design (wireframes, prototypes, mockups), and operations (RACI matrices, design systems), serving different audiences and project phases.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deliverables record work that has occurred** — a deliverable is fundamentally a document that communicates design ideas, research findings, and project context to various audiences.
- **Deliverables span the entire project lifecycle** — from discovery (affinity diagrams, personas) through design (wireframes, prototypes) to operations (design systems, roadmaps).
- **Different audiences need different deliverables** — researchers need research plans and screeners, designers need wireframes and mockups, executives need roadmaps and business cases.
- **Mapping types serve multiple purposes** — journey maps, service blueprints, ecosystem maps, and experience maps each visualize different aspects of user experience and organizational context.
- **Methodology connects to deliverables** — the deliverable produced depends on the research method (interviews produce interview guides and empathy maps, surveys produce demographic data and insights).

## Quotes

> In UX, a deliverable is a document that serves as a record of work that has occurred.

> UX practitioners produce a wide range of deliverables to communicate design ideas, research findings, and the context of projects to various audiences.
