# How to Build a Participant List for UX Workshops

## Summary

A workshop's value depends entirely on the participants assembled; the wrong people in the room can derail outcomes or create decisions that are later vetoed by absent stakeholders. This article provides frameworks for building effective participant lists by maintaining a manageable group size of 6-12 people while prioritizing diverse perspectives across departments, expertise levels, and seniority. Key roles include the facilitator (separate from participants if possible), a designated decider with veto power, and user representation through actual participants or proxies like customer-support representatives and researchers. Common mistakes like scheduling for convenience rather than competence or excluding powerful people to avoid conflict can undermine collaborative work and lead to wasted effort.

## Key Takeaways

- **Optimal group size** — 6-12 participants balances engagement with manageability; larger groups require more facilitator effort and risk passive virtual participation, while smaller groups may miss critical perspectives.
- **Departmental diversity** — Include representatives from departments that touch or are impacted by the project rather than many people from one department; cross-functional teams generate more diverse perspectives and collaborative outcomes.
- **Domain-knowledge diversity** — Mix subject-matter experts with novices; experts avoid assumptions-based decisions while novices ask questions that force fresh thinking and prevent groupthink.
- **Seniority diversity is critical** — Include decision makers to build buy-in while including independent contributors to ensure feasibility and impact are both considered; avoid homogeneous seniority levels that risk biased thinking.
- **Always include user perspective** — Bring actual users for empathy workshops or prioritization workshops, or use proxies like customer-support representatives who know users better than managers, plus UX researchers who have conducted prior research.
- **Designate clear roles** — Assign one person as facilitator who focuses solely on facilitation, not participation; include a decider if major decisions will be made, and never exclude this person to avoid conflict as they can later veto work.

## Quotes

> Workshops are a rare opportunity to bring together teams and departments that might otherwise not have a chance to work together.

> Sometimes it takes someone asking a “stupid question” to make progress and force the group to see things in a different light.

> Usually, it is more useful to include user proxies who have the most direct user interaction (e.g., the CSR as opposed to the manager).

## Concepts

- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — Workshop facilitation requires dedicated facilitators focused solely on managing group dynamics and maintaining focus, rather than participating; participant composition and full facilitator attention directly determine whether outputs will be adopted and implemented.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Including stakeholders with decision-making power builds buy-in; excluding powerful people to avoid conflict can lead to work being vetoed or rejected after the workshop.
- [[Diversity in Teams]] — Teams mixing expertise levels, seniority, and departments generate more robust decisions than homogeneous groups; diversity combats assumptions and groupthink.
- [[Decision Making]] — Workshops should include a designated decider to provide clear authority for decisions; this role acknowledges the reality that one person often has overwhelming power over project outcomes.
