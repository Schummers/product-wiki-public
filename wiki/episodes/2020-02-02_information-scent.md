# Information Scent: How Users Decide Where to Go Next

## Summary

Information scent is the user's imperfect estimate of how relevant a source will be in addressing their current information need, derived from visual and contextual cues. Like animals foraging for food by detecting scent, users navigate websites by evaluating link labels, accompanying content snippets, visual context, and prior knowledge about sources. High information scent occurs when links clearly indicate that clicking will lead to relevant content. Low scent results from vague labels, mismatched context, or generic descriptions. Social foraging extends this concept through recommendations and reviews that augment scent for others. Clickbait and overpromising damages long-term trust despite short-term click gains.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scent guides navigation decisions** — Users decide whether to click links based on their estimate of how relevant the destination will be. This estimate comes from the link label, accompanying text or images, the surrounding context, and any prior knowledge about the source.

- **Link labels must be clear** — Link labels should be succinct yet accurate descriptions of page content. Obscure, vague, or jargon-filled labels provide poor scent and users may miss valuable information. Clear, self-explanatory labels with user-centric language are essential.

- **Context augments scent** — Summary text, images, and the surrounding page content add cues that help users evaluate link relevance. However, context on mobile is often cut off, so specific link labels are more important than relying on context.

- **Prior knowledge influences scent** — Users' familiarity with a brand, domain, or previous experiences affect how they interpret links. This indirect controllable factor builds over time through consistent delivery of promised value.

- **Social foraging amplifies scent** — Recommendations, reviews, and word-of-mouth create information scent by providing signals from other users about source quality and relevance. These traces influence decisions even after first-hand experience.

- **Clickbait backfires long-term** — While misleading titles might generate clicks, they erode trust and future click-through rates. Users lose confidence in promises and recommendations from those who violated their trust previously.

## Quotes

> Like food scent guides animals to their meals, information scent guides people to those webpages that are likely to contain the content they're looking for.

> The link label, the content that accompanies the link, the context in which the link appears, and any background knowledge (including recommendations from others) that the user may have about the source all influence the information scent that that source emits and thus the likelihood that the page will be visited or ignored.

> The other side of the coin is that, if your brand is strong and people can trust you, you have slightly more room for error (not a lot — many mistakes will ultimately erode your brand, because brand is experience in interactive media). But basically if you can increase the expectation that users will find what they need on your site and that, whenever you promise something with a label or an image, you will deliver — then people will be more likely to give you the benefit of doubt for the occasional misstep.
