# User-Experience Quiz: 2016 UX Year in Review

## Summary

A ten-question quiz covering major UX concepts published by Nielsen Norman Group in 2016. The quiz tests knowledge across usability principles, design patterns, user behavior, and foundational UX theory. Questions cover topics including progressive disclosure, cognitive psychology, design layout conventions, navigation patterns, and user demographics. Answers reference specific Nielsen Norman Group articles published throughout the year, providing a snapshot of key UX concerns and research findings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Illusion of completeness** — content appears complete when viewed within the viewport, but additional information exists outside the viewable area; this mismatch between perceived and actual content availability affects user expectations.
- **Priming effects** — exposure to one stimulus influences subsequent responses; this concept underlies how initial design elements shape user perception of later content.
- **Logo placement does not significantly affect brand recall** — Nielsen Norman Group research found that placement (top left vs. top right) matters less than the logo's presence itself.
- **Trust hierarchy precedes sharing personal information** — users share personal data when sites meet basic relevance and trust requirements first, and when they perform better than competitors.
- **Hamburger menus reduce discoverability** — navigation hidden in hamburger menus is used less frequently than visible navigation bars, even on mobile devices.
- **Negativity bias dominates UX perception** — users weight negative experiences more heavily than positive ones, making it critical to prevent bad interactions rather than rely on good design to overcome them.

- [[Cognitive Psychology]] — exposure to one stimulus influences a subsequent response to a different stimulus; initial design elements shape user perception of later content.
- [[Gestalt Principles]] — the quiz addresses visual design principles that govern how users perceive and group elements, such as using different shapes and colors to distinguish items.
- [[User Interface]] — the quiz tests knowledge of UI patterns including hamburger menus, visual indicators, and menu types.
- [[Web Usability]] — covers topics directly related to usable web design including trust, brand recall, and navigation.
