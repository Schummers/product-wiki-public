# How Many Participants for a UX Interview?

## Summary

The common advice to "test with 5 users" applies to usability testing but not necessarily to interview-based research. Determining the right number of interview participants depends on reaching saturation — the point where conducting more interviews won't yield new insights. Unlike quantitative research, which uses statistical formulas to determine sample size, qualitative interview research must rely on saturation and considers factors like research scope, population diversity, interviewer experience, and interview structure. The article guides practitioners through key factors affecting saturation and recommends starting with a small sample (5-6), analyzing as you go, and recruiting additional participants until saturation is reached.

## Key Takeaways

- **Five participants is often too few for interviews** — While 5 users uncover 85% of usability issues in testing, interviews explore people's experiences and needs with more variability, requiring higher saturation points.
- **Saturation is the target, not a fixed number** — Saturation occurs when new interviews no longer yield new themes or insights. Academic research shows sample sizes ranging from 5 to 95, with estimates of 20-30 for many projects.
- **Two large factors affect saturation** — Broad, exploratory research on diverse populations requires more interviews than narrowly scoped research on homogeneous groups. Interviews about general healthcare access might need 20-30 participants; interviews about specific disease treatment might need only 5.
- **Start small and analyze iteratively** — Recruit 5-6 participants, analyze findings as you go, and continue recruiting until themes stabilize. Provide stakeholders with a range for budgeting purposes.
- **Additional factors influence saturation** — Experienced interviewers extract more insights; participants with domain expertise share more information; and semi-structured interviews with consistent questions reach saturation faster than unstructured conversations.

## Quotes

> Definition: Saturation in a qualitative study is a point where themes emerging from the research are fleshed out enough such that conducting more interviews won't provide new insights that would alter those themes.

> In an interview-based study, we're looking to understand people's experiences and needs rather than discover issues in an interface. Thus, because there's more variability in the kinds of information we're aiming to collect, the point of saturation is often higher than for user tests.

> To avoid doing more interviews than you need, start small and analyze as you go, so you can stop once you're no longer learning anything new.
