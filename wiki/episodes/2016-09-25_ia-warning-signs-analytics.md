# 5 Information Architecture Warning Signs in Your Analytics Reports

## Summary

Analytics data reveals information architecture problems when interpreted correctly. Five warning signs indicate IA issues: low traffic to categories, low conversion rates, high bounce rates on category pages, low entrance rates, and high volume of search queries. Each metric requires contextual interpretation considering strategic importance, visual prominence, page layout, and multi-stage user journeys. Organizations must examine analytics within the broader context of content strategy, business needs, and SEO value before making changes, and supplement analytics with A/B testing or user surveys in ambiguous cases.

## Key Takeaways

- **Low traffic to categories may signal irrelevance** — Traffic volume indicates how useful or interesting a category is to audiences. However, context matters: compare traffic to average, check for visual prominence biases, assess strategic importance, and consider alternative names that might improve discoverability.
- **Low conversions suggest reduced strategic value** — Some high-traffic categories convert less than expected. Investigate whether the category drives traffic to other important pages, supports longer user journeys requiring multiple visits, or has other strategic value before eliminating it.
- **High bounce rates indicate mismatch or layout issues** — Users arriving at category pages and leaving without clicking suggest unmet expectations (misleading labels), layout problems preventing content discovery, or content that doesn't match the category description and link promise.
- **Low entrance rates mean weak audience attraction** — Categories with few new visitor entrances may lack effective promotion or clear labeling, particularly important for strategically significant content. Analyze traffic by channel (search vs. social) to identify whether problems are SEO-related or cross-channel.
- **High search-query volume reveals IA gaps** — Frequently searched terms that don't match current categories indicate user vocabulary mismatches. Consider adding new categories or renaming existing ones, but only if strategic importance and content volume justify the change.

## Quotes

> Analytics data is especially useful when you have a large collection of content. Whether it's blog posts, products, or support articles, presenting your content in categories that are both understandable and interesting makes users more likely to browse (and also improves your search rankings).

> Interpret analytics data within the broader context of content strategy, business and user needs, and SEO value. Look at all these dimensions before deciding whether a category needs to be removed, renamed, or kept as is.
