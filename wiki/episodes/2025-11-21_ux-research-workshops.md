## Summary

When valuable research is only described in documents rather than experienced directly, stakeholders rarely internalize the findings. Research workshops bridge this gap by bringing people into the research through interactive, participatory activities. The article describes three workshop types that serve different purposes: research-alignment workshops (building shared understanding of findings through activities like gallery walks and affinity diagramming), empathy workshops (creating emotional connection through persona walkthroughs and journey mapping), and research-application workshops (translating insights into design directions and priorities through opportunity mapping and ideation).

The most effective workshops blend activities from these three categories based on audience readiness and context. Teams new to research benefit from understanding-focused activities first, while groups that already know the findings but lack emotional connection benefit from empathy-building exercises. Teams ready to act should focus on application methods. Facilitators who adapt their structure to their specific context create sessions that engage participants cognitively, emotionally, and practically, leading to deeper understanding and more user-centered decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three workshop types** — Research-alignment workshops build shared understanding of findings; empathy workshops create emotional connection with users; research-application workshops translate insights into design directions and priorities.
- **Interactive activities over documents** — Interactive gallery walks, insight discovery (affinity diagramming), and assumption comparison create deeper understanding, personal investment, and recall than passive consumption of reports or static presentations.
- **Experiential methods deepen empathy** — Persona walkthroughs, journey mapping, and empathy mapping help stakeholders experience users' frustrations and needs firsthand, connecting emotionally to real human experiences rather than abstract data points.
- **Blend activities to context** — Most effective workshops combine activities across these three goals based on audience readiness: start with understanding activities for groups new to research, use empathy activities for groups that know findings but haven't internalized them, focus on application activities for teams ready to act.
- **Structure sparks collaboration** — Timeboxed activities with clear prompts, facilitated discussions, and visual outputs encourage cognitive, emotional, and practical engagement, leading to shared ownership and user-centered decisions.

## Quotes
