# Perceived Value in User Interfaces

## Summary

Perceived value is the subjective worth users assign to a website or service relative to their current goal. Users make rapid judgments based on initial visual impression, and a negative reaction within seconds can cause them to abandon a site. Perceived value is distinct from interaction cost (the effort required to use a site) and is based on visual design, content presentation, and brand perception rather than logic or data. The user's initial perception frames all subsequent interaction, so conveying both the quality of the organization and ease of use is critical to keeping users engaged.

## Key Takeaways

- **First impressions determine engagement** — Users judge a website within seconds based on visual appearance, and a negative emotional reaction lowers perceived value and triggers site abandonment, often before any real interaction occurs.
- **Perceived cost drives initial assessment** — The perception of interaction cost (how hard the site appears to be to use) matters as much as actual interaction cost in determining whether users stay and explore.
- **Visual clutter signals low value** — A cluttered webpage suggests lack of attention to detail or inability to distill information; this activates the halo effect, leading users to assume poor quality across the entire organization.
- **Design must match business value proposition** — Visual design should communicate the correct value positioning; a budget airline should look budget-friendly, a luxury brand sophisticated. Misalignment confuses users and damages credibility.
- **Initial perception frames entire visit** — Users who perceive low brand value from visual design will half-heartedly complete tasks and stop at the first available exit point, exploring far less than users who perceived high value.

## Quotes

> Perceived value is the subjective worth attributed to a product or service in relation to a goal. It varies from user to user and from task to task: for example, if the goal is to read the latest world news, an e-commerce site selling shoes will have a zero perceived value in relation to that goal (however, it may have higher value if the goal was to kill time).

> Users' initial impression of your website plays a big role in the perceived value of that website and can determine whether they will engage with the site.

> You can increase the expected utility of your site by making sure that it conveys the right message and that it has good usability (that is, it requires little effort to interact with it).
