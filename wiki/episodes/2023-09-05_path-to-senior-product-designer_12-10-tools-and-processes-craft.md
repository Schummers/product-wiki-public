# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 10. Tools and Processes (Craft)

## Summary

While tools and design systems are frequently discussed among designers, this chapter argues that the more valuable skill is identifying problems in processes and owning their improvement. Someone who initiates and defines processes has significantly more leverage and impact than someone who only follows them. The chapter teaches a four-step framework: check if the process is already documented (and if not, volunteer to create or update it), map out the process by identifying steps, collaborators, and tools used, find inefficiencies through self-observation and team feedback, and solve them through tools, documentation, design systems, or policy changes. This skill translates across companies and career types, though the chapter notes the technique may be less powerful in a company that already has a design operations team, since the most important processes will already be owned by them. The chapter includes a real example of automating a repetitive asset export task in Photoshop, reducing time from up to one minute per asset to a few seconds, and two detailed examples of process solutions: creating a modular design file for marketing landing pages, and documenting legacy versus new design system elements.

## Key Takeaways

- **Problem identification beats execution** — Knowing how to identify process problems and define solutions has more leverage and impact than executing within a process. Someone who owns process improvement affects their entire organization's efficiency.
- **Four-step framework** — Check for existing documentation (and offer to create or update it), map the process by listing steps, collaborators, and tools, find inefficiencies through your own observation and team feedback, then solve them with tools, documentation, or policy.
- **Pitch for ownership** — When proposing process documentation or improvement, frame it as benefiting the company: onboarding efficiency, standardization, a reference point for future changes, and collaboration improvements.
- **Feedback collection** — Ask colleagues specific questions about frustrations, tool pain points, repetitive tasks, bottlenecks, and what they would change with a magic wand. Use an impact/effort matrix to prioritize which inefficiencies to tackle first.
- **Solution spectrum** — Solutions can range from simple documentation updates to new tools, design systems, modular component libraries, or even building internal automation tools. Start by interviewing stakeholders and researching best practices.

## Quotes

> "Knowing how to create or maintain documentation, design systems, processes, and tools is important. However, knowing how to identify the problems and needs of all of the above is a much more valuable skill."

> "Someone who initiates and defines processes has more leverage and impact compared to someone who is purely following and executing them."

> "You can approach process improvements in the same way you go about building products – identifying needs, understanding how people solve the problem today, observing them doing that, identifying room for improvement, suggesting solutions, etc."

## Concepts

- [[Design Ownership]] — The chapter emphasizes ownership of process improvement: taking responsibility for documenting workflows, identifying problems, and implementing solutions. This ownership directly impacts organizational efficiency.
- [[Design Leadership]] — Process improvement leadership translates across companies and career types; the framework for identifying and solving process problems is a fundamental leadership skill that creates leverage.
- [[Productivity]] — Optimizing processes and workflows directly improves team productivity; the chapter demonstrates how small improvements (like automating exports) can multiply efficiency across a team.
- [[Design Strategy]] — Strategic thinking about process design and improvement sits at a higher level than execution; identifying which processes to optimize and how creates organizational-level impact.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Process improvement starts with understanding how teams work together; the feedback-collection approach involves understanding pain points across team members and building solutions that improve collaboration.
