# GenAI for Complex Questions, Search for Critical Facts

## Summary

Users are increasingly turning to generative AI for exploratory information-seeking tasks, but not uniformly. A Nielsen Norman Group study examined how people choose between AI chatbots and traditional search. Users gravitate toward AI when they have vague goals, juggle multiple requirements, or need to synthesize information from many sources. They prefer traditional search when accuracy, control, and trusted sources matter most. Rather than AI replacing search entirely, the two tools serve complementary roles.

## Key Takeaways

- **Exploratory starting point** — Users turn to AI when starting with vague ideas or undefined search goals, letting the AI suggest possible directions and avoid the trial-and-error of keyword foraging.
- **Multiple constraints** — AI handles tasks with several simultaneous constraints (budget, timeline, location) better than search engines, which struggle to incorporate multiple filter criteria.
- **Information aggregation** — AI reduces interaction cost by consolidating information from multiple sources into a single response, saving users clicks and cognitive load compared to scanning many websites.
- **Validation mechanism** — Users employ traditional search to verify AI results, confirm pricing, and validate facts, especially for high-stakes decisions where errors carry consequences.
- **Trustworthiness concerns** — Users distrust AI's accuracy on specific data like pricing and prefer authoritative sources for specialized or regulated domains (medical, academic research).
- **Complementary roles** — The research shows AI and search serving different purposes rather than one replacing the other; users often ping-pong between them.
