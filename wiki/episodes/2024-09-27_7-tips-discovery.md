# 7 Tips for Successful Discoveries

## Summary

Maria Rosala presents seven practical tactics for running successful discovery efforts in UX projects. Discovery is framed as one of the most challenging parts of design because it is filled with unknowns about the problem, users, and their needs. The article emphasizes that discovery requires buy-in from stakeholders, clear team roles and norms, proper problem framing, prioritized research questions, time management, team involvement, and collaborative analysis. These practices ensure discovery efforts are effective, build team morale, and use time wisely.

## Key Takeaways

- **Secure stakeholder buy-in early** — teams and stakeholders must understand what discovery is and why it matters; without buy-in, discoveries can be cut short or not properly supported.
- **Establish roles and norms collaboratively** — clarifying who owns which tasks and agreeing on how the team will work together (meeting frequency, research participation, documentation) prevents confusion and boosts team morale.
- **Frame the problem with a clear statement** — a problem statement ensures everyone understands the discovery's scope and helps the team avoid researching the wrong things.
- **Prioritize unknowns strategically** — teams should identify and cluster unknowns about users and the problem space, then vote on which are most important and risky to address.
- **Timebox discovery activities** — setting time limits on discovery phases creates urgency and focus, counteracting the planning fallacy where teams underestimate how long research takes.
- **Involve the full team in research** — when team members participate in user research, they develop firsthand knowledge of user needs and pain points, creating empathy and consensus.
- **Analyze and synthesize together** — collaborative analysis where teams cluster observations, map learnings, and generate How-Might-We questions produces better insights and more creative solutions.

## Quotes

> Discovery is one of the most challenging parts of the design process because it's filled with unknowns. The problem you want to solve is perhaps undefined, and many unknowns exist about who the users are and what they need.

> **Establishing roles and norms as early as possible (such as in a**[kick-off workshop](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/discovery-kick-off-workshops/)**) sets teams up for success** and can boost team morale.

> Analyzing, synthesizing, and ideating as a team can provide better insights and more creative solution ideas.

## Concepts

- [[Product Discovery]] — the structured phase of UX design that focuses on understanding the problem space, user needs, and context before defining solutions.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — practices and frameworks for working together effectively across functional roles, including shared norms, role clarity, and collaborative research and analysis.
- [[Problem Framing]] — the articulation of what needs to be solved, who is affected, and why it matters, forming the foundation for focused discovery research.
