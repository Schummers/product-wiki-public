# Maintain Consistency and Adhere to Standards (Usability Heuristic #4)

## Summary

The fourth usability heuristic emphasizes that systems should adhere to both internal consistency (same patterns within a product or product family) and external consistency (following web and platform conventions). Consistency and standards reduce confusion and increase learnability because users bring expectations from other sites and expect familiar interactions. While consistency might appear to stifle creativity, it actually enables users to focus on content and goals rather than learning new interactions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Consistency operates on two levels** — internal consistency applies patterns uniformly within a single product or product family, while external consistency follows established web and platform conventions.
- **Jakob's Law shapes user expectations** — people spend most time on sites other than yours, bringing expectations from other sites; adherence to conventions helps users reach goals efficiently.
- **Multiple layers of consistency matter** — visual consistency (icons and imagery), page and button layout, user-entered data formatting, and content tone of voice all require consistency.
- **Consistency reduces learning curves** — when users encounter familiar interactions and patterns, they can transfer knowledge from other sites and focus entirely on content, messaging, and services.
- **Consistency requires active maintenance** — design reviews, consistency audits, and design systems help teams maintain consistency over time despite designers interpreting standards differently.
- **Breaking conventions has cognitive cost** — designing against conventions adds to users' cognitive load and should only be done if absolutely necessary to the task or significantly improving efficiency.

## Quotes

> "Think about the websites and applications you use: they all rely on well-established conventions. Blue underlined text is clickable, the shopping-cart icon shows the items you plan to purchase, the site logo is in the top left corner, a magnifier-glass icon stands for search — these are all examples of conventions that are used all the time in digital products and that make users' lives easier."

> "people spend most of their time on sites other than yours, which means that they bring their expectations from other sites when they visit your site."

> "Consistency doesn't happen by itself, but requires an active process."
