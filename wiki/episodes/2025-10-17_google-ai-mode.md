# Google AI Mode: Powerful Search, Poor Usability

## Summary

Google AI Mode combines web search breadth with LLM reasoning to answer complex questions and support followup queries. A study of 7 participants found users appreciated the synthesized answers and source citations, but significant usability problems hindered adoption. AI Mode has poor discoverability (4 of 7 participants hadn't noticed it before being directed there), unclear differentiation from other Google AI products, missed onboarding that users bypassed, wordy unformatted responses that overwhelmed readers, long conversations requiring excessive scrolling, and mental-model mismatches with the persistent tab bar. Despite these issues, AI Mode's power for information-seeking is significant when these barriers are addressed.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI Mode has poor discoverability despite multiple access points** — Four of seven participants hadn't discovered AI Mode; familiarity with Google's interface and task-focused information-seeking habits cause users to ignore new features even with rainbow animation attempts to grab attention.
- **Multiple poorly differentiated Google AI products confuse users** — Google offers Gemini (open-ended chat), AI Mode (search-specific chat), and AI Overviews (auto-summaries); participants frequently confused these offerings and their different capabilities.
- **Onboarding pages help only if users actually see them** — AI Mode's welcome page with guidance and prompt suggestions is missed because users bypass it through default browser search; help text is useless if people don't encounter it.
- **Wordy, unformatted responses overwhelm users** — Long paragraphs of dense text discourage skimming and cause some participants to abandon AI Mode entirely; simple formatting (bold, bullets, subheadings) and collapsible sections would improve readability.
- **Long conversations require excessive scrolling and navigation** — After several exchanges, revisiting previous responses demands substantial scrolling; jump links or tables of contents would save effort, especially critical on mobile where back-and-forth is physically taxing.
- **Tab bar mental models clash with AI Mode behavior** — Persistent tabs (Images, Shopping, Videos) reflect the current first query, not the user's most recent followup question, breaking user expectations about what tabs should do in search.

## Quotes

> "AI Mode is a powerful tool if you know how to find and use it — which is a big "if," considering the range of usability problems we observed."

> "AI Mode has poor discoverability — meaning that people are unlikely to notice it on their own."

> "I'd have to play around with it a little bit more to not get lost or not but (...) I think I might try it, use this instead of just regular Google."

## Concepts

- [[AI Search]] — Explores how AI Mode supercharges search through query fan-out (generating multiple related subqueries) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), letting LLMs synthesize results across searches that would take users hours.
- [[Information Seeking]] — Documents information-seeking behavior shifts with AI: users expect AI to aggregate information, eliminate keyword-foraging problems, but users still rely on traditional keyword phrases instead of natural language.
- [[Discoverability]] — Analyzes poor discoverability caused by interface familiarity, task-focus, vague naming, animation ineffectiveness, and lack of differentiation from other Google AI features; structural changes needed beyond visual cues.
- [[LLM Interfaces]] — Addresses design challenges specific to conversational AI: long responses, scrolling burden in mobile, formatting needs, mental-model mismatches with persistent navigation, and onboarding that users may never see.
