# Passive Information Acquisition on the Increase

## Summary

This survey replicating and expanding 1997 Xerox PARC research reveals that passive information acquisition (unintended discovery) has increased from 4% to 14% of critical information leading to significant decisions. The study identifies two passive acquisition types: explore (accidental discovery while browsing) and notified (information via email, text, or push notification). Analysis of 498 critical-incident survey responses compares five dimensions of active versus passive information acquisition.

## Key Takeaways

- **Passive Acquisition Growing** — passive information discovery has increased from 4% to 14% over 22 years; users discover critical information without intending to seek it, through browsing or notifications.
- **More Social Interaction Passively** — during passive acquisition, people are twice as likely to interact socially compared to active seeking, with sharing being the dominant interaction type (21% vs 2% for active).
- **Contextual Information Valued** — when discovering information accidentally, users lack surrounding context and want links to related content, comparison tables, or expanded information to act on discoveries.
- **Credibility Emphasized** — passive acquisition contexts make credibility assessment difficult; users value transparent sourcing, clear citations, and freedom from sensationalism and clickbait.
- **Customization Necessity** — users report overwhelming notification volume and desire personalization to receive only important information matching their interests.
- **Narrower Content Scope** — product info, news/politics, and people categories account for 81% of passive acquisition, compared to broader active-seeking categories; health information is actively sought, news passively discovered.
- **Ads Influence Passive Decisions** — paid content accounts for 37% of critical passive acquisitions versus 1% for active; ads are more effective when passive users are less goal-driven and browsing-oriented.
- **Mobile Dominance in Passive** — passive acquisition favors single-device mobile use; only 10% of passive acquisitions use multiple devices versus 22% for active.

## Quotes

> passive information acquisition increased from 4% to 14% of all incidents leading to significant decisions or actions.

> People were almost twice as likely to interact with others during passive information acquisition than during active information acquisition.

> Designers have the responsibility of making their content discoverable, by creating strong information scent for that content.

## Concepts

- [[Information Seeking]] — documents the growing role of passive, unintended discovery in critical decision-making and how it differs fundamentally from active information seeking.
- [[Decision Making]] — shows that important decisions increasingly rely on accidentally discovered information acquired through browsing or notifications rather than deliberate search.
- [[Social Interaction]] — reveals that passive information discovery triggers more social sharing and collaboration than active information seeking, particularly around warning others or recommending options.
