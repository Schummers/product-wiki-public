# Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty

## Summary

Flat design's popularity has coincided with a reduction in visual signifiers that help users identify clickable elements. Eyetracking research comparing strong signifiers (3D buttons, underlined blue links) with weak signifiers (ghost buttons, plain text links) reveals that weak signifiers increase task time by 22% and fixations by 25%. Users become uncertain whether they've found the right element, looking around the page more before committing to a click. However, flat designs can work when pages have low information density, traditional layouts, and important elements that stand out through contrast and positioning.

## Key Takeaways

- **Weak signifiers increase interaction cost and task time** — users spend 22% more time and make 25% more fixations on pages with weak clickability signifiers, indicating click uncertainty.
- **Users consider more elements when signifiers are weak** — broad fixation distributions indicate users examining multiple potential targets instead of confidently clicking the obvious choice.
- **In-line links need color contrast to be recognized** — as long as links are presented in a contrasting color, users recognize them even without underlines.
- **Flat designs work under specific conditions** — low information density, traditional layouts, salient high-contrast targets, and experienced UX teams can use flat design without damaging interaction.
- **Click uncertainty harms perception beyond task time** — even if measured slowdown is modest, the emotional impact of uncertainty and reduced decisiveness hurts brand perception and user confidence.

## Quotes

> Designs with weak clickability signifiers waste users' time: people need to look at more UI elements and spend more time on the page, as captured by heatmaps, average counts of fixations, and average task time.

> Users need to be able to look at a page, and understand their options immediately. They need to be able to glance at what they're looking for and know instantly, "Yep, that's it."

> The problem is not that users never see a weakly signified UI element. It's that even when they do see the weak element, they don't feel confident that it is what they want, so they keep looking around the page.

## Concepts

- [[Flat Design]] — a minimalist design approach that removes dimensional cues like shadows and gradients; effectiveness depends on maintaining strong visual hierarchy through contrast and positioning.
- [[Visual Hierarchy]] — the organization of page elements to emphasize importance; weak signifiers diminish visual hierarchy and cause users to struggle identifying interactive elements.
- [[Clickability Signifiers]] — visual cues that signal to users that an element is interactive; strong signifiers (color, underline, 3D effects) reduce task time and uncertainty compared to weak or absent signifiers.
