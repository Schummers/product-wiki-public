# Templates and Tools for UX Job Seekers

## Summary

This resource article provides actionable communication templates and tools for navigating each phase of a UX job search, from notifying networks of job intentions to declining offers professionally. The templates cover networking outreach, reference requests, mentor inquiries, assessment withdrawals, thank-you notes, feedback requests, and offer negotiations. The article also recommends free assessments for clarifying professional values and specialized tools like the NN/g UX Job-Interview Coach to help job seekers practice and improve their competitive positioning.

## Key Takeaways

- **Use authentic, personalized templates rather than generic AI-generated messages** — Generative AI can serve as a proofreader or editor, but recipients perceive genuine, personal touches more favorably than machine-generated content, which has become ubiquitous.
- **Request references and references proactively** — Secure at least one reference from recent employers or mentors who can speak to your work quality; students should secure two references before internships end.
- **Leverage free professional-values assessments** — Life Values Inventory, Career Anchors, and Values Sort are research-backed tools that help clarify what truly matters in your career, preventing regrettable job choices.
- **Find mentorship through established platforms** — ADPList, HFES Mentorship Program, and UXPA Mentorship Program connect job seekers with experienced UX professionals offering free or affordable guidance.
- **Withdraw from assessments that exceed reasonable effort** — Decline job opportunities with excessively demanding assessments or timeframes diplomatically; accept that employers overestimating assessment length is a red flag about hiring practices.
- **Thank interviewers and request feedback professionally** — Thank-you notes demonstrate attention to detail and provide another opportunity to reiterate qualifications; requesting feedback after rejection, though rarely granted, is worth attempting.

## Quotes

> Use genAI only as a proofreader or light editor of an authentic message drafted by you.

> Rushing into a new UX job based on job title, employer, or compensation is a recipe for dissatisfaction once the newness wears off (which happens in the span of months, not years).

> A mentor can be an invaluable resource of advice and perspective during your UX job search. But quality mentorship isn't just a handful of brief sessions, it's a commitment that lasts months, or even years.
