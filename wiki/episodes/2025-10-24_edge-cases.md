# The Edge Cases that Break Hearts (And Products)

## Summary

Design typically focuses on the "happy path" where everything works perfectly for most users most of the time. However, real users face edge cases like account lockouts, name changes, multiple accounts, bad actors, deceased users, collaboration conflicts, and slow internet connections. These edge cases are not rare exceptions but common scenarios that happen to somebody somewhere every day. Designers must account for complications including accessibility requirements, offline functionality, data exports, and social/emotional impacts like harassment and grief that typical user flows fail to consider.

## Key Takeaways

- **Edge cases are more common than assumed** — scenarios like name changes, account lockouts, and network issues that seem exceptional are surprisingly common occurrences that many users experience if they use a product long enough.
- **Design for disaster scenarios** — products must handle divorced parents sharing calendars, account recovery after phone theft, slow connections, and collaboration with multiple simultaneous editors working with varying internet quality.
- **Account flexibility is essential** — support multiple accounts, shared accounts, and account switching without forcing users to log out repeatedly or lose data when circumstances change (like breakups or children leaving home).
- **Safety and harassment require proactive design** — bad actors will exploit systems, so safety features must be built from the ground up with blocking, reporting, and friction in account creation, not added reactively after problems occur.
- **Death and legacy planning matter** — design for users passing away by providing memorial modes, designating legacy contacts, stopping collaboration invites to inactive accounts, and avoiding automated emotional triggers like photo retrospectives.
- **Accessibility is a design constraint, not a feature** — over 1 in 4 adults have disabilities, and accessibility must be planned from day one, not treated as an afterthought; design for offline-first, cache intelligently, and show clear sync status.

## Quotes

> An edge case is a rare or unexpected scenario that typically involves unusual user inputs, extreme data values, or uncommon usage patterns that weren't anticipated during the primary design and testing phases.

> Your "edge cases" aren't always edge cases. They're Tuesday afternoon for a good percentage of your users.

> There are no perfect users living perfect lives with perfect internet connections and perfect intentions. The sooner we design for the messy, complicated (sometimes heartbreaking) reality of how people actually live, the better our products will serve everyone.

## Concepts

- [[Interaction Design]] — Interaction flows must accommodate "edge cases" like name changes, account lockouts, collaboration with multiple editors on slow connections, and social scenarios (divorced parents, deceased users); these are not rare exceptions but common scenarios affecting significant user populations.
- [[Design]] — Products must proactively design for safety, harassment, death, data exports, and accessibility from the start, not as afterthoughts, because real users live complicated lives with shared accounts, unreliable connectivity, disabilities, and emotional needs that perfect-world flows ignore.
- [[User Research]] — Understanding edge cases requires recognizing that typical "happy path" user research misses the messy reality of how people actually live; research must explicitly explore failure modes, exceptional circumstances, and the complicated scenarios that affect somebody somewhere every day.
