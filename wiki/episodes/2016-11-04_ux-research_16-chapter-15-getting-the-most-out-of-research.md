# UX Research: Chapter 15: Getting the Most Out of Research

## Summary

The final chapter shifts perspective from a single research study to research as an ongoing practice that matures team knowledge over time. The chapter addresses how to continue research initiatives after observing positive impact from one study and recaps key themes from the book. Its central argument is that research is a lifestyle: as questions are answered, new questions emerge, and teams should continually investigate new areas while diving deeper into previously understood topics.

The chapter introduces the conscious competency matrix to illustrate how team knowledge grows through four phases: unconsciously incompetent (not knowing what you don't know), consciously incompetent (identifying what is missing), consciously competent (actively applying knowledge to roadmaps), and unconsciously competent (applying understanding across the organization). The chapter emphasizes that research is integrated across multiple areas of the product lifecycle—development, roadmap, strategy, and team culture—and that the ultimate goal is to move research from a discrete phase to a core component of how teams work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Research uncovers new insights that validate hypotheses** — Deep understanding of customers' cognitive, emotional, and behavioral characteristics cannot come from analytics and market research alone. According to Ofer Deshe (CEO, Tobias & Tobias) in his sidebar, structured observations, ethnographic studies, and methods rooted in sociology and psychology are essential for understanding unmet needs. The right research helps shape propositions that customers will perceive as valuable.

- **Avoid the minimally viable product trap** — Rather than letting brainstorming sessions and off-site ideas drive product development and only testing desirability when it is too late, Ofer Deshe advocates moving from a technology-focused paradigm to an evidence-based world of minimally lovable services. Good research provides high return on investment; bad or no research reduces revenues and profitability.

- **Research never stops; it continues throughout the product lifecycle** — Good product teams know research doesn't end after an initial study. Common areas of ongoing research include validation of recent changes, experimentation with microcontent and design patterns, identifying common pitfalls and drop-off points, and measuring overall user satisfaction.

- **Revisit "fully understood" topics from different angles** — It is rare to gain a complete understanding because teams must eventually build the product. Research data gets stale, and findings from two years ago may no longer apply. Teams should revisit topics they felt were fully understood and ask new questions. If they used only qualitative methods before, combining with quantitative methods provides a new point of view, and vice versa.

- **Research applies across product development, roadmap, strategy, and culture** — Rather than limit research to a single phase, research findings should inform immediate design decisions (development), future directions (roadmap), how consumers interact with the product (business strategy), and how teams approach development (team culture). By engaging with users, teams become closer to the people they are working for and rethink their approach.

- **The conscious competency matrix tracks team growth** — As teams conduct more research, they can track their level of understanding (from not knowing what they don't know, to knowing what is missing, to actively using knowledge, to unconsciously applying it). Reaching unconscious competence is the sweet spot where teams make educated decisions and recognize when they are approaching unknown territory.

- **Internal workshops move teams up the competency matrix** — Workshops get current information and understanding out in the open, and the resulting conversations inform which research methods to employ next.

## Quotes

> Embrace the thrill of discovery, exploration, and execution.

> The ability to identify real problems quicker and more efficiently increases your value to your team and product alike.

## Concepts

- [[Research Operations]] — the chapter frames research as an ongoing operational practice integrated throughout product development, not a discrete phase. Teams should plan their next research efforts based on internal workshop discussions and maintain continuous inquiry as market conditions and user behavior change.

- [[User-Centered Design]] — the chapter emphasizes that product development should be driven by understanding user goals and needs through research rather than assumptions. Teams that engage with users through research become closer to the people they are working for and are more likely to make decisions supporting user goals.

- [[Business Impact]] — in his sidebar, Ofer Deshe argues that good research provides a high return on investment by uncovering customers' unmet needs, while bad or no research reduces revenue and profitability and can contribute to brand failure.

- [[Team Collaboration]] — the chapter emphasizes that research should be a core component of team culture. Engaging with users and collaborating with stakeholders creates an empathic link and makes research findings more likely to influence team decisions. The chapter treats research as an addictive process that becomes part of how teams work together.

- [[Research Methods]] — the main chapter frames qualitative and quantitative methods as complementary, encouraging teams to combine them for depth and breadth as they revisit topics over time. In his sidebar, Ofer Deshe adds that as a CEO he personally relies on structured observations, ethnographic studies, and methods rooted in sociology and psychology.
