# What UX Consulting Clients Expect in the Age of AI

## Summary

AI hasn't changed why organizations hire UX consultants; clients seek experienced experts to solve real problems, not flashy demos. Clients value strong judgment over AI fluency, clear points of view grounded in user evidence (not AI output), respect for real-world constraints, rigor in research and interpretation, and plain language. Most clients already use AI; what they lack is confidence in when to deploy it responsibly and which problems suit it. Consultants who slow down early to ensure direction is right, synthesize evidence into recommendations, understand organizational complexity, and protect experience quality from automation's erosion outperform those pushing AI-first approaches. Usefulness matters more than being impressive; clients seek partners combining research rigor, good judgment, and practical reality.

## Key Takeaways

- **Strong Judgment, Not AI Fluency** — Clients lack confidence in responsible AI deployment, not access to AI; consultants who slow down and assess fit provide more value than those rushing to deploy.
- **User Evidence Beats AI Output** — Clients want informed design recommendations grounded in research, not neutral lists of ten options generated by AI; synthesizing evidence into a point of view is where value lies.
- **Respect Real-World Constraints** — AI often overlooks legal, compliance, security, legacy, and organizational complexity; consultants who design within these realities, not around them, deliver adoptable solutions.
- **Rigor Over Speed** — While AI accelerates transcription and tagging, clients value careful research design, thoughtful interpretation, transparency about confidence and limitations, and human validation.
- **Plain Language and Intentional Design** — Clients are sensitive to careless, generic, automated-feeling work; they value consultants who advocate for specific, intentional, well-crafted recommendations.
- **Usefulness Over Impressiveness** — Clients seek partners combining research rigor, good judgment, and practical reality; they don't want AI slop, they want better decisions for users.

## Quotes

> Most clients already have access to and use AI tools. What they lack is confidence in when and how to use them responsibly, as well as an understanding of which problems are suited for these tools.

> Consultants who slow down early and ensure that things are heading in the right direction, to avoid escalating ineffective solutions.

> Being useful matters more than being impressive. Develop strong expertise and judgment, not just familiarity with the latest and greatest AI tools and their claims.
