# The State of UX Maturity: Data from Our Self-Assessment Quiz

## Summary

Nielsen Norman Group analyzed over 5,000 responses to a free UX-maturity self-assessment quiz released in June 2021, which measures organizational UX maturity across six stages. The quiz consists of 13 questions based on the maturity model, with raw scores ranging from 0 to 100. The data reveals that stage 3 (Emergent) is the most common among respondents (49%), though this likely reflects selection bias since the respondents were primarily UX professionals already familiar with UX concepts. The analysis explores how role, geographic region, and company size impact maturity perceptions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stage 3 (Emergent) dominates respondents but selection bias skews results** — 49% of respondents scored in stage 3, but this reflects that quiz-takers were mostly UX-exposed professionals; many low-maturity organizations were likely not captured, creating a floor effect.
- **High-maturity stages are rare** — Only 4% and 0.04% scored in stages 5 and 6 respectively; a ceiling effect may exist since thriving organizations may not feel motivated to assess their practices.
- **Role strongly impacts maturity perception** — Research, management, and experience-design roles rated organizations higher (average scores 49, 47, 46) than engineering, visual design, or marketing roles (all 40 or below); UX roles may have greater awareness of UX work and more optimistic perspectives.
- **Region has no practical significance** — While some statistically significant differences emerged, all geographic regions averaged 42 to 46 points (stage 3); English-speaking Western regions scored only one point higher, which lacks practical impact.
- **Company size doesn't predict maturity** — Small, medium, and large companies all averaged around 44 to 45 points; while large companies have budget advantages, they struggle with cultural shifts; smaller companies can adopt UX practices faster or skip early stages altogether.
- **Individual perception varies within the same organization** — An organization's self-reported maturity depends on respondents' role, involvement, awareness, and perception; hiring outside assessors provides more objective measurements.

## Quotes

> "We believe that they were mostly people who have regular UX exposure (for example, NN/g newsletter subscribers and people who follow UX-related tags on social media). That fact suggests that these quiz respondents were not representative for all organizations (a selection bias)."

> "In stage 4, organizations start to build UX into their operations and culture. They often produce high-quality designs. Many companies become complacent at this stage and never move past it."

> "It's incredibly difficult for organizations to achieve and maintain those levels of UX success."

## Concepts

- [[UX Maturity]] — NN/g quiz data shows stage-3 (Emergent) maturity is most common among respondents, but selection bias means the distribution skews toward lower stages globally; individual assessments also vary based on respondent role, involvement, and awareness, with UX research and management roles rating organizations higher than engineering or visual design roles.
- [[Selection Bias]] — Quiz respondents were mostly UX-familiar professionals, likely missing many stage-1 and stage-2 organizations, creating floor and ceiling effects in the data distribution.
- [[UX Adoption]] — Geographic region and company size show no practical impact on UX maturity; adoption depends more on organizational priorities and leadership commitment than location or scale.
