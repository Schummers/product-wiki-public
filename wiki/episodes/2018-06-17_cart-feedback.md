# Adding an Item to a Shopping Cart: Provide Clear, Persistent Feedback

## Summary

E-commerce sites often fail to provide clear feedback when users add items to shopping carts, creating uncertainty about whether the action succeeded and whether the correct product was added. This causes users to double-check their actions, stemming from poor prior experiences. Research shows many sites do a poor job of confirming add-to-cart actions, either by not making it clear the action was processed or by not indicating whether the correct product with proper options was added. Clear, persistent feedback that includes product details helps users feel confident and continue shopping.

## Key Takeaways

- **Use cart icon badges with subtotals** — A noticeable badge showing item count and running subtotal on the cart icon clearly indicates a product was added and helps users verify if quantity seems correct.
- **Avoid transient overlays** — Disappearing popovers that fade quickly prevent users from reviewing what was added, creating stress and uncertainty. Use persistent overlays, banners, or interstitial pages instead.
- **Show product details in confirmation** — Include product image, name, price, quantity, and options (size, color) in the confirmation so users can verify they added the correct item.
- **Change button state for duplicate prevention** — When an item is already in the cart, change the button to indicate this with an "Add Another" option or similar messaging, helping users avoid accidentally duplicating items.
- **Consider site context** — Interstitial pages work well for sites where users purchase few items per session, while persistent overlays or banners suit sites where users browse and add multiple items.

## Quotes

> Clear feedback that this step was completed successfully gives users a sense of control and allows them to confidently continue shopping or proceed with their checkout.

> However, our usability testing of ecommerce sites shows that many sites do a poor job of confirming when a product has been added to a cart.

> The transient nature of these popovers prevents users from easily reviewing what specifically ended up in the cart — were the size and the color right? Viewing the feedback popover becomes a race against time and can create stress, as well as uncertainty about how to access this information again.
