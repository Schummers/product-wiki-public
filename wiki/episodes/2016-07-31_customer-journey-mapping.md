# When and How to Create Customer Journey Maps

## Summary

Customer journey mapping is a process that compiles user goals and actions into a timeline, enriches that timeline with user thoughts and emotions to create a narrative, and condenses the narrative into a visualization. Journey maps are essential tools for creating holistic views of customer experience and breaking down silos within organizations to build shared vision. They serve multiple business goals: shifting perspective from inside-out to outside-in, breaking down departmental silos, assigning ownership of touchpoints, targeting specific customers, and understanding why quantitative data shows particular patterns. Successful journey maps require clear business goals, research-based truth, cross-department collaboration, and complete data synthesis before jumping to visualization. The process of creating journey maps is often more valuable than the final artifact, as it engages stakeholders and spurs collaborative change.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three-zone map structure** — Journey maps contain zones for the lens (persona and scenario), the mapped experience (phases, actions, thoughts, emotions), and insights (discoveries and opportunities).
- **Always support a business goal** — Maps created without alignment to a business goal will not produce applicable insight; establish clear goals before beginning the process.
- **Base on truth, not assumptions** — Journey maps must result from actual research; quantitative data can support but cannot replace qualitative research and user observation.
- **Collaboration is the real value** — The activity of creating the map across departments is often more valuable than the final artifact; involve stakeholders throughout.
- **Don't jump to visualization** — Resist the temptation to create beautiful graphics before data synthesis is complete; the narrative must be well-understood before visualizing.

## Quotes

> In its most basic form, journey mapping starts by compiling a series of user goals and actions into a timeline skeleton. Next, the skeleton is fleshed out with user thoughts and emotions in order to create a narrative. Finally, that narrative is condensed into a visualization used to communicate insights that will inform design processes.

> Journey mapping creates a holistic view of customer experience, and it's this process of bringing together and visualizing disparate data points that can engage otherwise disinterested stakeholders from across groups and spur collaborative conversation and change.

> Fragmented understanding is chronic in organizations where KPIs are assigned and measured per individual department or group because many organizations do not ever piece together the entire experience from the user's standpoint.

## Concepts

- [[Journey Mapping]] — Journey mapping combines storytelling and visualization to create holistic views of customer experience and communicate insights across organizations.
- [[Research Methods]] — Journey mapping relies on qualitative research methods like field studies, contextual inquiry, and diary studies to ground maps in truth.
- [[UX Strategy]] — Journey maps support strategic goals such as shifting organizational perspective from inside-out to outside-in, or assigning ownership of different journey stages.
- [[Design Process]] — Journey mapping is a key UX research and strategy activity that identifies gaps and opportunities in customer experiences.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Journey mapping creates shared understanding across departments; the collaborative process of creating maps often produces more value than the final artifact.
