# Design Risks: How to Assess, Mitigate, and Manage Them

## Summary

Risk management is a systematic approach to identifying, assessing, controlling, and evaluating hazards in design decisions. Risk is calculated as likelihood multiplied by impact, helping teams prioritize mitigation efforts on high-severity, high-probability problems. A six-step process includes establishing objectives, identifying hazards through research and analytics, plotting risks on assessment matrices, developing controls, implementing mitigations while weighing costs against benefits, and continuously monitoring outcomes. Not all risks can be eliminated, but systematic analysis helps teams make informed decisions about which risks are worth taking and how to minimize harm.

## Key Takeaways

- **Risk equals likelihood times impact** — Hazards vary in both probability and severity; teams should prioritize mitigating high-impact and high-likelihood risks first.
- **Identify hazards through multiple sources** — Gather hazard information from behavioral research, analytics, support tickets, competitive analysis, and secondary research before designing.
- **Use assessment matrices to prioritize** — Plotting hazards on a two-dimensional matrix of severity and probability helps teams objectively compare risks and decide what to mitigate.
- **Mitigation reduces but doesn't eliminate risk** — Develop controls that address likelihood, impact, or both; sometimes mitigation introduces new risks like added friction.
- **Weigh mitigation costs against benefits** — Risk-reduction strategies have implementation costs; teams must analyze whether benefits justify effort, considering current risk tolerance.
- **Monitor and update continuously** — Risk profiles change as designs evolve and circumstances shift; teams should audit regularly and adapt mitigation strategies.
