# Solving Product Design Exercises: Chapter 1: Introducing the design exercise

## Summary

This chapter maps the typical hiring process at tech companies, situating design exercises within the larger interview flow. The chapter describes seven steps of the hiring process for product designers, from initial phone screening through offer, explaining what happens at each stage. Design exercises are presented as a critical step that heavily influences hiring decisions, distinct from other evaluation methods like portfolio review and product critique.

## Key Takeaways

- **Seven-stage hiring process** — Phone screening by recruiters (10-20 minutes to assess basic fit), on-site or phone interview covering process and team dynamics, portfolio review to walk through projects and decisions, optional product critique, design exercise (key decision point), optional informal follow-up interview, and offer.
- **Design exercise as critical assessment** — The design exercise is explicitly identified as a key step that heavily influences hiring decisions; candidates must solve a product design problem and explain their reasoning.
- **Portfolio review shows finished work** — Candidates present complete projects with context, explaining objectives, their contributions, decisions, and reasoning. This differs from the design exercise, which tests real-time problem-solving.
- **Optional design critique adds evaluation** — Some companies ask candidates to evaluate an existing product design, identifying what works, what doesn't, and improvement suggestions.
- **Framework applies across steps** — The problem-solving framework from the book can be applied to portfolio presentations, design critiques, and design exercises.
