# The UX Reckoning: Prepare for 2025 and Beyond

## Summary

UX professionals face a challenging period with UX job listings down to 70% of 2021 levels and organizational UX maturity regressing. However, UX has faced difficult periods before (like after the dot-com bubble), and there are actionable steps practitioners can take. The article addresses three major challenges: UX on the chopping block, post-hype AI with more realistic perspectives, and shallow UX proliferation via social media. Practitioners should adopt flexible mindsets, empathize with other disciplines, balance user and business goals, and recognize limitations of individual agency. The field is moving toward outcome-oriented design, valuing critical thinking and soft skills over templates and technical execution.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX has been here before** — The dot-com bubble burst nearly took NN/g down; when budgets tighten, research and design are considered "nice-to-have" and cut first, reflecting poor understanding of UX value.
- **Renaming alone won't fix problems** — Teams rebranding as "insights research" or "experience design" are addressing symptoms (misperception) without solving the underlying challenge of demonstrating UX's business value.
- **Be flexible and resourceful** — Accept that UX work, titles, and roles will evolve significantly; don't cling too tightly to how you've done UX work in the past.
- **Post-hype AI is becoming practical** — After initial exuberance, 47% of UX professionals found AI had "some value," with improved integrations in design and research platforms and development of domain-specific models.
- **Shallow UX proliferation via social media** — Influencer culture, uncredited sharing, and template proliferation have degraded the field; critical thinking and context matter more than shortcuts.
- **Automation will raise the bar** — As tools automate basic tasks, differentiators will be critical thinking, creativity, taste, soft skills, and the ability to discern and curate outputs.

## Quotes

> UX has always been like this (more or less). And there *are* things you can do to improve your situation in 2025 and beyond.

> AI for AI's sake didn't work, so let's return to our core principles of building things that people actually want and need.

> UX is messy. It can't be placed neatly in boxes or approached in the same exact way every time.
