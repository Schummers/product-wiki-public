# Transactional Notifications: Their Characteristics and When to Use Them

## Summary

Transactional notifications (SMS and push notifications) are increasingly popular for communicating with customers about orders, account changes, and service updates, especially as mobile device usage grows. Unlike marketing notifications intended to persuade, transactional notifications convey information about existing orders or customer-company relationships. Based on two longitudinal diary studies with respondents from four countries, this article distinguishes transactional notifications from transactional emails, compares SMS to push notifications, and provides guidance on when to use each channel.

Transactional notifications and emails share best practices: clear subject lines, concise information, and all necessary details. However, notifications have stricter constraints: push notifications are limited to 50-240 characters with minimal formatting, and users expect them to be more urgent and concise than emails. SMS is more durable and suitable for detailed information users may need later, while push notifications are better for nonurgent reminders and suggestions to return to an app. Cultural factors influence preferences; Chinese users historically favor SMS, while Western users prefer email. The article emphasizes that notifications should be reserved for genuinely urgent, time-sensitive content and that users should easily opt out from specific channels.

## Key Takeaways

- **Both notifications and emails need clear subject lines and concise information** — headlines must summarize purpose and content; concise messages indicate that the company respects users' time.
- **Notifications require stricter conciseness than emails** — push notifications are limited to 50-240 characters; frontload key information early as users scan only the first few words.
- **Notifications must be timely and reserved for urgent content** — users expect SMS and push notifications for time-sensitive information (deliveries, payment alerts); extraneous notifications cause frustration.
- **SMS is better for information users need to retain or act on quickly** — SMS is stable, durable, and suitable for pickup codes, delivery details, order changes, and confirmation requests.
- **Push notifications suit nonurgent app-specific content** — push notifications are better for reminders to return to an app (abandoned cart), suggestions, or nonurgent status updates; they allow users to opt out per app.

## Quotes

## Concepts

- [[Notification Design]] — transactional notifications must be concise, timely, and carry essential information only; both SMS and push notifications have specific use cases and require different strategies.
- [[Mobile Communication]] — cultural factors influence channel preferences (SMS dominates in China, email in the West); effective communication requires understanding audience expectations and preferences.
- [[E-Commerce]] — ecommerce notifications benefit users most when they contain order details, delivery information, and status changes; users appreciate options to opt out from specific channels.
- [[User Expectations]] — users expect SMS for urgent, time-sensitive, detailed information; push notifications for nonurgent suggestions and reminders; both channels need easy unsubscribe options.
