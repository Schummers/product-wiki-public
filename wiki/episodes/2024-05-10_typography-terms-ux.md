# Typography Terms: Glossary

## Summary

This comprehensive glossary defines the technical and conceptual vocabulary essential to typography in digital design. It covers the anatomy of type (ascenders, descenders, baselines), font properties (weight, style, size), spacing concepts (kerning, leading, letter spacing), and design elements (serifs, ligatures, monospaced fonts). Understanding these terms allows designers to communicate precisely with teams and make informed decisions that improve both legibility and visual polish of interfaces.

## Key Takeaways

- **Font vs. typeface distinction** — a typeface (or font family) is the group to which fonts belong; a font is the specific combination of typeface, size, style, and weight.
- **Kerning and letter spacing are different** — kerning adjusts space between specific character pairs, while letter spacing adjusts space between all characters uniformly.
- **Baseline and x-height define proportions** — these measurements establish how proportions of a typeface appear at the same font size compared to others.
- **Leading is measured baseline-to-baseline** — proper line spacing (leading) improves readability and should account for the specific typeface and size.
- **Serif vs. sans-serif affects tone** — serif fonts carry traditional, warm connotations while sans-serif fonts suggest modern and impersonal tone, influencing brand perception.
- **Web-safe fonts matter for accessibility** — non-web-safe fonts may not render correctly and cannot be read by screen readers, affecting accessibility, SEO, and usability.
