# Guidelines for Testing Mobile Augmented-Reality Apps

## Summary

Augmented-reality app testing shares foundations with mobile usability testing but requires additional considerations due to spatial interaction, physical movement, participant safety, and unfamiliarity with AR technology. Planning AR studies requires clear task wording, ample space to prevent hazards, longer session durations due to app download and AR complexity, recording equipment capturing both phone screen and room movement, careful participant screening for health conditions, informed consent about physical activities, and patience with participants unfamiliar with AR patterns. The article covers seven key areas: task design, study setting, session duration, recording equipment, participant selection, informed consent, and session conduct. Critical differentiators include allocating longer time for downloads and learning, recording participant movement with external cameras, communicating with wearable microphones, and monitoring for signs of discomfort during physically demanding tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Clear task wording is essential** — AR tasks should be straightforward and unambiguous; participants new to AR technology easily misunderstand tasks (e.g., "take a photo of animals" could mean real pets instead of virtual ones), requiring clear facilitation and patience.
- **Space and safety matter** — Allocate ample space to prevent hazards like furniture, sharp objects, or slippery surfaces; participants focused on AR objects disregard surroundings, creating safety risks; outdoor activities may require outdoor settings to function properly.
- **Longer sessions are necessary** — Allow extra time for app downloads, AR technology learning, and participant unfamiliarity; consider having participants download apps 15-20 minutes before sessions or starting downloads at session opening to fill the time productively.
- **Record movement plus screen** — Document both phone screen (via Zoom or similar) and participant movement in the room (via tripod-mounted camera) to capture interaction with physical environment; use wearable microphones for audio clarity when participants are far from cameras.
- **Screen for health and ability** — Participant age, health conditions, physical ability, and ability to wear glasses during physical activities affect participation; some AR apps are fitness games requiring substantial exertion, creating potential health risks.
- **Communicate expectations in advance** — Inform participants about physical activities, whether surroundings will be recorded, space requirements, and ability to withdraw; remind participants to charge phones before sessions to prevent tripping hazards.

## Quotes

> Be calm and patient with the participants — even more so during an AR study than in a regular user test. Participants are often new to the world of AR and might not be familiar with AR patterns. This unfamiliarity will make some of the tasks confusing to them.

> A significant part of this process is understanding your participants and putting yourself in their shoes, as many will not be familiar with AR technologies or may not be physically able to perform certain activities Explain your tasks clearly, using words that they can understand.

> While conducting an AR-usability study can seem a complex process at first glance, with proper planning and preparation you can achieve your research goals.

## Concepts

- [[Usability Testing]] — AR testing adds complexity to standard mobile usability testing through spatial interaction, movement, and AR-specific learning curves.
- [[Mobile Testing]] — AR apps on mobile devices present unique challenges including screen recording, participant movement, safety hazards, and audio recording when participants are far from equipment.
- [[Augmented Reality]] — Users are often unfamiliar with AR technology and patterns; tasks must be clear, sessions require extra time for learning, and testing environment must be safe for movement.
- [[Remote Research]] — Remote AR testing requires communication about space requirements, recording of surroundings with participant consent, and ability to help participants troubleshoot AR features remotely.
- [[Accessibility]] — AR health requirements should be screened in advance; participants should know whether they'll need glasses, how much movement is involved, and whether they can withdraw.
