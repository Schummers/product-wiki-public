# The 10x Method: Brand and Comms

## Summary

Chapter 7 argues that brand matters from day one and becomes your company's sustainable differentiator as product capabilities converge. Rather than treating brand as a luxury for later, the chapter shows founders how to build intentional early-stage brand through visual identity, voice, messaging, and communication channels. It covers defining your brand elements (vision, mission, benefits, values), translating them into a distinctive brand feeling and voice, and bringing them to life through website copy, visual identity, founder personal branding, and PR. The chapter emphasizes that founders do not need large budgets to establish strong brand, and that consistency of messaging across channels — anchored in a core emotional feeling — is what makes early-stage startups stand out and memorable.

## Key Takeaways

- **You already have a brand whether you've designed it or not** — Customers, investors, and partners form impressions from your landing page, pitch deck, LinkedIn bio, and job posts. The question is whether those signals are intentional and consistent or scattered and generic. Early clarity on brand prevents mixed messages.
- **Brand is the sustainable moat as product quality evens out** — With AI and faster development tools, product copying is easier than ever. As functional differences shrink, it is brand feeling—the emotional response people have to your company—that drives choice. Founders should invest in brand clarity early because it compounds over time.
- **Document your core brand elements to force clarity** — Write down your vision, mission, target users, key benefits, and company values in one place. Refer back to this document to keep landing pages, hiring, positioning, and strategic decisions aligned with a single narrative thread.
- **Translate brand elements into feeling and voice** — Define the emotional response you want (liberation, empowerment, quiet confidence) and choose 2-3 adjectives describing how your brand should sound to evoke it. This guides everything from website copy to customer support responses.
- **DIY most of the early brand** — Hexa says you can do 90-100% of your early brand yourself: a simple logo and a basic palette in Figma or Canva, 1-2 signature colours, a clean font, applied consistently. With a small budget (even under 5,000 euros) a freelance designer or a small studio agency can produce a logo, a basic graphic system and a lightweight style guide; large agencies are for later, when scale calls for strategic work. Separately, a PR agency is said to be a poor fit before Series A.
- **Founder personal brand is your most powerful early channel** — When your company is unknown, your visibility as founder is the entry point. Build authentic presence where your audience already is (LinkedIn, Twitter, podcasts, blogs), stay consistent, and diversify opportunistically through earned media. Founder story—why you started, what problem you experienced—humanizes your company.
- **PR shapes perception and builds credibility before product traction** — Early PR is not about advertising, it is about helping journalists tell stories that matter to their audiences. Build relationships before you need them, position yourself as an expert in your space, and pitch stories grounded in broader industry trends, not just your product.

## Quotes

> What matters most is that there's a genuine alignment between who you are and how you express yourself. That authenticity is what creates real connection.

> Most founders don't think much about brand in the early days. It doesn't feel urgent - building, shipping, and fundraising take priority. Brand can seem like something for later, once everything else is in place. But you already have a brand, whether you've thought about it or not.

> As product quality evens out, it'll be your brand - and the people behind it - that makes people choose you over a competitor.

## Concepts

- [[Branding]] — Early-stage brand is about defining core elements (vision, mission, benefits, values), translating them into a distinctive emotional feeling and voice, and bringing them to life consistently across website, visual identity, and messaging. Founders can DIY or work with affordable freelancers; consistency and emotional resonance matter more than polish.
- [[Public Relations]] — PR shapes long-term perception and builds credibility by positioning founders as expert voices in their industry. Rather than hiring agencies, founders should build direct journalist relationships, pitch stories tied to broader trends, provide journalists with complete assets, and only pursue coverage when truly newsworthy.
- [[Founder Mindset]] — A founder's personal brand and story are among the company's most valuable early assets. Authentic presence on one primary channel (LinkedIn, Twitter, blogging, podcasting) built through consistent effort compounds over time. The emotional core of your founder story should connect to your company's brand feeling while remaining genuine.
