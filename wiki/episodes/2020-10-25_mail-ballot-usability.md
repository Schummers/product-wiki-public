# Vote By Mail: Mistakes Are Too Easy

## Summary

This article examines usability and service design issues in Massachusetts' vote-by-mail materials. The author identifies several design problems in the ballot packet and instructions that make it easy for voters to make mistakes, leading to ballot rejection. The article calls for greater UX involvement in voting system design and highlights how poor design can have serious real-world consequences including invalid votes and unnecessary person-to-person contact during a pandemic.

## Key Takeaways

- **Multiple envelope confusion** — The packet requires voters to place one envelope inside another, which is unusual and not clearly explained, leading to confusion about which envelope to sign and how to assemble the materials.
- **Instructions lack visual support** — The instructions contain excessive text without images, making key steps hard to find, and important information appears in locations that suggest it is not critical (like a footer).
- **Design issues have real consequences** — Nearly 18,000 votes were rejected in Massachusetts in October due to poor design, resulting in wasted votes and potential COVID-19 exposure when voters felt compelled to vote in person instead.
- **Clear labeling is essential** — Instructions refer to colors (yellow envelope, white envelope) rather than using clear labels or numbers that voters could match with each step, increasing error likelihood.
- **UX expertise lacking in government** — Despite Massachusetts hosting many UX programs and design firms, government agencies do not invest in professional UX help for critical processes like voting.

## Quotes

> A piece of paper is more than tree pulp. It can hold the key to something as important as an election. Small usability issues can be magnified by the complexity inherent in interactions that involve multiple pieces of paper which cross-reference each other.

> Some of these very issues have already caused nearly 18,000 votes to be trashed in Massachusetts in October, according to an [article by Channel 10 Boston](https://www.nbcboston.com/investigations/voting-by-mail-avoid-these-ballot-mistakes-in-massachusetts/2208553/).

> Where are all the UX designers and researchers, service designers, and content writers and editors when voting process and materials are designed? **Not there or constrained or beaten by bureaucracy, laws, and deadlines?**

## Concepts

- [[Usability Testing]] — evaluating real voting materials to uncover design problems that could lead to user errors and invalid ballots, showing how critical testing is for high-stakes transactions.
- [[Service Design]] — designing the entire voting experience including physical materials, instructions, and processes across multiple touchpoints to ensure voters can complete their tasks without making critical errors.
- [[Error Prevention]] — anticipating where voters might make mistakes in a complex multi-step process and designing clear instructions, labels, and visual cues to prevent them from occurring.
