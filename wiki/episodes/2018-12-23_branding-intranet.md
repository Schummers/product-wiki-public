# Branding an Intranet

## Summary

This article addresses weak or nonexistent intranet branding, which leads to poor credibility, adoption, and employee acceptance. Intranets are often created by IT, HR, or Communications departments without branding expertise, resulting in uninviting experiences. The article focuses on three high-impact branding elements: the intranet's name, logo, and visual relationship with the organization's public-facing website.

## Key Takeaways

- **Intranet names should be memorable and pronounceable** — Intranet names serve two functions: conveying identity and providing a reference term. Clever acronyms, uncommon words, or wordplay often backfire, creating confusion about pronunciation and spelling. Simple, straightforward names (Portal, Center, Inside [Company], [Company] Hub) work reliably, though crafty names like JetNet can succeed if tested and can last for years.

- **Logo should include or accompany intranet name** — Effective logos often display just the intranet's name with no other fanfare. Using the company logo alongside the intranet name differentiates the intranet from the public site while maintaining brand connection. Using only the company logo can confuse users about whether they're on the intranet or public site.

- **Intranet can have different tone than public site** — While colors and fonts may adhere to corporate guidelines, the intranet can maintain a different tone of voice tailored to employees' needs and demographics. Professional external messaging need not match employee-facing communication style.

- **Avoid overbranding pitfalls** — Don't brand interface features with catchy names (e.g., "Upload Wizard" instead of "Upload a Document"). Don't use third-party software brand names as UI labels (use "Job Postings" instead of "SAS"). These practices create vagueness that obscures functionality.

- **Intranet naming enables future changes** — Getting employees to call an intranet by a new name is difficult; they continue using the old name even after redesigns. Choose a name the organization can live with long-term, though renaming during significant redesigns can signal transformation to employees.

## Quotes

## Concepts

- [[Intranet Design]] — Designing internal organizational digital spaces requires attention to naming, branding, and tone distinct from public-facing sites, balancing corporate identity with employee-focused communication.

- [[Branding]] — Establishing visual and verbal identity through strategic naming, logo design, and consistent visual language, adapted to different audiences (employees versus external customers).

- [[Information Architecture]] — Clear labeling and organization of intranet features using descriptive language rather than branded terms ensures discoverability and reduces cognitive load for employees seeking information.
