# User-Experience Quiz: 2017 UX Year in Review

## Summary

This article presents a 10-question quiz covering major UX concepts and research findings published by Nielsen Norman Group in 2017. The quiz covers diverse topics including the aesthetic-usability effect (the tendency for people to perceive attractive products as more usable), microcontent (small content elements like headlines and taglines), false-consensus bias, the difference between customer-journey maps and experience maps, exhaustive-review eye-movement patterns, service blueprints, quantitative research characteristics, eye-fixation patterns on webpages, sliders as UI controls, and tree testing. The quiz assesses readers' depth of UX knowledge through a scoring rubric ranging from 0 (study more) to 10 (send your resume). It serves as both a self-assessment tool and a pointer to articles where readers can deepen their understanding of core UX concepts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Aesthetic-Usability Effect** — People tend to perceive attractive products as more usable, even when usability is identical; aesthetics and usability are distinct dimensions but perception conflates them.
- **Microcontent Definition** — Microcontent includes page titles, headlines, and taglines but excludes body text; these small elements play outsized roles in helping users quickly understand content and decide whether to engage.
- **False-Consensus Bias** — People tend to assume others share their beliefs and responses; designers are prone to assuming their favorite patterns are more widespread than they actually are.
- **Journey Maps vs. Experience Maps** — Customer-journey maps focus on specific products and are chronological; experience maps are product-agnostic and explore a broader context, not necessarily in sequence.
- **Exhaustive-Review Eye Movement** — Eyes return repeatedly to areas where users expect content to be, based on mental models and prior experience; this behavior reflects how expectations guide attention.
- **Research Methodology Differences** — Quantitative research requires larger samples, controlled conditions, and statistical analysis; qualitative research allows flexibility, small sample sizes, and exploratory approaches.

## Concepts

- [[Aesthetic-Usability Effect]] — The psychological tendency for people to perceive visually attractive products as more usable than less attractive products, even when actual usability is identical.
- [[UX Writing]] — Small, discrete content elements like page titles, headlines, and taglines that guide user understanding; disproportionately important for usability despite their size.
- [[Eye Tracking]] — Research method measuring where users look on a page; reveals patterns like exhaustive review (repeated fixation on expected areas) and hot-potato scanning (avoidance of certain areas).
- [[Service Blueprinting]] — Visualization tool showing how a service is delivered, including customer actions, employee actions (frontstage and backstage), and support processes; used for service design optimization.
- [[Tree Testing]] — Research method for evaluating information architecture by testing users' ability to navigate a text-only hierarchy before adding visual design; reveals IA effectiveness independent of visual factors.
