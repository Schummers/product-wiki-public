# Venture Studios Demystified: Studio Structure

## Summary

Part 3b of Venture Studios Demystified examines the structural choices studios make when establishing legal entities for studio operations and fund management. The chapter documents a migration from single-entity studio models toward dual-entity models, where studios establish separate legal entities for an investment fund and an operating company. This structural decision has significant implications for employee compensation, fundraising dynamics, and how studios deploy capital across their portfolio companies.

The chapter explains that dual-entity models enable studios to separate the economics of venture building (operating company receiving common equity in portfolio companies) from fund investing (fund holding preferred equity from Seed and later rounds). This allows studio partners to participate in both streams of economic upside while addressing the practical challenge that a single entity funded only by management fees cannot support high upfront development costs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Dual-Entity Model Prevalence** — Most studios observed migrated from single entities to dual-entity structures by raising VC funds. The dual-entity model separates the operating company (receiving common/founder shares) from the investment fund (holding preferred equity), creating distinct economic profiles.
- **Partner Double-Dipping** — A particularly appealing trend for studio partners is the ability to participate in economic upside from both the operating company (through founder equity stakes in portfolio companies) and the investment fund (through carry and management fees), making the model potentially more lucrative than traditional VC.
- **Development Cost Funding** — The operating company's budget covers early ideation, research, validation, and testing costs, funded through a mix of capital raises, external revenue, and a portion of fund management fees. The company receives common founder shares in exchange for this pre-Seed development work.
- **External Funding Strategy** — The majority of studios only bring in external VCs to lead at Series A, preserving cheap founder equity for the studio until market validation. Some early-stage studios bring in external capital earlier to signal validation to the market.
- **Fund Ownership in Studio** — Studios must decide whether the investment fund will hold equity in the operating company. Fund ownership aligns LP incentives with studio operations but makes the fund profile less similar to traditional VC, potentially complicating fundraising.
- **Single Entity Limitations** — Single fund entity models require exceptionally large fund sizes ($100M-200M) to cover both operations and investments from a 2% management fee structure, making this approach more difficult for first-time studio founders despite its structural simplicity.
