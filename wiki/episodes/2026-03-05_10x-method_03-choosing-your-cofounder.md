# The 10x Method: Choosing Your Cofounder

## Summary

Chapter 3 addresses cofounder selection, arguing that finding the right cofounder is essential for most software startups and that cofounder issues are one of the two most common causes of startup failure. The chapter identifies three key ingredients for successful cofounder relationships: complementary skills and personalities, alignment on five non-negotiable values (long-term vision, ambition, commitment, ways of working, and culture), and transparency, trust, and communication. It explores how to find cofounders through personal networks and targeted searches, how to evaluate them through multiple conversations, working together, and reference checks, and how to maintain healthy partnerships through clear roles, fair equity, coaching, and regular realignment on values.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cofounders are essential** — Having a cofounder increases success odds because they fill skill gaps (you cannot be great at everything), provide emotional support during lonely and hard times, and help catch mistakes and course-correct faster. For software companies, you need both technical and business expertise at the founding level to make critical early decisions about architecture, scalability, and go-to-market.
- **Two is often optimal** — A single technical cofounder and a business-focused cofounder is the sweet spot for most B2B software companies. Three can work for operationally complex or regulated businesses where operations or compliance are critical, but going beyond three rarely works and typically slows decision-making and blurs accountability.
- **Three core ingredients** — Successful cofounder relationships require complementary skills and personalities that balance each other, alignment on five non-negotiable values (long-term vision, ambition, commitment, ways of working, culture), and transparency and trust maintained through regular communication to prevent misalignment from creeping in slowly.
- **Treat cofounder search like sales** — Expect to talk to 20+ people over 2–3 months, spending 2+ hours daily on the search. Make your project discoverable through social posts. Look for entrepreneurial intent signals: founder title (especially ex-founder), stealth startup, Chief of Staff, CTO as a service, or Fractional CTO.
- **Deep vetting prevents breakups** — Have at least three conversations (including an informal one like a walk or dinner). Spend a week building together on a short proof-of-concept. Get a second opinion from someone in their domain. Run references from two managers and two direct reports. Don't rush the process.
- **Establish clarity and rituals** — Define roles and ownership clearly using a skills-by-passion matrix. Keep equity splits equal when cofounders join simultaneously and contribute equally; it reinforces accountability and prevents power dynamic erosion. Use regular rituals (weekly check-ins, quarterly value realignment) to catch misalignment early before it compounds.

## Quotes

> We're radically transparent and never hold back. To outsiders, it can look like we're arguing but what they're seeing is intensity paired with deep respect. We'll say things like, 'No, that's nonsense, here's how we fix it,' and move forward without ego. That combination of honesty, challenge, and mutual care is why we make better decisions together.

> The strongest cofounder relationships are built on radical complementarity, so clear and natural that there's never any doubt about who takes what. In 15 years, with my cofounder Thibaud, we've never had to ask who's taking the lead on something. That clarity comes from deep mutual respect and a shared understanding of each other's strengths. Emotional complementarity matters just as much: when one of us is down, the other steps in. That kind of relay builds real resilience.

> I think the key step is to have an extremely open and honest conversation right from the start to align expectations: What is everyone's ambition? Will we still be aligned three years from now? If someone wants to leave, what happens? It might feel a bit uncomfortable or overly formal at first, but it's critical to tackle the difficult questions on day one. In my view, this is one of the healthiest practices you can adopt when starting a company.

## Concepts

- [[Cofounder Selection]] — Finding and evaluating cofounders requires treating it as a structured sales funnel: talk to 20+ people over 2–3 months, evaluate complementary skills and personality fit, assess alignment on five non-negotiable values, look for entrepreneurial intent signals in existing networks and targeted searches, run at least three conversations and a week-long build together, get outside validation, and run formal references.
- [[Operating Rituals]] — Regular rituals (weekly check-ins, quarterly value realignment, weekly self-assessments where each cofounder shares what went well, what could improve, feedback needs, and worries) create intentional moments of communication that prevent misalignment from developing silently and maintain relationship health under pressure.
- [[Team Compensation]] — Equity splits matter for founder motivation and accountability. Equal splits tend to work better when cofounders join simultaneously and contribute equally, as they reinforce mutual accountability and signal equal weight. Unequal splits can introduce subtle power dynamics where one founder may feel like an early hire rather than a true partner.
