# Foundational UX Workshop Activities

## Summary

This article presents seven foundational workshop activities that serve as building blocks for almost any UX workshop exercise. These core activities — post up, affinity diagramming, landscape mapping, storyboarding, forced ranking, role playing, and playback — can be combined and remixed to create diverse workshop experiences. Understanding and mastering these fundamental techniques enables facilitators to respond flexibly to different contexts and goals.

## Key Takeaways

- **Post Up** — the most fundamental and flexible workshop activity where participants individually generate content on sticky notes and post them on a wall for discussion and use in further exercises.
- **Affinity Diagramming** — clustering information (often from a postup) into relational groups based on similarities or themes to discover patterns and promote shared language among participants.
- **Landscape Mapping** — arranging groups of similar content into a preassigned structure to understand relationships and patterns across items, groups, and time.
- **Storyboarding** — communicating ideas or user scenarios through a sequence of illustrated panels to provide context and help teams understand user behavior holistically.
- **Forced Ranking** — any collaborative prioritization activity that weighs items directly against each other to create a strict order and achieve consensus.
- **Role Playing** — acting out another perspective or system to challenge assumptions and force participants to change how they think about a problem.
- **Playback** — sharing progress, process, or insights gained by individuals or subgroups to the larger group to ensure alignment and shared language across all participants.

## Quotes

> **Postup:** An activity in which participants individually generate content on sticky notes, then post them up on a wall. Contributions are then discussed, captured, shared, or used as input for a future exercise.

> **Affinity diagramming:** is the clustering of information, often sticky notes, into relational groups based on similarities or themes.

> By first working independently on a problem, then converging to share insights, teams can leverage progress made by others. Playbacks help converge insights across teams so that everyone within a workshop has an understanding of the outputs and the work of other teams.
