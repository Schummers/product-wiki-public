# How Practitioners Create Journey Maps: Typical Uses, Roles, and Methods

## Summary

A survey of 343 practitioners examined how journey maps are created in practice across different organizational contexts and methodologies. The research reveals common patterns: journey maps are most frequently used to evaluate existing products or envision future states, with active usage and end-to-end customer journeys being the most common focus areas. Most teams employ a hypothesis-first approach, relying on existing team knowledge, and use user interviews as their primary research method. Journey mapping is highly collaborative, involving multidisciplinary teams and using both physical and digital tools.

## Key Takeaways

- **Journey maps focus on existing experiences** — 89% of practitioners use journey maps to evaluate existing products/services, 73% for future-state envisioning; 61% also map new, potential products or services.
- **Active product usage most common** — 71% map end-to-end customer journeys or active product usage; support experiences receive least attention (26%), suggesting opportunity for improvement.
- **Hypothesis-first approach dominates** — 62% begin with a workshop-based hypothesis-first approach using existing team knowledge, while 35% conduct upfront research; both approaches are valid depending on context and goals.
- **User interviews are the primary method** — 86% use interviews for external research and 76% for stakeholder research; diary studies are underutilized (12% external, 6% internal) despite their suitability for understanding journeys over time.
- **Highly collaborative process** — 70% involve product, 46% marketing, and 42% customer service; 64% create maps collaboratively using physical (34%) or digital tools (30%), with 36% working solo.
- **Personas and phases identified at different times** — 49% use existing personas; 27% develop personas during research and 14% during workshops; most (62%) identify journey phases during research or workshops rather than at project start.

## Quotes

> "Journey mapping can be used as a process to evaluate and optimize existing products or services or as a visioning exercise for future-state experiences"

> "Diary studies are a particularly useful method for understanding users' thoughts, feelings, and actions over time."

## Concepts

- [[Journey Mapping]] — Journey maps are used flexibly across organizations to understand existing products, envision future states, or explore new opportunities; both hypothesis-first and research-first approaches are common.
- [[Research Methods]] — User interviews are heavily relied upon; diary studies, though underutilized, are well-suited to journey-mapping research given journeys occur over time and across channels.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Journey mapping is inherently collaborative, involving cross-functional teams (product, marketing, support); collaboration happens both in workshops and through digital tools.
- [[Persona]] — Most journey maps reference existing personas; some develop personas specifically for the mapping project while others identify personas during the mapping process itself.
