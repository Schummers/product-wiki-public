# Five Mistakes in Designing Mobile Push Notifications

## Summary

Mobile push notifications can effectively inform users and encourage app engagement, but poor implementation can frustrate users and drive app deletion. With users receiving an average of 56 notifications per day, proper design is critical. The article identifies five common mistakes: asking for notification permission immediately upon app launch before users understand value, failing to explain what notifications will contain, sending bursts of notifications, sharing irrelevant content, and making it difficult to turn off notifications. Each mistake is illustrated with specific examples of successful and unsuccessful implementations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Don't ask for notification permission on first launch** — Users don't trust new apps and typically decline without understanding value; instead, allow users to experience the app first and only ask in later sessions using the reciprocity principle.

- **Clearly communicate what notifications will be about** — Generic system prompts offer no information; specifying notification content types helps users make informed decisions and increases perceived credibility and transparency.

- **Avoid burst notifications in short timeframes** — Multiple notifications in sequence overwhelm users, appear sloppy, and prompt deletion of the app; instead, combine related notifications into single messages for quality over quantity.

- **Send only relevant content to individual users** — Irrelevant interruptions are especially frustrating when users feel they must clear notifications; personalization is critical since most users won't customize default notification settings.

- **Make disabling notifications easy and obvious** — Hiding notification settings in global device settings rather than within the app decreases user trust; settings should be easily accessible within the app's dedicated settings area.

- **Balance business interests with user respect** — While retention matters, making users feel manipulated through intrusive design backfires; respecting user autonomy builds long-term trust and loyalty.

## Quotes

> When properly executed, mobile push notifications can provide users with relevant information to encourage them to engage with an app. However, when poorly implemented, notifications can deter users from interacting and, in severe circumstances, can prompt them to delete an app altogether.

> In 2016, the average mobile phone user received 56 notifications per day according to a study from Telefonica Research in Spain [Pielot 2018].

> Notifications are disruptive and annoy users when they are irrelevant. Don't risk inciting negative sentiment.
