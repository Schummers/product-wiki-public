# Mobile Microsessions

## Summary

Mobile microsessions are sessions shorter than 15 seconds where users complete specific tasks quickly. Research shows that over 40% of mobile usage consists of microsessions. Designing for microsessions involves enabling quick task completion through low interaction cost and can benefit even apps with complex tasks. Four external entry point strategies allow users to bypass app launching: notifications, widgets, quick actions, and voice assistants, each enabling faster access to frequent tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Microsessions benefit user experience** — time on task inversely correlates with usability; allowing task completion in microsessions means users reached their goal quickly, likely due to good design support.
- **Even complex apps benefit** — while not all tasks can be completed in 15 seconds, reducing time to complete any task improves experience; external entry points can help users bypass app launching to access frequent tasks.
- **Self-sufficient notifications key** — notifications must convey fully formed ideas without requiring users to launch the app; truncated or incomplete notification text forces information gathering and degrades experience.
- **Widgets enable quick data inspection** — widgets allow users to quickly check data from their homescreen without launching apps; must be self-contained and not truncated like notifications.
- **Quick actions target frequent tasks** — long press or 3D Touch reveals quick actions directly on homescreen; should include top tasks performed often by users, not rarely used features.
- **Support for microsessions is easy to implement** — despite low usage adoption rates for widgets and quick actions, ROI remains high since implementation requires minimal effort; voice-assistant integration offers similar low-cost opportunities.

## Quotes

> Have you ever briefly turned on your phone, glanced at it, found whatever needed to find, and then turned it back off? That's an example of a microsession — a quick session with minimal interaction that completes a user goal.

> Microsessions are good for the user experience. Generally, time on task is inversely proportional with usability. Time translates in interaction cost, and low interaction cost leads to good user experience.

> Consider supporting the main actions that apply to the notification item within the notification itself. Doing so presents the user with the opportunity to complete the task without launching the app.
