# Affinity Diagramming for Collaboratively Sorting UX Findings and Design Ideas

## Summary

Affinity diagramming is a collaborative mapping activity where teams organize related observations, ideas, concepts, or findings into distinct clusters. Used across various UX stages from initial research through design refinement, the method follows three steps: generating ideas as sticky notes, organizing notes into thematic clusters, and prioritizing clusters for action. This approach leverages divergent thinking (individual idea generation) followed by convergent discussion (collaborative organization), promoting team alignment and enabling robust design ideas. The journey through discussion is more valuable than the final diagram format.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three-step process** — generate ideas independently as sticky notes, organize notes into thematic clusters, prioritize clusters through voting or discussion.
- **Divergent start** — have individual team members generate ideas independently on sticky notes to avoid bias from dominant participants; this typically takes 5-10 minutes.
- **Collaborative organization** — team members collaboratively group similar sticky notes, staying open-minded about different interpretations; small clusters hold value even if they contain few items.
- **Prioritization outcomes** — use techniques like dot voting to rank clusters; document action items, next steps, and designated point persons to ensure follow-through.
- **Discussion value** — the discussions occurring while building the diagram are more impactful than the final format; team alignment and shared understanding are the primary benefits.

## Quotes

> Affinity diagramming refers to organizing related observations, ideas, concepts, or findings into distinct clusters.

> As with any mapping activity, the journey is more important than the destination: the discussions that occurred while building the diagram are more impactful than the final format of the diagram.

> Don't try to force ideas together. Group sticky notes that reference similar ideas or thoughts and give each cluster a clear label representing all included sticky notes.
