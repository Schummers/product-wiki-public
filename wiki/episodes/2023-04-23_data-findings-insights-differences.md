# Data vs. Findings vs. Insights: The Differences Explained

## Summary

Data, findings, and insights represent three distinct levels of research analysis, each with increasing value and actionability. Raw data consists of unanalyzed observations and measurements from research activities. Findings emerge when researchers identify patterns in that data but lack broader context. Insights go furthest by explaining observed patterns and connecting them to business opportunities and user needs through analysis. Understanding these distinctions is crucial for teams to move beyond raw observations toward research that actually informs decision-making and design improvements.

## Key Takeaways

- **Data lacks significance alone** — Data points by themselves have no meaning; they require analysis to become useful information for decision-making.
- **Findings describe patterns, not causes** — Findings show what patterns exist in the data but do not explain why or how to act on them without additional context.
- **Insights tie patterns to opportunities** — Insights connect observed patterns to user needs and business objectives, making them actionable and valuable for design recommendations.
- **Context transforms findings into insights** — Adding background, past research, and organizational factors allows researchers to interpret findings and derive meaningful insights.
- **Triangulation mitigates bias** — Using multiple data sources, analysis approaches, and researchers reduces the chance that one researcher's bias leads to faulty insights.
- **Action requires insights** — Teams cannot make confident decisions based on findings alone; they need insights that connect research to business impact and user value.

## Quotes

> Data are simply a collection of data points which lack significance individually. As soon as researchers start to do any level of analysis on these data points, we have information.

> Insights are what researchers should strive to create.

> Interpreting findings in context yields insights.

## Concepts

- [[Data Analysis]] — The process of examining collected observations and measurements to identify patterns, requiring different techniques for qualitative versus quantitative data.
- [[Research Methods]] — Approaches like thematic analysis for qualitative data and statistics for quantitative data that help researchers move from raw data to meaningful patterns.
- [[Insight Generation]] — The final analytical level that explains observed patterns and connects them to business context and user needs, enabling actionable recommendations.
