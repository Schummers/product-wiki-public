# Crafting AI Explanations for Every Role in Your Enterprise

## Summary

This article addresses how enterprise AI systems require different types of explainability for different stakeholders, since various roles have different goals, expertise, and contexts. Unlike consumer AI explainability guidance, enterprise AI systems affect livelihoods, data, and organizational risk, requiring explanations calibrated to the specific job each role performs. The article identifies three enterprise roles—AI consultants and governance leads, builders, and domain experts—and describes what explanations each needs to understand and trust AI systems before deployment. The article emphasizes that trust is a prerequisite for adoption in enterprise settings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Explainability is role-specific, not one-size-fits-all** — AI consultants and governance leads need global, system-level explanations showing patterns and risks; builders need local explanations tied to specific inputs and configuration choices; domain experts need plain-language explanations grounded in familiar workflows.
- **AI consultants and governance leads need governance-level transparency** — This role requires global explanations about patterns across deployments, audit trails, compliance documentation, and visibility into where human oversight is still needed, not single-instance explanations.
- **Builders need local, interactive, actionable explanations** — Developers need to see why specific outputs occurred, how configuration changes affect behavior, interpretable evaluation metrics, and example-based explanations that help them develop intuition about AI's probabilistic nature.
- **Domain experts need plain language and familiar precedent** — Subject-matter experts who review outputs don't need to understand model internals; they need explanations tied to policies, workflows, and comparable past cases, plus lightweight feedback mechanisms.
- **Trust requires transparency across three dimensions** — All roles need answers to the same questions—Is this fair? Does it reflect data I trust? Can I defend it?—but the explanations differ by role, moment, and stakes.
- **Explainability creates shared control** — When users can question results, compare alternatives, or test changes, they become active participants in decision-making rather than passive recipients, which encourages adoption and responsible oversight.

## Quotes

> "AI explainability is the degree to which an AI system's decisions are understandable to humans. It helps users see how and why an outcome was reached."

> "In enterprise settings, explainability is how trust is built with the people responsible for approving, designing, deploying, and maintaining AI solutions."

> "trust a prerequisite for adoption, especially when AI systems influence customer outcomes, employee work, or compliance decisions. When users don't understand why the AI system made a decision, they may override it, ignore it, stop using the system altogether because the reasoning feels too opaque and the stakes are too high."

## Concepts

- [[Explainability]] — enables enterprise users across different roles to understand AI systems' decisions and behavior, which is essential for building trust and supporting adoption.
- [[Enterprise AI]] — requires explainability tailored to governance, technical, and domain-expert roles, each with different needs and levels of technical expertise.
- [[User Trust]] — is built through calibrated, role-specific explanations that address fairness, data integrity, and accountability concerns before deployment.
