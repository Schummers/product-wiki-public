# First Impressions Matter: How Designers Can Support Humans' Automatic Cognitive Processing

## Summary

Humans process information through two systems: System 1 (automatic, fast, pattern-based) and System 2 (deliberate, slow, effortful). System 1 operates largely outside conscious awareness, making snap judgments about relevance, aesthetics, and credibility based on initial visual cues. Within 50 milliseconds of landing on a page, users decide whether a site is beautiful—a judgment that rarely changes with more time. Designers can support System 1 by visually prioritizing information relevant to user workflows, maintaining visual simplicity, and clearly stating who they are and what they do. Testing with real users reveals whether first impressions align with design intent.

## Key Takeaways

- **System 1 makes rapid judgments about information relevance** — users quickly scan for relevant information using visual cues like color, size, and position; irrelevant-looking information is ignored even if it's useful.
- **Aesthetics are judged in 50 milliseconds** — decisions about beauty are made instantly and rarely change, influencing whether users perceive the site as usable and trustworthy.
- **Prominent elements significantly impact credibility** — per BJ Fogg's prominence-interpretation theory, salient design elements (positive or negative) shape overall credibility; a distracting ad diminishes trust while an endorsement enhances it.
- **Visual design should support user workflows** — identify what each persona needs at each stage and ensure relevant information looks important through visual hierarchy, contrast, and positioning.
- **Clear identity and value proposition prevent cognitive strain** — stating who you are and what you do upfront reduces the effort users need to invest to determine relevance, preventing suspicion and abandonment.

## Quotes

> People tend to be more forgiving of beautiful designs and less so of visually jarring ones. If a website is aesthetically pleasing, users will tend to remember the site as more usable than it actually was.

> The more prominent an element is on a page, the higher its impact will be on the overall credibility of the site.

> If users have to invoke System 2 to figure out whether they're in the right place, the increased cognitive effort will cause them to scrutinize and be suspicious of the site.

## Concepts

- [[Cognitive Psychology]] — understanding automatic versus deliberate cognitive processes helps designers anticipate user behavior and support intuitive decision-making.
- [[Aesthetic-Usability Effect]] — initial judgments about aesthetics, credibility, and information relevance are made rapidly and have lasting impact on how users perceive and interact with sites.
- [[Visual Design]] — effective visual design uses hierarchy, contrast, and positioning to support System 1's automatic pattern recognition and guide users toward task-relevant information.
