# 7 Tips for Memorable and Easy-to-Understand Imagery

## Summary

This article provides seven practical best practices for selecting and implementing memorable, comprehensible imagery in designs. The recommendations address relevance to text content, avoiding generic stock photos, displaying products in realistic contexts, positioning visuals near related text, avoiding imagery excess, using high-resolution photographs, and considering cross-cultural interpretations. Effective imagery strengthens text messaging, supports user comprehension, enhances memorability, and should be validated through usability testing. The article emphasizes that imagery and text work as complementary channels to reinforce key messages and guide user understanding.

## Key Takeaways

- **Relevance matters** — choose imagery that strengthens text content and promotes key messages; irrelevant visuals are missed opportunities that decrease message memorability.
- **Avoid stock photos** — generic stock images used by competitors reduce memorability; feature actual products or clear software screenshots instead to showcase capabilities.
- **Realistic context** — show products in contexts where they're actually used; include environment, timing, and target audience to communicate product purpose and benefits.
- **Proximity principle** — place visuals close to related text to facilitate comprehension; maintain this proximity across responsive design breakpoints on mobile devices.
- **Restraint in quantity** — use few solid visuals communicating key takeaways rather than overwhelming with many similar images; excess imagery weakens visual hierarchy.
- **Technical quality** — high-resolution photographs are easier to comprehend and remember; blurry, low-resolution images lack important details.
- **Cultural consideration** — images may be interpreted differently across cultures; localized imagery can resonate better with specific audiences.
