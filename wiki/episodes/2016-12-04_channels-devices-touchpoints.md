# How Channels, Devices, and Touchpoints Impact the Customer Journey

## Summary

Customer journeys today are dynamic and interconnected through multiple channels and devices. A channel is the medium of interaction between customer and organization (websites, apps, phone, in-person); devices enable access to channels (smartphones, computers, tablets, smartwatches). A touchpoint is a specific instance of interaction combining device, channel, and task. Organizations need to understand their unique channel ecosystem and how customers use channels and devices to complete tasks, rather than focusing narrowly on individual devices at the expense of omnichannel strategy.

## Key Takeaways

- **Channels are interaction mediums** — Channels can be bidirectional (websites, apps, live chat, phone, in-person) or unidirectional (mail, advertising, packaging); they represent how customers interact with organizations.
- **Devices enable channel access** — Smartphones, computers, tablets, and smartwatches are not channels themselves but provide means to access channels; the same channel (email) can be accessed from multiple devices with optimized experiences for each.
- **Organizations have unique ecosystems** — Each organization supports a different set of relevant channels; banks rely on physical locations while internet companies support multiple digital channels; some create proprietary devices.
- **Device-specific vs. multi-device channels** — Some channels are device-specific (mobile app, smartwatch app) while others exist across devices (email, live chat); each channel's experience should be optimized for its device context.
- **Touchpoints define customer interactions** — Each touchpoint captures the specific details of an interaction: which device, which channel, what task; customer journeys are series of touchpoints providing full context.
- **Understand the full ecosystem** — Organizations must map their channel ecosystem, identify gaps, study common customer journeys, and examine touchpoints to understand how customers use solutions.

## Quotes

> A channel is the medium of interaction between a customer and an organization.

> devices are not channels. Devices simply provide the means to access channels (usually digital channels, but there may be exceptions; for example, smartphones allow us to call an organization over the phone).

> A touchpoint represents a specific interaction between a customer and an organization. It includes the device being used, the channel used for the interaction, and the specific task being completed.

## Concepts

- [[Customer Journey]] — Journeys are composed of touchpoints across multiple channels and devices; understanding each touchpoint's context is essential for omnichannel design.
- [[Omnichannel Experience]] — Organizations must design across their full channel ecosystem rather than optimizing individual devices; consistency and continuity across channels improve customer experience.
- [[Interaction Design]] — Designing for multiple interaction channels requires understanding how devices enable access to channels, and how touchpoints define specific customer-organization interactions across the full ecosystem.
