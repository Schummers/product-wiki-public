# Remote Usability-Testing Costs: Moderated vs. Unmoderated

## Summary

Unmoderated remote usability testing is often assumed to be significantly cheaper and faster than moderated testing, but actual cost savings are smaller than expected and may come at the expense of research quality. This article compares typical costs for both approaches for a 5-participant qualitative study in the US. Moderated studies cost $415-$1,680 plus 32-48 researcher hours; unmoderated studies cost $250-$1,250 plus 11-27 hours. While unmoderated saves 20-40% in cost and time, hidden costs include potential cheaters or professional participants requiring overrecruiting, time spent watching and analyzing recordings, and shorter session durations (20 minutes vs. 60 minutes) that may not suit complex tasks. Facilitated sessions often yield more insights and focused discussions than unmoderated testing, an advantage that must be weighed against cost savings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Unmoderated testing saves only 20-40%, not dramatically** — While the numbers show savings, the difference is smaller than widely assumed. For a 5-participant study, unmoderated ranges from $250-$1,250 vs. moderated $415-$1,680. The savings may not justify losing facilitator insight.
- **Overrecruit for unmoderated studies** — Account for cheaters or professional participants who participate for payment alone. Overrecruit to ensure you have quality respondents after removing bad data. Some platforms replace poor participants free; others charge, raising true costs.
- **Analysis time is hidden in unmoderated studies** — You still must watch and analyze session recordings. Time saved during session facilitation is often shifted into the analysis phase, reducing overall time savings more than expected.
- **Session length and task complexity matter** — Unmoderated sessions typically max out at 20 minutes vs. moderated 60 minutes. Long, complex tasks with multiple parts are not well suited to unmoderated testing. If testing multiple tasks, you may need more participants, each doing only a subset.
- **Facilitators provide more insights than unmoderated testing** — A skilled facilitator can probe ambiguous answers, clarify misunderstandings, and uncover motivations. Moderated sessions often yield more focused, actionable insights than unmoderated observation alone, which may justify the cost difference.

## Quotes

> One reason why unmoderated remote usability testing tends to be more popular than moderated testing is that it's seen as cheaper and faster.

> Researchers often prefer unmoderated testing because they feel it will save them time — no need to run the sessions. But remember, particularly in qualitative testing, you should still be watching those recordings to analyze them.

> A facilitated session often results in more focused sessions and more insights than an unfacilitated one, and that's something that you might miss in unmoderated testing.
