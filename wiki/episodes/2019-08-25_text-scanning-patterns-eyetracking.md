# Text Scanning Patterns: Eyetracking Evidence

## Summary

Eyetracking research reveals four distinct text-scanning patterns users employ on the web, ranked by effectiveness from least to most: F-pattern, spotted pattern, layer-cake pattern, and commitment pattern. Each pattern emerges based on task, content layout, styling, and user motivation. Users scan rather than read to minimize effort while extracting needed information. The F-pattern, the least effective, occurs in unstructured prose; layer-cake (scanning headings then body text) is highly effective; commitment (reading everything) yields best comprehension but requires high motivation or stakes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four scanning patterns exist, ranked by effectiveness** — F-pattern (inefficient, used with plain text), spotted pattern (fixating on styled words), layer-cake (reading headings then body text), and commitment (reading all content) each serve different reading contexts.

- **F-pattern persists without subheadings or visual hierarchy** — In left-to-right languages, users fixate on top-left area, then skim down the left edge, ignoring right-side and bottom content; mirrored vertically for right-to-left languages.

- **Visual styling and structure strongly influence scanning behavior** — Bolding, bullet points, differently colored text, and meaningful headings guide users to the spotted and layer-cake patterns, which are more effective than F-pattern scanning.

- **Layer-cake pattern is nearly as effective as reading all text** — Scanning headings and corresponding body sections (resembling layers) is one of the most efficient ways to consume content while maintaining comprehension.

- **Commitment pattern requires high motivation or stakes** — Users fixate on most words only when they trust the source, are highly interested, or face consequences for missing information (studying for tests, returning items).

- **Design choices favor specific scanning patterns** — Content chunking, meaningful subheadings, visual emphasis on keywords, and bullet lists support layer-cake and spotted patterns rather than forcing less effective F-pattern scanning.

## Quotes

> On the web, people don't read every word on a page; instead, they scan. They naturally attempt to be efficient and put in the least possible work for achieving their goal.

> Aside from reading almost every word, the layer-cake pattern is by far the most effective way in which users can scan pages.

> Eyetracking research helps us to see the details of how users look at content and how they choose to skip or read it.
