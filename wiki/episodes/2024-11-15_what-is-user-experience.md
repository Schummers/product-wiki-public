# What Is User Experience (and What Is It Not)?

## Summary

The article provides a foundational definition of user experience and clarifies what UX is and what it is not. Coined by Don Norman in the 1990s, user experience refers to all possible aspects of a person's interactions with a company and its products or services. The UX discipline emerged as a professional practice focused on designing and enhancing interactions and overall experience for all users. However, UX is often misunderstood and confused with related but distinct concepts like usability, UI design, and customer experience.

## Key Takeaways

- **User experience is the holistic relationship** — UX encompasses perceptions, emotions, and interactions between a person and a product, service, or company; it is not just about a single interaction or product.
- **UX vs Usability** — While usability is a critical component of UX focused on task efficiency, UX encompasses a broader perspective including brand perception, emotional responses, and long-term relationships with products.
- **UX vs UI** — User interface is the set of components and design elements that enable interaction, while UX encompasses all aspects of the end-user's interaction with the company and its products across all touchpoints.
- **UX extends beyond digital products** — UX is not limited to websites or applications; it applies to entire ecosystems and journeys, not just single devices or products.
- **Good UX is not a nice-to-have** — Good user experience is a business imperative; organizations with adequate UX roles and knowledge create better products that contribute to business goals like reduced support costs and increased conversions.
- **UX maturity affects organizational success** — Many organizations still fall in the bottom half of the UX-maturity scale, often considering UX expendable when budgets tighten, which is a short-sighted approach.

## Quotes

> A **user experience** is the holistic relationship — encompassing perceptions, emotions, and interactions — between a person and a product, service, or company.

> The field of **user experience (UX)** is a professional practice focused on designing and enhancing the interactions and overall experience for all users of a product, service, or brand.

> Regardless of what we choose to call this work, UX as a mindset (i.e., a user-first mentality that prioritizes creating meaningful, positive experiences) should occur in every department.
