# Solving Product Design Exercises: Mia Blume: skills which future design managers should work on

## Summary

This guest interview features Mia Blume, founder and CEO of Design Dept and former design leader at Pinterest, Square, and IDEO, addressing the transition from individual contributor to management. Mia argues that designers possess foundational skills—creativity, vision, storytelling, empathy, and systems thinking—that directly transfer to business leadership, but often struggle with the critical skill of letting go of control and becoming a coach rather than a solution provider.

## Key Takeaways

- **Transition from solving to coaching** — When designers become managers, their instinct is to direct solutions based on their expertise, but the essential skill to develop is coaching teammates to solve their own problems instead.
- **Listening is the foundation of coaching** — The first and most important skill of coaching is deep listening; when someone seeks your answer, your role is to understand their problem before offering direction.
- **Ask empowering questions instead of providing solutions** — Direct problem-solving back to your teammate with questions like "What's the goal of this exercise?" or "If you had two more weeks, how would you prioritize the work?" to build their capabilities.
- **Empower decision-making when possible** — Avoid making decisions yourself unless absolutely necessary (high-risk projects or tight deadlines); explicitly tell team members you trust their ability and offer support and perspective, not direction.
- **Designers already have the foundational leadership skills** — Creativity, vision, storytelling, empathy, and systems thinking developed through design practice directly translate to effective business leadership; management training should focus on adding coaching and delegation capabilities.

## Quotes

> I believe designers, unlike other fields in tech, bring some of the most essential skills from years of honing their craft to business leadership. This includes: creativity, vision, storytelling, empathy and systems thinking.

> One of the most important things for designers to embrace when shifting to management is learning to let go of control and become a coach.

> They may approach you seeking an answer, but the first and most important skill of coaching is listening.
