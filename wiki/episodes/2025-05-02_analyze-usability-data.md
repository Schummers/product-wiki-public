# Analyze Usability Test Data in 4 Steps

## Summary

Analyzing qualitative usability test data is more complex than standard textbook presentations suggest. This article provides a practical 4-step framework for moving from raw data to trustworthy insights: collect relevant data points, assess for accuracy, explain the data through synthesis, and check explanations against data for good fit. The framework emphasizes iteration between steps 3 and 4, recognizing that initial explanations based on memorable data points often require refinement when tested against the broader dataset. The process combines analysis (breaking down complexity) with synthesis (recombining into meaningful insights).

## Key Takeaways

- **Analyzing qualitative data requires careful data selection and assessment** — gather multiple relevant observations and quotes from session notes and recordings that relate to research questions; assess each data point for accuracy before forming conclusions; not all data points have equal weight.
- **Analysis and synthesis alternate iteratively** — analysis breaks down data into manageable units; synthesis recombines information into insights; step 3 (explain) and step 4 (check fit) often cycle multiple times as initial explanations are tested against broader data.
- **Initial explanations are often biased by memorable data** — researchers tend to form initial explanations from data points that are easy to recall or stand out; when these explanations are tested against the full dataset, inconsistencies and overlooked patterns often emerge.
- **Hypothesis testing frame validates explanations** — test explanations against collected data by making predictions; if data doesn't support predictions, reject or refine the explanation; this systematic approach prevents misleading conclusions from single data points.
- **Complex research questions require triangulation** — multiple pieces of data from different points in sessions, study design considerations, recruitment details, and facilitation events must be weighed together to provide trustworthy answers to complex research questions about discoverability, comprehension, and mental models.

## Quotes

> The mark of a good analyst is exploring their data from multiple angles, testing multiple explanations against the data, and putting forward some sensible hypotheses to explore in future research.
