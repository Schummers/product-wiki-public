# Ink Thinking Improves UX-Decision Making

## Summary

Ink thinking is a reflective journaling practice designed specifically to improve UX practitioners' intuition and decision-making skills. Similar to practices recommended by management consultant Peter Drucker, it involves recording decisions and predictions, letting reality unfold, and regularly reviewing notes to identify patterns. Research shows that practitioners who reflect on experiences outperform those who simply practice more. Ink thinking counteracts hindsight bias (the tendency to overestimate prediction accuracy after knowing outcomes) and helps UX professionals recognize patterns in their decision-making. The practice requires a small, thin journal, 5-10 minutes of scheduled reflection time, and consists of two steps: deciding and predicting, then reflecting and advising once outcomes are known.

## Key Takeaways

- **Reflection improves performance more than additional practice** — research participants who journaled answered 23% more math puzzles correctly than those who practiced more.
- **Ink thinking counteracts hindsight bias** where people overestimate their ability to predict outcomes once they know results.
- **A small, thin, ruled journal** works best because small pages force brevity and a thin journal feels less daunting to complete.
- **Schedule 5-10 minutes weekly** for ink-thinking sessions to ensure consistency and establish the habit.
- **Decide and predict first** — record a decision and what you expect to happen in 2-3 sentences on the left page.
- **Reflect and advise second** — record the actual outcome and write advice to yourself on the right page when results are known.
