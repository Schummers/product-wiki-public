# Top 10 Intranet Trends of 2016

## Summary

The best-designed intranets for 2016 share common themes and features despite having unique styles and personalities. Based on the Intranet Design Annual 2016, this article identifies 10 emerging trends that should spread to mainstream intranets over the next years. While not every feature works for every organization, trends from outstanding intranets can inspire intranet redesigns. The trends span from interface design (simple, minimalist aesthetics, better photos, fat footers) to functionality (help systems, improved search, video) to social and business communication features.

## Key Takeaways

- **Help and tutorials return** — After years of being out of fashion, well-designed help is making a resurgence; help features aid discoverability, expedite learning of new features, and increase employee confidence and productivity.
- **Simple, minimalist design prevails** — "Flat and boxy" designs of prior years give way to simple, sometimes minimalist aesthetics; generous white space, elegant gray palettes with accent colors, and legible typography improve scannability.
- **Better photography improves engagement** — Winning intranets feature authentic photos of employees doing actual work; users prefer real images over generic stock photos; photos create engagement and sense of community.
- **Search evolution** — Leading intranets improve search through user feedback, keyword adjustment, metadata enhancement, and "Best Bets" highlighting; search evolution is a critical differentiator in reducing information-seeking friction.
- **Carousels and heroes** — While carousels remain popular, hero images (single, prominent images) are making a comeback; both organize featured content, though single heroes create stronger visual impact.
- **Fat footers provide second chance** — Large, organized footers give employees additional opportunities to find needed content; footer content can repeat navigation, suggest related items, or highlight popular links.
- **Left-side global navigation** — Vertical navigation down the left side translates well from desktop to mobile (unlike megamenus); growing adoption on winning intranets for consistent multi-device experience.
- **Targeted social features** — Social tools work best when presented with clear context about content types and guidelines; moving away from undescribed feed walls toward categorized, purposeful social sections.
- **Video accessibility improves** — Video creation and editing tools are more accessible; video is no longer expected to be high-quality production; realistic, just-shot-themselves videos are welcomed; videos stored in dedicated sections with filtering and search.
- **Business communication emphasis** — Leaders use intranets to communicate organizational goals and status, helping employees understand organizational direction and their role; demonstrates respect and builds engagement.

## Quotes

> Each winning intranet has its own style, feature set, and personality, unique and special in its own way. But, great minds do think alike, and some themes and features are common in multiple or all of the winning intranets in our Intranet Design Annual 2016.

> After many years of being chastised for being unhelpful, online help went out of fashion. This was reinforced by the idea that an interface should stand on its own, and not need help to be usable. This year, however, we saw a resurgence of Help that is helpful.

> Whether your organization is large or small; formal or informal; public, private or government; consider which of the trends here can be implemented, deployed, and used successfully on your intranet.
