# Quantifying UX Improvements: A Case Study

## Summary

Baileigh Industrial, a B2B manufacturer of industrial machinery, worked with Marketade to improve their website's information architecture. Sales representatives were flooded with calls from customers who couldn't find needed information. Through qualitative usability testing, card sorting, and tree testing (baseline measurement at 4/10 and post-redesign at 7.4/10), Marketade identified navigation problems and redesigned the information architecture. The revised IA achieved an 85% improvement in findability—exceeding the typical 75% average—and led to substantial increases in website revenue and qualified leads.

## Key Takeaways

- **Sales and support teams reveal experience problems** — interviews with people who regularly interact with customers reveal top issues and pain points better than assumptions.
- **Tree testing measures navigation findability** — a tree test establishes baseline performance metrics (4/10 originally) and identifies which navigation paths confuse users most.
- **Card sorting uncovers mental models** — users' categorization of products reveals how they think about and organize concepts, informing better information architecture.
- **Improved IA increases self-service adoption** — by reducing navigation confusion and product-finding difficulty, better IA frees support staff from basic inquiries and enables focus on high-value work.
- **Quantified improvements exceed typical gains** — the redesign achieved 85% improvement in findability, higher than the typical 75% average for UX-driven redesigns, possibly because B2B and specialized domains have greater improvement potential.
