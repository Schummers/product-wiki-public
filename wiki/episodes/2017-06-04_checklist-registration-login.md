# A Checklist for Registration and Login Forms on Mobile

## Summary

Typing passwords and filling forms are more painful on mobile than desktop due to keyboard switching, password recall difficulties, and higher error rates. The article provides practical guidelines for reducing friction in mobile registration and login processes. Key recommendations include making login optional, offering alternative authentication methods, minimizing data requests, and using visual feedback mechanisms to help users verify their input before submission.

## Key Takeaways

- **Minimize data collection** — Ask only for essential information like email and password; allow users to provide additional details later through their profile. Avoid requesting unnecessary fields at registration time.

- **Show passwords on demand** — Allow users to make passwords visible when typing, and for login specifically, offer a Show Password checkbox since some users may feel insecure seeing unmasked characters by default.

- **Avoid repeat fields** — Do not require users to type passwords or emails twice; instead, provide password visibility and a confirmation page showing both entries so users can verify their input.

- **Display password requirements upfront** — Disclose password constraints before users attempt entry, preventing the frustration of discovering requirements through rejection after submission.

- **Use biometric authentication** — Support fingerprint authentication like TouchID on mobile to remove most login friction for supported devices.

- **Offer alternative registration methods** — Enable registration via social login or Google Account to reduce the burden of creating and remembering new credentials.

## Quotes

> Typing passwords is more difficult on mobile because it involves switching keyboards to access numerical or special characters. In fact, a study carried out at University of Munich shows that entering a password character on mobile takes almost twice as much time than on the desktop, and as a result people tend to create weaker passwords on mobile.

> Disclose password constraints upfront. Nothing is more annoying than having to guess what the site's password requirements might be, and later discovering that you guessed wrong.

> Allow people to use fingerprint authentication (such as TouchID) to log in. This method will remove most pain from the login process.
