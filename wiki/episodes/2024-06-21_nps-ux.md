# Net Promoter Score: What a Customer-Relations Metric Can Tell You About Your User Experience

## Summary

Net Promoter Score is a customer-loyalty metric based on a single question asking likelihood to recommend on a 0-10 scale. Responses are categorized as Promoters (9-10), Passives (7-8), and Detractors (0-6), with NPS calculated as percentage of Promoters minus percentage of Detractors. While NPS correlates with company growth and has strong management buy-in, the metric has significant limitations when used alone: it conflates loyalty with usability, requires large sample sizes, ignores valuable middle-range data, and can be gamed. The article recommends using NPS as one of several metrics, supplemented by behavioral measurements.

## Key Takeaways

- **NPS measures customer loyalty, not usability** — while correlated with user experience, NPS reflects overall brand perception and recommendation likelihood, not specific usability problems.
- **Passives are excluded from calculation** — moderate-satisfaction users (7-8) are counted in the total but do not contribute to the NPS score, ignoring valuable information about improvement.
- **NPS varies significantly by country and culture** — countries like India and Mexico show higher NPS, while Japan and South Korea show negative NPS even for liked companies, reflecting cultural differences in rating tendencies.
- **NPS requires large sample sizes** — scoring 5 users in a qualitative study is meaningless; NPS requires statistical significance that small samples cannot provide.
- **NPS is often gamed** — teams may manipulate surveys (asking only satisfied customers, offering incentives for good ratings) to inflate scores rather than improving actual experience.
- **Use NPS with supplementary metrics** — behavioral data (task success rates, task times) and lower-level satisfaction measures provide essential context that NPS alone cannot supply.

## Quotes

> "The net promoter score (NPS) is a quantitative metric that measures how many more people are likely to strongly recommend your site or product compared to those likely to criticize it."

> "Usability is never entirely captured by subjective scores."

> "While other satisfaction metrics also suffer from the disadvantages mentioned above, NPS has another major problem: its calculation method ignores a lot of the information provided by the ratings by grouping them into three bins (promoters, passives, and detractors) and then ignoring the passives."

## Concepts

- [[Net Promoter Score]] — a widely-used customer loyalty metric based on willingness to recommend; effective as one component of a multi-metric measurement strategy.
- [[Customer Satisfaction]] — NPS is one approach to measuring satisfaction, but has limitations that make it insufficient as a standalone measure.
- [[Metrics]] — the article argues for sophisticated metrics strategy that combines subjective (NPS, satisfaction surveys) and behavioral measures for complete understanding.
- [[UX Metrics]] — measuring user experience requires multiple metrics; NPS reflects loyalty but not usability, requiring supplementary behavioral measurements.
- [[User Retention]] — NPS is specifically designed to measure loyalty and its correlation with business growth, though loyalty is not identical to usability.
- [[Analytics]] — implementing NPS measurement requires attention to statistical significance, sample size, and potential bias sources like sampling bias and rating racketeering.
