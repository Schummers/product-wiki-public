# Solving Product Design Exercises: Helen Tran: the skill most designers overlook

## Summary

This chapter features Helen Tran, a Design Lead at Shopify (where she built a team of 30 designers) and product designer mentor, who identifies research and communication as the most overlooked skills in design careers. Tran argues that designers must validate assumptions about target markets and user needs rather than trusting the directives they are given, which are typically steeped in biases. She emphasizes that designers should bridge business and user needs through practical deliverables like user experience mapping, design sprints, and market interviews, and develop soft skills—leadership, communication, presentation, and group facilitation—to convince teams to build what makes sense for both the business and user.

## Key Takeaways

- **Assumption Validation is Core** — designers are typically handed assumptions about target markets that are wrong and steeped in biases; a designer's job is to double-check these assumptions through thorough research to prevent investing in products nobody needs or wants.
- **Bridge Business and User Needs** — a designer cannot explain the bridge between business needs and user needs, they have not done their research; building this bridge requires knowing what the user needs and what the business needs.
- **Practical Research Deliverables** — user experience mapping, design sprints, and in-depth market interviews are concrete ways to validate assumptions and inform what the team should build.
- **Soft Skills Are Learnable** — leadership and communication skills are as learnable as practical deliverables; practice through group critiques, company speaking opportunities, retrospectives, Toastmaster groups, improv groups, and expanding beyond the tech scene.
- **Challenge Your Bubble** — push beyond comfortable circles; if it is uncomfortable, it is a good sign of growth.

## Quotes

> A designer's job is certainly to help build the product or feature, but a large part of their job is to ensure that what everyone is building is the right thing for the user.

> When a designer is handed a target market, they typically trust the people who gave them the directive that their assumptions are correct. Except they usually aren't, and they're typically steeped in someone else's biases.

> Challenge your bubble. If it's uncomfortable, it's a good sign.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — Designer growth requires stepping beyond specialization; practice soft skills like public speaking, group facilitation, and cross-functional communication, especially outside the tech industry.
- [[Product Thinking]] — Designers must independently validate assumptions about user and business needs rather than accepting directives at face value; thorough research on both sides is foundational to sound product decisions.
- [[Problem Framing]] — A designer's role includes ensuring the team is solving the right problem; this requires validating that assumptions about the target market and user needs are correct before building.
- [[Design Presentation]] — Designers must develop and practice communication skills—presenting research findings, leading group critiques, running retrospectives, and facilitating team alignment—to convince teams to build what serves both business and user.
