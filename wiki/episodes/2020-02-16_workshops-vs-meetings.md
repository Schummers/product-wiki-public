# UX Workshops vs. Meetings: What's the Difference?

## Summary

Workshops and meetings serve fundamentally different purposes and require vastly different levels of preparation. Meetings enable information exchange across multiple topics with passive participation, while workshops solve specific problems through hands-on collaboration and active participation. Workshops follow a diverge-and-converge structure with activities like sketching, brainstorming, and affinity diagramming. Meetings have structure too (standups, retrospectives, one-on-ones) but are fundamentally about sharing information. Workshops require extensive preparation: defining clear objectives, selecting appropriate activities, gathering materials, and building stakeholder buy-in. Confusing these formats wastes time and damages organizational credibility for future workshops.

## Key Takeaways

- **Meetings share information; workshops solve problems** — Meetings are for exchanging information across multiple topics. Workshops are for solving a predefined problem through focused, collaborative idea generation and hands-on activities that produce actionable outcomes.

- **Breadth versus depth** — Meetings handle shallow coverage of many topics. Workshops handle deep, focused coverage of single issues. Meetings don't require decisions or action items during the session; workshops produce them.

- **Workshops require extensive preparation** — Workshop preparation pales compared to meeting prep. Workshops need: workshop brief, clear objectives, intentional activity sequencing, detailed agendas, material gathering, stakeholder alignment, and design documentation. Meetings need: agenda outline, invitations, and slides if needed.

- **Diverge-and-converge structure** — Workshops follow sequences where divergent activities generate many ideas or contributions (sketching, brainstorming, brainwriting), followed by convergent activities that identify patterns and prioritize ideas (affinity diagramming, forced ranking).

- **Structure without magic** — Simply gathering people in a room doesn't create results. Without predefined problems to solve, real collaboration needs, or adequate planning, workshops waste everyone's time. High-level stakeholders notice and won't attend future workshops.

- **Define standards for your team** — Explicitly define what workshops and meetings mean at your organization to prevent misuse and ensure proper preparation levels.

## Quotes

> Meetings are where things get discussed. Workshops are where things get done.

> If there's no predefined problem to solve, no real need for collaboration, or no adequate advance planning, workshops are just a waste of time.

> Understanding the differences between workshops and meetings can save everybody's time, make the most of group collaboration, and avoid creating a bad reputation for workshops.
