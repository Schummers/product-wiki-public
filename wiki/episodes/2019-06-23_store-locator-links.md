# Store Finders: Why People Still Need Locator Links

## Summary

Store locators (or store finders) allow customers to locate physical outlets of a retailer. Despite reliance on external tools like Google Maps, websites still need integrated store-locator features for several reasons: users may need to confirm details (hours, amenities) after using external tools, shoppers already on the site may seek local options as their next task, and locators improve location indexing on external mapping tools. The article presents ecommerce research showing that users access locators from product-listing pages, product detail pages, shopping-cart and checkout pages, and customer-service pages. Strategic placement of locator links (either direct links or contextual filters) at these decision points supports complex omnichannel shopping journeys and keeps users on-site.

## Key Takeaways

- **Store Locators Support Omnichannel Journeys** — many shoppers research online and buy in-person; a locator helps users determine which nearby stores have items in stock, avoiding wasted travel or disappointment.
- **Contextual Links Are Essential** — place locator links on product-listing pages (with store-availability filters), product detail pages (as "choose your store" options), and checkout pages to support in-store pickup; users may turn to external services if the locator is not readily accessible.
- **Multiple Entry Points Reduce Friction** — provide locator links on FAQ and return-policy pages so users can transition smoothly from reading about a service to finding a local location that offers it.
- **Locator Placement Increases Discoverability** — prominent, contextual placement of store-locator options (like a header filter or button near call-to-action buttons) primes users to consider in-store options and reduces the risk of them leaving the site.

## Quotes

> Even after using an external tool, users may turn to your site to confirm details such as business hours, holiday closings, or location-specific amenities.

> A tool that allows users to see only products available in a specific store helps in-person purchasers to avoid the disappointment of investing time researching an item that isn't locally available.

## Concepts

- [[Store Locators]] — integrated store-finder features with contextual links support omnichannel shopping by helping users locate physical outlets without leaving the website.
- [[Omnichannel Commerce]] — store locators are part of seamless omnichannel experiences; strategically placed links on product, checkout, and service pages support mixed online-offline purchase journeys.
- [[Contextual Navigation]] — locator links placed near relevant decision points (e.g., near "Add to Cart") increase discoverability and reduce user effort in transitioning between channels.
