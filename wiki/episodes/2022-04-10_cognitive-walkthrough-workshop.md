# How to Conduct a Cognitive Walkthrough Workshop

## Summary

A cognitive walkthrough is a group-based usability evaluation technique that assesses how easily users can learn to use a system, without involving actual users. It relies on expert reviewers evaluating an interface from a new user's perspective in a structured workshop setting. The article provides detailed guidance on preparation (selecting evaluators, defining inputs, establishing roles and rules), conducting the walkthrough (determining success or failure at each step using four key questions), and documenting findings. This method is particularly useful for early-stage designs or when user testing is cost-prohibitive.

## Key Takeaways

- **A cognitive walkthrough evaluates learnability** without requiring users, making it relatively cheap and enabling early design testing.
- **Assemble 2-6 evaluators of diverse roles** (product experts, UX practitioners, engineers, domain experts) to ensure comprehensive evaluation.
- **Define the action sequence explicitly** before the session to keep the group focused and prevent derailment.
- **Four analysis questions guide success determination** at each step: Will users try to achieve the right result? Will they notice the correct action? Will they associate the action with the result? Will they see progress after the action?
- **Assume success when one step fails** and continue analyzing subsequent steps to identify all problems.
- **Two full tasks can be evaluated in a 90-minute session** with proper facilitation and preparation.

## Quotes

> Unlike user testing, it does not involve users (and, thus, it can be relatively cheap to implement).

> Although cognitive walkthroughs can be conducted by an individual, they are designed to be done as part of a group in a workshop setting where evaluators walk through a task in a highly structured manner from a new user's point of view.

> The facilitator will remind everyone of the ground rules during the walkthrough.
