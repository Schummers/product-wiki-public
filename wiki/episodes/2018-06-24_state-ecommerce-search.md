# The State of Ecommerce Search

## Summary

Ecommerce search has improved dramatically over 17 years, with overall search success increasing from 64% in 2000 to 92% in 2017. Improvements include refined search algorithms, standardized search box presentation, declining use of complex advanced searches, widespread adoption of faceted search, and popular use of search suggestions. However, as the industry improves, user expectations rise higher. Sites can no longer simply be functional; they must provide seamless, smooth experiences that make users feel in complete control of their options.

## Key Takeaways

- **Improve ranking algorithms** — Modern search engines should be smart enough to return good results without requiring users to use complex operators. Algorithms should prioritize results matching all keywords rather than any keyword.
- **Standardize search box placement** — Search boxes are now uniformly located in the global navigation area, helping users learn where to look. Explicit labels are less necessary with magnifying glass icons now widely recognized.
- **Replace advanced search with faceted search** — Faceted search allows users to narrow results after submitting queries rather than before, eliminating confusion and increasing usability compared to advanced or scoped searches.
- **Leverage faceted search as expected feature** — Faceted search has become expected rather than exceptional; users now notice and complain when facets are absent from e-commerce sites.
- **Apply weighted review sorting** — When users sort by reviews, use weighted scores rather than simple averages to prioritize products with many reviews over those with few five-star reviews.

## Quotes

> Over the 17 years in which we conducted our three studies, we've seen steady improvements in search success.

> If people can't find your products, they can't buy them. A bad in-site search can frustrate users enough to make them abandon you for a competitor.

> When everyone is capable of providing a functional search, just being functional won't be enough to set you apart from your competitors.
