# The Methodological Problems Hiding in Your Research Tools

## Summary

Research platforms have evolved from data hosting to systems that plan, moderate, and analyze studies for users. Many of these tools lack a foundation in research methodology, creating risk of flawed research presented with confidence at scale. The article documents methodological problems in established platforms and warns that AI-powered research tools amplify these risks. Many tool creators lack research expertise, and their products have shaped how new researchers learn the field, sometimes teaching flawed practices.

## Key Takeaways

- **Tool maturity gap** — Research platforms have been around 15-20 years, but many lack features essential to proper quantitative testing, task randomization, or multiple success URLs for complex task paths.
- **Analysis tools miss behavior** — Tools like Dovetail enabled qualitative analysis but missed features for tagging video directly, forcing researchers to rely on transcripts which miss silent participant behaviors and errors.
- **Method blurring** — Platforms confuse interviews and usability tests—two distinct methods with different goals—through labeling and templating, leading researchers to blend methods ineffectively.
- **AI tools amplify problems** — New AI-powered research tools generate leading tasks, overly many interview questions, and flawed test plans that novice researchers cannot catch; the stakes of tool design are higher now.
- **Training effect** — Tools shape how researchers learn the field; platforms' templates, guides, and certifications teach research practices that may reflect commercial incentives rather than best practices.

## Quotes

> "A persistent and damaging confusion in the field is mixing up user interviews and usability tests — two distinct methods with fundamentally different goals."

> "Many builders of UX research tools don't understand research best practices for the methods supported by their software."
