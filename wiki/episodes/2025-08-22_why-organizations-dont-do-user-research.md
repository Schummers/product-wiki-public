# Why Organizations Don't Do User Research and How to Change That

## Summary

Organizations consistently cite common excuses to avoid user research: lack of time, high cost, belief that users don't know what they want, fear of negative feedback, reliance on A/B testing, and various other reasons. The author, drawing from decades of experience, provides rebuttals to each excuse, demonstrating that every excuse misses the point: research is faster and cheaper than building wrong things repeatedly, users know their problems even if not the solutions, negative feedback enables better products, and A/B testing cannot replace qualitative research. The deeper issue, however, is misaligned incentives: in many organizations, teams are rewarded for shipping fast and new features, not for delivering real user value. By the time a feature fails, the decision-makers have moved on, creating no accountability for poor research or bad decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Research is Faster Than Rebuilding** — Building the wrong thing, discovering failure, and rebuilding costs far more time than conducting research upfront to understand user needs and get it mostly right the first time.
- **Research Doesn't Have to be Expensive** — Remote usability testing, informal interviews with existing customers, and quick research methods provide tremendous learning value for minimal cost compared to unproductive meetings and failed features.
- **Users Know Their Problems** — Users don't necessarily know feature solutions, but they absolutely know what problems they face and what they don't want; user research focuses on understanding problems and needs, not collecting feature requests.
- **Talk to Users, Don't Ask for Features** — Asking people what features to build typically fails; instead, ask about their problems, behaviors, and workflows to understand needs holistically and create better solutions.
- **A/B Testing Cannot Replace Qualitative Research** — A/B testing reveals whether one variant performs better but cannot explain why; qualitative research provides the reasoning necessary to make better decisions in the future.
- **Misaligned Incentives are the Real Problem** — The fundamental barrier isn't time or cost but organizational incentives that reward shipping fast and new features over delivering real user value; without accountability for failures, teams lack motivation to do research.

## Quotes

> Too many organizations still aren't doing user research before building new products and features.

> User research is understanding people's needs, motivations, and problems and then helping the product team figure out how to build something that satisfies those needs, motivations, and wants.
