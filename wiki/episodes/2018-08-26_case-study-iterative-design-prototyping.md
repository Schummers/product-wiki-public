# Case Study: Iterative Design and Prototype Testing of the NN/g Homepage

## Summary

This case study documents Nielsen Norman Group's redesign of their own homepage, demonstrating the iterative design and testing methods they teach. Starting with qualitative surveys to understand audience expectations, the team established design goals, created low-fidelity wireframes and high-fidelity visual prototypes, conducted three rounds of testing with both desktop and mobile users, and used content hierarchy to drive specification and development through a modular component system.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design goals aligned with outcomes** — establish goals focused on outcomes, not just features: demonstrating credibility through work examples, creating an appealing minimalist aesthetic, and maintaining access to primary offerings.
- **Iterative prototype testing** — move from low-fidelity wireframes to high-fidelity visual prototypes before testing; test multiple versions, narrowing the set successively while observing what users actually do rather than relying on what they say.
- **Content hierarchy drives implementation** — prioritize content importance to establish a visual hierarchy for desktop and mobile; use this ranked list throughout iterations to ensure consistency and inform layout decisions.
- **Modular component approach** — design components once and add to a master stylesheet for efficient specifications; developers reference detailed specs in the stylesheet, avoiding redundancy when components change.
- **Discount usability methods at scale** — iterative design with user testing doesn't require large budgets; planning for multiple rounds and using discount methods produces steady design improvements without unreasonable project delays.

## Quotes

> Even though we've conducted thousands of usability tests on a wide variety of websites and applications, we still find that user research and iterative design are essential to achieving the best outcomes.

> Pay attention to what people do, which is often different than what they say.

> A good user-centered design process doesn't require huge budgets.

## Concepts

- [[Prototyping]] — creating representations of designs at varying fidelity levels for testing and validation; high-fidelity prototypes effectively test visual design and aesthetic appeal before development.
- [[Iterative Design]] — a design process of successive refinement through cycles of creation, testing, and improvement; especially effective for homepage and complex interface design.
- [[Design Process]] — the structured approach to creating user experiences, including goal setting, research, prototyping, testing, and specification; this article models a practical, lean process.
- [[Usability Testing]] — validation of designs with real users to identify problems and validate solutions; multiple rounds of testing with different prototypes generates more feedback in shorter timeframes.
