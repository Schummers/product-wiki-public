# Tools for Remote UX Workshops

## Summary

This article guides practitioners in selecting appropriate digital collaboration tools for remote UX workshops. It addresses common challenges of remote work including engagement, tool intimidation, and time constraints. The article categorizes workshops into generative, evaluative, and mapping types, recommending tool features for each and discussing factors that influence tool selection such as synchronous vs. asynchronous timing, tool complexity, and budget constraints.

## Key Takeaways

- **Synchronous Requirements** — live remote workshops need time on the calendar, videoconferencing, and a collaborative tool for real-time contributions and collective output creation.
- **Asynchronous Requirements** — distributed workshops need clear deadlines to combat procrastination and a collaborative tool with version control or change history to track contributions over time.
- **Generative Workshops** — ideation and brainstorming activities require tools that can organize large quantities of flat information and allow easy modification and repositioning of items, such as drag-and-drop whiteboarding tools.
- **Evaluative Workshops** — prioritization activities require voting capabilities that are democratic (voting not skewed by role or seniority) and display results as matrices or lists.
- **Mapping Workshops** — journey and service-blueprint activities require tools that align items and maintain alignment when new objects are added, plus optional nesting for context and references.
- **Complexity Consideration** — teams should favor familiar, low-complexity tools over specialized but unfamiliar ones to avoid barriers to participation, especially for first-time remote workshop efforts.
- **Tool Simplicity** — chances are the best tool is one you've already been using; keep it simple and remember that the most important step is to start doing remote workshops.
