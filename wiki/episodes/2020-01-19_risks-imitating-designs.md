# The Risks of Imitating Designs (Even from Successful Companies)

## Summary

While looking to successful companies for design inspiration is reasonable, blindly copying their designs is risky. Successful companies like Apple, Google, and Amazon operate in different brand contexts and have resources for constant experimentation and monitoring. What works for a large tech company may not work for smaller organizations, and successful companies frequently discard unsuccessful design experiments after finding they don't work. Designers should test solutions with their own users and context rather than assuming that a design works simply because a successful company uses it.

## Key Takeaways

- **Context is not transferable** — Giant companies operate in completely different brand contexts. What works for Apple may not work for your company. Customer loyalty, pricing, selection, and other factors give large companies latitude to use risky designs that smaller companies cannot afford.

- **Big companies experiment constantly** — Successful companies run continuous experiments, meaning many released designs are actually unsuccessful attempts that get discarded after monitoring. Outside observers cannot tell which designs have been thoroughly tested and which are guesses.

- **Minimalist input fields had limited success** — Google adopted minimalist single-line input fields based on design trends, but after testing with hundreds of users, found that enclosed rectangular boxes performed better for usability and user preference. Google reverted this design back to boxes.

- **Decorative elements hide content** — Amazon once used thematic background images behind product menus, but some menu labels became difficult to read. Amazon later replaced this with high-contrast text on plain backgrounds. What seems attractive may reduce usability.

- **Contrast requirements apply to all** — Apple uses low-contrast gray text on gray backgrounds for pricing information, falling short of minimal accessibility requirements. While Apple may absorb this cost, it creates accessibility issues and is unjustifiable for most organizations.

- **Test with your users** — Do your own prototype testing or A/B testing to ensure a design solution works with your specific audience before investing in building it.

## Quotes

> Just because a company is successful does not mean you can assume everything about its design is well-executed.

> You're not Apple. Make sure to bring up this context to explain to colleagues how industry, market, and customers' prior experiences make each brand's UX unique.

> Do your own prototype or A/B testing to ensure that a design solution works with your audience before you invest in building it, only to discover that it does not deliver the results you need.
