## Summary

McDonald’s Netherlands and Coca-Cola (2024-2025) produced AI-generated holiday ads that sparked intense public backlash despite involving substantial human labor (5-10 AI specialists, 100+ staff, weeks of production). The criticism stemmed from technical shortcomings (uncanny-valley effects, workarounds like rapid montages and distant shots masking AI limitations), lost authenticity (perceived as soulless and algorithmic), and ethical concerns about human-labor displacement. Companies proceeded despite predicted backlash because high-arousal negative emotions (anger, disgust) drive social sharing more than traditional "safe" ads — yet this strategy erodes long-term brand trust.

The failures reveal four principles for responsible AI use in UX: prioritize long-term trust over short-term virality; start with purpose and user problems, not technology; use AI to assist with internal processes rather than replace human creative work; and embrace healthy skepticism while running low-risk experiments to form firsthand judgment rather than relying on hype.

## Key Takeaways

- **Technical limitations were masked but visible; audiences can perceive when narrative serves technology, not vice versa** — AI-generated visuals trigger uncanny-valley effects (almost human, but not quite) that break emotional connection; workarounds like rapid montages, distant shots, and minimal close-ups conceal technical flaws but create fragmented, disorienting narratives; viewers recognize when storytelling is bent to accommodate technology rather than driven by creative intent.
- **AI adoption is perceived as a signal of organizational priorities** — People judge not just output quality but what the choice to use AI signals: efficiency over craft, novelty over authenticity, cost-cutting over care; in emotional domains (holiday campaigns, UX experiences), this signal carries high stakes and can trigger rejection as a perceived betrayal of brand heritage.
- **High-arousal negative emotions (anger, disgust) drive social sharing despite being unfavorable** — Even intensely criticized campaigns spread faster than "safe" traditional ads because strong emotions motivate sharing; companies may exploit this for visibility, but short-term virality from backlash erodes long-term brand trust and cannot be easily rebuilt.
- **AI should assist internal processes, not replace end-user creative work** — Appropriate uses include early-stage ideation, copy refinement, or testing microcopy; retain human ownership of creative direction and foreground human intent, vision, and storytelling in the final output rather than emphasizing technical novelty.
- **Begin with purpose and clear value proposition, not "powered by AI"** — Ask whether AI solves a real user problem better than existing approaches; "AI-powered" is meaningless to users; teams need intentional AI strategy tied to specific user needs, not metrics rewarding AI adoption for its own sake.
