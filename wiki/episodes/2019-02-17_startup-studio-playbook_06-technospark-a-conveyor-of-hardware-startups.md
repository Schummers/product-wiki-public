# Startup Studio Playbook: TechnoSpark: A conveyor of hardware startups

## Summary

This guest chapter by Denis Kovalevich, CEO of TechnoSpark, presents a hardware-focused venture builder model that radically compresses development timelines from 15-20 years to 2-3 times faster. TechnoSpark demonstrates that the studio model extends beyond software to hardware industries by combining product startups with contract services, using hyperspecialization to accelerate innovation cycles. Founded in 2012 as a partnership between private entrepreneurs and Russia's Rusnano, TechnoSpark has built over 100 high-tech startups across robotics, biotech, materials science, and related fields, showing how studios can operate at scale across multiple geographies.

## Key Takeaways

- **Conveyor belt model for innovation** — TechnoSpark launches 10-15 new companies annually, with startups progressing through defined stages and exiting at various maturity points, from launch through market leadership ranking.
- **Dual company structure** — Studios support both contract services companies (70% of investment, high CapEx, immediate revenue) and product companies (30% of investment, low OpEx, revenue reinvested), with contract companies providing manufacturing and component supply for product ventures.
- **Hyperspecialization reduces time-to-market** — By dividing complex hardware development into focused companies with specialized tasks, each company solves problems faster than vertically integrated competitors, creating a networked ecosystem.
- **Entrepreneurship professionalization** — TechnoSpark treats venture building as a learnable profession with divisible specializations, not as requiring unique talent, making it possible to scale successful practitioners across regions.
- **International partnerships and scale** — With regional HQs in the Netherlands and China and partnerships with leading research centers (IMEC, KU Leuven, TNO), TechnoSpark demonstrates how studios can scale models across borders and geographies.

## Quotes

> The aim of our venture building technology is to radically reduce the end cost of building a startup. That is why we have to reduce the time it takes for them to be set up. Currently, an average startup in hardware industries takes 15–20 years to be set up and to grow; we aim to achieve this 2–3 times faster.

> The key issue for the success of such a robot in the market is to optimize its design for mass production. The objective is not to create a single sample of a device, but the ability to produce it in hundreds of thousand copies in a year with reasonable economy.

> The goal is to make venture building a profession. Let me remind you that in the relatively recent past managers seemed to be unique talents, but now it is a regular profession, which you can learn at a certain expenditure of labor and without any special talent.

## Concepts

- [[Venture Studio]] — Extended to hardware industries, demonstrating that studio economics apply to capital-intensive, manufacturing-dependent ventures through specialized company structures and hyperspecialized teams.
- [[Studio Economics]] — The cost structure of TechnoSpark's dual model: 70% contract services providing cash flow and infrastructure, 30% product companies capturing upside, with shared equity and cost-reduction focus.
- [[Studio Funnel]] — The multi-stage progression model where startups launch, mature, and exit at various points, tracked through 7-9 year cycles with detailed decision and market access data collected for future ventures.
