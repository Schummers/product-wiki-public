# 5 Steps to Creating a UX-Design Portfolio

## Summary

UX-design portfolios showcase designers' specialties, strengths, processes, and styles. Misconceptions treat portfolios as collections of final UI designs, but strong portfolios tell the story of the entire UX process from problem to solution. Hiring managers surveyed want to see how designers turn opportunities into user and organizational value, why elements were included or excluded, and the messy process behind polished designs. Portfolios must appeal to multiple audiences (hiring managers, recruiters, fellow professionals) and be scannable, as hiring managers rarely read word-for-word. The five-step process is: (1) take inventory of projects aligned with desired career directions; (2) choose 3-5 detailed case studies showcasing varied skills and aligning with job duties; (3) select format (web-based, PDF/slide deck, physical artifacts); (4) create projects using a cohesive template including problem, role, solution approach, impact, challenges faced, and learnings; (5) get feedback, iterate, and save process documents for future case studies. Common roadblocks include NDA restrictions (show process, redact information, or remake designs generically), time constraints (focus on projects with varied skills), and student status (include internship projects with realistic constraints).

## Key Takeaways

- **Show Process, Not Just Polished Designs** — portfolios must include problem statement, role, research, ideation, prototyping, testing iterations, challenges, and learnings; final screenshots alone tell an incomplete story that hiring managers want.
- **Quality Over Quantity** — choose 3-5 detailed case studies over many shallow projects; prioritize projects demonstrating varied UX skills aligned with desired job responsibilities.
- **Format Matters for Accessibility** — web-based portfolios are most common and findable; PDF/slide decks allow tailoring to specific jobs; physical artifacts support in-interview discussions; choose based on hiring manager requirements and your comfort.
- **Case Study Structure** — include problem/hypothesis, specific role and collaboration, solution approach, how solution solved the problem, challenges and iterations, business and user impact, and learnings; use visuals (sketches, wireframes, photos) to tell the story.

## Quotes

> Many of our top 10 recommendations for UX-research portfolios also apply to design portfolios. A common misconception about design portfolios is that they are only made up of final UI designs and screenshots.

> Don't just show me the finished product. I want to see the messy process and all the work and research that was put in to land on that shiny polished design.

> Remember, the final screenshots only tell part of the story. Hiring managers want to understand how you work and giving them a glimpse of your process will help them envision how you fit in with their teams.

## Concepts

- [[Portfolio]] — a curated collection of 3-5 detailed case studies showcasing design process from problem through impact; demonstrates variety of UX skills and ability to deliver user and business value.
- [[Case Study]] — detailed narratives of design projects including problem, role, solution approach, iterations, challenges, impact, and learnings; case studies demonstrate design thinking and process, not just final designs.
- [[UX Career]] — building a strong portfolio by documenting process and impact enables career growth; iterating portfolio over time and tailoring it to job opportunities improves hiring outcomes.
