# Venture Studios Demystified: Working With Founders

## Summary

Part 3a of Venture Studios Demystified examines how studios recruit, compensate, and structure relationships with external founders and entrepreneurs in residence (EIRs). Based on interviews with 19 venture studios, the chapter identifies three primary approaches: structured founder programs (37% of studios), limited full-time positions (37%), and assigning founders to pre-validated concepts (26%). Each approach reflects different trade-offs between scalability, upfront investment, and founder motivation. The authors detail cash compensation models (full-time salaries and term-based contracts), equity structures, and the profile of founders studios target—particularly second-time founders with proven track records. The chapter emphasizes that bringing founders on as early as possible, even if it means higher equity allocation, tends to produce better venture outcomes than assigning them to fully developed concepts later.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three founder relationship models** — Structured programs use formalized selection to attract founders and scale to many participants; limited full-time positions hire 2–4 founders at a time through the studio's network; assigning to established concepts puts emphasis on internal ideation with external founder brought in as CEO. Each model optimizes for different ROI metrics and organizational capacity.
- **Structured founder programs** — These programs (37% of studios) feature rolling application cycles, public role descriptions, and candidate selection based on entrepreneurial likelihood rather than idea quality. Short-term programs (~3 months) screen ideas and founders cheaply while building a network; longer-term programs invest more but expect founders to generate investable concepts within 6–12 months.
- **EIR compensation and structures** — Full-time EIRs often earn $150,000+ in major markets, paid from studio operating budgets. Term-based contractors receive monthly or full-term stipends over 6–12 month contracts. Founders who are retained to run ventures may draw salary either from studio operating funds or from the invested capital in their specific venture.
- **Early founder entry advantage** — Studios observed stronger outcomes when founding teams or CEOs joined ventures earlier, even if that meant the studio giving them higher founding equity. Later assignment of a founder to a developed concept reduces their personal investment and ability to "put a stamp on the business," increasing venture risk.
- **Founder profile targets** — Most studios prioritize second-time founders despite difficulty recruiting them, valuing their existing networks and entrepreneurial experience. However, studios also target first-time founders with domain expertise, technical skills, and entrepreneurial spirit. Ideal founder traits include catalytic persona, grit, resilience, domain expertise, and familiarity with the studio's focus.
- **Competitive challenges** — Studios compete with traditional VC investment by positioning themselves as offering de-risked venture building with strong administrative support, lower founder distraction, and access to shared infrastructure. This value proposition appeals most to second-time founders who want to focus on product rather than logistics, but requires strong studio reputation and favorable equity terms.

## Concepts

- [[Founder Recruitment]] — Studios use three distinct recruitment models: public structured programs with formal selection, limited full-time positions filled through personal networks, or on-demand assignment once an idea is validated. Most studios target second-time founders but face difficulty recruiting them; studios compete for talent by offering administrative support and de-risked venture building.
- [[Entrepreneur in Residence]] — EIRs are founders and operators in various stages of engagement with studios—from short-term participants in accelerator-like programs (3 months, minimal equity) to long-term apprentices working on multiple studio ventures before leading their own. Programs range from $150,000+ salaries in major markets to term-based contractor stipends ($50k–$150k+ over 6–12 months).
- [[Equity Ownership]] — Studios with structured programs or limited EIR positions typically take 35%+ equity in startups, while exploring lighter-touch models (10% equity) to support more ventures without full co-founder involvement. Bringing founders on earlier generally correlates with higher founder equity allocation, suggesting studios accept higher dilution in exchange for better venture outcomes.
- [[Studio Team]] — The effectiveness of external founder recruitment depends on the studio's internal ideation capacity, core team size, and network depth. Studios with robust internal ideation (research and business design teams) can assign founders to pre-validated concepts; those with lighter internal processes need either structured programs or limited EIR slots and rely on founder-led ideation.
