# Personas vs. Jobs-to-Be-Done

## Summary

Personas and Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD) are both valuable frameworks in user-centered design, yet some advocates argue that JTBD should replace personas entirely. This article argues against that dichotomy. Both tools serve complementary purposes: personas represent rich behavioral and attitudinal characteristics of user groups, while JTBD focuses on the specific outcomes users want to achieve. Well-executed personas go far beyond demographics to capture mental models, pain points, and contextual information that helps designers make balanced decisions across diverse user groups.

## Key Takeaways

- **Jobs-to-be-done focuses on outcomes** — the specific goals and success criteria users want to achieve when using a product, rather than prescribing how they should accomplish tasks.
- **Good personas include behavioral and attitudinal data** — not just demographics. They capture mental models, pain points, goals, and emotional considerations that guide design decisions.
- **Personas help prioritize among different users** — when one job-to-be-done may have different requirements for different user groups, personas help teams balance competing needs.
- **JTBD doesn't promote empathy the same way** — while JTBD captures emotional criteria, personas use narrative and personal details to build emotional connection and team empathy.
- **The two methods are compatible** — organizations can integrate JTBD information into personas, use them side by side, or choose one based on organizational context and resistance to change.

## Quotes

> People don't want to buy a quarter-inch drill, they want a quarter-inch hole.

> Jobs-to-be-done are typically summarized in a single sentence describing what the user needs to accomplish, and any important context that might impact this job (in this example, work travel for a conference , rather than vacation travel).

> If you design from a clean slate, trying to solve the jobs-to-be-done in a way which suits the brand new users, you will likely severely alter the workflows of your dedicated, existing customers (and thus negatively impact their productivity, as they have to relearn the product).

## Concepts

- [[Persona]] — personas provide rich behavioral and attitudinal representations of users that help teams build empathy and balance design decisions across different user groups, complementing job-to-be-done analysis.
- [[Jobs to be Done]] — a framework for identifying what users are trying to accomplish and the functional and emotional success criteria they use to judge whether a product solves their problem.
- [[User-Centered Design]] — both personas and JTBD are tools to keep users central to the design process by understanding their needs, goals, and contexts.
