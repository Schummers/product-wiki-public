# Solving Product Design Exercises: The framework

## Summary

This chapter provides an overview of the seven-step framework for solving design exercises. Dashinsky explains that while the framework may initially seem demanding, most steps require only a few minutes in practice and flow naturally. He emphasizes that Step 6 (UI/UX execution) is the critical step where designers must demonstrate core competencies, unlike other steps which typically involve collaboration with product managers and analysts in real-world scenarios. The chapter stresses using judgment rather than blindly following the framework.

## Key Takeaways

- **Seven-Step Process** — The framework consists of seven steps that guide designers through solving an exercise, with practice making each step faster and transitions feel more natural.
- **Collaborative Real-World Context** — In actual product development, most framework steps involve collaboration with other team members like product managers and data analysts, so candidates should not worry about achieving perfection in every step.
- **Step 6 as Critical Differentiator** — UI/UX execution in Step 6 is the core skill expected at any seniority level and is typically the designer's sole responsibility, unlike other steps where collaboration is normal.
- **Flexible Application** — The framework should not be applied blindly; designers must use judgment to adapt it appropriately for specific tasks.
- **Support Tools Provided** — A framework canvas for printing and a cheat sheet are available to guide practice and remind designers of each step and relevant questions.

## Quotes

> The framework may feel like a lot of work at first, but after practicing this technique you'll see that most of the steps take just a couple of minutes and the transition between them feels natural.

> However, the single step you must perfect is Step 6, where you have to demonstrate your UI/UX skills. These are the core skills a designer at any level of seniority is expected to have and, unlike other steps, there is a chance you're the only person in the company who will be able to solve it.

> Finally, before we dive into the framework — don't follow it blindly. Like any framework, it can't perfectly fit every task, so use your judgement as to when to apply it.
