# Journey-Mapping Impact: Research Findings

## Summary

Nielsen Norman Group surveyed over 300 UX professionals to understand how journey maps are used in industry and their perceived success. Journey maps visualize a user's journey toward a goal over time and across channels, and serve as powerful tools for evaluating experience and establishing shared understanding among team members. The research examined different approaches to creating journey maps, the impact and success ratings across seven organizational outcomes, and the critical factors for successful journey-mapping initiatives.

## Key Takeaways

- **Collaborative creation is more common** — 64% of respondents created their last journey map collaboratively with a team, while 36% created maps independently.
- **Digital tools dominate but tool choice doesn't matter for success** — 56% used digital tools like Miro or Google Sheets, versus 44% using tangible tools like sticky notes; success ratings were similar regardless of tool choice.
- **Journey maps succeed most at education and alignment** — Practitioners rated journey-mapping initiatives as moderately successful (3.5/5 or higher) across six of seven areas, particularly excelling at educating internal parties about pain points (4.06/5) and creating alignment among teams (3.97/5).
- **Collaborative teams report higher alignment success** — Those who created maps collaboratively reported marginally significant higher success rates for creating internal alignment compared to independent creators.
- **Involvement of customers and cross-functional teams matter most** — Of eight critical factors for success, involving customers (rank 6.08) and cross-functional teams (rank 5.81) ranked highest, followed by selecting the right journey (5.21) and persona/segment (4.85).
- **Visual design ranks lowest among success factors** — While graphically designed and visually appealing maps matter for communication, visual design received the lowest importance ranking (2.18) among eight critical factors for successful initiatives.

## Quotes

> "most journey maps are created collaboratively with a team of individuals. Out of 259 responses, 64% (95% confidence interval: 58% to 70%) said they created their last journey map collaboratively with a team."

> "Across the board, practitioners rated their last journey-mapping initiative as moderately successful in 6 out 7 areas."

> "those who created their maps collaboratively with a team reported higher success rates for creating internal alignment. This is logical because, when a team cocreates the map (rather than being presented with it later), it is more likely to trust the insights."
