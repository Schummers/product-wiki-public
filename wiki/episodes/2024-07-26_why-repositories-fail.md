# Why Research Repositories Fail and How to Get Them Right

## Summary

This article synthesizes research from over 400 UX and ResearchOps professionals to identify why research repositories often fail to gain adoption. Key barriers include low organizational UX maturity, tool dissatisfaction, lack of clear ownership, and high contribution effort. The article provides seven practical recommendations from practitioners including choosing appropriate tools, setting clear goals, taking a user-centered design approach to the repository itself, and sustaining commitment to advocacy and maintenance.

## Key Takeaways

- **Tool choice impacts adoption** — collaboration software has lowest satisfaction and adoption rates; user-research platforms and database tools report higher adoption despite higher cost due to better usability and search capabilities.
- **Repositories need clear ownership** — 29% of repositories had no owner; without dedicated governance and advocacy, repositories quickly become disorganized and forgotten.
- **Low UX maturity prevents success** — organizations without sufficient UX maturity struggle to see research repository value; stakeholder buy-in and understanding of research importance are prerequisites.
- **Contribution must be effortless** — if adding research to a repository requires excessive tagging or duplicated work, researchers deprioritize it; success requires integration into existing workflows.
- **Post-creation advocacy is essential** — building a repository is only half the work; ongoing marketing through newsletters, team meetings, Slack sharing, and regular reminders sustain adoption and engagement.
