# Articulating Design Decisions: Chapter 2. Stakeholders Are People Too

## Summary

This chapter shifts perspective from what designers communicate to how they should understand the people they communicate with. Greever argues that the foundation for better communication is improving relationships and earning trust with stakeholders by recognizing them as complex human beings with lives, pressures, and contexts beyond the current project. The chapter teaches four techniques for seeing stakeholders' perspective: seeing them as human (recognizing external factors influencing their reactions), creating shared experiences (finding common ground), developing empathy (understanding their viewpoint so deeply that you're driven to help them succeed), and asking good questions (a skill that builds relationships and reveals what matters to stakeholders). Greever then categorizes stakeholders into three types (team influencers, executive influencers, external influencers) and examines what specific roles (executives, developers, product owners, project managers, marketers) value. The chapter concludes that good relationships make communication easier and that simple practices—being yourself, doing things for people, and giving meaningful gifts—strengthen stakeholder relationships and make designs more acceptable.

## Key Takeaways

- **Relationships are the foundation** — Better communication with stakeholders requires first improving relationships, earning trust, and establishing rapport; the quality of relationships matters more than the words spoken in meetings.
- **Stakeholders are human with unpredictable lives** — Stakeholder reactions to design work are often shaped by external factors (hospital visits, personal stress, budget cuts) rather than personality or the design itself; designers should interpret feedback generously.
- **Shared experiences build trust** — Creating common ground through lunch, conferences, or finding connections (pets, interests, hobbies) makes stakeholders more willing to support designers and more receptive to their expertise.
- **Empathy drives action** — True empathy for stakeholders means seeing the project from their perspective, shifting from defensive positioning to helping them succeed; this mindset enables better communication and willingness to accommodate legitimate needs.
- **Asking good questions reveals perspectives** — Like UX practitioners with users, designers should ask stakeholders open-ended questions about their work, priorities, and lives to understand what drives their reactions and what matters to them.
- **Different roles value different things** — Executives care about vision and reporting, developers care about quality and feasibility, product owners care about innovation and simplicity, project managers care about schedule and scope—understanding these values shapes how to communicate with each.
- **Simple relationship practices multiply impact** — Being authentic, checking in regularly, sending handwritten notes, and giving thoughtful small gifts all signal that the designer values stakeholders as people, not just decision-makers.

## Quotes

> What's ironic to me is that UXers are so good at putting the user first, at garnering empathy for and attempting to see the interface from the perspective of the user. Yet, we often fail to do the same thing for the people who hold the keys to our success.

> Our job is to learn how to make our stakeholders successful, because if we can help them be successful, that will help us be successful as a byproduct.

> Communication is much easier in good relationships.

## Concepts

- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — This chapter provides a framework for understanding and working with stakeholders: seeing them as human, understanding their role-based values, recognizing influencer types, and building relationships as the foundation for productive conversations.
- [[Active Listening]] — Greever teaches the skill of asking good questions—both personal and work-related—as the primary tool for understanding stakeholders' perspectives and what drives their reactions.
- [[Empathy]] — The chapter emphasizes that designers must develop true empathy for stakeholders, shifting from seeing them as obstacles to seeing their success as intertwined with design success.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Understanding different team roles and what each values enables better collaboration; developers, product owners, project managers, and marketers each need different approaches.
- [[Design Presentation]] — By understanding stakeholders' priorities and constraints, designers can present work in ways that appeal to their needs rather than designers' own priorities.
