# Continuous Discovery Habits: Chapter Six: Mapping the Opportunity Space

## Summary

This chapter addresses the challenge of taking an inventory of countless customer needs, pain points, and desires—all discovered through interviews—and giving structure to them so teams can systematically prioritize. The chapter introduces the opportunity solution tree as the core technique for organizing and visualizing the opportunity space in a way that prevents teams from jumping randomly from one customer need to another.

Rather than managing flat, unstructured backlogs of opportunities, the tree structure allows teams to organize opportunities by parent-child (subset) and sibling (distinct but related) relationships. This structure makes visible the dependencies and overlaps that plague flat lists, enabling teams to break large intractable problems into smaller, more solvable sub-opportunities and to work iteratively rather than on massive, long-running projects. The chapter shows how both experience maps (from Chapter 4) and interview patterns can drive the identification of distinct moments or branches in the opportunity space, ensuring no overlap between parent opportunities.

## Key Takeaways

- **Trees organize complexity better than flat lists** — Parent-child and sibling relationships reveal how sub-opportunities relate to larger needs; this structure lets teams solve smaller problems in sequence, delivering iterative value rather than waiting for big solutions.
- **Identify distinct moments as top-level branches** — Map your top-level opportunities to distinct moments in the customer's journey (e.g., deciding, choosing, watching, ending); this ensures branches are independent and non-overlapping.
- **Filter opportunities through discovery questions** — Only add opportunities that (1) are framed as customer needs, not solutions, (2) appear in more than one interview, and (3) align with your desired outcome; avoid opportunities that are side quests.
- **Add structure by grouping and finding parent opportunities** — Group similar opportunities (siblings), identify parent opportunities that encompass them, and merge redundant phrasings; iterate until each branch descends from its top-level parent.
- **Avoid anti-patterns: solutions, broad framing, missing parents** — Distinguish opportunities (multiple potential solutions) from solutions (one path); keep opportunities specific and customer-framed; fill in missing parent opportunities to avoid vertical stacks; reframe opportunities that belong under multiple parents as too broad.

## Concepts

- [[Opportunity Solution Tree]] — This chapter details the core tool for organizing customer opportunities: a tree structure that breaks large customer needs into hierarchical parent-child and sibling relationships, preventing overlap and enabling iterative problem-solving.
- [[Product Discovery]] — Continuous interviewing generates countless opportunities; opportunity mapping is the structural process that organizes these discoveries into a frame teams can reason about, assess, and systematically prioritize.
- [[Decision Making]] — The tree structure fundamentally changes how teams make strategic decisions; instead of comparing many opportunities at once, teams now compare sibling opportunities at each level, using the tree to optimize decisions and avoid analysis paralysis.
