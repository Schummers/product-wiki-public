# Intranet-Search Essentials

## Summary

Based on usability testing across 15 organizations, this article identifies critical practices for effective intranet search. Employees expect intranet search to work like Google but often become frustrated when it fails. The article outlines 11 design practices and process practices to improve search, emphasizing that intranet search quality depends on both interface design and the underlying content, metadata, and organizational processes that support it.

## Key Takeaways

- **Expose search prominently** — Use a persistent search box in the upper right corner, not just a magnifying glass icon; this primes users to search and is consistent with web conventions.
- **Unify search across content** — Avoid multiple search boxes for different content types; instead, index all content, people, and tools in a single search to reduce confusion.
- **Design placeholder text carefully** — Placeholder text should indicate what users can search for without appearing pre-filled; it should persist until typing begins and reappear if users delete text.
- **Type-ahead suggestions need timing** — Search suggestions should be automatic and relevant, but must preserve the ability to search the original keyword entered and not interfere with Enter-key behavior.
- **Clean, scannable results** — Separate results into distinct sections for content, people, and tools; include dates, metadata labels, and icons to help users assess relevance quickly.
- **Strong metadata is foundational** — Coherent page titles, meta descriptions, and tags are essential; without clear naming conventions and processes, search results become meaningless.
- **Recency matters** — Users check publication dates to assess relevance; recent content ranks higher than old, and outdated content should be removed through auditing and retirement processes.

## Quotes

> Intranet search is a huge thing for our employees and many of them prefer to search. Because of that, we have to get our search right. When people become frustrated by it, they're not likely to engage with search or even the intranet again.

> Employees' initial expectations for intranet search were high. They expected the intranet's search to work like Google and were repeatedly disappointed when it didn't.

> Intranet search is only as good as the content behind it, so don't leave this foundational design element behind.

## Concepts

- [[Search]] — A critical employee tool in intranets that must be fast, accurate, and integrated; requires careful design and ongoing maintenance to meet user expectations shaped by Google.
- [[Information Architecture]] — The underlying structure that supports effective search through clear naming conventions, consistent metadata, and organized content; essential elements include page titles, meta descriptions, and tags that enable search to deliver relevant results.
