# Startup Studio Playbook: Core Team

## Summary

Part III of the Startup Studio Playbook introduces the foundational challenge of building a core team for a venture studio. Unlike standalone startups where teams focus on a single product, studio teams must simultaneously build multiple ventures, requiring broader competencies and flexible organizational structures. The chapter addresses how to assemble a team with the necessary skills, allocate resources across projects, and create a culture of trust that enables both experimentation and accountability.

The core insight is that studio success depends less on individual talent than on the cultural foundation that allows teams to work across multiple initiatives with clear prioritization. Compensation, trust, and flexibility are the three levers that enable sustainable venture production at scale.

## Key Takeaways

- **Skill Matrix — A studio must cover all competencies** a startup needs internally: development, design, marketing, sales, legal, finance, HR. This is not a luxury but a cost optimization strategy; by spreading specialized roles across multiple ventures, the per-startup payroll shrinks compared to independent startups building their own full teams.

- **Team Structure Varies — Different studios organize differently,** from fully dedicated teams per venture (Betaworks model) to centralized competencies with rotating leadership (Quasar Ventures) to almost entirely shared resources with only the CEO dedicated (Drukka). The choice depends on studio strategy and portfolio size.

- **Resource Allocation is Psychological — Beyond the logistics** of who works on what, the emotional weight matters. Team members and CEOs must accept that multiple projects run in parallel, some fail, and people move between initiatives. Early studios struggle most with this mindset shift.

- **Time Tracking and Standard Tools — Use simple time management** tools to track allocation across projects so effort is transparent, and adopt standard stacks (Slack, Dropbox, GSuite, Jira) rather than building studio-specific infrastructure.

- **Trust Enables Scaling — The foundational culture** must generate ownership in team members beyond equity, enable safe experimentation, and allow startups to become independent from the studio. Micromanagement from leadership dilutes responsibility and wastes the founder's time on execution rather than strategy.

- **Compensation: Salary and Portfolio Equity — Pay core team salaries** competitive enough to attract and retain talent building multiple ventures. Consider an equity pool that gives every team member a symbolic share in all portfolio companies, so success of any venture benefits everyone.

## Quotes

> Building the Core Team of a startup studio is even bigger challenge than building the initial team of a standalone startup. In the latter case, people will have to focus on one product, one business. In case of a studio, people will build multiple startups at the same time.

> If you want to be able to produce more than a dozen ventures, you have to learn to give room to your Team. Trust them.

> The base compensation of your core team will be salary: pay them well if you want them to create new ventures with passion.
