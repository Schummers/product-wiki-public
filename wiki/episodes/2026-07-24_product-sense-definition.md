# A Concrete Definition of "Product Sense" (and How to Build It)

## Summary

This article provides a concrete, measurable definition of product sense to replace loose existing definitions that often reduce to "empathy plus creativity." The article proposes that product sense is the ability to recognize when current problems match past successes or failures and reliably estimate how similar solutions will affect desired outcomes. This definition turns product sense from a vague combination of qualities into a skill that depends on closing the full experimentation loop: facing problems, choosing solutions, measuring outcomes, and reflecting on results. The article also explains that strong product sense includes knowing when past patterns do not apply, and identifies three categories of situations based on validity—high-validity, low-validity, and wicked situations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Product sense is recognizing pattern matches and reliably estimating outcomes** — It is the ability to identify when a current problem resembles past successes or failures and predict how similar solutions will perform. This is more measurable and concrete than definitions based on empathy and creativity.
- **Product sense requires closing the full experimentation loop** — Builders must face problems, choose solutions, implement them, measure outcomes, and reflect on results. Missing pieces of this loop weakens future product sense. You cannot develop intuition without seeing the results of your work.
- **Strong product sense means knowing when patterns don't apply** — Experts must recognize both when an experience-based pattern is likely to work and when it is not. High-validity situations (familiar environments) allow accurate pattern matching. Low-validity situations (unfamiliar or lacking clear feedback) require more evidence. Wicked situations feel familiar but their solutions do not fit, which is most dangerous.
- **Too many builders work without closing the loop** — Teams receive requests, create solutions, and move on without tracking results. They rely on intuition without data, or spend time fixing bugs in products they did not design. AI accelerates this pattern, robbing builders of the chance to develop product sense.
- **Document your hypotheses before looking at results** — Be specific about what quantitative or qualitative outcomes you expect from your choice. Measure real outcomes, not just output quality. Reflect on how well decisions held up and which past experiences this situation most resembles.
- **Stay long enough to see results before changing projects** — If you move departments or jobs without finishing what you started and observing outcomes, you are not developing robust product sense.

## Quotes

> "AI can now handle many tasks that once made product builders valuable. As a result, designers and engineers are under growing pressure to decide what to build, not just how to build it."

> "Product sense requires you to close the experimentation loop while keeping intuition sharp."

> "Intuition comes with time and repeated exposure to real product decisions and results. AI may rob you of the chance to develop product sense if you aren't careful. Stay in the ring, own your decisions, and face the results."
