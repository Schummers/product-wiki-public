# Cookie Permissions 101

## Summary

With increasing data protection regulations like GDPR, CCPA, and VCDPA, websites must provide users clear options to control cookie permissions. A usability study of 10 participants revealed common user behaviors when interacting with cookie permission designs and identified five main design considerations. Users divide into distinct types based on their willingness to accept cookies, from deniers who minimize data sharing to enthusiasts open to personalized experiences. Effective cookie-permission designs must balance providing users autonomy over their data with building trust.

## Key Takeaways

- **User types vary significantly** — from deniers who adjust cookies to share minimal data, to impatient users who click accept all to dismiss the overlay quickly, with different types requiring different design approaches.
- **Multiple options must be immediately available** — hiding deny or customize options behind additional clicks (like "Learn more" buttons) reduces trust and makes users feel the site is forcing them toward accepting all cookies.
- **Avoid deceptive UI patterns** — toggle switches with unclear labels, high-contrast accept buttons, and ambiguous close buttons that trigger accept-all instead of deny trick users into opting in to unwanted cookies.
- **Use plain language in cookie descriptions** — vague or similar-sounding options like two options both starting with "Accept" prevent users from quickly scanning and choosing the option right for them.
- **Minimize overlay size and avoid competing overlays** — large cookie permission boxes that obscure page content annoy users, and multiple simultaneous overlays (cookies, newsletters, chat) significantly deteriorate the user experience.

## Concepts

- [[Privacy]] — cookie permission designs are a critical way websites communicate respect for user privacy and data control.
- [[Consent Management]] — the article discusses how to effectively obtain and manage user consent for different cookie categories in compliance with regulations.
- [[Deceptive Patterns]] — specific deceptive patterns in cookie designs (toggle switches, high-contrast buttons, unclear close buttons) trick users into sharing more data than intended.
- [[User Control]] — designs must provide users with clear autonomy to accept, deny, or customize which types of cookies to allow.
- [[Regulatory Compliance]] — cookie permission designs must comply with GDPR, CCPA, and VCDPA requirements that users actively opt into non-necessary cookies.
