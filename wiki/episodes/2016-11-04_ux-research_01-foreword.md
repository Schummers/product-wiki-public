# UX Research: Foreword

## Summary

Written by Steve Portigal, this foreword frames the philosophy of user research in product design. It contrasts the data-driven approach of earlier academic research (exemplified by Roger Barker's decades-long contextual study of a Kansas town) with the question-driven approach that characterizes modern product design. The foreword emphasizes that effective research requires skill, reflection, mentorship, and training; it is fundamentally an act of human connection and learning about what we did not know we did not know.

## Key Takeaways

- **Question-driven research** — In product design, research begins with specific questions we need answered in order to build or improve something, unlike earlier academic research that privileged data collection over questions.
- **Discovery of unknowns** — A vital outcome of research is learning about what we didn't know that we didn't know; answering initial questions is necessary but often insufficient to meaningfully impact a product.
- **Research as human practice** — Research is fundamentally about interaction between people; it gives practitioners a different way of engaging in all their relationships, and it is unabashedly human.
- **Skill and practice required** — While research may seem to have a low barrier to entry, it actually requires practice, reflection, mentorship, and training to do well; people must learn to be patient, present, and curious.
- **Professional and personal balance** — Researchers must navigate competing pressures: planning and reflecting while teams demand schedules and budgets, remaining present and patient while teams show impatience and excitement.

## Quotes

> In product design today, we start with questions. Those questions may include a hypothesis, an assumption, or a set of missing information, but for the most part we set out to conduct research because there's something that we need to know in order to build or improve a product, tool, service, or the like.

> But a vital outcome of research (not just the data that is gathered but the experiences we have in gathering it) is learning about what we didn't know that we didn't know.

> Research is about us as people and how we are with other people, which is fundamental to how we spend our time on earth.

## Concepts

- [[User Research]] — The philosophical foundations of user research as a question-driven, human-centered practice distinct from purely academic data collection.
- [[Research Planning]] — The importance of starting with good questions and planning research around specific information needs rather than the collection of data for its own sake.
- [[Empathy]] — Research as an exercise in human connection: Portigal writes that it is "about us as people and how we are with other people," and reports that people learning research skills say it gives them a different handle on their other interactions, with coworkers, friends and loved ones.
