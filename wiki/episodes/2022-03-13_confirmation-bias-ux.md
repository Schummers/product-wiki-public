# Confirmation Bias in UX

## Summary

Confirmation bias, a tendency to interpret new information in ways that conform to existing beliefs, poses a significant threat to UX research and design. The stronger the emotional investment in a design, the stronger the confirmation bias becomes. The article explains how confirmation bias manifests in UX (people ignore contradictory evidence, seek confirming evidence, interpret ambiguous evidence in favor of their beliefs), and provides practical strategies for preventing it. These strategies include adopting a research mindset instead of validation, obtaining early empirical data, asking nonbiasing questions, using triangulation from multiple data sources, and involving fresh eyes in analysis.

## Key Takeaways

- **Confirmation bias intensifies with investment** — designers who have invested more time and emotion in a design are more likely to interpret research findings as validation.
- **People discard contradictory information** even when it is factual, preferring to maintain belief consistency.
- **Leading survey questions** prime participants and bias the study toward confirming the researcher's hypothesis rather than discovering actual issues.
- **Multiple data sources** provide credibility and make it harder to twist findings to match a hypothesis than when relying on a single source.
- **Fresh perspectives** from uninvolved colleagues can help identify confirmation bias that invested team members miss.
- **Start with research rather than validation** — approach studies with an open mindset testing hypotheses rather than confirming them.

## Quotes

> Confirmation bias will lead people to discard information that contradicts their existing beliefs, even if the information is factual.

> The more invested you are in your assumptions about the design or the users, the stronger the confirmation bias.

> By understanding how confirmation bias can impact both researchers' output and users' responses, UX practitioners can use practical methodologies for collecting actionable data that leads to well-designed products.
