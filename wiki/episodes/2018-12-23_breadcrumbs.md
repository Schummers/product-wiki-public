# Breadcrumbs: 11 Design Guidelines for Desktop and Mobile

## Summary

This article presents comprehensive guidelines for using breadcrumbs, a navigational element supporting wayfinding by showing users their location within a website's hierarchical structure. Breadcrumbs have been recommended by Nielsen Norman since 1995 due to their high usability benefit at minimal UI cost. The article covers 11 design guidelines split between desktop and mobile implementations, addressing breadcrumb purpose, structure, and context-specific considerations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Breadcrumbs supplement, don't replace primary navigation** — Breadcrumbs are a secondary navigational tool intended to augment global navigation bars and local section navigation, not replace them. They help users who arrive via external links understand site structure and navigate to related content.

- **Show hierarchical structure, not session history** — Breadcrumbs should represent the site's information hierarchy, not the sequence of pages visited during a session. Showing session history creates long, confusing trails with repetition and provides no value to users arriving from external links.

- **Include current page as non-linked final item** — The last breadcrumb should represent the current page and should not be a link, visually differentiated from preceding linked items through underline or color to avoid confusion.

- **Mobile breadcrumbs create space costs** — On mobile devices, breadcrumbs often wrap to multiple lines or become too small/crowded to tap accurately. Consider truncating trails to show only the final level(s) or omitting breadcrumbs entirely on mobile if users' tasks don't require hierarchical navigation.

- **Breadcrumbs not needed for shallow hierarchies** — Sites with only 1–2 levels, flat structures, or linear experiences (like tutorials) don't benefit from breadcrumbs. Reserve breadcrumbs for hierarchically structured sites where users benefit from understanding location.

## Quotes

> "Breadcrumbs are an important navigational element that supports wayfinding — making users aware of their current location within the hierarchical structure of a website."

> "If users reach a deep page after traveling from the homepage through all the ancestor pages of that page, they will have a fairly clear understanding of where they are in the site's information hierarchy."

> "On mobile sites, breadcrumbs can quickly wrap to multiple lines, and eat up valuable space on an already crowded mobile display."

## Concepts

- [[Breadcrumb Navigation]] — A secondary navigation element showing a user's location within a site hierarchy, presented as a trail of links to ancestor pages, separated by symbols like ">".

- [[Information Architecture]] — Hierarchical site structure that breadcrumbs represent and help users navigate, showing the relationship between pages and helping users understand overall site organization.

- [[Navigation Design]] — Helping users understand where they are within a larger space and how to navigate; breadcrumbs support wayfinding by displaying hierarchical location without requiring navigation controls.
