# Customer Journeys and Omnichannel User Experience

## Summary

Modern customer journeys are more connected than ever, spanning multiple devices and interaction channels such as web, mobile apps, email, online chat, social media, text message, phone, and kiosks. However, UX remains siloed to individual products and channels rather than being designed holistically across the entire customer journey. Omnichannel user experience addresses this gap by designing the complete journey as a unified experience rather than a collection of isolated channel interactions. Five components create effective omnichannel experiences: consistency (familiar experience across channels), optimization (channel-specific experiences suited to each platform), seamlessness (effortless transitions between channels), orchestration (proactive personalized interactions at the right time), and collaboration (enabling simultaneous use of multiple channels). Organizations must stop thinking about discrete channel solutions and instead design for holistic journeys that reflect how customers actually experience their interactions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Omnichannel vs. multichannel** — Simply existing on all channels is not enough; multichannel is not omnichannel. Omnichannel design focuses on both creating appropriate experiences on each channel AND designing smooth transitions and experiences across channels.
- **Five components of omnichannel** — Consistency, optimization, seamlessness, orchestration, and collaboration together create effective omnichannel experiences.
- **Users think holistically, not by channel** — Users do not think in terms of channels and devices; they experience their interaction with an organization as one holistic journey. Fragmented experiences across channels are not tolerated.
- **Complexity of multiple devices** — New devices like smartphones, tablets, and smartwatches introduce new interaction possibilities and contexts but also multiply opportunities for failure if not coordinated.
- **Changing customer expectations** — Users now expect streamlined, personalized, enjoyable experiences with the ability to smoothly transition between devices and engage across many channels simultaneously.

## Quotes

> A customer journey is the end-to-end process that a customer goes through in order to complete a task over time. This process may use multiple devices and interaction channels (e.g., web, desktop or mobile apps, email, online chat, social media, text message, phone, kiosks).

> To every single customer, a journey is one holistic interaction with your organization, one experience rather than a collection of individual experiences. Users don't think in terms of channels and devices, so they will not understand or tolerate experiences that are fragmented across channels.

> Omnichannel strategies focus on designing the most appropriate experience on each channel based on the customer's journey, and on designing the transitions and experiences across channels.

## Concepts

- [[Customer Journey]] — Modern customer journeys span multiple channels and devices; designing for the complete journey requires understanding all touchpoints and their relationships.
- [[Omnichannel Experience]] — Omnichannel design integrates experiences across channels, ensuring consistency, seamlessness, and personalization throughout the customer journey, while optimizing for each channel's specific context and constraints.
- [[Touchpoints]] — Touchpoints are moments when customers interact with the organization; in omnichannel design, all touchpoints must work together to support the overall journey.
- [[UX Strategy]] — Effective UX strategy must encompass the full customer journey, not just individual channels or products.
