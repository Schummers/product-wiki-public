# Remote Moderated Usability Tests: Why to Do Them

## Summary

This article advocates for remote moderated usability testing as a middle-ground approach combining advantages of in-person and unmoderated testing. Remote moderated testing allows facilitators to ask follow-up questions, clarify instructions, reorder tasks, and coach participants to think aloud while avoiding in-person constraints and costs. The article compares three formats (in-person moderated, remote moderated, remote unmoderated) across eight dimensions and identifies when remote moderated is most appropriate.

## Key Takeaways

- **Hybrid Approach Benefits** — remote moderated testing delivers high-quality findings comparable to in-person studies while maintaining convenience and low cost similar to unmoderated remote testing.
- **Facilitator Advantages** — facilitators can change, skip, and reorder tasks as needed; ask follow-up questions and clarifications; encourage think-aloud protocols naturally; conduct longer sessions (about one hour) enabling in-depth exploration.
- **Reduced Participant Distraction** — having a facilitator present makes sessions feel more natural and reduces multitasking or environmental distractions compared to unmoderated self-directed testing.
- **Team Observation** — teams can watch sessions simultaneously and discuss findings immediately afterward, enabling real-time insight capture and reduced analysis time.
- **Unmoderated Drawbacks** — limited to short sessions (typically 20 minutes), no clarification of ambiguous instructions, inability to ask follow-ups or request elaboration, unrepresentative testers, variable participant motivation and focus.
- **Resource Requirements** — recruiting matching target audiences requires effort but can be sourced through recruitment agencies, existing user databases, websites, social networks, and referrals; meeting software setup requires planning.
- **When to Use** — choose remote moderated when seeking deep, rich insights; when participants are geographically distributed or cannot travel; when researchers have time for individual sessions.

## Quotes

> In contrast, remote moderated usability testing combines the advantages of both methods (in-person and remote unmoderated): it can deliver high-quality findings (comparable to in-person testing), but is convenient and inexpensive (like remote unmoderated).

> Acting under the (correct) assumption that any user data is better than no data, many teams are turning to remote usability testing.

> Remote unmoderated testing has the benefit or being fast, inexpensive, and easy.
