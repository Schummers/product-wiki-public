# Scrolling and Attention

## Summary

Recent eyetracking studies show that users spend about 57% of their page-viewing time above the fold and 74% in the first two screenfuls, compared to 80% above the fold in 2010. While the fold's importance has decreased somewhat, attention still drops sharply below it. Content organization follows an F-pattern with more attention to top content; users rarely scroll beyond the third screenful. Designers must prioritize content, use visual hierarchy to guide attention, include scrolling signifiers to combat the illusion of completeness, and test page length with real users.

## Key Takeaways

- **Attention concentration** — over 40% of viewing time on general websites occurs in the top 20% of content; content farther down the page receives dramatically less attention.
- **Fold barrier evolution** — the fold has moved down from the first screenful to approximately the third screenful; 81% of viewing time occurs in the first three screenfuls of content.
- **F-pattern scanning** — users concentrate on content toward the top of the page and top-left areas, spending more fixations and time on opening text before attention tails off.
- **Visual signifiers** — false floors created by minimalist design and excess white space reduce scrolling; text cut-off and other signifiers help indicate content below the fold.
- **Content prioritization** — high-priority content and major calls-to-action must appear above the fold; secondary or supporting information can occupy lower positions.

## Quotes

> In our most recent study, users spent about **57% of their page-viewing time above the fold. 74% of the viewing time was spent in the first two screenfuls**, up to 2160px.

> The interaction cost of scrolling reduces the likelihood that content will be viewed in lower parts of a longer page.

> People still don't scroll a lot — they rarely go beyond the third screenful of info.

## Concepts

- [[User Behavior]] — understanding how users predictably allocate visual attention when scanning pages, prioritizing top content over lower sections.
- [[Eye Tracking]] — using eyetracking data to reveal attention distribution and identify which interface elements capture user focus.
- [[Web Usability]] — designing page layout and content hierarchy to align with natural user scanning patterns and attention allocation.
