# Applying UX Principles to the Visual Design of Graphical Artifacts: The Case of the Heuristics Posters

## Summary

This article demonstrates how user-centered design methods typically applied to interactive products can also improve static artifacts. The authors describe creating and iterating on a set of posters explaining the 10 usability heuristics through a three-phase process: low-fidelity wireframing to explore layout and structure, high-fidelity iteration focusing on visual hierarchy and illustrations, and user testing with both general participants and UX professionals. The project exemplifies how defining clear design goals, wireframing, iterative design, and testing produce better artifacts even when creating non-interactive deliverables. The iterative approach prevented premature pixel-perfect work and allowed the team to catch and fix details that significantly impact user perception.

## Key Takeaways

- **Define design goals before starting** — Clear project goals (improve readability, make posters visually appealing, ensure relevance to diverse expertise levels, create a cohesive set) provide a reference point for resolving tradeoffs throughout the project.
- **Start with low-fidelity exploration** — Hand-drawn and digital low-fidelity wireframes allow rapid exploration of layout alternatives without investing time in visual details; work with real content to identify structural issues early.
- **Iterative high-fidelity refinement improves execution** — Play with typeface sizes, color, element scale, and visual hierarchy; use techniques like the squint test to verify that visual attention flows where intended.
- **Illustrations need context** — Illustrations that work in isolation often confuse users without surrounding context; add visual cues (3D perspective, scale references, people) to make illustrations immediately recognizable.
- **User testing reveals misaligned associations** — Testing with target users identified that readers were linking illustrations to the wrong text because of proximity; color-coding the illustration to match its corresponding text resolved the confusion.
- **Physical prototyping informs final design** — Printing tile-printed posters at home revealed visual dynamics that screen design alone could not show, allowing refinement of details that impact the physical artifact's actual use.
