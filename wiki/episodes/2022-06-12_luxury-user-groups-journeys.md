# Luxury Shopping User Groups and Journeys

## Summary

Based on qualitative research with 9 participants, this article identifies four types of luxury shoppers and documents their shopping journeys. Professional stylists buy for clients using platforms like Farfetch; window shoppers aspire to luxury but cannot yet afford it; occasional splurgers view purchases as investments and special treats; big spenders are habitual luxury buyers. The luxury journey consists of four phases: Discover, Consider, Purchase, and Use. The Consider phase is the critical opportunity for digital experiences, as luxury shoppers need information and reassurance before committing significant investments.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four luxury shopper types** — Professional stylists prefer third-party platforms; window shoppers aspire but don't buy; occasional splurgers invest carefully; big spenders purchase frequently and have brand loyalty.
- **Big spenders are brand experts** — They invest time understanding brand history and leadership before purchasing, treating luxury as a hobby; they expect limited product information to act as an entry barrier.
- **Relationships matter** — Big spenders often have dedicated sales representatives they trust; they value personal relationships and extra perks, treating purchases as club membership experiences.
- **Consider phase is critical** — This longest phase involves extensive research; luxury sites often fail with poor organization, few photos, missing details, and bad recommendations, damaging brand perception.
- **Online research dominates** — Luxury shoppers visit brand sites, department stores, and platforms like Net-a-Porter; they look for reviews and ask friends before deciding.
- **Anxiety about returns** — Luxury purchases are significant financial and emotional investments; customers worry about returns, shipping damage, and ungenerous return policies.
- **Logo preferences vary** — Occasional splurgers want visible logos; big spenders prefer subtle branding to avoid appearing flashy or attracting theft; reversible designs appealed to those managing multiple contexts.

> The person who's gotten me the majority of the [Chanel] pieces that I have, she knows me. She knows about my life. It's almost like you have a friend in the industry who's recommending pieces.

## Concepts

- [[E-Commerce]] — Luxury e-commerce requires special attention to the consideration phase, product information, and brand storytelling to meet shopper expectations shaped by offline luxury retail experiences.
- [[Customer Journey]] — Understanding luxury shopper journeys across discover, consider, purchase, and use phases reveals where digital experiences succeed or fail to meet expectations.
- [[Persona]] — Luxury shoppers segment into professional buyers, aspirational followers, occasional investors, and habitual collectors, each with distinct behaviors and needs.
