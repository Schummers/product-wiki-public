# 27 Tips and Tricks for Conducting Successful User Research in the Field

## Summary

This article compiles practical advice for conducting field research — user studies conducted in participants' natural environments rather than labs. Drawing from Nielsen Norman's extensive experience, the article covers three phases: pre-research planning, during-study execution, and post-research wrap-up. Key themes include recruiting diverse participants, managing observers, thorough documentation, and maintaining data confidentiality. The guidance emphasizes that successful field research requires careful planning, flexibility to adapt protocols as learning occurs, and attention to environmental and cultural context.

## Key Takeaways

- **Recruitment must prioritize authentic users** — monitor recruiting closely to avoid professional research volunteers or superusers; instead seek diverse participants in terms of ethnicity, role, gender, and task experience.
- **Observers enhance and complicate sessions** — observers can provide valuable context and become advocates for change, but their presence must be managed carefully to avoid overwhelming participants.
- **Documentation is critical and immediate** — take notes even when recording, number participants and documents for privacy, date everything for version control, and debrief observations right after each session.
- **Adapt protocol as you learn** — prepare stakeholders for the reality that research questions may evolve across sessions as insights emerge, unlike surveys where consistency is required.
- **Environment shapes findings** — carefully consider whether sessions should occur in natural settings (to capture realistic context and distractions) or controlled spaces (for privacy and focus).
- **Context cues provide deep insight** — pay attention to environmental details, habits, assumptions, and the cultural norms of the space, not just explicit participant statements.

## Quotes

> "User research in the field is an effective way to learn about users and their context. It typically takes place where users live, play, or work. The following lessons learned from our own studies can help you avoid common problems."

> "When conducting research at a business location, **reserve a conference room or another private area** for the researchers and observers to use when not in sessions. A whiteboard and a projector might come in handy too. Make snacks and drinks available. Ensure that research participants can't overhear the team talking."

> "**Take good notes, even if you are allowed to make recordings**. Recordings take just as long to listen to as the original session, and recordings sometimes fail. Capturing observations and insights in real-time can be crucial."

## Concepts

- [[Field Research]] — conducting research in participants' natural environments (homes, workplaces) requires planning for environmental factors, managing observer presence, and careful attention to cultural context.
- [[Research Methods]] — field research is most effective when combined with clear documentation practices, diverse participant recruitment, and protocol flexibility to accommodate emerging insights.
- [[Research Planning]] — successful field studies require pre-research pilots, stakeholder preparation for adaptive protocols, consideration of location trade-offs, and post-session debriefing to capture insights while fresh.
