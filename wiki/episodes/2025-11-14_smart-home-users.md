# Beyond the Primary User: 3 Types of Smart-Home Users

## Summary

Smart home design typically assumes a single primary user, but household research reveals complexity. Smart devices are shared among family members with different roles, control needs, and decision-making authority. A diary study identified three user roles: device managers who drive adoption and configure devices, everyday users who control basic functions but rarely adjust settings, and restricted users with limited control despite being affected. Device location (private versus shared zones) and household rules determine control access, creating fluid role distributions where the same person has different roles for different devices, and the technical implementer often is not the decision maker.

## Key Takeaways

- **Location and rules separate control** — devices in private zones are controlled by occupants; shared devices can be accessed by everyone, but restrictions limit control (parents forbidding children from adjusting thermostats).
- **User roles are contextual and fluid** — same household member may be device manager for lights, everyday user for washing machine, restricted user for thermostat; roles shift over time as children gain access.
- **Device managers execute others' preferences** — tech-savvy managers act as household proxies, configuring automations based on others' unspoken preferences, deviating from UX assumptions that interface users are sole decision makers.
- **Fragmentation creates burden** — device managers must switch between manufacturers' apps for granular control and central hubs for basic functions, leading to app overload and becoming default intermediary for all requests.
- **Everyday users lack automation visibility** — when device managers set schedules, everyday users don't understand why devices behave unexpectedly and may turn off useful automations that conflict with immediate needs.
- **Minimize friction and dependency** — all basic device functions must work through on-device buttons or physical remotes, not just apps; support delegation, provide alerts about schedule overrides, and make automation behavior transparent.

## Concepts

- [[User Roles]] — Smart homes require design for three interdependent user roles (device managers who configure, everyday users who control basics, and restricted users with limited access), where user roles are fluid across different devices and household members often shift roles based on context and time.
- [[Household Technology]] — Shared household technology must account for how physical location determines access and how household rules distribute control; fragmentation across manufacturers' apps and central hubs creates cognitive burden on device managers who coordinate multiple members' needs and preferences.
- [[Device Design]] — Smart devices must support users beyond the primary installer; everyday users need transparent automation alerts and basic on-device controls, restricted users need reduced dependency, and all users need clearer visualization of how automations affect their immediate needs and device behavior.
