# Boost Design Autonomy with an Information Pipeline

## Summary

Design autonomy—the ability to make informed decisions about product direction, not just surface UI—is achieved by building an information pipeline within organizations. Interviews with designers who gained high autonomy revealed they gathered and synthesized information from multiple sources: cross-team analytics, support tickets, past research, roadmaps, and communication archives. They built relationships across teams, created cross-functional spaces for information sharing, and synthesized insights into credible recommendations. The pipeline requires ongoing maintenance but empowers designers to become strategic partners rather than executors of predefined solutions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Information as foundation** — Autonomy comes from becoming sufficiently informed to credibly shape shared product decisions, not from deciding alone.
- **Four pipeline parts** — Gathering from multiple sources, building relationships, creating cross-functional spaces, and synthesizing into big-picture context.
- **Information tracking** — Use central tracking (spreadsheet with project, owner, files, status, notes) to spot cross-team connections, avoid duplicate work, and preserve rationale.
- **Relationship building** — Partner with domain experts, map upstream/downstream dependencies, set expectations through design-operations guides.
- **Synthesis into recommendations** — Use evidence from multiple sources to propose solutions; show, don't tell through tradeoff tables comparing options.
