# F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web: Misunderstood, But Still Relevant (Even on Mobile)

## Summary

The F-shaped reading pattern, identified in 2006, persists in current eyetracking research on both desktop and mobile. Users read horizontally across the top, then horizontally across a shorter middle section, then scan vertically down the left side. However, the F-pattern is only one of several scanning patterns (layer-cake, spotted, marking, bypassing, commitment) and occurs only when text lacks formatting, users prioritize efficiency, and engagement is low. Good web formatting prevents F-pattern scanning by directing attention to important information rather than relying on user initiative.

## Key Takeaways

- **F-pattern characteristics** — horizontal reading at top, shorter horizontal movement middle, vertical scan of left side; skips right-side content and important information later in paragraphs.
- **Other scanning patterns exist** — layer-cake (headings), spotted (searching), marking (fixed gaze during scroll), bypassing (skipping repeated words), and commitment (full reading) are alternative patterns depending on content and motivation.
- **F-pattern is bad for users and businesses** — users miss important content simply because it appears on the right or below the top sections; responsive design creates flow variability that worsens the problem.
- **Root causes** — F-pattern occurs when text has no formatting, users seek efficiency, and engagement is low; it's the default when no strong visual cues guide eyes toward meaningful content.
- **Antidotes** — use important points in first two paragraphs, employ headings and subheadings, bold key words, group related content visually, use bullets and numbers, start headings with information-bearing words, and cut unnecessary content.

## Quotes

> The F-pattern is the default pattern when there are no strong cues to attract the eyes towards meaningful information.

> People tend to minimize interaction cost and maximize the benefit they get from the work they do.

> Make no mistake, the F-shaped scanning pattern is bad for users and businesses: it means that users may skip important content simply because it appears on the right side of the page.
