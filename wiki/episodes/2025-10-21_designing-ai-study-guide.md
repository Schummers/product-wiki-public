# Designing AI Products and Features: Study Guide

## Summary

This study guide compiles resources for designing AI products and features that actually solve real user problems rather than simply adding AI for novelty. Organized into five sections—strategy and value proposition (does AI add value?), prompt assistance (helping users craft better prompts), product-specific recommendations (implementing particular AI features well), state of AI-product design (time-stamped findings), and podcast episodes—the guide emphasizes that merely adding AI doesn't create value. Teams must ask hard questions about whether and how AI solves real pain points before investing.

## Key Takeaways

- **"Powered By AI" is not a value proposition** — Companies should lead with the value AI provides (better accuracy, faster synthesis, new capabilities), not count on AI to magically create value from novelty.
- **Narrow scope makes AI features understandable and adoptable** — Broadly scoped AI features are harder for users to understand and have lower adoption; focused, specific use cases are easier to grasp and more likely to succeed.
- **AI's four superpowers are content creation, summarization, data analysis, and perspective-taking** — Most successful AI integrations leverage one of these capabilities rather than attempting to replace entire workflows.
- **Prompt suggestions support users who don't know what to ask** — Users often don't know what AI is capable of or what to request; well-designed prompt suggestions contextualized to tasks help users explore and set realistic expectations.
- **Long responses, hallucinations, and reliability concerns are design challenges** — Even powerful AI outputs require design interventions: formatting for web readability, source citation for trust, and transparency about limitations.
- **Discoverability remains a major barrier** — Valuable AI features have low impact if users don't notice them; designers must address both discovery and understanding when integrating new AI capabilities.
