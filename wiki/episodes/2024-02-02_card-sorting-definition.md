# Card Sorting: Uncover Users' Mental Models for Better Information Architecture

## Summary

Card sorting is a specialized UX research method where participants organize labeled cards into groups based on their own logic, revealing how users naturally categorize content. This method helps designers create navigation structures that align with users' mental models. There are multiple card-sorting variations (open, closed, hybrid; moderated, unmoderated; in-person, remote) suited to different research goals. The process involves preparing 30-50 cards, recruiting 15+ participants for qualitative studies or 30-50 for quantitative studies, asking participants to sort and label groupings, and analyzing results using tools like OptimalSort. While valuable for generating information-architecture ideas, card sorting has limitations: it lacks broader context, shows only one categorization level, and can produce inconsistent results across participants requiring designer judgment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Card sorting reveals users' mental models** — observing how people naturally group items uncovers their assumptions about content relationships and organization.
- **30–50 cards is the optimal range** — this prevents participant fatigue while ensuring thoughtful groupings; with over 50 cards, participants tend to create oversized "Miscellaneous" categories.
- **Recruit 15+ for qualitative, 30–50 for quantitative** — qualitative studies emphasize understanding reasoning through think-aloud, while quantitative studies track frequency of groupings across larger samples.
- **Ask participants to label groups after sorting** — labeling after grouping prevents participants from limiting themselves to predefined categories and provides insight into their categorization logic.
- **Open card sorting is the most common approach** — researchers provide no predefined categories, allowing unbiased discovery of how users naturally organize content.
- **Results require designer judgment** — participants often group the same content differently; results are ideas for possible structures, not prescriptions, requiring additional research and critical thinking.

## Quotes

> Card sorting is a research method in which study participants place individually labeled cards into groups according to criteria that make the most sense to them.

> We recommend 30­–50 cards to prevent user fatigue and encourage thoughtful groupings from participants.

> In an open card sort, don't expect participants to create effective labels. Category names they come up with will not necessarily be your final labels.
