# The User Experience of Chatbots

## Summary

Chatbots are domain-specific conversational interfaces that support users with limited sets of tasks, requiring natural-language processing and intelligent interpretation but not necessarily voice output or agency. The article distinguishes between customer-service bots that handle support inquiries and interaction bots that enable transactions or tasks. Research with 8 participants revealed that while users appreciate the speed advantage of bots over humans, they encounter significant usability challenges. Bots struggle with script deviation, context preservation, and handling complex or ambiguous requests, limiting their practical value for many use cases.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two chatbot types serve different purposes** — Customer-service bots handle support queries while interaction bots enable transactions; customer-service bots are faster than human agents but perceived as less helpful and requiring transparency about being a bot.

- **Users quickly abandon scripts when encountering difficulties** — Bots work smoothly when users follow expected paths but fail when users deviate with unexpected inputs, requiring them to start over or forcing escalation without preserving context.

- **Transparency about bot identity improves interaction quality** — When users know they're talking to a bot, they adjust language to be more direct and keyword-focused, avoiding politeness markers that bots struggle to understand.

- **Linear flows with limited branching are the current bot limitation** — Bots function as decision trees that guide users through simple processes; they cannot handle complex needs or real conversation, making them feel like wizards rather than intelligent systems.

- **Carousels and limited display space constrain result presentation** — Showing long lists through carousels doesn't work well; users doubt they're seeing all options and worry about missing better alternatives not displayed.

- **Alternative input methods should complement text input** — Offering both predetermined buttons and free-text input gives users flexibility; forcing only one input method frustrates users.
