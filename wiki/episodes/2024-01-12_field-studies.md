# Field Studies

## Summary

Field studies are context research conducted in users' natural environments rather than labs or orchestrated settings. They fill gaps left by other research methods by revealing how products and services actually work in realistic use contexts with distractions, noise, and concurrent activities. Field studies can be purely observational (direct observation), involve researcher inquiry (contextual inquiry), combine observation with tours/walks (customer-site visits), or require complete immersion (ethnography). Field studies are best conducted before design begins in the discovery phase, though they can also serve as evaluative research later. Remote moderated research and lab research are alternatives when field studies are impractical, expensive, or when studying scenarios that cannot be ethically observed.

## Key Takeaways

- **Field studies observe users in their natural context** — providing data that directly applies to the audience and revealing problems that arise when new tools are introduced into existing work practices.
- **Four types of field studies range from purely observational to immersive** — direct observation (fly on wall), contextual inquiry (observation plus interview), customer-site visits (guided tour), and ethnography (complete immersion).
- **Field studies are most valuable in the discovery phase** — before design begins, though they can also evaluate existing prototypes in their intended context (field testing).
- **Remote moderated research and lab research are cost-effective alternatives** — when travel is expensive, participants are distributed geographically, or the scenario is impossible/unethical to observe.
- **Planning field studies requires careful consideration of research questions, participants, setting, timing, method, and permissions** — side-by-side chatter can bias results; observers must be managed; facility permissions are required.

## Quotes

> A field study is a type of context research that takes place in the user's natural environment (sometimes referred to as in situ, Latin for "in place") as opposed to a lab or an orchestrated setting.

> Observing users in real scenarios will provide you with specific data that directly applies to your audience. Field studies that focus on specific tasks help researchers learn how to best support these tasks.

> The context in which people do their tasks can reveal things you wouldn't know to ask about, such as problems that crop up when new tools or processes are introduced into existing work practices.

## Concepts

- [[Contextual Research]] — field studies are a primary contextual research method that reveals how context shapes user behavior in ways lab research cannot.
- [[Research Methods]] — field studies are one of several research methods; understanding when to use field studies vs. remote/lab methods is key for research planning.
- [[Field Studies]] — ethnography is one type of field study requiring complete immersion to understand cultural and social contexts affecting product use.
- [[Qualitative Research]] — field studies are qualitative research methods generating rich descriptive data about user behavior, mental models, and workarounds.
- [[Observational Research]] — observation is the primary method in field studies; researcher interventions (interviewing, probing) vary by field study type.
