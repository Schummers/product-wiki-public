# Information Architecture: Study Guide

## Summary

This article serves as a compilation and study guide for information architecture (IA) topics, organizing resources into three categories: IA and organization principles, research methods for IA, and navigation design. The guide is structured for readers new to IA to begin with fundamentals before progressing to specialized topics. IA principles covered include the distinction between IA and navigation, different organizational models, findability concepts, and common mistakes. Research methods include card sorting and tree testing, while navigation design addresses both desktop and mobile patterns, including utility navigation and specialized elements like breadcrumbs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Information architecture differs from navigation** — IA is the underlying organizational structure while navigation is the visible interface for accessing that structure.
- **Card sorting uncovers users' mental models** for how they naturally categorize content and information.
- **Tree testing evaluates whether users can find information** within a proposed IA structure without the distraction of visual design.
- **Navigation placement follows user expectations** — users look for navigation in consistent locations (top left/right corners on desktop).
- **Search and navigation coexist** — offering search does not eliminate the need for good navigation structures.
- **Mobile navigation requires different patterns** than desktop, including hamburger menus and alternative placement for language/country switchers.
