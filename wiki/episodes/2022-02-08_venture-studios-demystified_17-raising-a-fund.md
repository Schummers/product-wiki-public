# Venture Studios Demystified: Raising a Fund

## Summary

This chapter models the impact of raising a separate investment fund structured as a dual-entity alongside the studio operating company. Using a base assumption of four close-ended funds over a decade (starting at $45M and growing to $120M) with 3-year horizons, 2% management fees, and 20% carry, the chapter demonstrates how fund economics transform studio operating company viability. Management fees offset operating expenses, reducing incremental capital needs; carry returns can be reinvested to accelerate growth. The chapter compares fund-backed scenarios to the base case and concludes that raising a fund is essential to modern studio economics.

## Key Takeaways

- **Operating Model Transformation** — raising a fund reduces the operating company's incremental capital needs from $10M to $4M and keeps the studio near break-even through year 5, with management fees offsetting most operating expenses.
- **Management Fee Impact** — at 2% annually on a 3-year horizon, management fees serve as ongoing operational revenue that subsidizes studio activities and reduces fundraising requirements for the operating entity.
- **Carry Returns** — GPs receive 20% carry on fund exits; if reinvested into the studio holding company, this capital can accelerate growth without requiring additional external fundraising.
- **Combined Entity Returns** — the fund and operating company together yield 37% IRR and 24x MoM, compared to 24% IRR and 9x MoM for the operating company alone—a 50% IRR increase and nearly 3x multiple improvement.
- **Early Exit Scenarios** — with one early exit assumption, the studio would require only $24M cumulative capital and the operating company would achieve 50% IRR and 57x MoM, demonstrating leverage from fund structure.

## Quotes

> Having a fund makes the studio operating entity an even more attractive investment. Since the fund management fees are subsidizing studio operations, investors have to put in less capital for the same level of returns.

> Thus, raising a fund is beneficial for studios not only because it brings in a source of revenue through management fees but also because GPs receive stellar returns from follow-on investments at cheap valuations.

> Because of these benefits, it is no surprise that we are seeing most studios move toward including an investing fund.

## Concepts

- [[Studio Fundraising]] — the chapter's primary subject, modeling the financial mechanics and returns of a dedicated investment fund run alongside the operating company.
- [[Venture Capital]] — the fund is modeled as a venture investing vehicle with standard carry and management fee structures, enabling studios to participate in follow-on rounds.
- [[Studio Economics]] — fund structure directly impacts operating company cash flow, capital requirements, and returns, demonstrating the centrality of fundraising to studio viability.
- [[Equity Ownership]] — the fund invests in preferred equity at cheap valuations across multiple rounds, allowing studios to maintain and increase ownership stakes through Series A and B.
