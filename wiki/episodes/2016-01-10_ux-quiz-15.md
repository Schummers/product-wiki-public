# User-Experience Quiz: 2015 UX Year in Review

## Summary

This article presents a ten-question quiz designed to test UX professional knowledge based on Nielsen Norman Group articles published in 2015. The quiz covers foundational UX concepts across typography, navigation, user behavior, interaction design, persona creation, error handling, and e-commerce best practices. Topics include distinguishing legibility from readability, identifying appropriate navigation patterns, understanding user behaviors like page parking, designing effective sliders for mobile, differentiating slips from mistakes, and implementing optimal checkout flows. The quiz includes scoring guidance to help professionals gauge their expertise level and identify areas for further study.

## Key Takeaways

- **Legibility vs. Readability** — Legibility refers to how well people can perceive and distinguish individual characters and symbols in text; readability refers to how complicated and easy-to-understand the words are (text complexity and comprehension).
- **Navigation Hub Pattern** — The navigation-hub pattern, where all navigation options exist on the homepage with links back to it from every other page, is best suited for task-based sites where users rarely explore different navigation branches during a single session.
- **Page Parking** — A user behavior of opening multiple pages in rapid-fire succession via browser tabs to save items for later revisiting; distinct from bookmarking or memorizing pages.
- **Navigation Localization Cues** — Logo, breadcrumbs, and window title are typical mechanisms for signaling where users are in the site hierarchy; footer is not typically used as a you-are-here indicator.
- **Persona Creation Timeline** — Gathering data for personas (Stage I) typically requires the most time investment; analysis and crafting take less time than the initial data-collection phase.
- **Slider Controls on Touchscreens** — Most appropriate for approximate values (time ranges, approximate distances) rather than precise values (exact numeric inputs like weight or age).
- **Slips vs. Mistakes** — Slips occur when users intend to perform one action but instead end up performing a similar action (execution error); mistakes occur when users have goals that are inappropriate for their tasks (planning error).
- **E-commerce Best Practice** — Allow guest checkout and offer registration at the end of the checkout process, rather than requiring registration upfront or omitting the option entirely.

## Concepts

- [[Typography]] — the ability to perceive and distinguish individual characters and symbols in text (legibility, a visual/perceptual property distinct from semantic complexity) versus the ease with which people can understand the meaning of words and text (readability, concerning text complexity and word choice); legibility is about seeing letterforms clearly while readability concerns comprehension rather than visual clarity.
- [[Navigation Design]] — covers specific navigation architectures like the navigation-hub pattern (suitable for task-focused sites with limited branch exploration) and user navigation behaviors like pogosticking (repeating hub-and-spoke navigation, indicating difficulty finding content).
- [[User Behavior]] — addresses specific behaviors like page parking (using tabs to save items for later), navigation patterns that reveal user intent, and how users interact with different control types in various contexts.
- [[Usability Testing]] — referenced through questions about avoiding bias in testing, differentiating slips from mistakes, and designing interfaces that support user goals; testing concepts underpin best-practice validation.
