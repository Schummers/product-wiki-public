# UX Hiring: Insights from a Design Recruiter

## Summary

Design recruiter and former UX designer Hang Xu reveals how power dynamics, subjective criteria, and hiring process misunderstandings shape the job market. The power has shifted back to employers, bringing lengthy hiring processes and take-home assignments. Applicants often misunderstand what hiring managers evaluate—craft is subjective and depends on audience, most first reviewers aren't designers, and ATS systems are far less powerful than rumored. Successful candidates treat the hiring process like design: understand their audience, tailor strategically, and focus on legibility over flashiness.

## Key Takeaways

- **Power Has Returned to Employers** — The 2021–2022 hiring boom is over; take-home assignments and lengthy processes have returned; candidates now spend 10–30x more time on hiring than hiring managers do.
- **Craft Is Highly Subjective** — "Craft" means different things to different reviewers: invisible details (Steve Jobs approach) versus visual impressiveness (newer designers) versus holistic attention to detail (Michelin restaurant analogy).
- **Understand Your Real User** — Most resume reviewers aren't designers; they're recruiters, PMs, or founders seeking solutions to their problems, not lessons on what design is.
- **Tailor Strategically, Not Universally** — Tailoring every resume is unsustainable; instead, assess your market position, identify your primary audience, and focus efforts where returns are highest.
- **ATS Isn't Eliminating Candidates** — ATS systems track data but don't decide interviews; humans review resumes, often in real-time as they arrive. Quick rejections come from knockout questions, real-time review, or filled roles.
- **"ATS-Friendly" Is a Red Flag** — Focus on legibility (not on being "ATS-optimized") by using standard tools like Google Docs instead of Figma, making it easy for systems to parse and for you to apply.

## Quotes

> When the hiring spree happened in 2022, virtually all take-home design exercises disappeared overnight because recruiters couldn't get any candidate to progress in the process if they had a take-home design exercise.

> Designers from a certain era might consider craft to be more like how a carpenter would design things. Like the things that are invisible, the things that you don't see.

> You really need to figure out who your audience is and when the right moment is to talk about something like your personal design beliefs. And more importantly, when to give up on your closely held beliefs.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — As hiring power has concentrated, success requires strategic positioning, understanding what different evaluators value, and strategic tailoring to audience; focusing on high-probability opportunities outperforms generic applications.
- [[Design Hiring]] — Take-home assignments have returned; candidate effort outpaces hiring-manager effort; knockout questions and real-time review drive fast rejections.
- [[Craft and Taste]] — Craft definitions vary widely across evaluators; communicating craft to non-designers requires translating invisible details into language that resonates with their goals.
- [[Portfolio]] — Portfolios succeed by addressing the hiring manager's needs, not impressing other designers; legibility matters more than visual complexity.
