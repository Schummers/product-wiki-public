# Match Between the System and the Real World (Usability Heuristic #2)

## Summary

Jakob Nielsen's second usability heuristic requires that systems speak users' language with familiar words, phrases, and concepts rather than system-oriented terms, while following real-world conventions. This principle recognizes that humans find comfort in familiarity and build mental models based on past experiences. Effective designs leverage this by using specific and familiar language, creating interactions that mimic real-world objects and activities, and presenting information in natural and logical order aligned with user expectations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Use user-centric language** — Systems should use specific, familiar words that users search for and understand without requiring dictionary lookups. Technical jargon and unexplained acronyms create confusion and frustration.
- **Leverage real-world metaphors** — UI elements reflecting material objects should behave similarly to those objects, capitalizing on users' existing mental models and reducing the need for training.
- **Apply natural mapping** — Use spatial or cultural conventions that users already understand, such as placing the volume increase button above the decrease button to match the metaphor that "more equals up."
- **Present information in logical order** — Content should appear in a sequence that matches users' expectations and workflows, especially important for forms and enrollment processes.
- **Design for familiarity** — Interactions like sending text messages, viewing photo albums, and highlighting text should feel familiar to users through their resemblance to real-world equivalents, making experiences enjoyable and intuitive.

## Quotes

> The system should speak the users' language, with words, phrases, and concepts familiar to the user, rather than system-oriented terms. Follow real-world conventions, making information appear in a natural and logical order.

> Never assume that our own interpretations and understanding of words or objects match those of our users.

> Familiarity is what makes these experiences enjoyable.
