# The 10x Method: Getting Your First Customers

## Summary

Chapter 6 addresses the critical transition from product to market by teaching founders how to establish go-to-market strategy and acquire their first customers. The chapter emphasizes that early-stage GTM is fundamentally about experimentation, not polished execution. Rather than over-engineering growth channels or hiring specialists, founders must personally master GTM fundamentals: sales, lead generation, growth marketing, and account management. The chapter guides readers through defining an Ideal Customer Profile (ICP), running a Design Partner program to validate product-market fit while acquiring paying customers, and then scaling from 10 to 100 customers through founder-led sales and strategic outbound. Key themes include testing everything, learning from early signals, and building repeatable processes before automation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Founder-led GTM is non-negotiable** — Before delegating to specialists, founders must personally understand and master sales, lead generation, growth marketing, and account management. This hands-on phase is where you learn what actually works and build the foundation for hiring specialists later.
- **Define and test your ICP iteratively** — An Ideal Customer Profile starts broad and becomes refined through conversations and customer behavior. Early on, defining 2-3 possible ICPs is normal; your job is to validate them through design partners and refine based on usage patterns, not to nail it on day one.
- **Design Partners are your first paying customers** — Run a 3-month program with 5-10 carefully selected partners who fit your ICP and have demonstrated need. Charge them (even a modest but meaningful fee), use weekly check-ins and monthly workshops, and expect roughly 80% to convert to long-term customers.
- **Structure the DP program like a sales process** — Use the BANT framework (Budget, Authority, Need, Timing), send formal offers with validity dates, qualify rigorously, and only accept partners who genuinely align with your vision. Quality of partners matters far more than quantity.
- **Lookalike outreach amplifies early traction** — Once you have design partners, use them as a foundation for "not-so-cold" outreach: identify companies similar to your best DPs and reference them in your messaging. Every successful partner opens a network of warm leads.
- **Founder presence and founder-led sales drive growth until at least $1M ARR** — Prospects buy into the founder as much as the product in early stages. Sales conversations are learning opportunities, not just closures. Content, positioning, and personal brand matter as much as acquisition channels.

## Quotes

> In the beginning, like many entrepreneurs, we focused almost entirely on the product... But the problem is, even if your product is great, if nobody uses it, it's worthless... From day one, think hard about how you're going to distribute it. Search for your Product-Market Fit and your Go-To-Market Fit at the same time.

> The goal of the early-stage GTM phase is to establish direct contact with the market and define your ICP. At this stage, you should avoid over-optimizing or automating too early. It's important to not automate processes that you haven't first validated manually.

> Sales is not an art form that needs to be reinvented for every company. There are tried-and-tested methods for selling software that have been working for years. Entrepreneurs have plenty of space to innovate but innovating on sales methodology is usually not a good idea.

## Concepts

- [[Go-To-Market]] — Early-stage GTM is about founder-led experimentation across sales, lead generation, growth marketing, and account management. The chapter emphasizes testing multiple channels, killing what doesn't work, and doubling down on early signals before automating or scaling.
- [[Ideal Customer Profile]] — ICP definition is iterative: founders start with broad attributes (company size, sector, role, tools used, willingness to buy) then refine through design partners and customer behavior. It remains a living hypothesis, not a fixed definition after initial validation.
- [[Design Partner]] — Design partners are the bridge from MVP validation to first revenue. They pay a meaningful fee, receive early access and iteration, and provide structured feedback via weekly check-ins and monthly workshops. A well-run 3-month program typically converts 80% of partners to long-term customers.
- [[Founder Mindset]] — The founder's personal involvement in early sales, content, and brand-building is essential. Prospects invest in the founder and team as much as the product; founders should personally lead sales until at least $1M ARR and close the first $50K themselves to internalize what works.
- [[Early Hiring]] — Before hiring growth specialists or salespeople, founders must prove they can sell and understand GTM mechanics. This first-hand knowledge directly informs hiring decisions and allows founders to recognize when a new hire will excel or struggle in the role.
