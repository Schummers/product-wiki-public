# Horizontal Attention Leans Left

## Summary

Eyetracking research confirms that users disproportionately view the left half of web pages compared to the right. Even on wider 1920×1080 screens, 80% of fixations fall on the left half, with only 20% on the right. This pattern holds for general websites and is even more pronounced on search engine result pages (94% left, 60% in the leftmost 400 pixels). The finding has remained stable since 2010 despite web evolution, and adhering to conventional top and left navigation placements remains the most effective strategy for user success.

## Key Takeaways

- **Left-leaning pattern persists** — even on modern wide screens, 80% of fixations concentrate on the left half; a 900-pixel screen-width increase only shifted peak attention 200 pixels right.
- **Search results even more left-biased** — SERPs show 94% of fixations on the left, with 60% concentrated in the leftmost 400 pixels, influenced by search field placement and content width.
- **Conventions work** — users' viewing patterns depend on what they've seen on other websites; Jakob's Law means users expect and look where they've found information before.
- **Codependency of design and behavior** — layouts and viewing patterns reinforce each other; if all sites suddenly moved navigation right, users would adapt, but individual deviations from convention force users to search.
- **Prioritization strategy** — place critical content front and center (top-left area), use secondary content for right rail, and increase visual prominence if right-side content is necessary.
