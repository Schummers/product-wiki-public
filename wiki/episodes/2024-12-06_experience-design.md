# Experience Design: The Next Iteration of UX?

## Summary

The term "experience design" is increasingly being used as an umbrella term encompassing UX, service design, product design, and other related disciplines. While the term itself is not new (used since the early 2000s), it has recently gained renewed interest, particularly in job titles and team names. The shift reflects two trends: roles in the field are blending, with designers doing research and product managers overlapping with UX work; and some teams are attempting to distance themselves from the misconception that UX is primarily about "making things pretty." Experience design offers a more intuitive label that focuses on the human-centered philosophy underlying all these related practices.

## Key Takeaways

- **Experience design as umbrella term** — Experience design (XD) encapsulates UX, service design, product design, and related disciplines, unifying them under a focus on creating optimized, seamless experiences.
- **Roles are blending toward generalism** — Research democratization, product managers stepping into UX territory, and CX/UX mergers are shifting the field from specialization back toward generalist roles.
- **AI accelerates generalism** — AI tools enable people without technical skills to produce outputs previously requiring specialized expertise, encouraging broader generalist approaches.
- **Renaming attempts to distance from misconceptions** — Some teams rebrand as "experience design," "insights research," or "business innovation" to escape the limiting perception that UX is about aesthetics.
- **Experience design is more intuitive** — The term removes loaded prefixes like "user" and "customer," making it more self-explanatory to people outside the design field.
- **Core principles remain constant** — Regardless of terminology, the philosophy and practice remain focused on human-centered problem-solving across products, journeys, services, and relationships.

## Quotes

> **Experience design** describes an approach to creating high-quality, optimized, seamless experiences. It holistically encompasses all levels of the user's experience, focusing on individual interactions with individual products, the user's journey over time, and the lifetime relationship the customer has with the organization.

> This doesn't really feel like a true rebrand. By using the term, I don't feel like we're abandoning much — instead, we're externally communicating that UX is the philosophy and practice of being human-centered.

> Call it whatever you want, as long as it's human-centered, common-sense problem solving.
