# Closing the Loop: What to Do After a Design Critique Ends

## Summary

Design critiques generate valuable feedback, but that value evaporates without followup communication that closes the loop. When stakeholders contribute thoughtfully to a critique and never learn what happened to their input, they learn to disengage. The article describes two followup tactics: immediate post-session recaps and deeper followups that connect changes to specific feedback. Demonstrating that feedback influenced decisions builds trust and long-term critique culture. Skipping followups creates accumulated damage: each missed opportunity makes the next critique slightly less engaged. The article provides templates for acknowledging feedback that wasn't acted on and explaining why decisions were made.

## Key Takeaways

- **Immediate Post-Session Recap** — A brief message listing what's moving forward, what's still under investigation, and what won't be pursued makes feedback feel valued and clarifies next steps without requiring extensive effort.
- **Show Before-and-After with Attribution** — Connecting specific changes to specific feedback ("This changed because you said that") closes the loop; without attribution, stakeholders assume their input was ignored.
- **Acknowledge What You Didn't Act On** — Explaining why valid feedback was deprioritized or overruled builds credibility more than a highlight reel of wins; use a template: name the feedback, state the decision, give the reason, indicate what happens next.
- **When to Follow Up** — Trigger followups after significant design changes, before handoff to development, and when feedback influenced major direction; stakeholders should know final decisions before they encounter the product in demos or live.
- **Missed Followups Accumulate** — Each skipped followup erodes engagement incrementally; stakeholders become less prepared and less thoughtful over time because evidence doesn't show their input matters.

## Quotes

> feedback is valuable only if someone closes the loop on it

> The damage won't always appear immediately, but it does accumulate.

> The followup is where trust actually gets built.

## Concepts

- [[Design Critique]] — Effective design critiques require structured facilitation during the session and deliberate followup after; the followup is as important as the session itself for building sustainable critique culture.
- [[Feedback Loop]] — Closing the feedback loop requires communicating what changed and why; without this closure, stakeholders perceive that their input doesn't matter and disengage from future critique sessions.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Showing how feedback influences decisions builds stakeholder investment in the design process; transparent communication about which feedback was adopted and which wasn't sustains long-term engagement.
