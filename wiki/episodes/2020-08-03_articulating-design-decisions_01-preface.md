# Articulating Design Decisions: Preface

## Summary

This preface introduces Tom Greever's book on design communication in professional settings. Greever recounts how he almost missed the opportunity to write this book—initially submitting what seemed like mundane topics alongside more exciting design technique talks, only to have the conference select his proposal on articulating design decisions. The preface traces his journey from discovering that communication skills directly impact design success, through his early career revelation that stakeholders care primarily about what a design does rather than its visual appearance, to understanding that designers' ability to articulate their choices determines whether their work gets implemented or compromised by committee.

## Key Takeaways

- **Unexpected opportunity** — Greever submitted a presentation on articulating design decisions almost as an afterthought, expecting it would be rejected in favor of his more flashy design technique proposals, but the conference chose the communication topic instead.
- **Communication drives design success** — The author realized that better communication about design decisions directly improved design outcomes and was as important as the design itself.
- **Stakeholders care about impact, not aesthetics** — Early in his career, a VP challenged his assumption that design questions were about technical specifications (print vs. web, color vs. black and white), revealing that the real first question should be "What are we trying to communicate?"
- **Without support, the work does not ship** — Greever states that the success of design work depends on getting support from everyone on the team, and that without it "our designs will never see the light of day." He describes it as a common experience for designers to feel their expertise and judgment overruled by stakeholders who know little about design, and names this as the gap the book sets out to bridge.
- **Communication is learnable** — The author argues that articulating design decisions is a skill that can be practiced, learned, and made habitual through deliberate effort.

## Quotes

> I realized that all my favorite designers were people who could intelligently explain why they did what they did.

> The way we talk about design with others has a significant impact on whether we succeed at creating great experiences.

> For me to be successful as a designer, I had to figure out how to communicate with my clients what my designs did. I had to answer their questions in a way that made sense to them, not me.
