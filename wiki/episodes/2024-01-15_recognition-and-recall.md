# Memory Recognition and Recall in User Interfaces

## Summary

Recognition and recall are two types of memory retrieval with different difficulty levels. Recognition involves identifying whether information is familiar; recall involves retrieving related details from memory. Recognition is easier because it provides more cues that spread activation to related information in memory. Interfaces should promote recognition over recall, making information and functions visible and easily accessible. Menus exemplify recognition-based interfaces by showing available commands; password login exemplifies recall-based interfaces requiring users to remember credentials. Providing history, favorites, and previously-viewed content supports recognition. Visible, intuitive interfaces with buttons and menus support recognition better than command-line interfaces or gestural interfaces requiring memorization.

## Key Takeaways

- **Recognition is easier than recall because it provides more memory retrieval cues** — multiple-choice questions are easier than open-ended ones; recognizing a familiar person is easier than recalling their name.
- **Memory activation depends on practice, recency, and context** — frequently used information (high practice) or recently-used information (high recency) activates more easily from memory.
- **Associations link current context to previous knowledge** — when current context spreads activation to associated information in memory, retrieval becomes easier (like Proust's madeleine).
- **Menus exemplify recognition-based UI** — showing available commands lets users recognize what they need rather than recalling command names (unlike command-line interfaces).
- **Promote recognition by providing visible, accessible information** — history, favorites, wishlists, and recently-viewed items all support recognition by making past context immediately visible.

## Quotes

> Think of meeting a person on the street. You can often tell quite easily if you have seen her before, but coming up with her name (if the person is familiar) is a lot harder. The first process is recognition (you recognize the person as familiar); the second involves recall.

> Recognition refers to our ability to "recognize" an event or piece of information as being familiar, while recall designates the retrieval of related details from memory.

> The difference between recognition and recall is the number of cues that help memory retrieval; recall involves fewer cues than recognition.
