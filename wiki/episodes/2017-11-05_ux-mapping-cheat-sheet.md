# UX Mapping Methods Compared: A Cheat Sheet

## Summary

Four UX mapping methods help teams build common understanding of processes, behaviors, and needs: empathy mapping (user mindset), customer journey mapping (specific user interaction with a product), experience mapping (general human behavior across products), and service blueprinting (organizational perspective on service delivery). Each serves different purposes and should be selected based on three decisions: current vs. future state, hypothesis vs. research-based, and low-fidelity vs. high-fidelity. All four work best as a toolkit applied at different points in the design process.

## Key Takeaways

- **Empathy mapping** — captures user perspective (thinks, feels, says, does) across four quadrants; best used at process beginning to build shared understanding and alignment around a user type.
- **Customer journey mapping** — visualizes specific person's interaction with a product in chronological order with actions and emotions; used to identify touchpoints, pain points, and organizational ownership.
- **Experience mapping** — generalizes journey mapping across user types and products to understand generic human behavior independent of specific business; precedes journey mapping for baseline understanding.
- **Service blueprinting** — adds organizational perspective with frontstage, backstage, and support processes; discovers internal weaknesses and identifies optimization opportunities across departments.
- **Three decision framework** — every mapping requires choices on current/future state, hypothesis/research basis, and fidelity level; decisions determine how to build and who contributes.
