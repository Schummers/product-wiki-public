# Three Levels of Pain Points in Customer Experience

## Summary

Pain points are problems in the customer experience across three levels: interaction (interface usability issues), journey (obstacles users encounter while pursuing a goal), and relationship (longitudinal experience with a company). This article distinguishes pain points from traditional usability issues and explains how each level requires different research methods and prioritization strategies. Identifying and addressing pain points should drive design changes more than arbitrary feature requests because they center the discussion around actual customer needs and problems. The article provides methods to identify and prioritize pain points at each level.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three Levels of Pain Points** — Pain points exist at interaction level (usability issues), journey level (obstacles across a goal-completion path), and relationship level (longitudinal trust and satisfaction), with each requiring different research and prioritization approaches.
- **Pain Points vs. Usability Issues** — Pain points encompass usability issues but extend beyond them; they include higher-level issues in customer journeys and organizational relationships that affect cost, time, money, and trust.
- **Cost to Users** — All pain points incur costs: increased interaction cost and cognitive load for usability problems, time costs for delays, financial costs for failures, and loss of trust and confidence affecting relationship longevity.
- **Interaction-Level Identification** — Usability testing identifies interaction-level pain points and severity is prioritized by impact, frequency, and likelihood of recurrence.
- **Journey-Level Identification** — User interviews, diary studies, and customer-journey mapping reveal journey-level pain points and prioritization considers impact across journey phases and organizational feasibility of solutions.
- **Relationship-Level Identification** — Benchmarking surveys, analytics, and customer behavior tracking reveal relationship-level pain points; prioritize by impact across multiple journeys, churn rate, and brand loyalty erosion.

## Quotes

> UX professionals aim to create end-to-end customer experiences that serve the user goals as effectively as possible. To that end, we conduct user research to understand our users, their needs, and the hurdles they may encounter as they are trying to address those needs. We usually refer to these hurdles as pain points — problems in the customer experience with a product or service.

> Identifying and fixing pain points, and better yet, preventing them, is core to what we do as UX professionals. They give purpose to our work and help us focus our time and resources. Pain points, more than random feature requests, should be a driver for design changes.

> While pain points are never ideal, it is not cost-efficient to solve all of them.

## Concepts

- [[User Research]] — Frameworks identify customer problems across three levels (interaction, journey, and relationship) using distinct research methods: usability testing for interaction, interviews and studies for journey, and benchmarking for relationship.
- [[Customer Journey]] — Journey-level pain points require mapping entire goal-completion paths and considering cumulative impacts on the user experience.
- [[Usability]] — Interaction-level pain points are usability issues identifiable through testing and prioritized by severity, frequency, and impact.
- [[Interaction Cost]] — Pain points increase interaction cost when users must take additional steps, seek help, or navigate complicated workflows to achieve their goals.
