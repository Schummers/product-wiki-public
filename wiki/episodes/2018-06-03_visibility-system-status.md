# Visibility of System Status (Usability Heuristic #1)

## Summary

Visibility of system status is the first of Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. It relates to communication and transparency between system and user, ensuring people can make informed decisions by understanding what is happening. Systems should keep users informed about current status through appropriate feedback within reasonable time. This principle extends beyond UI design to encompass how systems communicate their state through progress indicators, status messages, and other signals that help users maintain control and build trust.

## Key Takeaways

- **Provide immediate feedback** — Users need to know whether interactions were successful; even simple visual changes like button color shifts or progress indicators prevent users from wondering if their action registered.
- **Inform about status changes** — When system state changes unexpectedly (e.g., items becoming out of stock), clearly communicate this to users rather than silently removing items, which erodes trust.
- **Show relevant backstage information** — Communicate backstage events that impact users, such as low stock levels or progress toward free shipping thresholds, using notifications or indicators.
- **Build trust through transparency** — Users feel in control when they understand system status; predictability of interaction builds trust in both the mechanics and brand of a site.
- **Apply heuristic universally** — Visibility of system status applies to all interfaces including voice-only devices, mobile apps, and desktop applications where appropriate feedback is equally important.

## Quotes

> Visibility of system status: How well the state of the system is conveyed to its users. Ideally, systems should always keep users informed about what is going on, through appropriate feedback within reasonable time.

> A lack of information often equates to a lack of control.

> Don't blindfold your users!
