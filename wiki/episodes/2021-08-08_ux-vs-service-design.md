# UX vs. Service Design

## Summary

User experience and service design are two sides of the same coin. UX is the "what"—what users encounter (apps, websites, kiosks). Service design is the "how"—how an organization orchestrates people, processes, and technology to deliver that experience. UX design alone is insufficient because poor internal processes, insufficient staffing, and misaligned technology directly harm customer experience. Organizations historically structured around separate touchpoints (marketing, sales, support) create silos that prevent seamless journeys. Service design addresses the internal requirements and organizational alignment necessary to sustain an exceptional user experience.

## Key Takeaways

- **Complementary Disciplines** — UX focuses on what users encounter; service design focuses on how internal processes, people, and technology create that experience; both are required for successful customer experiences.
- **Internal Impact on Experience** — Poor service design directly degrades customer experience; for example, long wait times to connect with support agents, lack of system integration, or requiring customers to re-explain their issue to multiple agents all stem from service design failures.
- **Siloed Organizations Fail** — Historically organizing around separate touchpoints (marketing, sales, product, support) creates internal silos; seamless customer experiences require cross-departmental collaboration and synchronized processes orchestrated through service design.
- **Service Blueprints Reveal Gaps** — Service blueprints visualize customer touchpoints alongside internal actions, revealing where internal weaknesses undermine user experience and showing dependencies that create delivery gaps.
- **Efficiency Gains** — Strong service design streamlines internal processes, reduces redundant work, improves employee experience, and ultimately enables employees to create better user experiences through fewer wasted resources.

## Quotes

> They are two sides of the same coin.

> Poor service design will negatively impact the customer's experience.

> You can't have a good customer experience without considering the people or processes used to create it, and it's useless to build something that end users don't need or can't use.

## Concepts

- [[Service Design]] — Service design refers to planning and organizing business resources (people, processes, technology) to deliver customer experience; it addresses the internal "how" that enables the external "what" users experience.
- [[User Experience]] — User experience encompasses all aspects of user interaction with a company, including the app, website, kiosk, or mailer—the tangible touchpoints users encounter; it is the external manifestation of service design.
- [[Cross-Functional Design]] — Delivering seamless customer experiences requires design and coordination across traditionally separate departments; cross-functional collaboration ensures that all touchpoints and internal processes align to create coherent, continuous journeys.
