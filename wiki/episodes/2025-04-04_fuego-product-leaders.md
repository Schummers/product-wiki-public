# UXers Need to Think Like Product Leaders

## Summary

This article addresses the tension between UX, product, and engineering teams and argues that UX professionals can increase their influence and effectiveness by adopting product-leader mindsets. The authors, co-founders of a UX agency, identify common sources of conflict: power imbalances in decision-making, misaligned priorities and communication barriers, and resource constraints. They provide strategies for understanding organizational business context, building strategic alliances, and adapting communication to various organizational contexts and stakeholder levels.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three-legged stool breaks due to power dynamics** — decision-making authority and funding allocation typically rest with product or engineering leaders, leaving UX teams frustrated; tension increases in engineering-led organizations where UX may be excluded from strategy entirely.
- **Understanding business context is prerequisite to influence** — UXers must learn how product leaders operate within their organization's specific context, observing who gets invited to strategic meetings, which teams receive priority funding, and what information formats matter to different stakeholders.
- **Organizational context determines communication strategy** — data-driven companies require quantified design impact on metrics like retention and conversion; engineering-led organizations respond to efficiency and implementation cost language; product-led organizations value competitive differentiation and market positioning.
- **Political intelligence and strategic alliances drive results** — successful UX leaders develop awareness of organizational climate, choose battles wisely by distinguishing fundamental usability issues from negotiable preferences, and build deliberate alliances with influential stakeholders from product and engineering.
- **Scaling skills across disciplines accelerates career value** — understanding product management, research, and development languages and practices enables closer collaboration; even baseline knowledge of other disciplines' approaches allows UX professionals to navigate traditional boundaries more effectively.
