# Five Questions for University UX Professionals

## Summary

This article presents five key insights from research on university website design, focusing on prospective students' needs. Prospective students prioritize answers to four top questions: does the university have the program, can I afford it, what are my admission chances, and what is the campus culture? Universities often underestimate the power of search engines in student decision-making and struggle with personalization in recruitment advertising. The research highlights opportunities for universities to improve information findability, accommodate online program questions, and send truly personalized rather than spam-like recruitment messages.

## Key Takeaways

- **Top four student questions matter** — Prospective students prioritize program availability, affordability, admission chances, and campus culture; universities should make answers to these questions easily findable in minutes.
- **Graduate students struggle with online clarity** — Prospective graduate students cannot easily picture online program experiences; program-detail pages should clarify schedule control, instruction format (synchronous/asynchronous), and duration.
- **Students aren't convinced by recruitment spam** — Advertisements from universities not on students' short lists are ignored; major personalization (showing specific program details) persuades better than minor personalization (just changing names).
- **Search engines are answer engines** — Students often use Google rather than university websites; featured snippets provide quick answers and show-good abandonment happens; universities must ensure SERP information is accurate.
- **Homepage should surface top questions** — Universities should follow SERP patterns by surfacing answers to top four questions on the homepage with high information scent; don't hide answers in menus.
- **Navigation must have high information scent** — Main navigation options should clearly guide students to information they seek; search-log analysis reveals frequently searched terms that should inform navigation labels.
- **Timing of advertisements matters** — Effective advertisements reach students during their active consideration phase; ads sent too early to uninterested students feel like spam and harm brand perception.

## Quotes

> The answers to these top 4 questions are generally enough to convince a student whether a particular school is worth seriously considering.

> Students are annoyed by advertisements that come from schools they are not already considering.

> Major personalization. The content of these advertisements is obviously unique to the interests of the student.

## Concepts

- [[User Research]] — Understanding prospective students' information needs, search behaviors, and decision factors through qualitative testing and diary studies informs effective website design.
- [[Higher Education]] — University websites serve prospective students who have specific information needs and limited time; effective design prioritizes the top questions and makes answers discoverable.
- [[Website Design]] — University sites should follow SERP patterns, provide high-information-scent navigation, and surface critical information on the homepage rather than hiding it in menus.
