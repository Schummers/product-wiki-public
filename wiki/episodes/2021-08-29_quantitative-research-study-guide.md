# Quantitative Research: Study Guide

## Summary

Quantitative UX research collects numerical metrics representing aspects of user experience—success rates, task completion times, satisfaction scores. This study guide compiles NN/g resources on quantitative methods organized by category: benchmarking and ROI, quantitative usability testing, analytics and A/B testing, surveys, card sorting and tree testing, analyzing quantitative data, and visualizing results. Quantitative research differs from qualitative research in purpose: it measures how many users experience problems and how widespread or severe they are, rather than exploring why problems exist. Quantitative methods are essential for demonstrating design impact, prioritizing problems, tracking improvement over time, and comparing design alternatives experimentally.

## Key Takeaways

- **Quantitative vs. Qualitative Goals** — Quantitative research measures the scale and priority of problems; qualitative research explores why problems exist and how to solve them; both are necessary for comprehensive understanding.
- **Benchmarking and ROI** — Organizations use metrics to benchmark performance over time, compare against competitors, and calculate return on investment for design work; benchmarking tracks improvements and demonstrates UX value.
- **Multiple Quantitative Methods** — Quantitative research includes usability testing with metrics, analytics tracking user behavior on live products, A/B and multivariate testing, surveys measuring perception and satisfaction, and card sorting and tree testing for information architecture.
- **Statistical Rigor** — Interpreting quantitative data requires understanding confidence intervals, margins of error, statistical significance, and study design (within-subjects vs. between-subjects); without this rigor, conclusions may be unreliable.
- **Data Visualization** — Presenting quantitative data clearly requires attention to context, contrast, and minimizing clutter; effective charts communicate key takeaways rather than overwhelming viewers with data.

## Quotes

> Quantitative research is great for determining the scale or priority of design problems, benchmarking the experience, or comparing different design alternatives in an experimental way.


## Concepts

- [[Quantitative Research]] — Quantitative research in UX collects numerical metrics (success rates, task times, satisfaction) to measure problem scale, prioritize issues, benchmark performance, and compare design alternatives; it complements qualitative research by answering "how much" questions.
- [[UX Metrics]] — UX metrics are numerical representations of user experience aspects; they include behavioral metrics (success, time, errors), perceptual metrics (satisfaction, perceived usability), and business metrics (conversion, revenue, retention).
- [[Analytics]] — Analytics data describe live user behavior (where users go, what they click, features used, churn points); analytics inform UX by revealing what users actually do, supporting monitoring and hypothesis generation for qualitative research.
