# Selection Criteria: How to Pick Your Participants

## Summary

Effective research depends on selecting participants who can genuinely answer research questions. Poorly defined selection criteria lead to misrecruitment, which undermines research validity through reduced external validity. The article distinguishes between three types of criteria—inclusion, exclusion, and diversity—and emphasizes that behavioral and attitudinal information is a stronger predictor of relevant participation than demographics alone. A recruitment matrix helps teams maintain balanced representation and avoid over-representing a single user type. The real cost of misrecruitment appears not immediately but insidiously, when misleading data influences business decisions without researchers noticing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Beyond Demographics** — Demographics alone are weak predictors of behavior; selection criteria should prioritize behavioral history and attitudes that shape mental models and predict future engagement with the product.
- **Three Types of Criteria** — Inclusion criteria define who is a good fit; exclusion criteria rule out people who would introduce bias; diversity criteria ensure realistic population representation and prevent skewed perspectives.
- **Misrecruit Categories** — Poor-fit candidates lack necessary experience; professional testers are over-experienced in research and unrepresentative; bad actors exploit the system by lying about qualifications.
- **The Invisible Cost** — Misrecruitment discovered during the session incurs direct costs; more problematic is undetected misrecruitment, where flawed data gets incorporated into findings and leads to poor business decisions.
- **Recruitment Matrix as Balancing Tool** — A matrix maps behavioral segments against diversity criteria to prevent over-representation; it is a flexible guide, not a rigid quota system, that helps identify remaining gaps.

## Quotes

> failing to thoughtfully define exactly *who* you need in your study — and who you don't — is a recipe for wasted time and invalid data.

> When considering the total cost of misrecruitment, it's important to recognize there are two distinct scenarios that can negatively impact the research process.

> A study has [external validity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/internal-vs-external-validity/#toc-external-validity-2)if the participants and the study setup are representative of the real-world situation in which the design is used.

## Concepts

- [[Participant Recruitment]] — Rigorous screening prevents misrecruitment by focusing on behavioral and attitudinal criteria that predict meaningful participation; screening quality determines whether gathered data reflects real user experience.
- [[Research External Validity]] — External validity requires participants and setup to represent the real world; poorly selected participants undermine external validity and cause research findings to be inapplicable to actual users.
- [[Misrecuitment Risk]] — Undetected misrecruitment has far-reaching consequences; inaccurate data incorporated into findings can lead organizations to develop wrong products and prioritize incorrect features.
