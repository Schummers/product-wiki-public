# Touch Targets on Touchscreens

## Summary

Touch targets must be large enough to be selected accurately and quickly on touchscreen devices. The minimum physical size should be 1cm × 1cm (0.4in × 0.4in), accounting for the average fingertip width of 1.6-2cm and thumb impact area of 2.5cm. Targets that are too small lead to longer acquisition times, slips (accidental taps), and user frustration. View-tap asymmetry occurs when elements are visible but too small to tap, a common problem in desktop-to-mobile conversions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Minimum size 1cm × 1cm for accuracy** — Based on research on fingertip size and speed, targets must measure at least 1cm × 1cm physically rendered; pixel dimensions are meaningless across device densities.

- **Blame targets, not fingers** — The issue is not "fat fingers" but tiny targets; designers who prioritize aesthetics over functionality create targets too small for comfortable selection.

- **Crowding causes mistakes** — Targets too close together increase the chance of slips; even when errors are avoided, users perceive the interface as difficult when spacing is inadequate.

- **View-tap asymmetry is a real problem** — Elements large enough to see (especially text labels) may still be too small or densely packed to tap without struggling; desktop designs adapted for mobile often suffer from this.

- **Context and audience demand larger targets** — Primary calls-to-action, apps used while moving, or interfaces for children and seniors may need targets larger than the 1cm minimum; consider context of use and audience abilities.

- **Spacing and size work together** — Large targets without spacing or adequate spacing without large targets both fail; ensure targets are both large enough and far enough apart to prevent accidental taps.

## Quotes

> "for users to quickly and accurately select a touch target, its minimum size should be 1cm × 1cm (0.4in x 0.4in). Note that this is a physical measurement: although our designs may be digital, we use our hands to manipulate these digital elements on a touch screen."

> "Designing touch targets to account for the physical dimensions of users is basic user-centered design."

> "the fat fingers are not the real culprit; the blame should lie on the tiny targets."

## Concepts

- [[Touch Targets]] — Critical dimension for touchscreen usability; minimum 1cm × 1cm with adequate spacing between targets for accurate selection.

- [[Mobile Design]] — Mobile apps must account for physical reality of finger size and context of use; desktop designs often create unusable touch targets when converted to mobile.

- [[Accessibility]] — Large touch targets benefit users with limited dexterity (young children, seniors, people with disabilities) and users in challenging conditions (moving, one-handed use).

- [[Interaction Design]] — Fitts's law applies to touch: acquisition time depends on target size and distance; larger targets and better spacing improve usability.

- [[Touchscreen]] — Design for physical interaction with screens, not mouse cursors; fingers are much larger and less precise than pointers, requiring larger, more forgiving targets.
