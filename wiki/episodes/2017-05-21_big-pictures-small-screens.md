# Big Pictures on Small Screens: Remove, Resize or Reorganize

## Summary

Images designed for large screens often fail on small screens; simple resizing creates problems including obscured or illegible overlay text, poor cropping that changes meaning, and excessive scrolling when grid layouts stack vertically. Three solution strategies address these issues: remove non-essential images, resize or crop meaningful images appropriately, and edit or reposition associated text. The key is evaluating information value and planning responsive image behavior early rather than applying desktop images unchanged to mobile layouts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Simple resizing doesn't work** — Images that function well on large screens often fail on small screens through clarity loss, inappropriate cropping, orientation mismatches, or excessive scrolling.
- **Evaluate information value** — Keep only images that meaningfully contribute to content understanding; unclear or non-contributing images should be removed, reducing scrolling and page load.
- **Text and image clarity suffers** — Text over images becomes illegible on small screens; text positioned in specific image areas gets cropped differently; wrapping text changes relationships.
- **Portrait vs. landscape issues** — Images shot in landscape don't translate to portrait mobile screens without cropping; meaningful subjects may be cropped out.
- **Stacked grids increase scrolling** — Desktop grid layouts become single-column on mobile; tall images separate from associated text, obscuring grouping and requiring extra scrolling.
- **Solution 1: Remove images** — Candidates for removal: unclear at smaller size, can't be cropped without meaning loss, obscured by text, cause excess scrolling, or add little content value.
- **Solution 2: Resize and crop** — Crop to retain meaningful detail, change orientation if needed; consider placing small images inline next to text rather than between blocks.
- **Solution 3: Edit text placement** — Shorten text, move it from overlay to below/beside image, increase contrast, clarify link tappability; text repositioning must accompany image cropping.

## Quotes

> Images that work well on large screens do not always translate well to small screens.

> With any image, consider its information value: how much information it adds to the page.

> Being critical of images and their use on small screens may result in high-quality and high-impact images on large screens as well.
