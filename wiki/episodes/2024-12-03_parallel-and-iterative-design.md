# 3 Design Processes for High Usability: Iterative Design, Parallel Design, and Competitive Testing

## Summary

There is no one perfect user-interface design; good usability comes from trying and testing multiple design ideas. The article describes three complementary approaches for exploring design alternatives: iterative design (refining one design through repeated testing and revision cycles), parallel design (creating multiple alternative designs simultaneously and testing them), and competitive testing (testing your design against 3-4 competitors' designs). While each method independently improves usability, combining them enables organizations to explore the best ideas at lower cost than committing to a single design approach.

## Key Takeaways

- **Iterative design is simplest and cheapest** — Iterative design uses testing and revision cycles to improve designs progressively; it is the oldest foundation for user-centered design and can often be completed in just a few hours per iteration.
- **At least 2-10 iterations recommended** — Research shows 38% usability improvement per iteration in traditional development, with measurable improvements continuing indefinitely; the first redesign typically still has many usability problems.
- **Parallel design explores diverse solutions** — Create 3-5 different design alternatives simultaneously, test them with users, and merge the best ideas into a single design before proceeding to iterative refinement.
- **Parallel design adds 14% additional gains** — Research demonstrated 70% usability improvement using merged designs versus 56% improvement when simply picking a winner, with additional iteration adding 48% more improvement.
- **Competitive testing provides deep insights** — Testing your design against 3-4 competitors' designs reveals how users behave and what features confuse them, providing money-in-the-bank insights for future sprint decisions.
- **Avoid design fixation** — Design diversity prevents being stuck with your best idea; combining these three methods maximizes chances of finding even better solutions.

## Quotes

> There's no one perfect user-interface design, and you can't get good usability by simply shipping your one best idea. You have to try (and test) multiple design ideas.

> An "iteration" is an intentional repetition of a step in the design process with the goal of improving the design at that stage.

> Instead of being limited to your one best idea, you try a range of designs and see which ones work with your customers in user testing.
