# Startup Studio Playbook: Culture is a verb

## Summary

Backspace, a venture studio founded by Jons Janssens and Barbara Putman Cramer, argues that culture is not a set of values posted on a website but an active practice—a verb, not a noun. Part II of the book presents case studies; this chapter documents Backspace's six-month experiment (spring 2016) in deliberately engineering culture for an entirely new 11-person team with no inherited practices or "legacy." The authors position culture as the foundational strength that enables venture studios to navigate uncertainty and maintain team cohesion through repeated idea-testing and failure cycles. Backspace works on three portfolio ventures (Florin, BYBORRE, Soverin) while maintaining itself as a fourth "company"; their thesis is that people are the company in early stages, and a healthy culture—defined through explicit values, shared rituals, and transparent processes—is what separates success from chaos when things get hard.

## Key Takeaways

- **Culture must be engineered, not assumed** — Backspace spent one full day per month deliberately building culture: working with positive psychology experts to surface core and aspirational values, using meditation and visualization for mission/purpose alignment, designing rituals that embed values in daily behavior; the team treated culture-building with the same rigor as product development; without deliberate effort, new teams default to ad hoc behaviors and invisible norms.

- **Rituals override bad habits and make values operational** — Backspace designed rituals like daily lunch together (work-life balance), monthly off-sites (team bonding), quarterly governance reviews (holacracy-inspired, transparent role reassessment), weekly "proud moments" (celebration), and random acts of kindness; rituals succeed because they make desired behaviors repeatable and social; Backspace notes that some rituals emerge naturally (regular feedback sessions) while others require deliberate introduction to stick.

- **Purpose and mission must be personally meaningful to each team member** — the studio used visualization exercises to have each person envision their future, then synthesized these into a shared mission ("a verb, target population and an outcome" in ~8 words); this bottom-up approach ensures the mission reflects what people actually care about, not what leadership thinks they should care about; a mission that feels imposed lands as empty corporate speak.

- **Balance of three skills is a non-negotiable template** — Backspace assesses every venture against three pillars: technology, design, and traction (business/marketing); all three roles must be filled from day one with no concessions; imbalance is a leading predictor of failure; this template applies to the studio itself and to portfolio companies, ensuring multidisciplinary collaboration from inception.

- **Culture spreads from the studio to portfolio companies but is not imposed** — Backspace does not install its culture into ventures; instead, it shares tools (values worksheets, rituals, processes) and lets each venture develop its own DNA; learning flows bidirectionally—Backspace copied portfolio company ideas (Florin's merchandise, BYBORRE's strategy days, Soverin's Slack practices) into its own culture; this creates alignment without uniformity.

## Concepts

- [[Studio Culture]] — Backspace defines culture as an active practice built through six core practices: (1) defining core and aspirational values through individual reflection and team clustering, distinguishing between embedded values (already natural) and aspirational values (desired behaviors to cultivate); (2) visualizing mission and purpose through meditation and group envisioning; (3) designing rituals that embed values, including daily lunch, weekly celebrations, monthly off-sites, and quarterly governance reviews; (4) sharing personal stories to build trust across disciplines; (5) off-grid time for memory-making; (6) assigning one person responsibility for ongoing culture maintenance. The studio also fills three skill pillars (technology, design, traction) with no concessions, using a template to assess founder gaps and team composition; imbalance is a leading risk and multidisciplinary balance enables faster pivots and more balanced decision-making. Values must be actionable and revisited as the organization evolves, formulated as phrases that resonate emotionally and are easily recalled by the team. Culture-building is cyclical and never complete, requiring continuous attention.



- [[Venture Studio]] — Backspace's studio function is to enable portfolio founders to focus on execution; the studio's shared resources (funding, recruiting, operations) and the culture-building methodology (values, mission, rituals) are transmitted to ventures through shared tools and template processes, but each venture develops its own implementation; the studio itself ("Backspace") is counted as the fourth company and subject to the same culture-building discipline.
