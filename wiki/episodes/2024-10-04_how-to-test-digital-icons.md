# Icon Usability: When and How to Evaluate Digital Icons

## Summary

Kate Kaplan provides a practical framework for evaluating icon usability, emphasizing two critical variables: recognizability (can users identify the shape) and interpretation (what meaning users assign to the icon in context). The article demonstrates through research examples how even simple icon shapes are often misrecognized, and how the same icon can have wildly different interpretations depending on context and user background. The framework includes additional factors like relevance, findability, discoverability, and clickability, and provides guidance on choosing appropriate testing methods (out-of-context and in-context) based on research questions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Test for both recognition and interpretation** — users must both identify the icon shape correctly and understand its intended meaning in the interface; testing should assess both dimensions.
- **Simple shapes are often misrecognized** — design cannot assume users will recognize everyday objects depicted in icons; variations in user backgrounds and experiences lead to high variability in shape recognition.
- **Interpretation and recognition don't always align** — even when users correctly identify a shape (e.g., a storefront), they may interpret its function completely differently than intended (homepage vs. store locator vs. shopping categories).
- **Choose testing methods based on research questions** — out-of-context testing (surveys, rating scales) works for recognition and visual appeal; in-context testing (usability testing, click tests) is better for interpretation, relevance, and findability.
- **Test early and iterate** — icon evaluation should happen early in design and repeatedly as designs evolve, especially for novel or ambiguous icons.
- **Other factors matter too** — relevance, findability, discoverability, tappability/clickability, and visual appeal all contribute to overall icon usability and should be evaluated.

## Quotes

> **Icon recognizability** is concerned with whether people can identify the shape or symbol that the icon depicts. (Does the star look like a star?)

> **Achieving high icon recognizability is the first step in making your icons usable**. As one study participant trying to deduce an icon's shape wisely complained:

*"I'm not sure what that icon is, and when you are unsure of what the icon looks like, you are unsure of the meaning behind it!"*

> Because of all these complications, it's critical to **test your icons for both recognition and interpretation early i** n the design process.

## Concepts

- [[Icon Testing]] — research methods and frameworks for evaluating whether icons effectively communicate their intended meaning to users through both recognition and interpretation testing.
- [[Icon Recognizability]] — the ability of users to identify the physical object or shape that an icon depicts, independent of the intended function or meaning.
- [[Icon Interpretation]] — the meaning users assign to an icon within the context of a specific interface, which may or may not align with the designer's intended meaning.
