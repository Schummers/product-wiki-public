# 5 Facilitation Mistakes to Avoid During User Interviews

## Summary

User interviews gather nonjudgmental, objective information about user needs, expectations, and interactions. Five common facilitation mistakes can bias data: insufficient rapport-building that limits data quality, insufficient probing questions that miss depth, multitasking and note-taking during interviews that erode attention and rapport, allowing observers to influence interviews, and leading or priming participants. Each mistake has practical tactics: personalized greetings and slower speech build rapport without oversharing; prepared followup questions and generic probe banks enable deeper questioning; recording with transcript or designating a dedicated notetaker eliminates the need for researcher note-taking; limiting and controlling observers preserves interview conditions; and vague study descriptions with open-ended questions and consistent neutral body language prevent priming.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rapport Building Balance** — Spend adequate time building rapport (introduce self, ask about participant's day, speak slowly, use "chat" not "interview") but avoid sharing own experiences, which can skew responses and cause withdrawal.
- **Probing Questions Essential** — Followup questions ("How does that make you feel?", "Why?", "Tell me more?") uncover motivations and rationale; lack of probing limits depth and specificity; prepare optional followups and maintain generic probe banks.
- **Recording Eliminates Note-Taking Bias** — Recording and transcribing preserves data and gives team access to raw data; if recording impossible, assign dedicated notetaker separate from facilitator; live note-taking diverts attention and signals selective interest.
- **Observers Impact Rapport** — Interview observers make it feel like interrogation not conversation; record interviews for later viewing to keep observers out of room; set expectations that only facilitator asks questions; limit room occupants to 3 people.
- **Leading Questions Prime Responses** — Revealing study purpose, using leading phrasing ("How important is price?" implies price matters), and vigorous head-nodding all prime participant responses; keep purpose vague, ask broad open-ended questions first, maintain neutral body language.
- **Participant Mirrors Interviewer** — Participants mirror researcher behavior (slow speech keeps them calm), try to please interviewer (altering behavior if interest signals received), and withdraw if interviewer shares dissimilar experiences.

## Quotes

> Research interviews focus on nonjudgmentally and objectively gathering information about user needs, expectations, and interactions with a product or class of products.

> Participants will often "mirror" the researcher, so speaking slowly will help them remain calm as well. It also will give participants time to think about their responses. Aim to have the participant speak for roughly 80% of the interview time.

> Interviews are a method to learn about how your users view the world. To maximize the quality of your research, spend the time to build rapport and focus on being an open-minded listener.

## Concepts

- [[User Interviews]] — Research methodology for gathering objective information about user needs; success depends on minimizing facilitator bias and building appropriate rapport.
- [[Research Methods]] — Interviews must focus on unbiased questioning and data collection; distinguishes research interviews from journalistic or casual conversations.
- [[Interview Facilitation]] — Skills include rapport-building, probing, attention-maintenance, observer management, and avoiding leading questions to ensure data quality.
- [[Bias and Priming]] — Facilitators inadvertently prime participants through vague study descriptions, leading questions, selective note-taking, and body language; awareness and consistent technique prevent priming.
- [[Data Quality]] — Objective, high-quality data requires attention to facilitation details; recording and transcription ensure accuracy better than note-taking during sessions.
