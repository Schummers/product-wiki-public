# Solving Product Design Exercises: Joel Califa: two tips for getting a job

## Summary

This guest interview features Joel Califa, Senior Product Designer at GitHub and former design lead at DigitalOcean, addressing strategies for designers seeking jobs. Joel presents two central tips: first, approach the job search as a design process by researching and customizing your application for each position rather than sending identical applications broadly; second, build genuine professional relationships over time through authentic networking and community engagement.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treat job search as a design process** — Frame yourself as the product and the hiring manager as the user; research them thoroughly to understand their needs and deliver a tailored experience rather than a generic pitch.
- **Customize every application** — Research each hiring manager's background, the team's values, previous hires, and job posting language to personalize your approach; "spray and pray" applications do not work for competitive roles.
- **Optimize your presentation** — Polish your CV, portfolio, and cover letter with attention to design, typos, and clarity; hiring managers review hundreds of applications and develop strong pattern-recognition habits, so first impressions require care.
- **Network authentically, not transactionally** — Build real relationships by genuinely connecting with interesting people in design, helping others without expecting returns, and maintaining a good reputation; this creates advantages in getting your foot in the door and securing roles.
- **Invest time in sustained effort** — The strategies require significant work over years, but the payoff is both a stronger professional position and a network of genuine friendships and colleagues.

## Quotes

> You are the product and the hiring manager is the user. You want them to have a great experience and to buy what you're selling.

> When I was a hiring manager, I looked at dozens, maybe hundreds of CVs and portfolios a week. I developed habits, an eye for patterns, as well as a good amount of pet peeves.

> Do this for a few years and you'll know everyone you need to know. It's much easier to get in the door and, more importantly, much easier to get the job, when people know you and your reputation.
