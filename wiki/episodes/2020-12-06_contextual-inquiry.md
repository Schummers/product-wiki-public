# Contextual Inquiry: Inspire Design by Observing and Interviewing Users in Their Context

## Summary

This article explains contextual inquiry, an ethnographic field study method for understanding user work practices and behaviors through observation and interview in users' natural environments. Contextual inquiry follows an apprenticeship model where the researcher learns about tasks by observing and asking questions. The article outlines the method's four grounding principles, a four-part session structure, and common risks to avoid when conducting contextual inquiry.

## Key Takeaways

- **Context and inquiry combined** — Contextual inquiry is powerful because it happens in users' natural environments where they perform tasks normally, with the researcher able to ask follow-up questions about reasoning and motivation.
- **Reveals habitual and invisible details** — In-context observation uncovers interruptions, superstitious behaviors, workarounds, and illogical processes that users wouldn't mention in interviews because these have become invisible through habit.
- **Not useful for simple interfaces** — Contextual inquiry is not appropriate for straightforward tasks like redesigning an ecommerce page or newsletter signup; direct observation is better for these simpler interactions.
- **Four grounding principles** — Context (natural environment), Partnership (user and researcher collaborate), Interpretation (shared understanding of work), Focus (researcher understands project purpose).
- **Four-part session structure** — Primer (build rapport, explain goals), Transition (shift to observation mode), Contextual Interview (watch and discuss), Wrapup (clarify and summarize understanding).
- **Avoid six risks** — Participants defaulting to interview mode, sessions becoming complaint sessions, researcher bias, user bias, missing important detail, or bringing preconceived notions into observation.

## Quotes

> Contextual inquiry is a type of ethnographic [field study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/field-studies/) that involves in-depth observation and interviews of a small sample of users to gain a robust understanding of work practices and behaviors.

> One of the greatest strengths of this methodology is that you get to see things you wouldn't anticipate and uncover low-level details that have become habitual and invisible.

> People are still fairly familiar with the idea and able to draw inspiration from it. Just as a master craftsman teaches an apprentice a skill through doing, a researcher ("the apprentice") learns about users' tasks by observing a user ("the craftsman") and asking questions.

## Concepts

- [[Research Methods]] — contextual inquiry as one of several qualitative methods for understanding user behavior and work processes, appropriate for complex systems and expert users.
- [[Field Studies]] — conducting research in users' natural environments where they perform actual tasks, revealing details and behaviors that interviews and labs cannot capture.
- [[Mental Model]] — understanding the underlying reasoning and thought processes users employ when completing work, uncovered through observation combined with probing questions about their actions.
