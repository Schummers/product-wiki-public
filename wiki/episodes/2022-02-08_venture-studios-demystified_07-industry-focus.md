# Venture Studios Demystified: Industry Focus

## Summary

Part 3a of Venture Studios Demystified examines how studios choose their focus areas. The chapter analyzes whether studios tend to specialize in particular industries or remain agnostic. Against the authors' initial expectations, approximately 50% of studios declare a specific focus, with the other half remaining industry agnostic despite the operational advantages specialization appears to offer.

The chapter explains this finding through the studios' ideation constraints: requiring 30-100 ideas to produce one launched company, studios can quickly exhaust opportunities if they specialize too narrowly. Since studios can only draw ideas from their own employees' creativity, unlike traditional VCs who source globally, many opt to remain generalist. When studios do specialize, B2B SaaS emerges as the most common focus due to its scalable business model and repeatability.

## Key Takeaways

- **Ideation Funnel Depth** — Studios require approximately 30-100 ideas to produce one successfully launched company, creating a significant supply constraint for specialized studios. This funnel depth means specialized studios risk running out of opportunities within their chosen industry or business model.
- **Founder Expertise Drives Focus** — Studio focus is heavily informed by the founder's own network and specialized knowledge. Founders lacking industry expertise tend to establish generalist studios, while those with deep domain expertise are more likely to specialize.
- **Industry Agnosticism Trend** — Approximately 50% of studios maintain no specific industry focus, with the authors predicting this proportion will increase as competition rises and studios face greater pressure to maximize opportunities.
- **B2B SaaS Specialization** — Among studios that do specialize, B2B SaaS is the most common focus (4 out of 19 interviewed studios). B2B SaaS is chosen for its sustainable subscription model, locked-in recurring revenue, and playbook-like scalability.
- **De-Specialization Hypothesis** — As competition increases and studios compete for limited idea sources, more studios are expected to abandon specialization and become industry agnostic rather than competing within narrow verticals.

## Quotes

> Given that the funnel of venture studio ideation requires ~30-100 ideas to produce one launched company, studios can rapidly run out of opportunities if they focus on too few industries.

> B2B SaaS business models are known to be sustainable, typically subscriptions combined with business contracts that result in locked-in recurring revenue over time. After achieving product-market fit, scaling a B2B SaaS businesses is almost like following a playbook.

> When we first came across the SaaS model in 2011, it seemed like a perfect match. Selling software to SMBs was not just about developing a product, but its success also lied in the go-to-market strategy. We were thus immediately drawn to this type of venture.
