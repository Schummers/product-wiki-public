# Teenager's UX: Designing for Teens

## Summary

This article presents findings from a fifteen-year longitudinal usability study with 100 teenagers aged thirteen to seventeen, covering 210 websites and 30 apps across multiple genres. The research refutes stereotypes that teens are tech wizards wanting entertainment through multimedia and graphics. Findings show teens are goal-oriented, increasingly successful at navigation, but less patient than adults and more prone to giving up quickly. The study identifies 130 usability guidelines addressing teens' specific needs and behaviors.

## Key Takeaways

- **Teens are goal-oriented, not aimless browsers** — Like adults, teens use websites to accomplish specific tasks; usability is equally important for this audience as for any other group.
- **Avoid heavy text and use visual formatting** — Teens get enough reading at school; cluttered screens with dense text bore and frustrate them; proper formatting with white space and bullet points aids retention.
- **Use professional content and clear presentation** — Teens appreciate aesthetics and expect professional presentation; they detest cluttered sites and pointless multimedia; interesting content presented clearly works better than fancy interactive features.
- **Speed is critical** — Slow-loading websites are deal-breakers for impatient teens; complex widgets and high-resolution multimedia may fail on older devices with slower connections.
- **Avoid condescending tone and childish design** — The word "kids" repels teens; teen-specific sections should feel age-appropriate without overly childish content, animations, or garish colors.
- **Design for mobile as primary device** — Teens often access content on mobile devices without laptops; avoid complex mouse gestures, small buttons, and rollover effects that don't translate to touch.

## Quotes

> Teens are wired. Technology is so integrated with teenagers' lives that creating useful and usable websites and apps for them is more critical than ever.

> Teens perform worse than adults for three reasons:

> **They don't blame themselves, they blame you**.

## Concepts

- [[Young Users]] — User-experience design specifically for teenage users aged thirteen to seventeen, accounting for developmental differences in reading, patience, and risk assessment.
- [[Mobile Design]] — Mobile-first considerations for teen audiences, including touch interface requirements, responsive design, and avoidance of complex mouse-dependent interactions.
- [[Content Strategy]] — Writing and formatting content for teen audiences at sixth-grade reading level, using short sentences, bullet points, and chunked information.
- [[Web Usability]] — Best practices for mainstream websites serving teen audiences including professional presentation, fast loading, clear navigation, and age-appropriate social features.
