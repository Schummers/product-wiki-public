# Design Process Isn't Dead, It's Compressed

## Summary

Recent arguments against formal design process claim that intuition, iteration, and skipping discovery steps are strengths. This article challenges that view. What appears to be "skipping process" is actually experienced designers compressing and internalizing process. Solution-first design works only in narrow contexts with mature problem spaces and high organizational maturity. For most teams and domains, even a modest amount of upfront problem framing remains essential. AI tools now enable faster design cycles, but they do not eliminate the uncertainty that process frameworks exist to manage.

## Key Takeaways

- **Process compression, not abandonment** — Experienced designers run discovery, ideation, and evaluation nonlinearly and faster; this compressed process is not the absence of process, but internalized expertise.
- **Intuition requires foundation** — What appears as intuition is process internalized through years of domain knowledge, user research, and pattern recognition; junior designers cannot operate on intuition alone.
- **Solution-first limits** — Solution-first design succeeds only when problem space is mature, users are sophisticated, and organizational maturity is high; most teams do not meet these conditions.
- **Process literacy over abandonment** — The skill is matching the right process to the problem, not eliminating process; some challenges need deep discovery while others benefit from quick iteration.
- **Accountability and regulation** — In regulated domains (healthcare, finance, government), process is a safeguard against harm, not bureaucratic ritual.

## Quotes

> What looks like "skipping the process" is just compressing it — running faster through the stages and using experience as a guide.

> What gets called "intuition" is really process, compressed and internalized through years of doing the work.
