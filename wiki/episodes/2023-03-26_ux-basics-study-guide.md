# UX Basics: Study Guide

## Summary

This comprehensive study guide indexes Nielsen Norman's foundational content for learning user experience, organized into five sections: Introduction to UX (definitions and concepts), UX Philosophy (user-centered principles and slogans), UX Planning and Research (methods and best practices), User Interface Design (visual and interaction principles), and UX Careers (starting and growing a UX career). The guide serves as a structured entry point for newcomers, offering articles, videos, and course recommendations. Key themes include that UX is fundamentally about people rather than technology, that research and validation are essential (not optional), and that design is iterative. The guide acknowledges that learning UX requires understanding both the philosophy of user-centered design and practical methods for research, design, and evaluation.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX is people-focused, not technology-focused** — user experience is about creating value in people's lives, not about technologies; "UX is People" captures that design serves human needs first.
- **Research is non-negotiable** — "UX Without User Research Is Not UX" underscores that understanding real user needs through research is essential; design without validation risks building the wrong thing.
- **Simple designs work better** — multiple slogans emphasize simplicity (Keep It Simple, Less Is More, Stop Solutioneering) reflecting that reducing complexity and solving real problems beats features.
- **Context and constraints drive decisions** — "It Depends" and "Make It Easy" acknowledge that good UX decisions depend on specific context, user needs, and the problems being solved.
- **Validation matters** — "If You're Not Checking, You're Guessing" and "Show Me the Data" highlight that observing user behavior and measuring outcomes are essential to knowing if design works.
- **Multiple research methods serve different purposes** — discovering needs, exploring possibilities, testing designs, and listening to feedback each require different methods (user interviews, card sorting, usability testing, surveys).
