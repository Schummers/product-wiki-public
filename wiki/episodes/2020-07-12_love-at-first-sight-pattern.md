# The Love-at-First-Sight Gaze Pattern on Search-Results Pages

## Summary

The love-at-first-sight gaze pattern occurs when users examine only one result on a search engine results page (SERP) and ignore the rest, a behavior documented in eye-tracking research since 2005. This pattern appears in 20% of analyzed search instances, an increase from 17% in 2006. Users engage in this pattern when they trust the search engine to provide the best answer and when featured SERP elements (featured snippets, integrated panels) make the top result easy to locate and relevant. Low motivation, time pressure, or knowledge of exactly what they want (navigational queries) also enable the pattern. Content providers can increase the likelihood of featuring in SERP elements by understanding user questions and tailoring content to answer them precisely.

## Key Takeaways

- **Love at first sight occurs in 20% of searches** — Users often fixate on a single SERP result without scanning alternatives, trusting that the search engine's top pick is sufficiently close to the best answer. This represents a satisficing strategy rather than a maximizing one.
- **Trust in search engines enables the pattern** — Users believe search engines effectively rank results by relevance and quality. This "Google gullibility" is cross-cultural and forms the foundation of the love-at-first-sight pattern.
- **SERP features attract attention and enable the pattern** — Featured snippets, tables, event calendars, and other integrated SERP elements look distinct and guide the eye, often providing information directly on the results page. When these features answer the user's question, good abandonment occurs.
- **Low motivation and time pressure increase the pattern** — When users lack time or motivation to explore multiple options, they rely on the search engine's best bet without questioning it. This is similar to grabbing the first item in a grocery store rather than comparing options.
- **Make content attract attention at first glance** — Understand user questions precisely and tailor content to address them directly. Structure content to be featured in SERP elements (lists, tables, definitions) where users expect to find answers. High-quality, relevant content optimized for featured snippets increases the chance of capture.
