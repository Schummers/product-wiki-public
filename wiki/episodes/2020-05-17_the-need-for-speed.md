# The Need for Speed, 23 Years Later

## Summary

Despite dramatic increases in internet connection speeds over the past decade, page load times for websites have not improved. Httparchive.org data tracking 6 million popular websites shows that desktop page load times have remained flat at around 6 seconds over 10 years, while mobile load times have more than doubled to 20 seconds despite 10-fold increases in mobile connection speeds. This performance gap persists because increased network speed enables heavier page content. Research since 2009 consistently demonstrates that even half-second delays reduce conversions; Google found that a 3-second mobile load time results in 53% abandonment. The gap between network capability and actual performance presents opportunity: incremental speed improvements of even one second can significantly improve user experience and increase conversion rates.

## Key Takeaways

- **Median page load times have stagnated despite faster networks** — desktop load times remain at approximately 6 seconds while mobile times have doubled to 20 seconds, despite massive improvements in connection speeds.
- **Delays as short as 1 second harm user experience** — delays of just 1 second are enough to interrupt conscious thought and change the experience from direct control to waiting, reducing conversion.
- **Mobile performance is significantly worse than desktop** — mobile page load times have increased over the past decade, creating a worse user experience on the device most commonly used for internet access.
- **Conversion impact is well-documented** — decades of research show half-second delays reduce conversions (Google 2009, Bing 2009), reducing load time by 2 seconds increases conversions 15% (Mozilla 2010), and 3-second loads cause 53% mobile abandonment (Google 2016).
- **Being faster than average is insufficient** — when the average website is frustratingly slow, being merely faster than competitors doesn't solve the user experience problem; subsecond load times should be the target.
- **Incremental speed improvements pay off** — every additional second of optimization improves conversion rates; the slower your site, the more you gain from making it faster.

## Quotes

> Today's websites aren't that much faster than they were 10 years ago.

> each incremental improvement in speed pays off. Instead of settling for being 'fast enough,' investment in performance optimization should be driven by asking, 'how much more successful would we be if we were 1 second faster?'

> The slower your website is, the more you have to gain from making it faster.
