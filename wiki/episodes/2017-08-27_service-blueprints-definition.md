# Service Blueprints: Definition

## Summary

Service blueprinting is a mapping tool used in service design to visualize the relationships between people, props, and processes involved in delivering a customer experience. Unlike customer journey maps which focus on the user's perspective, blueprints also reveal backstage processes and employee actions that support front-stage interactions. Blueprints help organizations discover weaknesses, identify optimization opportunities, and bridge cross-departmental silos by showing how all parts of a service contribute to the customer experience. They are particularly valuable for complex, omnichannel services involving multiple touchpoints.

## Key Takeaways

- **Service blueprints visualize both visible and invisible service components** — they show customer actions, front-stage employee actions, backstage processes, and support processes that together create the customer experience.
- **Blueprints discover organizational weaknesses** — by mapping the full ecosystem, organizations find root causes of poor experiences that aren't obvious from single touchpoint design.
- **They identify optimization opportunities** — visualizing dependencies reveals where information can be reused, processes can be streamlined, or redundancies can be eliminated.
- **Blueprints bridge cross-departmental silos** — they force departments to see the full journey and understand how their work affects downstream touchpoints and overall customer goals.
- **Key separating lines organize the blueprint** — the line of interaction (customer-organization boundary), line of visibility (front-stage/back-stage), and line of internal interaction structure the visualization.

## Quotes

> A service blueprint is a diagram that visualizes the relationships between different service components — people, props (physical or digital evidence), and processes — that are directly tied to touchpoints in a specific customer journey.

> Blueprints are treasure maps that help businesses discover weaknesses. Poor user experiences are often due to an internal organizational shortcoming — a weak link in the ecosystem.

> Users encounter many touchpoints throughout one journey and don't know (or care) which department owns which touchpoint. While a department could meet its goal, the big-picture, organization-level objectives may not be reached. Blueprinting forces businesses to capture what occurs internally throughout the totality of the customer journey - giving them insight to overlaps and dependencies that departments alone could not see.

## Concepts

- [[Service Design]] — the practice of designing the people, processes, and physical/digital resources that together create a service experience for customers.
- [[Customer Journey]] — the full sequence of touchpoints a customer experiences when interacting with a service, from initial awareness through support and beyond.
- [[Service Blueprints]] — visual maps that show customer actions, employee actions, backstage processes, and systems, revealing dependencies and opportunities for improvement.
