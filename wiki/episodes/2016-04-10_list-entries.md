# The Anatomy of a List Entry

## Summary

List entries are individual items displayed on a listing page, which serves as a hub for users to evaluate and narrow down options. The usability of listing pages depends critically on how individual list entries are designed. Each entry must balance providing enough information for users to make informed decisions without overwhelming them or forcing them to "pogo stick" (repeatedly navigate to detail pages). The challenge lies in creating a clear information hierarchy, reflecting that hierarchy in visual design, and maintaining consistency across list entries to support efficient comparison.

## Key Takeaways

- **Balance information density** — List entries must provide enough detail for decision-making without overwhelming users; too little information forces users to visit detail pages (pogo sticking), while too much clutters the view.
- **Prioritize attributes through research** — Use analytics (filtering and sorting behavior) and user research (diary studies, usability testing) to identify which attributes users prioritize for decision-making.
- **Visual hierarchy maps information priority** — Use placement (top-most and left-most areas get more attention), font size/weight, color, iconography, and white space to emphasize the most important attributes.
- **Consistency enables comparison** — List entries should be predictable and consistent in layout, visual treatment, and attribute placement so users can efficiently compare items by scanning between entries.
- **Selective use of exceptions** — While maintaining consistency, highlight special situations (sale, sold-out) with unique callouts or indicators, but limit this to 2-3 situations to avoid cluttering the page.

## Quotes

> Like in the Goldilocks story,theamount of product detail in the list entry needs to be just right: too much will overload users and prevent them from seeing enough choices in one view, too little will make them pogo stick.

> When users have to select among several options presented on the listing page, they often look from one list entry to another and back again — comparing similar information across different entries.

> Determine what information most of your users need to narrow down their choices. Prioritize this information and create a consistent mini-IA of details in each list entry to make it easy and efficient for users to understand and narrow down options.
