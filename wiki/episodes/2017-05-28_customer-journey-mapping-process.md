# The 5 Steps of Successful Customer Journey Mapping

## Summary

Effective customer journey mapping follows five sequential phases: Aspiration and Allies (team building and scope definition), Internal Investigation (mining existing organizational data), Assumption Formulation (creating draft hypothesis maps), External Research (validating via customer research), and Narrative Visualization (creating final visual communication). Success requires establishing cross-disciplinary buy-in early, defining clear personas and journeys, synthesizing scattered organizational data, and using research to validate rather than invent. The process balances efficiency with rigor, scalable to project scope and timeline.

## Key Takeaways

- **Phase 1: Build allies first** — Establish cross-disciplinary team with decision-making authority before research; stakeholder buy-in ensures findings drive action.
- **Define scope clearly** — Answer "Whose experience?" (critical persona) and "What experience?" (prioritized journey); ensure team shares mutual understanding of boundaries.
- **Phase 2: Mine existing data** — Search organizational departments for market research, surveys, support logs, voice-of-customer feedback; piece together holistic understanding.
- **Conduct stakeholder interviews** — Interview sales, marketing, support, R&D teams across organizational silos to gather role-specific insights about customer pain points.
- **Phase 3: Create hypothesis map** — Synthesize internal research into draft journey framework; hold workshop sessions to collectively map assumptions; invite customers for early input.
- **Phase 4: Validate via research** — Use hypothesis map to identify gaps and shape customer research methods; employ qualitative approaches (interviews, observation, contextual inquiry, diary studies).
- **Phase 5: Visualize narrative** — Workshop with core team to evolve draft map based on primary research; create polished visual if stakeholder sharing is required.
- **Sample sizes matter** — 6-8 research participants enough to start; continue involving stakeholders throughout so findings don't surprise them.
