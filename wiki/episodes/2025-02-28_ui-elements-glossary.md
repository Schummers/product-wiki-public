# User-Interface Elements: Glossary

## Summary

This comprehensive glossary defines over 70 user-interface elements and controls used in digital design and usability research. Organized alphabetically with cross-references and related articles, it covers foundational components from buttons and textboxes to complex patterns like carousels, accordions, and megamenus. Each definition includes context for when and why to use specific components, alternative names, and related elements. The glossary serves as both a reference for UX professionals standardizing terminology and a learning resource for understanding interaction-design fundamentals and when specific components are appropriate.

## Key Takeaways

- **Button** — A clickable UI element executing actions like form submission or process start; digital buttons often resemble physical buttons and require descriptive labels indicating the action.
- **Accordion** — An expandable element compressing content on long pages, especially useful on mobile; typically represented by a label and arrow or plus sign.
- **Dropdown list** — A picker allowing single selection from hidden options; differs from listboxes (which display options visibly) and combo boxes (which allow custom text entry).
- **Dialog** — A small window interrupting workflow to provide critical information or request input; can be modal (blocking background interaction) or nonmodal, and must be used judiciously to avoid overwhelming users.
- **Tooltip and Popup Tip** — Brief overlays providing informational messages; tooltips appear on hover (desktop) or are unavailable on touch devices, while popup tips appear when tapping information icons (touch).
- **Tab Bar (Tabs)** — Navigation element displaying concise descriptions of content panels; users click tabs to view associated content, commonly used for organizing both commands (ribbons) and content (tab bars).
