# Accot-Zhai Steering Law: Implications for UI Design

## Summary

The Accot-Zhai steering law, discovered by HCI researchers Johnny Accot and Shumin Zhai, predicts the time required to steer a pointer (mouse cursor or finger) through a bounded path with borders (a "tunnel"). The law states that long, narrow tunnels take more time to navigate than short, wide tunnels. Common UI elements that involve steering tasks include dropdown and hierarchical menus, sliders, scrollbars, and video scrubber bars. Users struggle to maintain a straight path due to human physiology (the elbow and wrist describe an arc, not a line) and lack of precision, making long, narrow controls difficult to use. Designers can make steering-friendly interfaces by keeping menus short, avoiding hierarchical designs, using mega menus instead, providing secondary controls for precision tasks, and adding padding to tunnels for wiggle room.

## Key Takeaways

- **Steering Tasks Are Common** — many UI elements (dropdown menus, hierarchical menus, sliders, scrollbars, video scrubbers) require users to steer through bounded paths; these are affected by the steering law.
- **Tunnel Dimensions Matter** — narrow, long tunnels are harder to navigate than short, wide ones; human physiology makes it difficult to move in a perfectly straight line, especially over longer distances.
- **Redesign Hierarchical Menus** — hierarchical menus create competing tunnel-width constraints; mega menus allow free 2D movement and avoid steering-law constraints, making them preferable.
- **Add Secondary Controls** — sliders, scrollbars, and similar controls require precision; supplement with secondary controls (like numeric input boxes) for fine-tuning rather than relying solely on steering.
- **Generous Padding Helps** — padding around tunnel boundaries gives users wiggle room to steer without dropping out due to minor deviations.

## Quotes

> The steering law predicts the time necessary to steer a pointer (such as a mouse cursor) through a bounded tunnel (such as a menu, a scroll bar, or slider). The steering time depends on the length and the width of the tunnel: the longer and the narrower the tunnel, the more time will be required to successfully steer through it.

> Narrow, long tunnels are more difficult to navigate than short, wide ones. Dropdown menus, hierarchical menus, sliders, and other path-following UI elements should be designed so that the corresponding tunnel is as wide and as short as possible.

## Concepts

- [[Steering Law]] — a HCI principle predicting navigation time through bounded paths; long narrow tunnels are slower to navigate than short wide ones, directly applicable to menu and control design.
- [[Interaction Design]] — the steering law informs design choices for controls that require path-following; understanding motor constraints helps create faster, more usable interfaces.
- [[Menu Design]] — hierarchical menus create steering-law challenges; modern alternatives like mega menus or flat structures reduce navigation time and errors by avoiding narrow tunnels.
