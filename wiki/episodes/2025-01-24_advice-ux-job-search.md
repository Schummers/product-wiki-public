# Advice for Approaching a UX Job Search

## Summary

Navigating a UX job search requires more than applying to openings. A successful search aligns with your values and preferences, which requires understanding yourself first. The article emphasizes that a UX job misaligned with your values creates long-term dissatisfaction, making it critical to determine what truly matters to you before pursuing opportunities. Beyond self-knowledge, building and leveraging your professional network significantly improves outcomes, while maintaining healthy pacing and self-care ensures sustainable effort throughout the process.

## Key Takeaways

- **Determine your values before starting** — Research shows that value misalignment in the workplace reduces trust and open communication over time, making it essential to identify what matters most to you before accepting a role, even in a competitive job market.
- **Define job constraints and assess flexibility** — Establish clear preferences around location, compensation, employment type, and industry, then rate how willing you are to make tradeoffs on each constraint to focus your search efforts.
- **Diversify your UX skillset** — In tough job markets, skill specialization limits opportunities; learning complementary skills broadens the types of roles you can compete for.
- **Activate your professional network strategically** — Many UX hiring managers rely heavily on referrals; maintain a LinkedIn presence, notify past connections, and secure references proactively to give yourself an edge in the screening process.
- **Seek mentorship and community** — UX mentors and community events provide feedback, support, and connections; platforms like ADPList, HFES, and UXPA offer affordable or free mentorship options.
- **Pace yourself and practice self-care** — Cognitive fatigue impairs interview performance; limit focused job-search effort to 4 hours per day, take 20-30 minute breaks between tasks, and maintain small weekly goals if employed.

## Quotes

> A successful UX job search isn't about finding *any* UX role — it's about finding the *right* role that aligns with your values, preferences, and career goals.

> Many UX hiring managers rely (and perhaps overrely) on their professional network to get referrals from people they know and trust when they have a job opening.

> A study by Rebecca MacGowan and colleagues found that job seekers who took regular 20-to-30-minute mental breaks after cognitively demanding job-searching tasks were more likely to secure interviews due to having restored their depleted focus and reinvigorated their efforts.
