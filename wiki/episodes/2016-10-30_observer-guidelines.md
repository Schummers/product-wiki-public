# Observer Guidelines for Usability Research

## Summary

Observers in usability research sessions need clear guidelines to prevent inadvertently compromising methodology and to maximize insights from their presence. Observer involvement adds brainpower and diverse perspectives to analysis, but requires discipline to avoid biasing participants or contaminating data. These guidelines cover how to conduct themselves in the research room, how to take structured notes, and how to participate effectively without influencing results.

## Key Takeaways

- **Audio is recorded** — Observers must remember that recordings are active and remind others not to discuss participants or sessions in bathrooms, hallways, or outside the research room where the next participant might overhear.
- **Minimize physical distraction** — Avoid laughing, talking, or making noise during sessions; if commenting on observations, pass notes rather than speaking; turn off notifications and phone ringers.
- **Participate in structured note-taking** — Observers should write one observation per note, include the scenario letter and their initials, and track observations by participant color or document to enable later sorting and analysis.
- **Observe strategies and click-paths** — Note not just mistakes but also how users navigate, what search terms they use, what tools they employ, and what quotes reveal their understanding or confusion.
- **Ask questions in writing** — When observers think of questions for the facilitator to ask, write them down rather than interrupting; the facilitator will collect them near the end of the session.
- **Don't answer or correct** — Observers should never engage participants in conversation, offer advice, answer questions, or correct misconceptions, as this introduces bias and invalidates the session's data.

## Quotes

> Write one observation per note, so we can sort them into categories later. Don't write paragraphs with several ideas; just keep notes in sequence when they go together.

> Make many notes. Write about everything, because you don't know what might prove valuable during data analysis.

> Even when you see others taking notes, write your own observation too, because others may overlook what you noticed, and your particular point of view and expertise will be very helpful.
