# Research Plans: Organize, Document, Inform

## Summary

Maria Rosala explains the essential role of research plans in UX research, defining them as documents that outline the goals, methods, and logistical details of research studies. Research plans are presented not as bureaucratic overhead but as practical tools that structure thinking, keep teams organized, inform stakeholders, and enable research replication. The article outlines the key components (purpose and goals, participants, method and procedure, relevant documents) and provides practical tips for creating effective research plans, including sharing them with stakeholders for buy-in, providing observer guidelines, and updating them after research is complete.

## Key Takeaways

- **Research plans structure thinking and prevent omissions** — the planning process itself helps researchers consider important details; without a plan, key considerations can get missed.
- **Plans keep teams and stakeholders informed** — documenting purpose, scope, and findings enables stakeholders to understand what's being learned and when to observe sessions or access deliverables.
- **Documentation enables replication and organizational learning** — well-documented research plans allow other researchers to replicate studies, understand recruitment and methodology, and make decisions based on prior research.
- **Plans must include purpose, participants, methods, and documents** — effective research plans cover what's being studied, who will participate, how the research will be conducted, and links to supporting materials like consent forms and interview guides.
- **Inclusion and exclusion criteria define the sample** — clearly specifying which user characteristics are required and which would invalidate the study ensures recruitment targets the right participants.
- **Method sections should be detailed enough for replication** — describing session length, tools, tasks, and procedures in sufficient detail allows other researchers to conduct similar studies and achieve comparable results.
- **Avoid including personally identifiable information** — use codes (P1, P2) instead of names to preserve anonymity; update plans after research to reflect any changes made during sessions.

## Quotes

> A research plan is a document that outlines the goals, methods, and logistical details of a research study. (When planning a usability test, the research plan is often referred to as a test plan.)

> As the popular saying goes, failing to plan is planning to fail. All UX-research studies should start with a plan. Without a plan, key considerations can get missed and stakeholders may misunderstand what the research is about or will find.

> A research plan ensures that no important information is missed in the planning process, creates transparency, fosters trust in the research, and is crucial for future reference and study-replication purposes.

## Concepts

- [[Research Planning]] — the process of structuring and documenting research studies before they are conducted, including defining goals, selecting methods, and preparing all necessary materials.
- [[Study Design]] — the decisions about which research methods to use, who to recruit as participants, what tasks or questions to ask, and how to organize the research session for maximum learning.
- [[Documentation]] — the creation of records and supporting materials for research studies that enable effective communication with stakeholders, ensure research replication, and preserve organizational knowledge.
