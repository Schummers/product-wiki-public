# Mental Models

## Summary

Mental models are users' beliefs about how a system works, formed from their background knowledge and past experiences. They shape user expectations and influence how people interact with interfaces. A key challenge for designers is the gap between their detailed mental models of their own products and users' less-developed mental models, which are influenced by experiences with other websites and cultural conventions. Designer success depends on ensuring users form accurate mental models through clear interface design.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mental models are belief-based** — users' predictions and decisions about a system are based on what they think they know, not necessarily on facts or how the system actually works.
- **Users leverage existing knowledge** — people form mental models of new systems by drawing on their knowledge from other sites and real-world experiences, following patterns they already understand.
- **Mental-model inertia is powerful** — once users form mental models, they tend to stick with them even when not helpful, creating challenges when designers introduce new interaction patterns.
- **Make the system conform to mental models** — when users have erroneous models, the preferred solution is often to move content or reorganize navigation to match where users expect to find it, as revealed through research methods like card sorting.
- **Or improve users' mental models** — alternatively, designers can teach better models through clearer labels, explanations, and documentation to guide users toward accurate understanding.
- **Leverage mental-model inertia with skeuomorphism** — using real-world metaphors and familiar design patterns helps new users understand digital interfaces by connecting to their existing mental models.

## Quotes

> A mental model is what the user believes about the system (web, application, or other kind of product) at hand. Mental models help the user predict how a system will work and, therefore, influence how they interact with an interface.

> Users spend most of their time on websites other than yours. Thus, a big part of customers' mental models of your site will be influenced by information gleaned from other sites.

> Leveraging this mental-model inertia instead of fighting it. One example is skeuomorphism, a design trend that involves incorporating elements from the physical world into digital interfaces to guide users in acclimating to new interaction styles.
