# Incentive Structures for Diary Studies

## Summary

Diary studies are vulnerable to high participant attrition; incentive structure is one of the strongest levers against it. Unlike moderated sessions paid for time, diary studies compensate participants for effort. Three common approaches exist: minimum-entry payment, pay-per-entry, and tiered pay-per-entry. Each encourages different reporting behaviors and introduces different tradeoffs. Bonus payments can further incentivize consistency, completeness, or responsiveness. Payment timing matters equally; lump-sum end-of-study payments suit shorter studies, while regular intervals (weekly or biweekly) encourage ongoing engagement in longer studies. Clear communication of the incentive structure to participants prevents confusion and disappointment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Compensate for Effort, Not Time** — Diary studies tie compensation to quantity and quality of submissions rather than time; participants shouldn't be compensated if they provide too few or no entries.
- **Pay for Minimum Entries** — Simplest to administer but participants submit exactly the minimum; best for shorter studies; risk of clustering submissions at beginning or end rather than distributing throughout study period.
- **Pay Per Entry** — Motivates more submissions and rewards those with more to share; best for effortful entries (videos, photos, extensive answers); risk of low-effort or fabricated submissions if not controlled.
- **Tiered Pay-Per-Entry** — Different rates for different entry types; useful when certain events matter more; encourages specific behavior patterns; requires more explanation and administrative tracking.
- **Payment Timing Affects Engagement** — End-of-study payment works for short studies; regular intervals (weekly) encourage ongoing participation in longer studies; multiple payments create more administrative work but combat attrition.

## Quotes

> A poorly designed incentive structure can encourage participants to delay submissions, submit low-effort entries, or report only enough to qualify for payment.

> One last consideration when establishing your incentive structure is payment cadence: **when participants are paid can be just as important as how much they are paid,** particularly in longer diary studies.

> There is no single "best" incentive structure. The most appropriate incentive structure is one that meets your study priorities and yields the types of data you need.

## Concepts

- [[Participant Recruitment]] — Incentive structure shapes participation behavior; cash incentives are most flexible; timing and amount both influence engagement and data quality.
- [[Study Design]] — Diary studies require attention to attrition risk; incentive structure, payment timing, and clear participant communication are critical design elements.
- [[Research Participation]] — Incentive structures should be transparent and clearly communicated; participants need to understand compensation amounts, timing, and what qualifies as valid entries.
