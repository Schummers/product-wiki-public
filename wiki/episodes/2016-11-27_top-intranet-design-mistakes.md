# The Top Enduring Intranet-Design Mistakes: 7 Deadly Sins

## Summary

Despite 17+ years of intranet UX experience, certain problematic design patterns persist stubbornly. Seven common mistakes repeatedly damage employee productivity, information access, and satisfaction: poor search results, content in silos, poor visual layout, illegible text, advertisements masquerading as announcements, disappearing global navigation, and portal pages that only promise content. These problems are often rooted in organizational challenges, staffing constraints, and political priorities rather than lack of knowledge.

## Key Takeaways

- **Poor search results** — Content not crawled by search engines, poorly tagged, or poorly categorized remains hidden; employees waste time finding information or act on inaccurate content.
- **Content in silos** — Walled-off content in team spaces, collaboration platforms, or separate subsites is difficult to discover and isolates employees; organizational politics often drive silo creation despite their costs.
- **Poor visual layout** — Lack of informed page-layout strategy, clear hierarchy, and visual appeal make pages cluttered; text styling, graphics positioning, and whitespace usage directly impact information scannability and hierarchy perception.
- **Illegible text** — Small light-gray text on white background, poor contrast, tiny fonts, and flowery typefaces are common; glare on portable devices makes low-contrast text invisible.
- **Ads disguise announcements** — Important internal promotions styled to resemble external advertisements trigger banner blindness; bright colors, animations, and stock-art people repel employees from critical messages.
- **Navigation disappears** — Global navigation often vanishes on subsites or deep pages, stranding employees; while progressive disclosure suggests hiding unnecessary elements, navigation is rarely unnecessary.
- **Portal pages promise only** — Links alone are less helpful than offering content preview; showing stock price, personalized values, or post titles gives employees reason to click.

## Quotes

> The end result of poor visual design is that employees waste time and miss information they should be paying attention to.

> The ramifications of poor search results range from employees wasting precious time in order to find what they need to their acting on inaccurate content.

> It is, however, surprising that banner blindness is also alive and well on intranets.

## Concepts

- [[Intranet Design]] — Seven persistent mistakes repeat despite known solutions; fixing them requires addressing organizational issues, not just applying design techniques.
- [[Enterprise UX]] — Employee productivity and satisfaction depend on effective information architecture, visual hierarchy, and navigation consistency; organizational silos mirror and compound design silos.
- [[Information Architecture]] — Preventing content silos requires integrated information architecture and proper linking; search and navigation integration across potential subsites is critical.
- [[Visual Design]] — Poor visual layout with unclear hierarchy, illegible text, and cluttered pages cause employees to miss critical information; text styling, graphics positioning, and whitespace directly impact scannability.
