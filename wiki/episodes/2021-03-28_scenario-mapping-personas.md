# Scenario Mapping: Design Ideation Using Personas

## Summary

Scenario mapping is a workshop-based ideation method where design teams use personas to collaboratively develop design solutions for specific user tasks. A scenario is a brief narrative centered on a persona completing a task, including five elements: an actor (persona), a motivator, an intention, an action, and a resolution. Scenario-mapping workshops bring together 4-6 crossfunctional team members to brainstorm design ideas, questions, and considerations using sticky notes organized by scenario steps. The method bridges research findings and design solutions, making abstract requirements tangible through storytelling while avoiding design specifics that would prematurely constrain ideation. This article provides practical guidance on workshop structure, the right level of detail, and how to facilitate productive ideation sessions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scenarios make abstract requirements tangible** — Brief narratives grounded in personas provide life and context to abstract user tasks, making it easier to empathize with users and generate more insightful solutions than requirements alone.
- **Keep scenarios high-level without design specifics** — Avoid injecting UI details or solution concepts that bias ideation; focus on user needs and motivations while leaving design approaches open.
- **Limit scenario detail to essential context** — Include only a few high-priority user needs relevant to the task; excessive detail about location, devices, or specific preferences constrains thinking and creates overly tailored solutions.
- **Scenario-mapping workshops need diverse participation** — Bring together 4-6 people of varying roles including facilitator, participants, personas, and supplies (whiteboard, sticky notes, markers) for structured ideation.
- **Use color-coded sticky notes for ideation categories** — Assign different colors for design ideas, questions to resolve, and comments/considerations; this helps participants organize thinking and creates visible patterns.
- **Discussion after ideation surfaces priorities** — Review sticky notes collectively to acknowledge ideas and decide which to pursue; move non-actionable or redundant ideas to a parking lot.
