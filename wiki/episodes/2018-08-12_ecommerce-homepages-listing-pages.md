# UX Guidelines for Ecommerce Homepages, Category Pages, and Product Listing Pages

## Summary

Ecommerce homepages, category pages, and product listing pages are critical touchpoints in the online shopping journey, serving both users arriving via search engines and those browsing within the site. Based on research across 49 ecommerce sites, this article provides guidelines for designing these intermediate pages to help users understand product offerings, locate desired items, and make informed purchasing decisions with minimal friction.

## Key Takeaways

- **Welcoming, informative homepage** — the homepage should clearly communicate what the site sells and differentiate it from competitors, avoiding excessive clutter while showcasing representative product offerings, similar to a physical storefront window display.
- **Clear product organization** — navigational options and category labels must make sense to users independently and in relation to other options; leverage polyhierarchy to allow items to appear in multiple parent categories for improved findability.
- **Subcategories and product listings merged** — successfully combine category and listing pages by highlighting subcategories separately above product listings to encourage browsing and reduce choice overload while maintaining discoverability.
- **Differentiating product information** — product listings should include concise names, large identifying photos, available colors/styles, price, and optionally customer ratings, availability status, and quick-view tools to enable informed decisions.
- **Mobile considerations** — on mobile, every click matters; provide sufficient differentiating information on listing pages so users can understand products and make decisions without visiting detail pages.

## Quotes

> Homepages are the first introduction to a site and the range of products or services sold.

> Usable homepages, category pages, and listing pages are critical to the browsing experience.

> The homepage is to a website what a front-window display is to a physical store: the more items crammed into the window, the lower the overall perceived value (more clutter often means lower quality).

## Concepts

- [[E-Commerce]] — specialized product design discipline addressing unique challenges of online retail, including homepage clarity, category organization, and shopping workflow efficiency.
- [[Information Architecture]] — the structural design of ecommerce sites; proper IA helps users navigate product hierarchies, understand category relationships, and locate items efficiently through clear labeling and logical organization.
- [[Product Discovery]] — the mechanisms enabling users to find desired products, including search, browsing through categories, faceted filtering, and recommendation features.
