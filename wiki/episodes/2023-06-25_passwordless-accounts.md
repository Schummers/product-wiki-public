# Passwordless Accounts: One-Time Passwords (OTPs) and Passkeys

## Summary

Passwordless authentication methods including one-time passwords (OTPs) and passkeys offer significant improvements to the frustrating user experience of password management. OTPs, sent via text or email, eliminate the need for users to create or remember passwords. Passkeys are invisible passwords stored encrypted on devices and authenticated through biometrics, providing even lower interaction cost and better security. However, passkeys have cross-device challenges when used across different device ecosystems. The article provides recommendations for designing passwordless account experiences that accommodate user preferences and reduce friction in login and registration.

## Key Takeaways

- **OTPs eliminate password recall burden** — users no longer need to remember, create, or retrieve passwords, though they must wait for delivery and access the OTP, which can be problematic with poor connectivity or when received via email.
- **Passkeys offer superior security and usability** — encrypted and invisible to users, passkeys cannot be reused for phishing, are less susceptible to data breaches, have lower interaction cost than OTPs, and require biometric authentication to use.
- **Users benefit from choice** — research showed 83% of users employ biometric authentication occasionally; offering multiple options (password creation, biometric, OTP) accommodates diverse preferences and device situations.
- **Cross-device usability is passkey’s main limitation** — while passkeys work seamlessly within single ecosystems (all Apple devices), users on multiple device types must scan QR codes, reducing the experience advantage compared to OTPs.
- **Allow adding passwords later** — even for passwordless accounts, users should have the option to later add traditional password authentication for faster login on trusted devices.
