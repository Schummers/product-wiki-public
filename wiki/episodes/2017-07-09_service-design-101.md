# Service Design 101

## Summary

Service design focuses on planning and organizing a business's resources (people, props, and processes) to improve employee and customer experiences. Unlike traditional UX design which emphasizes customer-facing touchpoints, service design addresses backstage operations that indirectly impact the customer experience. Services now blur the line between goods and services (streaming music as a product-service hybrid). Service design maps both frontstage (visible channels, touchpoints, interfaces) and backstage (policies, technology, systems) components. The practice surfaces organizational conflicts, fosters cross-functional solutions, reduces redundancies, and aligns internal and external experiences.

## Key Takeaways

- **Service design spans backstage and frontstage** — Design both visible customer touchpoints and invisible operational systems. A restaurant's customer experience depends equally on in-kitchen processes and server-diner interactions; both must be designed.

- **Three core components need integration** — People (employees, customers, partners), Props (physical and digital artifacts), and Processes (workflows, rituals) must work together as a system to deliver coherent service.

- **Service design surfaces organizational misalignment** — When internal processes conflict with business models or don't align with customer needs, service mapping exposes these gaps. Example: asking customers to repeat information to multiple agents reveals a data-sharing process flaw.

- **Service design is not the same as designing a service** — Service design is about how an organization operates internally; designing a service addresses the customer journey and touchpoints. Both matter; they are complementary, not interchangeable.

- **Goods-services continuum eliminates clear boundaries** — Modern products increasingly blend tangible goods with intangible services (Spotify delivers music as a service rather than product), requiring organizations to design both.

## Quotes

> Service design: The activity of planning and organizing a business's resources (people, props, and processes) in order to (1) directly improve the employee's experience, and (2) indirectly, the customer's experience.

> Though not ever seen by the audience, the backstage plays a critical part in shaping the audience's experience.

> If a server does not successfully communicate allergies to the chef, a diner could consume food with severe consequences. If a restaurant is overcrowded, but has a systematic process for clearing tables and assigning seating, customers never have to wait or know its overcrowded in the first place.

## Concepts

- [[Service Design]] — discipline of designing organizational resources and processes to optimize both employee and customer experiences, emphasizing systems thinking.
- [[Customer Journey]] — the complete customer experience including frontstage touchpoints and backstage support systems that enable seamless interactions.
- [[Organizational Culture]] — internal workflows, procedures, and systems that, though invisible to customers, directly impact service quality and customer satisfaction.
