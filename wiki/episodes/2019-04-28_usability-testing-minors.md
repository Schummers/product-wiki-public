# Usability Testing with Minors: 16 Tips

## Summary

Testing with minors (ages 3-17) requires special considerations beyond adult usability testing. Children and teens get distracted more easily, have lower reading proficiency, get tired quickly, and require parental consent. Research with minors demands careful attention to recruitment, task writing, environment design, and facilitation. The differences between a 7-year-old and a 17-year-old are pronounced, making age and maturity segmentation critical for meaningful findings.

## Key Takeaways

- **Recruit extra participants to account for no-shows** — When working with minors, both parent and child can affect attendance; additionally, sessions may have interruptions for breaks and environmental distractions, so recruit a few extra participants.

- **Segment by maturity and interest, not just age** — The skill level spectrum and content interests vary significantly by year; 6-year-olds can use a trackpad while younger children use only touchscreens; teenagers' interests vary by school grade.

- **Use age-appropriate incentives** — Gift cards, small toys, or stickers appeal more to young children than cash; remember that parents may control monetary incentives, so offer tangible items too.

- **Keep sessions short with breaks** — Children ages 3-12 tolerate 60-minute sessions; teenagers manage 90 minutes; kids lose motivation quickly and need breaks to prevent fatigue and frustration.

- **Prepare abundant varied tasks** — Children and teens get bored and disengage easily; prepare more tasks than you think you'll need and include different activity types (games, ecommerce, entertainment, school) to maintain engagement.

- **Facilitate with warmth and praise** — Dress casually, be friendly, and use generic praise to build confidence; young children become quieter if facilitators use a "poker face," making think-aloud harder to elicit.

## Quotes

> "Approximately one in nine recruited users fail to show up to a study."

> "The differences between a 7- and 17- year old are much more pronounced than, for example, a 37- and 47-year old."

> "Youths are likely to feel uncomfortable talking with an unfamiliar adult in a lab setting."

## Concepts

- [[Usability Testing]] — Testing with minors requires adaptations to all phases: recruitment, task writing, environment, and facilitation to get valid insights.

- [[Participant Recruitment]] — Recruiting minors involves parents; no-show rates are higher; segmentation by maturity rather than age alone produces more relevant findings.

- [[Research Methods]] — Qualitative methods must be adapted for young users: shorter sessions, simpler language, more varied tasks, and different think-aloud techniques.

- [[Young Users]] — Children and teenagers have developmental differences in cognitive ability, physical skill, and motivation; designing for them requires age-specific research.

- [[Study Design]] — Testing protocols for minors demand careful attention to environment (child-friendly but distraction-free), task realism, and facilitator demeanor.
