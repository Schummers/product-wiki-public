# A Research Agenda for Generative AI in UX

## Summary

Generative AI is reshaping how UX research is conducted and what it studies. Four major research areas emerge as critical for the field: researching interfaces for genAI systems, studying new types of UIs like generative and agent-based interfaces, supporting current UX practices through AI augmentation, and replacing current practices with new AI-driven methods. This research agenda is essential for understanding when AI provides value and what risks emerge from treating cheaper, AI-driven research as a replacement for human studies.

The fundamental challenge is that genAI has potential to both enhance and replace UX research methods, yet the field lacks rigorous evidence about trade-offs, accuracy, and when each approach is appropriate. Academic institutions and leading UX organizations must actively investigate these questions to establish guidelines that prevent poor results from leading to incorrect design decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four research domains require investigation** — researching genAI interfaces, studying new UI types, supporting existing methods through AI, and replacing practices with AI-driven alternatives each raise distinct research questions.
- **AI can enhance current practices** — secondary research, ideation, writing, facilitation, data extraction, analysis, and visualization are candidates for AI-assisted workflows with lower risk.
- **Synthetic users and digital twins show promise** — AI-generated profiles can simulate user behaviors and predict reactions without requiring human participants, though accuracy and bias remain open questions.
- **Automated interface evaluation is emerging** — AI can conduct heuristic reviews and identify usability issues, but benchmarking against expert evaluation is needed to assess reliability and utility.
- **Cheaper is not better without validation** — while genAI-driven research reduces costs significantly, poor results will lead to incorrect design decisions and high long-term costs if validation is skipped.

## Quotes

> "UX researchers must explore the open questions around how genAI changes what we study and how we study it."

> "It is an amalgam simulation of many users' behaviors and characteristics, tuned to represent a particular target demographic or user segment (almost like an interactive persona)."

> "Even if the cost of evaluating an interface with AI may be low, poor results will lead to incorrect design decisions and high long-term costs. That is why it is imperative for the UX field to actively research these questions and create clear guidelines for using AI."
