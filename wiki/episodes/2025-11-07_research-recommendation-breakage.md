# Insights Aren't Outcomes: Research Recommendation Breakage

## Summary

Insights from user research do not automatically translate into product improvements. "Breakage" occurs when research recommendations get lost, diluted, or ignored somewhere between being delivered and being implemented. This widespread practice of delivering insights and hoping stakeholders will act creates a fundamental disconnect: researchers measure whether they learned something but not whether recommendations made it to users, allowing teams to cherry-pick convenient findings or quietly deprioritize high-value suggestions without accountability.

## Key Takeaways

- **Insights alone don't create change** — the real test of research is whether recommendations are actually adopted and implemented, not whether stakeholders understand problems or nod in agreement.
- **Breakage is systematic** — recommendations get lost through cherry-picking, design reinterpretation into familiar patterns, deprioritization during crunch, and fuzzy ownership where no one owns followup.
- **Hope is not accountability** — unlike sales, product, and finance, research teams deliver findings and hope adoption happens; without tracking adoption, problems repeat across quarters.
- **Breakage drains researchers** — repeatedly running studies where same user pain appears, watching recommendations become vague action items, and being asked to validate pre-made decisions leads to burnout.
- **Measure adoption explicitly** — tracking breakage shows where value leaks (prioritization, design, development), which teams follow through, and whether researchers should fix adoption blockers or generate more data.
- **Complete chains require every link** — data to findings to insights to recommendations to adoption must all connect; a broken link makes the entire chain incomplete and wastes resources.

## Quotes

> Insights themselves do not change anything. They describe the problem and perhaps even provide ideas for how to solve it, but they do not actually fix it.

> In UX research, breakage works the same way — when the value created through recommendations never reaches the user. Somewhere between the readout and the release, the recommendation gets lost, watered down, deprioritized, or even ignored.

> What burns people out is running the same study again because the first one did not lead to change.

## Concepts

- [[Design]] — Design teams often reinterpret research recommendations into familiar patterns, deprioritize high-value fixes during crunch, or cherry-pick convenient findings, creating "breakage" where research-identified problems persist despite evidence; closing this gap requires tracking adoption through implementation.
- [[User Research]] — The real test of research is not whether stakeholders understand insights but whether recommendations actually reach users; without tracking adoption, researchers cannot distinguish between teams that follow through and teams that ignore research, leading to repeated studies of the same problems and researcher burnout.
- [[Design Thinking]] — Design thinking frameworks fail when breakage occurs in the chain from data through recommendations to action; the complete value chain requires every link (insight → recommendation → adoption) to connect, and a broken link anywhere makes the entire chain incomplete and wastes resources.
