# How to use Screening Questions to Select the Right Participants for User Research

## Summary

Screening questions assess participant background and characteristics to recruit study participants who match target audiences. However, screening questions face a critical conflict: they must elicit specific information while obscuring the study's purpose to prevent respondents from exaggerating responses to increase participation odds (and receive incentives). Two techniques balance this conflict: open-ended questions and distractor answer choices. Open-ended questions ask respondents to answer in their own words, making it difficult to guess the "right" answer; they effectively assess past experiences, disqualifying occupations, and genuine interest or experience levels. Disadvantages include time to produce responses and analyze free-text answers, making them impractical for unmoderated studies. Distractor answer choices provide plausible but incorrect responses alongside target responses, disguising study purpose while enabling instant evaluation; distractors should be realistic alternatives relevant to the research context. Each approach has trade-offs; combining both techniques (open-ended for occupations, distractors for behaviors) often works best.

## Key Takeaways

- **Screening Questions Serve Two Conflicting Goals** — they must elicit specific target-audience information while avoiding revealing study purpose to prevent respondent bias and exaggeration.
- **Open-Ended Questions Elicit Authentic Responses** — asking "What activities do you do online?" rather than "Do you play games?" makes it harder for respondents to guess and exaggerate; effective for assessing genuine interest, experience, and disqualifying occupations.
- **Distractors Disguise Purpose** — provide multiple plausible answer choices (walk, ride bike, rent scooter, use rideshare) rather than obvious targets and obviously wrong options (hang gliding); distractors look realistic and help identify genuinely qualified participants.
- **Multiple-Choice Enables Unmoderated Recruiting** — multiple-choice questions with distractors allow instant evaluation, suitable for unmoderated studies; watch for respondents selecting all answers as a sign they may be too eager to participate.

## Quotes

> Screening questions have two conflicting goals: 1. They must elicit specific information about users. 2. They should also avoid revealing specific information about the study. Achieving both goals is tricky, but it can be done by carefully preparing screening questions using two main techniques: open-ended questions and distractors.

> If you ask her to describe some of her favorite games and why she likes them, you will quickly be able to distinguish the hard-core gamer who can immediately list the names and details of many games from the person who can barely remember any.
