# How to Measure Learnability of a User Interface

## Summary

Learnability is one of five quality components of usability and measures how easily users can accomplish tasks when encountering an interface for the first time and how quickly they become efficient with repeated use. This article explains how to design and conduct learnability studies using quantitative research methods to measure learning curves, which reveal the relationship between task repetitions and performance improvements. Learnability studies are particularly valuable for complex applications that users access frequently, where understanding the speed of user skill acquisition directly impacts training costs and user satisfaction.

## Key Takeaways

- **Learnability** — the measure of how quickly users can accomplish tasks on first encounter and become efficient with repeated use; differs from efficiency which focuses on optimal performance after learning.
- **Learning curve plateau** — a point where users reach saturation and cannot improve their performance further; indicates the maximum efficiency achievable with a given design.
- **Three aspects of learnability** — first-use ease (one-time tasks), curve steepness (how quickly users improve), and ultimate plateau efficiency (speed at full mastery) each matter differently depending on user type and frequency of use.
- **Tradeoff between learnability and efficiency** — systems optimized for easy first use (like wizards) may plateau quickly and feel inefficient for experienced users; designers must balance these based on business value of different user groups.
- **Quantitative study methodology** — gather 30-40 participants with little prior experience, measure time-on-task across multiple trials until plateau is reached, randomize tasks to avoid bias, and conduct statistical significance testing.
- **When to conduct learnability studies** — appropriate for complex systems used frequently; not cost-effective for one-time tasks like signing up or filing taxes where users will behave as novices each time.
