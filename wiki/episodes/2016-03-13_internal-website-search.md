# 7 Ways to Improve Your Website's or Intranet's Built-In Search Engine

## Summary

While external search engine optimization receives much attention, built-in site search is often neglected despite its importance for conversions. Two key metrics evaluate search performance: precision (percentage of results that are relevant) and recall (percentage of all relevant results retrieved). For internal searches, recall is often more important than for web-wide search, as users assume the site doesn't carry items that don't appear. Seven techniques improve site search: manual result curation for common queries, curated search suggestions (predictive search), synonym recognition, stemming for word variants, graceful misspelling handling, homophones support, and stop-word exclusion.

## Key Takeaways

- **Precision vs. recall trade-off** — web search prioritizes precision (top results matter most), but internal search should balance both; missing a relevant item causes users to assume it doesn't exist.
- **Manually curate common queries** — review search logs and integrate manually promoted results with algorithm-generated results; avoid separate "best bets" lists that users ignore like ads.
- **Offer curated search suggestions** — show predicted queries as users type, based on curated content metadata rather than raw search logs; highlight matching terms to clarify the suggestion.
- **Accommodate synonyms and jargon variants** — users may not know industry terminology; map common user terms to your content vocabulary via synonym tables.
- **Use stemming for word variants** — enable stemming (reduction to morphological stem) so "reducing" matches "reduction"; but be aware it can lower precision.
- **Handle misspellings gracefully** — provide "Did you mean" suggestions and auto-retrieve corrected results without requiring user action; make the correction obvious.

## Quotes

> While search-engine optimization (SEO) is certainly important for bringing visitors to your website, the quality of the built-in search is often overlooked.

> For internal searches on a single website or intranet, recall can be quite important. For example, if you search an engineering company's intranet for information about all suspension bridges the company has consulted on in the past, it would be unfortunate to miss one or two bridges and leave them out of the References section of a new bridge proposal.

> Different techniques that we discuss below trade off precision for recall, or vice versa, so it's important to understand the conceptual difference.
