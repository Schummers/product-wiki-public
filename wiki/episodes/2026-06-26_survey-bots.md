# Kick the Bots Out of Your Survey Data

## Summary

Survey bots are automated programs or people using automation tools that fill out surveys with fraudulent responses. They have become an increasingly common problem for researchers, particularly when surveys are distributed through open channels or involve monetary incentives. Bot responses can distort findings and lead to wrong conclusions because they do not reflect the needs and opinions of real people. This article provides guidance on identifying bot responses through various indicators like completion time, open-ended response patterns, IP addresses, and email addresses, and emphasizes the importance of documenting bot-screening decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Survey bots are an increasing risk in online research** — Bots range from crude scripts selecting random answers to AI-assisted tools generating plausible text; many can pass basic quality checks like attention-check questions.
- **Completion time is the fastest bot indicator** — Look for extremely fast completions far below the pilot-tested median or uniform completion times (e.g., dozens of responses at exactly 5 minutes), which suggest automated scripts, not human variation.
- **Open-ended responses are powerful bot detectors** — Look for very long generic answers, uniform response length across many respondents, unusually polished writing without typos, and an AI-like vague tone that lacks specific details about the respondent's actual experience.
- **No single sign is conclusive, but patterns matter** — A fast completion time alone might be a power user, duplicate IP might be household members, but two or three converging signals (fast completion plus duplicate IP) typically warrant removal.
- **Bot responses arrive in batches and may vary** — Different bot batches look different; one batch may have exact 5-minute completions while another has 30-second ones. Stay alert for new patterns as automation tools improve.
- **Always document your bot-screening decisions** — Record how many responses were flagged, removed, and which signs led to each decision. This protects the integrity of analysis and helps stakeholders understand that sample size reflects a deliberate quality-control process.

## Quotes

> "If bot responses remain in your final dataset, they can distort your findings and lead you toward the wrong conclusions because they simply don't reflect the needs and opinions of real people."

> "These questions are among your most powerful bot-detection tools because it's much harder for a bot to produce a believable free-text answer than to select a random multiple-choice option."

> "Bots are improving quickly. Bot behavior is changing as generative AI and automation tools become more accessible. The signs that worked six months ago may not catch bots that show up today, and today's signals might miss next quarter's bots."

## Concepts

- [[Data Quality]] — requires screening survey responses for automated fraud to ensure the final dataset reflects only genuine participant experiences and opinions.
- [[Survey Design]] — depends on identifying and removing bot responses to maintain the validity of findings and conclusions drawn from collected data.
- [[Fraud Detection]] — involves examining patterns across completion time, open-ended responses, IP addresses, and email metadata to identify responses that are not from real participants.
