# Counterfeit or Credible? UX Design for Authenticity in China

## Summary

Chinese online shoppers face a heightened concern about counterfeit products, making trust and credibility central to their purchasing decisions. Beyond generic credibility markers like professional design, Chinese users specifically look for indicators of local presence, official websites, detailed product documentation, customer testimonials with contact information, and accessible customer service via online chat. A single poorly-produced element (like a low-quality video) can damage the overall credibility of an organization through the halo effect. Localization goes beyond translation to include region-specific design conventions and pricing presentation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Professional design is table stakes for credibility** — poor design quality, low-production videos, or inconsistent visual elements signal inauthenticity to Chinese consumers.
- **Users actively search for official websites** — Chinese shoppers include the phrase "official website" in search queries and rely on search-engine indicators to verify legitimacy.
- **Local presence dramatically increases trust** — physical stores, local distributors, or visible regional expertise make organizations feel tangible and accountable in ways that pure online presences cannot.
- **Photos and customer references are powerful credibility signals** — detailed before-and-after photos, customer testimonials with direct contact information, and visible client logos convince users of authenticity and past success.
- **Online chat is critical for credibility and service** — Chinese users prefer online chat over phone calls to ask questions, prepare inquiries, and maintain a record of conversations; service representatives who evade direct questions damage credibility.

## Quotes

> Just having the possibility to see products in person made the organization immediately feel more trustworthy and authentic. Participants commented that they trusted those products would be real, not fakes.

> A well designed website can be undermined by a single, poorly produced element.

> The more details you can provide, the more authentic the information appears.

## Concepts

- [[International Usability]] — credibility signals and design conventions vary by culture; Chinese online shoppers prioritize different trust indicators than Western users.
- [[User Trust]] — in markets with high counterfeit prevalence, organizations must actively demonstrate authenticity through professional production, local presence, and transparent communication.
- [[Localization]] — effective localization for China requires understanding region-specific conventions beyond translation, including pricing presentation and search-engine optimization tactics.
