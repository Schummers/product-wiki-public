# Sticky Headers: 5 Ways to Make Them Better

## Summary

Sticky headers (or persistent headers) remain visible at the top of the screen while users scroll, providing persistent access to navigation and utility features without requiring users to scroll back to the top. While this pattern increases discoverability and accessibility of header elements, poorly implemented sticky headers are annoying, distracting, and reduce the effective content area. This article provides five practical guidelines for implementing sticky headers effectively: minimize header size to maximize content-to-chrome ratio, ensure high contrast with page content, use motion sparingly and naturally, consider partially persistent headers as an alternative, and critically evaluate whether a sticky header is actually needed for your users' tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Minimize sticky header height** — Sticky headers inherently take up valuable screen space on every page; ensure headers are as small as possible while maintaining readable text and adequate tap targets, especially important on mobile devices.
- **Ensure sufficient contrast** — Sticky headers must have high visual contrast with content; avoid translucent headers that reduce contrast and make both the header and obscured content difficult to read.
- **Limit animation and motion** — Animation is disruptive and annoying; only use animation when necessary (e.g., shrinking a large header) and keep it smooth, fast, and responsive—not using the "stalker menu" pattern.
- **Consider partially persistent headers** — Headers that hide when scrolling down but reappear when scrolling up offer a middle ground, remaining unobtrusive while still accessible; implement these with natural 300-400ms animations.
- **Evaluate actual user need** — Conduct a cost-benefit analysis; determine whether header elements (navigation, search, utility buttons) are actually needed frequently enough to justify occupying screen space on every page.

## Quotes

> When done appropriately, sticky headers allow users to quickly access the navigation, search, and utility-navigation elements without scrolling up to the top of the page. They increase the discoverability of the elements in the header and the chance that users will take advantage of them.

> However, when implemented poorly, sticky headers are annoying, distracting, and obstruct page content.

> Sticky headers inherently take up space on the screen that could be used for content, so it’s important that you use that space responsibly.
