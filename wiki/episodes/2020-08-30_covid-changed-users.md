# COVID-19 Has Changed Your Users

## Summary

The coronavirus pandemic dramatically changed people's daily lives and routines, fundamentally altering how users interact with products and services. Changes include behavioral shifts (commuting to work becoming remote work), psychological shifts (increased anxiety and different priorities), changes in user groups (new segmentation by risk tolerance), regional effects (different virus severity and political responses by location), and temporal effects (uncertain duration of changes). Organizations need more research, not less, to understand these changes. Research methods must adapt to new realities, emphasizing qualitative methods and sensitivity to users' emotional states.

## Key Takeaways

- **Behavioral shifts** — Common shifts include remote work replacing office commuting, homeschooling replacing traditional schools, home exercise replacing gyms, online shopping replacing stores, and telehealth replacing in-person doctor visits. These changes impact product usage patterns significantly.
- **Psychological shifts** — Users' concerns and anxieties have changed. Many experience increased depression and anxiety. People have different expectations and concerns than in 2019, some potentially lasting long-term. Brands must adopt softer voices and avoid high-pressure sales tactics.
- **New user segmentation** — Pandemic-driven factors like risk tolerance, life stage, and living situations have created new user-group divisions. Risk-averse and risk-tolerant travelers have different needs and preferences, requiring research-based persona updates.
- **Regional effects** — Severity of changes depends on infection rates, government responses, and local restrictions. Organizations serving multiple countries face different user behaviors per region, requiring localized strategies.
- **Temporal uncertainty** — Difficult to predict how long behavioral and preference changes will last. Some may fade gradually, others may be permanent. Answering this requires continuing user research over months and years.
- **Research is essential** — User research is risk reduction, and greater risk (from rapid change) requires more research, not less. Each user group is unique; organizations must conduct their own research to understand changes specific to their users.

## Concepts

- [[User Behavior]] — Pandemic-driven changes include behavioral shifts (what activities people do), psychological shifts (anxiety and priorities), changes in user grouping (risk tolerance), regional variations, and temporal uncertainty about duration.
- [[Research Methods]] — Qualitative research (interviews and surveys) becomes invaluable for uncovering deep insights about new user behaviors. Remote research is safest and most practical, with careful advance planning ensuring quality similar to in-person studies.
- [[Crisis Design]] — Design challenges during crises can become opportunities for UX teams to improve user experiences in ways not previously possible, such as local governments prioritizing digital tools during lockdowns.
