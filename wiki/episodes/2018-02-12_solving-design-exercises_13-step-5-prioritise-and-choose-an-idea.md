# Solving Product Design Exercises: Step 5: Prioritise and choose an idea

## Summary

Part of the seven-step framework for solving product design exercises in interviews, this chapter addresses how to evaluate and select among multiple solution ideas. Dashinsky presents four key dimensions for weighing ideas: reach (how many customers), value for customer (satisfaction), potential revenue, and implementation effort. He introduces the effort/impact matrix as a visualization tool to compare solutions, noting that the ideal zone (quick wins) must still demonstrate sufficient design sophistication for interview contexts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four evaluation dimensions** — Solutions should be assessed across reach (market size), value for customer (satisfaction with the solution), potential revenue (business alignment), and implementation effort (development complexity).
- **Effort/Impact matrix** — Plot each solution on a two-axis graph with impact (reach + value + revenue) on one axis and effort on the other; aim for the "great" area (quick wins) while ensuring ideas are sophisticated enough for the exercise context.
- **Present the reasoning** — Explain why each solution scores high or low on impact and effort dimensions, particularly for the chosen idea, so the interviewer understands your trade-off analysis.
- **Consider hidden constraints** — A technically elegant solution may have limited reach (e.g., smartwatch apps only work for smartwatch owners), while a strong solution may face implementation barriers (e.g., physical products requiring unfamiliar expertise like industrial design).

## Quotes

> There will always be upsides and downsides for each solution.

> For the design exercise though, make sure that ideas placed in this area are sophisticated enough to demonstrate your design talent.

> Although it may succeed in solving the user need, the minimal reach would translate to a low impact.

## Concepts

- [[Prioritization]] — Using a structured matrix with effort and impact dimensions to evaluate and rank solution concepts against business and user constraints.
- [[Design Exercise]] — The interview format where candidates demonstrate their ability to articulate reasoning for design choices, including trade-offs in reach, value, and implementation effort.
- [[Product Thinking]] — Balancing multiple stakeholder concerns (customer satisfaction, business revenue, engineering feasibility) when selecting which solution to pursue.
- [[Business Impact]] — Assessing how a solution's reach and revenue potential align with company goals, recognizing that a solution solving a user need may still fail if it cannot reach enough customers.
