# Just Enough Research: Conclusion

## Summary

The conclusion reframes the book's central message: that good research begins with asking the right questions rather than seeking perfect answers. Hall encourages readers to embrace curiosity and a research habit as a means of meeting real user needs and solving real problems. The chapter emphasizes that research should be integrated into existing workflows rather than treated as a separate burden, advocates for testing ideas early and cheaply to fail fast, and stresses that checking assumptions before beginning design work can prevent wasted time and resources.

## Key Takeaways

- **Questions over answers** — effective research begins with asking hard questions about why products should exist and who benefits; questions are more powerful than answers, and asking often takes more courage than sticking with comfortable assumptions.
- **Research as competitive advantage** — the right questions keep teams honest, improve internal communication, prevent wasted time and money, and guide toward workable solutions to real problems.
- **Make friends with reality** — when blue-sky thinking meets reality, reality always wins, so always inquire into the real-world context around the work and cultivate a desire to be proven wrong as quickly as possible and for the lowest cost.
- **The fastest way to fail** — in a culture that prizes failing fast, the chapter says there is no faster way to fail than testing an idea still on the drawing board, "except maybe checking your assumptions before you even get down to drawing".
- **Research as habit, not burden** — research needn't be luxury or burden; it is simply a means to develop insights and can be integrated into existing processes with available resources and goals.
- **Enough is enough** — the chapter closes on its own title question, "How much research is just enough?", and answers that you will need to do just enough to find out.

## Quotes

> Every time you find a product or service that's a joy to use, meets a need maybe you didn't even know you had, and fits seamlessly into your life, you know that someone on the other end asked hard questions.

> Cultivate a desire to be proven wrong as quickly as possible and for the lowest cost.

> The right questions will keep you honest.

## Concepts

- [[Research Methods]] — the conclusion frames research as a toolbox of techniques (outlined throughout the book) to be selected based on goals and resources; emphasizes that the specific methods matter less than having a research habit and asking the right questions.
- [[Research Planning]] — stressed as a matter of determining which approaches fit your context, available resources, and goals; the process is "Form questions. Gather data. Analyze," with many possible approaches depending on constraints.
- [[Problem Framing]] — framed as the foundational work of asking hard questions before design work begins; checking assumptions early prevents wasted time and resources; competitive advantage comes from asking the right questions about why products should exist and who they serve.
