# Explicitly State the Difference Between Options

## Summary

This article addresses how websites can help users make confident decisions by explicitly stating the differences between choice options. Drawing on research with university students comparing tuition rates, the article demonstrates that users cannot distinguish between options when differences remain implicit or unclear. It provides two core guidelines: highlight the few attributes that differ when options are similar, and highlight the key differences when many distinctions exist. The article includes examples from ecommerce, education, and search results.

## Key Takeaways

- **Don't assume users can figure out differences** — users are likely to assume all choices are the same or fret over smallest distinctions when differences are not made explicit; this slows decisions and complicates user experience.
- **Highlight differentiating attributes for similar options** — when options differ only on a few attributes, use comparison tables or move those features to the top of the list; merge cells for shared attributes to clarify commonality.
- **Focus attention on important differences** — when options have many differences but only a few matter, highlight the key differences and use progressive disclosure for details only those who need them will access.
- **Explicit copy prevents misunderstanding** — terms like "in-state tuition" that are obvious to insiders may be unclear to first-time users; provide context, examples, or explicit servings instead of quart sizes.
- **Clear differentiation affects trust** — good UX ensures users make the right choice the first time; when differences are implicit, users select incorrectly, suffer interaction cost to fix mistakes, and lose trust in the website.

## Quotes

> Emphasizing the attributes that differ in choices with minimal distinction and noting the key differences in choices with many dissimilarities will greatly improve users' confidence in making choices on your site.

> You are not the user. And you are likely not the person making these important decisions on your website.
