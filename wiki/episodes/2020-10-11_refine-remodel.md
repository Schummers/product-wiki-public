# Refine, Remodel, Rebuild: 3 Strategies for Experience Improvement

## Summary

This article examines three strategic approaches organizations can take to improve customer experience across their journeys. The author argues that experience performance on customer journeys is more important than performance on individual touchpoints, and that designing entire journeys should be a priority for UX investment. Organizations can adopt three distinct strategies, which differ in their investment level, required organizational change, and potential impact.

## Key Takeaways

- **Journey over touchpoints** — Experience performance across entire customer journeys affects customer satisfaction and business outcomes more significantly than optimizing individual touchpoints or channels in isolation.
- **Refine strategy** — The most conservative, low-investment approach focuses on smoothing rough edges of existing experiences through small, iterative improvements to existing journeys without radical changes.
- **Remodel strategy** — The medium-investment approach reimagines existing journeys by making moderate adjustments to suit customer needs, requiring operational and foundational changes within the organization.
- **Rebuild strategy** — The high-investment, most transformational approach builds entirely new experiences by leveraging new technologies and business models to deliver something innovative customers didn't know they needed.
- **Align with organizational readiness** — Organizations should select their strategy based on their current state, investment capacity, organizational transformation commitment, risk tolerance, unmet needs, and competitive landscape.

## Quotes

> "Experience is business." It means that your business is not based just on a product or service. People buy a product or service *experience* from you, so, whatever that experience is, it is what you are selling.

> Data from a study by McKinsey shows that, across industries, experience performance on customer journeys affects customer satisfaction more than performance on touchpoints.

> An easy way to think about improving customer journeys is to liken the process to how you might improve and update a home or physical space. **Do you refine, remodel, or rebuild?**
