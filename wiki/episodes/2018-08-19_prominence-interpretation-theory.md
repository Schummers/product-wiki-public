# Prominence-Interpretation Theory

## Summary

BJ Fogg's prominence-interpretation theory explains how users assess a website's credibility by judging prominent (salient) attributes of the site and interpreting them based on personal experience, cultural context, and expectations. This theory, validated through behavioral research where participants used actual sites before rating credibility, provides practical guidance for UX design by highlighting the importance of visual prominence and positive interpretation of key page elements.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two-part credibility framework** — credibility judgments depend on both prominence (which elements stand out and capture attention) and interpretation (how users judge those elements); if either fails, credibility assessment cannot occur.
- **Factors influencing prominence** — what stands out depends on user involvement, site topic, user task, user expertise level, and individual differences in literacy and background, making prominence relative and context-dependent.
- **Factors influencing interpretation** — how users judge prominent elements depends on their assumptions (culture, experience, biases), skill/knowledge level in the subject, and context (expectations and situational norms).
- **Visual tone of voice matters** — visual elements communicate tone that can be appropriate for one brand/industry but inappropriate for another; prominent elements should match user expectations for the site type.
- **Testing prominence and interpretation** — practitioners can assess prominence using five-second tests and evaluate interpretation through usability testing with follow-up questions about what stood out and how elements were judged.

## Quotes

> Prominence-interpretation theory suggests that people determine a site's credibility by judging prominent attributes of the site that grab their attention.

> Visual prominence of certain elements can influence the credibility of a site, and words alone are not enough to establish credibility.

> First impressions and visual design do carry power.

## Concepts

- [[Web Credibility]] — the perception that a website is trustworthy and authoritative; credibility judgments are based on prominent page elements and how users interpret them in context.
- [[Visual Design]] — the aesthetic and functional arrangement of visual elements; visual design significantly influences credibility assessment through prominence and interpretation of design choices.
- [[Aesthetic-Usability Effect]] — users form quick credibility judgments based on prominent visual attributes; these initial impressions guide whether users engage further with the site.
