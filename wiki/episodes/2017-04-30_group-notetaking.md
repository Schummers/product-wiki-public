# Group Notetaking for User Research

## Summary

When teams collaborate in user research by observing sessions together and taking group notes, they gain multiple benefits: shared understanding of users and problems, faster consensus on findings, actionable insights, and team ownership of UX. The article outlines two primary note-capturing methods (chronological logs and topical notes) and a group insight-collection method (whiteboard top-findings list), with guidelines for choosing the best approach based on team size, location, research goals, and available resources.

## Key Takeaways

- **Group observation builds team understanding** — Multiple perspectives interpreting the same incidents yield higher-quality solutions; everyone understands problems together, enabling faster consensus and execution.
- **Capture individually, then collect together** — Avoid shared collaborative documents during sessions; have each observer take separate notes, then combine and sort afterward to prevent groupthink and distraction.
- **Chronological logs for sequence and detail** — Digital note templates recording observations in order work best when sequence matters, observers are remote, time-on-task is measured, or detailed technical observations are needed.
- **Topical notes for faster analysis** — One-incident-per-sticky-note with color-coding by participant enables quick post-session sorting into emergent categories, supporting rapid consensus-building and Agile environments.
- **Run whiteboard top-findings after each session** — Short team debriefs capturing key observations on a shared board keep findings visible, let stakeholders get up to speed, and enable quick tallying of recurring issues.
- **4-10 observers is practical** — Rotate stakeholders to ensure everyone observes at least 2 sessions; prevent single-observation judgment formation and build organizational alignment.
- **Method choice depends on context** — Consider observer locations, research outcomes, team dynamics, team size, distractibility, and emphasis on speed vs. detail when selecting note format.

## Quotes

> Teams should collaborate on user experience activities in order to be sure to make products that are usable and desirable.

> Seeing others' notes can lead to groupthink or can diffuse responsibility and discourage people from taking notes, because someone else is already doing it.

> Preliminary top findings can be transcribed, sorted, and conveyed to the larger group almost immediately after the study ends. For this reason, the whiteboard method is particularly suited for fast-paced Agile environments.
