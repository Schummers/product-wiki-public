# Wireflows: A UX Deliverable for Workflows and Apps

## Summary

Wireflows are a design-specification format combining wireframe-style page layouts with simplified flowchart-like interaction representations. They emerged from mobile app design where small screen sizes made wireframes practical for each interaction step. Wireflows excel at documenting user workflows through apps and dynamic websites where content changes based on interaction, but work less well for documenting flows through many static pages.

Traditional wireframes show layout without documenting interactions well; flowcharts show complex workflows without page context. Wireflows solve this by showing actual screen designs at each step with arrows indicating interaction hotspots and resulting changes (content updates, feedback, error messages, layout changes). They work for mobile, desktop, and web apps, and also serve as effective collaboration tools in design workshops.

## Key Takeaways

- **Wireflows combine two formats** — They merge wireframe layout details with flowchart interaction sequences, capturing both page context and workflow progression.
- **Ideal for dynamic apps** — Wireflows excel when applications have few pages but change content and layout dynamically through user interaction, like AJAX-based products, faceted search, or creative applications.
- **Show feedback and state changes** — Unlike traditional wireframes, wireflows document system feedback (confirmation popups, color changes, error messages) that communicates to users their action was recognized.
- **Unsuitable for many static pages** — Wireflows are less useful for documenting traditional websites with many discrete pages linked together; they become too large and lose context at that scale.
- **Arrows indicate hotspots** — Clear indication of clickable UI targets that trigger the next step prevents ambiguity, especially important for complex apps with multiple actionable items per screen.
- **Work at any fidelity** — Wireflows can be low-fidelity sketches for ideation workshops or high-fidelity mockups for detailed specifications; collaborative rough sketches build team understanding.
