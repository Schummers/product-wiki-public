# Common Ground and UX

## Summary

This article applies the psycholinguistic concept of common ground (mutual knowledge, beliefs, and assumptions shared by communication partners) to UX work. Common ground enables efficient communication by allowing people to use shortcuts instead of explaining everything from scratch. Since UX is fundamentally about communication with users and teammates, establishing common ground is central to effective user interface design and team collaboration. The article explains how factors like synchronous vs. asynchronous communication, communication medium, familiarity with parties, and number of participants affect grounding, and provides practical strategies for building common ground within organizations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Common Ground Definition** — Common ground is the mutual knowledge, beliefs, and assumptions that communication partners share, enabling efficient communication through shortcuts rather than complete explanations.
- **Grounding Process** — Establishing common ground requires confirmation signals (nodding, asking clarifying questions) and relies on shared context, shared history, or explicitly created reference points.
- **Communication Efficiency Factors** — Synchronous communication establishes common ground more easily than asynchronous; shared artifacts (maps, whiteboards, documents) significantly improve communication efficiency.
- **UX as Communication** — User interface design is dialogue design; UX professionals must establish common ground with users (asynchronously through products) and with teammates (through alignment activities).
- **Building Organizational Common Ground** — Teams must evangelize UX concepts, establish shared vocabulary, create visual artifacts (personas, journey maps), engage in one-to-one clarification, and learn about coworkers to build efficient communication.
- **Artifact Power** — Personas, customer-journey maps, service blueprints, and roadmaps serve dual purposes: they establish and record common ground while avoiding repetitive explanation in future communications.

## Quotes

> Common ground refers to the mutual knowledge, beliefs, and assumptions that partners in a conversation rely on in order to communicate efficiently.

> UX is ultimately about communication: with our users and with our teammates. User-interface design is dialogue design.

> Establishing common ground with the other members of your organization is crucial for the success of most UX-related activities and for the productivity of a team.
