# Intelligent Assistants Have Poor Usability: A User Study of Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri

## Summary

Current intelligent assistants (Alexa, Google Assistant, Siri) combine five UI technologies: voice input, natural language understanding, voice output, intelligent interpretation, and agency. A usability study with 17 frequent users found that delivered usability is grossly inferior to promised usability across all six components (five technologies plus integration). Results felt like "a return to the dark ages of the 1970s" requiring users to memorize command syntax rather than supporting natural interaction. While some areas show promise for improvement, natural language processing and integration with other apps remain major challenges.

## Key Takeaways

- **Natural language still limited** — Multiclause sentences are often misunderstood, equivalent query formulations produce different results, and pronoun references are unclear, requiring users to decompose complex questions into multiple simple queries.
- **Voice output is inefficient** — Speech is a slow output modality; users get visibly annoyed listening to long verbal responses, especially when they realize they're uninterested in an option but can't interrupt.
- **Skills and actions lack discoverability** — Users must remember exact skill names and magic words to invoke them, with little guidance on what skills do or how to use them, making skills nearly useless in practice.
- **Comparison shopping is nearly impossible** — Lack of visual comparison options, difficulty returning to previous results, and inability to easily go back and forth between alternatives make shopping tasks particularly frustrating.
- **Integration with other apps is poor** — Assistants don't work well with third-party services users want (Spotify, Google Maps), forcing workarounds or expensive duplicate subscriptions.

## Quotes

> Our user research found that current intelligent assistants fail on all 6 questions (5 technologies plus integration), resulting in an overall usability level that's close to useless for even slightly complex interactions.

> You should think of your question before you ask it — because it's hard to fix it while you're saying it to an assistant. You've just got to think of it beforehand, because it's not like a person where in a conversation with them you can be vague.

> What I don't like is that Alexa doesn't shut up when I start talking to her. This is what a more human interaction should be.
