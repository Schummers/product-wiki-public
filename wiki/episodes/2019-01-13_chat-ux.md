# The User Experience of Customer-Service Chat: 20 Guidelines

## Summary

This article presents 20 guidelines for designing customer-service chat based on usability testing with eight participants contacting businesses through chat. Chat offers advantages over traditional support channels: less emotional investment than phone calls, written record of conversations, and multitasking capability. However, poor implementation creates friction. Guidelines cover chat discoverability, accessibility, waiting-time communication, message clarity, bot transparency, and response appropriateness, addressing the entire chat experience from finding the feature to concluding interactions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Make chat discoverable via explicit labeling** — Chat should be labeled "Chat" (not vague terms like "Ask a Question"), placed on Contact Us pages, and prominently featured through dedicated pages rather than relying solely on floating buttons. Users typically look under Help or Contact Us for chat access.

- **Don't rely exclusively on floating buttons** — While floating buttons can work, they are frequently overlooked, especially when positioned unconventionally (left side instead of right). Provide chat access through standard navigation and contact pages as primary discovery paths.

- **Minimize wait times and communicate state** — Users dislike waiting, especially on mobile where multitasking is difficult. Provide time estimates before chat starts ("Wait approximately 2 minutes"), show "Agent is typing" status, and include timestamps so users understand response timing.

- **Use separate chat windows and visual differentiation** — Opening chat in a separate window allows users to reference other pages (product details, order numbers) while chatting. Visually differentiate messages from different participants (user, agent, system) using color or styling.

- **Avoid asking users to repeat information** — When preliminary information is collected before connecting with an agent, the agent should have access to it. Don't make users type their question again after already providing context to a chatbot.

## Quotes

> "Chat is less personable. You have time to think. You can have it in writing. You have to build less courage to talk. And it takes less time [than a phone call]."

> Typically, people interpreted hard-to-find chat as "it's pretty clear that they don't want you to contact them."

> People can better calibrate their language and their expectations when they know that their conversation partner is a bot.

## Concepts

- [[Chat UX]] — Designing real-time text-based customer service experiences, requiring attention to discoverability, waiting-time communication, message clarity, and response appropriateness.

- [[Customer Service]] — Support interactions delivered through chat channels, requiring understanding of user expectations around response time, clarity, and agent helpfulness.

- [[Live Chat]] — Synchronous text communication with support agents, distinct from email or phone support, with unique usability considerations around multitasking and real-time expectations.
