# Setting UX Roles and Responsibilities in Product Development: The RACI Template

## Summary

The RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) matrix is a framework for defining roles and participation in product development tasks. It clarifies who does what, prevents gaps and misalignment, and supports collaborative Agile workflows. The article explains the four involvement levels, discusses when and why to use a RACI, and provides guidance on assigning roles based on skills, relationships, and context. Regular use of RACIs helps teams establish understanding of who should be involved in discovery, design, and delivery phases.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four involvement levels** — Responsible means completing the task; Accountable means providing final review and determining completion; Consulted means providing input and expertise; Informed means being kept aware.
- **One accountable per task** — Each task needs exactly one accountable person to ensure ownership and clear decision-making authority; multiple responsible people are fine.
- **Flexible by context** — RACIs adapt to team needs and should consider skillsets, relationships, historical product knowledge, and individual ambitions, not just job titles.
- **Flexible by phase** — RACI assignments vary across strategic context, discovery, design, and delivery phases; use it especially during strategic and discovery phases where roles are most often unclear.
- **Avoid handoff culture** — Visualizing roles with a RACI encourages design-as-process collaboration rather than design-as-service handoffs; partnered roles should check in regularly.
- **Graduate from training wheels** — Over time as teams work together, understanding of when to involve other roles becomes obvious without formal tools.

## Quotes

> The RACI helps teams zoom out from the work to see where other roles should be involved.

> For each task, there should be only one accountable person. Accountability is essential for an organization and team. Without it, it's difficult to get people to take ownership and get things done.

> Taking time to openly discuss what's happening and who's doing what in product development enables teams to identify gaps in their processes and avoid common misalignments that impair product outcomes.
