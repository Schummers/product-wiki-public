# Startup Studio Playbook: Vision and strategy

## Summary

This chapter frames studio planning as designing a mini-ecosystem or empire: the studio plus its startups, investors, partners, accelerators, and co-working spaces. Planning requires an iterative, Design Thinking process to define a studio's characteristics, key performance indicators, and six strategic steps: core principles and constraints, market focus and startup profile, funnel design, core team recruitment, financial modeling, and reality checks before investor pitches.

The chapter identifies four characteristics of a "good studio": sustainability (multiple startups over years), efficiency (economies of scale and knowledge reuse), viable startups (revenue and growth), and antifragility (resource recycling from failures). It argues that the primary performance indicator is cumulative revenue (market signal of product-market fit, scalability, and prioritization discipline), not vanity metrics like startup count or user numbers.

## Key Takeaways

- **One Metric That Matters** — Cumulative revenue of startups plus the studio itself, not vanity metrics like startup count; revenue indicates that products solve real needs, the studio can sell effectively, the platform scales, prioritization is disciplined, and future fundraising leverage is strong.

- **Market Focus and Insider Advantage** — Studios must focus on markets where founders have personal experience and network; going into unknown markets without industry understanding is riskier than exploiting existing insider knowledge, connections, and potential customer access.

- **High-Margin Business Model** — Aim for high-margin segments where the studio can differentiate and avoid competing on price; high margins enable team scaling beyond agency workload and create capacity for building own startups rather than only contracting work.

- **Proven Market Needs over Unfounded Ideas** — Follow client money and build solutions for demonstrated market problems (know who the first paying customer will be), not idealistic "Uber for X" concepts; long-run commitment is 3-5 to 7 years before profitability.

- **Six-Step Strategic Framework** — Core principles and personal constraints; market and startup profile; funnel processes (ideation, validation, build, scaling); core team and EIR recruitment; financial modeling and fundraising strategy; and final reality check before pitching investors.

- **Reality Check Questions** — Before fundraising, confirm alignment with personal preferences, unfair competitive advantage, access to key people, initial capital availability, studio approach benefits, realistic financial model, knowledge of follow-on investors and acquirers, and genuine willingness to spend 5-10-15 years building and releasing ventures.

## Quotes

> Planning a studio is like planning a factory. First need to know what the product will be - in this case, the product is the startup.

> Know who your first client is and that they will pay for your product.

> Chose to build resilient startups over fragile unicorns. Leave behind the common urge for an instant-win. Commit to results over promises.

## Concepts

- [[Business Strategy]] — Studio planning requires defining high-margin market focus, demonstrating unfair advantages through insider knowledge and customer access, and building solutions for proven market needs rather than untested ideas.

- [[Studio Strategy]] — A studio's strategy is multi-layered: it defines the studio's characteristics (sustainable, efficient, viable startups, antifragile), selects the market to attack (based on founder experience and network), sets the funnel processes, and allocates resources across core team and founder recruitment.

- [[Portfolio Strategy]] — The primary metric is cumulative revenue rather than startup count; this reflects the studio's ability to build multiple viable companies that generate recurring revenue and maintain a sustainable competitive advantage over 3-5 to 7 years.

- [[Market Positioning]] — Studios should pick markets aligned with founder background, stay away from shiny but unknown markets, and focus on high-margin segments where unique value can be delivered; this positioning provides scalability and competitive moat.

- [[Idea Validation]] — The strategic framework includes a dedicated validation stage where new CEOs perform bare-minimum concept testing (offering only, no product) across 3-5 candidate ideas in parallel with investor market testing, trashing most ideas and graduating 1-2 to the build phase.
