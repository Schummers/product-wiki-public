# Solving Product Design Exercises: Why should you read this book?

## Summary

This chapter outlines the book's intended audiences and practical benefits for each. The book is designed to help professionals at all career stages prepare for and understand design exercises in hiring processes at major tech companies like Google, Facebook, WeWork, and Amazon. It serves multiple constituencies: designers preparing for interviews and career advancement, business leaders learning to hire designers, and collaborators from other fields seeking to better understand design thinking.

## Key Takeaways

- **Five audience categories** — Entry-level designers learning required skills and building portfolios; mid-level and senior designers improving product thinking and learning to interview others; career switchers entering product design; business leaders hiring designers; and other professionals (product managers, engineers, data scientists) seeking to make better product decisions.
- **Entry-level designers gain foundation** — The book helps junior designers understand what top companies expect, practice solving real problems, and build portfolios that demonstrate problem-solving rather than visual skills alone.
- **Mid-level progression path** — For experienced designers, the book provides a framework to advance career progression by deepening product thinking and developing hiring and mentorship skills.
- **Organizational value** — Business leaders can use the material to define what they expect from designers and how to evaluate candidates and their role's scope.
- **Cross-functional collaboration** — Product managers, engineers, and other roles can improve collaboration and decision-making by understanding how designers approach problems.

## Quotes

> This book is for anyone who wants to understand the Product Designer role at tech companies, to get a sense of what kind of challenges they are dealing with and to practice solving them.

> More specifically, I'll talk about how hiring processes work at tech companies, and focus on design exercises both from a candidate's and an interviewer's perspective. These exercises are inspired by those used in hiring processes by companies such as Google, Facebook, WeWork and Amazon.
