# How to Run a Journey-Mapping Workshop: A Step-by-Step Case Study

## Summary

This article provides a practical, step-by-step guide to running a journey-mapping workshop, divided into three phases: preparation, execution, and follow-up. Before the workshop, build a crossfunctional team, prioritize specific actors and scenarios, gather existing research, and assign homework to prime participants. During the workshop, use trivia to refresh knowledge, review research, map current-state assumptions, interview customers to validate and evolve the map, generate and prioritize pain points, brainstorm future-state "big ideas," and sketch future-state flows. After the workshop, capture artifacts, create prototypes, and maintain momentum. The structure is flexible and should be adapted to the facilitator's style and organizational context.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prepare thoroughly before the workshop** — Build a crossfunctional team of allies, decide which actor and scenario to map, consolidate existing research into a shared repository, and provide attendees with background reading and thought-starter questions to mentally prepare them.
- **Create current-state assumption maps first** — Have teams consolidate their collective knowledge into a hypothesis map before interviewing customers. This approach surfaces what the team knows or assumes, creates a shared baseline for discussion, and provides a structured frame for customer feedback.
- **Use customers to validate and evolve maps** — Invite actual users into the workshop to react to assumption maps. Provide tangible tools (stickers, sticky notes) to help customers mark agreement or disagreement, then discuss and adapt the map based on real user experiences.
- **Prioritize pain points before ideating solutions** — Generate needs statements from identified pain points using fill-in-the-blank prompts (e.g., "I need _____ so that _____"). Use dot voting to identify the most critical pain points to guide future-state ideation and prevent jumping to solutions prematurely.
- **Combine customer ideas with internal sketching** — Involve customers in brainstorming "big ideas," then have internal teams translate ideas into sketched flows using design-studio techniques (timed rounds of sketching, presentation, critique). This leverages customer insight while preserving intellectual property and practical feasibility.

## Quotes

> Journey mapping is a collaborative process.

> If you create your map in a silo without involving others, you run the risk that the people whose support you need to get things done post mapping will not believe in it or be passionate about your findings.

> Move quickly after the workshop in order to maintain momentum and make use of the excitement generated in the workshop.
