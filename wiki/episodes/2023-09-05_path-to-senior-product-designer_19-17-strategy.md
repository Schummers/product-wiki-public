# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 17. Strategy

## Summary

This chapter addresses the competency required to shift from tactical to strategic work, particularly for senior designer roles and leadership positions. Strategy competency involves understanding the internal and external factors that drive business decisions: company objectives and metrics, mission and vision, product roadmap, organizational structure and team incentives, and competitive landscape. The chapter argues that designers increase their impact by learning product management skills—not necessarily financial modeling or roadmap ownership, but the shared competencies around what to build, advocating for business and users, and optimizing the customer experience end-to-end.

## Key Takeaways

- **Be more like a PM** — Designers grow their impact and career velocity by adopting product thinking skills (what to build next, end-to-end customer experience) without taking on full PM responsibilities. The second designer who suggests how to simplify a task to reduce development effort while maintaining business impact provides more value than one who simply executes the original design brief.
- **Understand KPIs and OKRs** — Companies internally define key performance indicators (revenue per customer, conversion rate, host churn) and specific goals (OKRs) tied to business strategy. Designers should ask their manager for documentation of these metrics and contribute to the team's progress on them, as the CEO's primary question about any design is how it increases revenue or reduces costs.
- **Know the product roadmap, mission, and organizational structure** — Designers should familiarize themselves with the company's vision statement, the planned features and timelines, and an organizational chart showing departments, roles, and reporting relationships. This helps identify what design conflicts to avoid, what feature ideas are already planned, and who to partner with to influence decisions.
- **Map stakeholder objectives** — Different roles (PMs, engineers, marketing, sales, support) have different incentives and objectives. Understanding these incentives explains behavior and reveals who has authority over specific topics, enabling better collaboration and influence.
- **Study competitors and industry leaders** — Identify direct competitors and track their product updates, feature launches, and business moves using tools like G2, Product Hunt, or Google auto-complete searches. Read reviews of competitors both ways: complaints about their solutions show gaps your product can cover, positive reviews show what users find especially valuable. Follow industry-leading companies and their leadership on social media to understand the broader business context.
- **Build a reusable framework** — The first time a designer analyzes a business is effortful, but understanding one business creates a mental framework that makes understanding the next business faster and enables applying lessons across companies.

## Quotes

> Your impact inside an organization is correlated with how likely you are to increase revenue or reduce costs for the company.

> What I believe made me a good designer is getting my craft to a good enough level that I could redirect my learnings toward non-design skills that eventually made me a more valuable designer.

> To become a better designer, you have to understand how your company makes money and how it spends money to bring in customers (most probably via ads from Google, Facebook, an app store, or sales outreach).

## Concepts

- [[Design Strategy]] — This chapter defines strategy as understanding internal business factors (objectives, vision, roadmap, people) and external factors (competition) to make informed design decisions and increase career impact.
- [[Business Impact]] — The chapter emphasizes that designer impact is measured by contribution to business metrics like revenue and cost reduction, and that designers who understand business context can suggest changes that maintain business impact while reducing development effort.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Understanding the incentives and objectives of different roles (PMs, engineers, marketing, sales) across the organization is essential for knowing who to partner with and how to influence decisions.
- [[UX Career]] — Strategic thinking is a key competency for advancing beyond senior designer roles, and designers accelerate their career by learning product management skills and business understanding rather than design craft alone.
