# Design for Kids Based on Their Stage of Physical Development

## Summary

Children's physical abilities develop in stages, affecting how they interact with digital devices. Gross motor skills develop earlier than fine motor skills, which influences appropriate interaction patterns for different age groups. Children under 5 have limited motor abilities and require simple touchscreen interactions. Children 6-8 can handle simple clicking and keyboard use. Around age 9, more advanced interactions become possible. By age 11, children can use similar interaction ranges as adults. Understanding these developmental stages is critical for designing usable digital products for children.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design touchscreen interfaces for kids under 9** — Emphasize swiping, tapping, and dragging as these gestures require large arm and hand movements that develop earlier than fine motor skills. Design buttons at least 2cm x 2cm for young children.
- **Use simple keyboard interactions for desktop** — Avoid complex gestures like dragging or scrolling on desktop for kids under 9. Stick to simple clicks and arrow key controls instead.
- **Avoid complex motor coordination demands** — Don't require two-handed use or quick manual reactions for kids under 8, as motor coordination develops later and games exceeding their abilities cause frustration rather than engagement.
- **Make designs flexible** — Allow alternative interaction methods (dragging or tapping) for the same task to accommodate varying motor abilities and preferences within age groups.
- **Reduce navigation requirements** — Recommend similar content rather than requiring kids to navigate back to a homepage to find related content, supporting both limited motor skills and working memory constraints.

## Quotes

> When designing apps for kids, designers must consider the physical abilities and constraints of target age groups.

> Although the precise timeline will depend on the child and on the complexity of the actions involved, gross motor skills tend to develop earlier than fine motor skills.

> UIs that exceed the kids' motor ability will discourage them and even make them lose confidence in the device.
