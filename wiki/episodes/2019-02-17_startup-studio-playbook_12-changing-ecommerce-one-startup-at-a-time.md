# Startup Studio Playbook: Changing eCommerce, one startup at a time

## Summary

This chapter features the story of Innonic Group, a startup studio founded in 2017 by a core team that evolved from a web design agency into a global ecommerce solutions player. The chapter describes how the studio applied rigorous, cost-efficient validation processes to build multiple startups simultaneously while maintaining strong organizational culture based on core values (AWESOME: Always Keep Improving, Wow Your Customers, Experiment, Sweep Floor, Owner Mentality, Mutual Respect, Enjoy the Game).

Innonic operates a five-stage funnel: ideation through continuous collection of ideas, research-based pre-validation (2-4 weeks), validation with a Minimal Awesome Product (3-6 months), formation with significant capital investment (18 months), and growth toward independence or venture capital fundraising. The studio self-funds all ventures from internal cash flows generated by legacy businesses like ShopRenter.hu and OptiMonk.com, reducing founder and investor dependence.

## Key Takeaways

- **Studio Funnel** — Innonic's five-stage process systematically moves ideas from ideation through research validation (customer interviews and landing page conversion testing), to MVP validation with dedicated CEOs, to formation with EUR 200,000 capital allocation, and finally to growth and independence or VC funding.

- **Minimal Awesome Product over MVP** — Rather than buggy minimum viable products, the studio builds products that solve a painful problem uniquely or deliver superior user friendliness from day one, because modern users expect beautiful, well-functioning software with no tolerance for poor initial quality.

- **Self-Funding Model** — By generating profit from legacy businesses (ShopRenter.hu held 30% ecommerce market dominance; OptiMonk was ranked #1 on-site messaging toolkit), Innonic funds 2-3 new ventures annually without external investor timelines or venture capital constraints.

- **Culture as Competitive Advantage** — The AWESOME core values and investments in employee development (academy talks, management training, massages, gym passes, supported coaching) create competitive advantage and differentiate the studio from competitors.

- **Ideation as Continuous Process** — Ideas are systematically collected from customer conversations, conferences, and reading in a shared spreadsheet, then evaluated by the Validation Team using multiple evaluation models; no single "golden standard" method dominates.

- **Cost Discipline in Validation** — Pre-validation costs 2-4 weeks and only a couple thousand dollars per idea; full validation with a dedicated CEO costs EUR 20,000 across 3-6 months, enforcing ruthless prioritization of only the most promising concepts.

## Concepts

- [[Venture Studio]] — Innonic demonstrates the studio model built not from investor capital but from a team that evolved from a web design agency, emphasizing self-funding and the ability to launch 2-3 ventures per year from own capital.

- [[Studio Funnel]] — Innonic's funnel operationalizes the five stages (ideation, research, validation, formation, growth) with specific timelines (2-4 weeks for research, 3-6 months for validation, 18 months for formation) and resource allocation (couple thousand dollars for pre-validation, EUR 20,000 for full validation).

- [[Studio Culture]] — The AWESOME core values (Always Keep Improving, Wow Your Customers, Experiment, Sweep Floor, Owner Mentality, Mutual Respect, Enjoy the Game) and human investment practices are positioned as foundational competitive advantage, supporting retention and performance of both core team and venture CEOs.

- [[Idea Validation]] — Innonic validates ideas through customer interviews, Business Model Canvas analysis, landing page conversion measurement, and traffic generation via Facebook, Google Ads, and cold emails; this process is fine-tuned enough to warrant an online course.

- [[Portfolio Strategy]] — Legacy businesses (ShopRenter.hu, OptiMonk.com, Ecommerce Expo) generate profit that funds new ventures; example portfolio companies include CodersRank.io and Conversific.com, which have raised external venture funding.
