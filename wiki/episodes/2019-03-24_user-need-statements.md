# User Need Statements: The 'Define' Stage in Design Thinking

## Summary

User need statements, also called problem statements or point-of-view statements, are actionable problem definitions created during the define stage of design thinking. They summarize who a user is, what they need, and why the need matters to them. By framing needs as verbs (goals and end states) rather than nouns (solutions), teams avoid premature solution fixation and keep the focus on real user problems rather than specific features.

## Key Takeaways

- **Format: three components** — A complete need statement combines a specific user, a genuine need rooted in research, and an insight about the goal or outcome the user seeks.

- **Needs as verbs, not nouns** — Users need to accomplish outcomes, not specific UI components; focusing on dropdown lists or dashboards prematurely constrains the solution space.

- **Benefits beyond alignment** — Need statements capture user and problem knowledge into one sentence, provide metrics for success, and serve as a guiding force throughout the project lifecycle.

- **Process requires research** — Effective need statements come from qualitative research like user interviews, field studies, and diary studies, not from team assumptions.

- **Distinguish from development statements** — Need statements focus on empathetic understanding of user context; development statements come later and describe specific implementation tasks.

## Quotes

> "User need statement: An actionable problem statement used to summarize who a particular user is, the user's need, and why the need is important to that user."

> "A great solution to the wrong problem will fail."

> "users do not always know what they need, even though they may say so."

## Concepts

- [[Design Thinking]] — User need statements are the foundation of the define stage, ensuring teams solve real problems before ideating.

- [[User Research]] — Qualitative methods like interviews and field studies uncover genuine user needs; need statements that capture goals rather than solutions keep focus on what users are trying to accomplish in their lives.

- [[Problem Framing]] — The statement forces teams to articulate what they're solving before generating solutions, reducing wasted effort.

- [[Design Process]] — Need statements serve as a north star for alignment across team members and stakeholders throughout the project.
