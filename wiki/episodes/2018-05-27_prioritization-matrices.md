# Using Prioritization Matrices to Inform UX Decisions

## Summary

Prioritization matrices are two-dimensional visuals that show the relative importance of items based on two weighted criteria. UX practitioners use these matrices to identify the most important problems and achieve collaborative consensus while balancing user needs with business goals. The matrices can be adapted to different team needs, incorporating multiple criteria, various scales of measurement, and different voting approaches. The method combines expert voting with team discussion to prevent individual bias from dominating outcomes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Structure voting by expertise** — Team members vote on items using colored dots, with each color representing an area of expertise (e.g., developers for feasibility, designers for user impact), preventing the loudest person from dominating.
- **Use the Eisenhower Method as foundation** — The technique builds on decades of prioritization methods, originally from time management but adapted for UX to balance urgency and importance.
- **Discuss and negotiate placement** — After initial plotting based on votes, teams should collaboratively discuss and potentially adjust item placement to reach consensus agreement.
- **Create multiple matrices for complex decisions** — When more than two criteria matter, split them into pairs of matrices, keeping the best outcome consistently in the top-right or top-left of each axis for easy comparison.
- **Apply democratic voting** — Vote privately or digitally if your team is prone to group think or HIPPO effect, ensuring the process remains democratic rather than biased toward influential individuals.
