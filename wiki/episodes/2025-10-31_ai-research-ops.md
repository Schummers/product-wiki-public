# Stop Making Your Team Figure Out AI on Their Own

## Summary

Organizations are telling employees to use AI tools without providing clear guidance on how, why, or which problems they solve. This creates chaos including performative usage, fragmentation across tool choices, security risks from individuals uploading sensitive data, collaboration breakdown, and burnout from repeated undiscussed expectations. The solution is to treat AI adoption like any significant organizational change: analyze current workflows to understand real problems, design targeted interventions, invest in integrated tools with security controls, create clear policies, build a shared prompt repository, pilot before scaling, and provide proper change management and training rather than expecting individuals to figure it out alone.

## Key Takeaways

- **Measure value before adoption** — teams are evaluated on using AI without clear success metrics, creating performative usage where people mention AI in status updates just to appear current, not because it improves outcomes.
- **Individual AI mastery creates silos** — experienced practitioners develop elaborate "grimoires" of prompts and workarounds that are opaque to others and trapped in individual heads, fragmenting team knowledge and preventing shared practices.
- **Context switching wastes effort** — using different AI tools for different workflow stages (ChatGPT for brainstorming, Claude for analysis, Perplexity for research) requires manually copying context between tools, with each tool losing valuable information from earlier steps.
- **Security risks arise from unclear policies** — without explicit guidelines, individuals inadvertently upload customer data, participant transcripts, and strategy documents to public AI tools, creating compliance and privacy violations.
- **Fragmentation breaks collaboration** — teams using different AI tools and methodologies cannot easily share or build on each other's work, making peer review harder and preventing effective collaboration on shared projects.
- **Apply established adoption practices** — treat AI like any other tool rollout: analyze workflows, design specific interventions, pilot with small teams, establish clear goals and success metrics, provide training, and support teams through transition rather than asking individuals to pioneer alone.

## Quotes

> People are being told to use AI without being given any real guidance on how, why, or which problems it should solve. It's creating chaos, security risks, and collaboration problems.

> We're measuring adoption before we've measured value. It's like grading developers on how many new JavaScript frameworks they've tried without asking whether their code is actually better.

> The problem isn't that you haven't found the right prompt or the right tool yet. The problem is that you're being asked to solve an organizational problem by yourself.
