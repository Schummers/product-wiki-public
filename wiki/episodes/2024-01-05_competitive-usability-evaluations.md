# Competitive Usability Evaluations

## Summary

Competitive evaluations assess whether a design is better or worse than competitors and identify relative strengths and weaknesses of competing designs. They can be conducted as expert reviews analyzing multiple products or as usability tests where users complete tasks on competing designs. Typical evaluations focus on 2-4 competitors; selecting the right competitors involves considering similar content/functionality, best overall experience, innovative designs, and most important competitors to customers. Competitive evaluations inform design direction, reduce risk through data-driven decisions, and help teams learn from competitors. The goal is not to declare a winner but to improve one's own design through insights about what works and what doesn't.

## Key Takeaways

- **Competitive evaluations compare multiple product designs to assess relative strengths and weaknesses** — conducted through expert reviews or usability testing with users completing tasks on competing designs.
- **Focus on 2-4 carefully selected competitors** — choosing competitors involves considering similar content/functionality, best user experience, innovative designs, strongest competition, and most likely comparison targets for customers.
- **Competitive reviews by experts are cost-effective** — experienced reviewers identify trends, patterns, holes in content/functionality, and design approaches that could inspire improvements.
- **Competitive testing with users requires realistic scope and randomization** — participants typically test 2-3 sites; alternating order and site pairs prevents learnability effects; asking participants to compare sites provides additional insights.
- **Learn from both well-designed and poorly-designed competitors** — while poorly-designed sites show what to avoid, well-designed competitors may have solved the same problems through usability iteration.

## Quotes

> Competitive evaluations let you assess if your design is better or worse than your competitors and discover the relative strengths and weaknesses of competing designs. They allow you to take an in-depth look at how others solve the same design problems.

> The goal of any competitive evaluation is to see what competitors are doing, how they're doing it, what's working, and what's not.

> You want to beat the competition, not copy them.

## Concepts

- [[Competitive Analysis]] — competitive evaluations are a form of competitive analysis that combines design review with user research to understand market positioning.
- [[Usability Testing]] — both expert review and usability testing methods can be applied to evaluate competitors' designs using established UX evaluation approaches.
- [[Research Methods]] — competitive testing is a research method where users complete tasks on competitors' products to gather comparative performance data.
- [[Benchmarking]] — competitive evaluations establish benchmarks showing relative performance of competing designs on metrics like success rates and time on task.
- [[Heuristic Evaluation]] — expert reviewers can conduct competitive reviews more cost-effectively than testing, identifying trends and opportunities across multiple competitors.
