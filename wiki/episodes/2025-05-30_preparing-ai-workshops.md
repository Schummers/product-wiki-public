# 4 Tips for Preparing AI-Enhanced Workshops

## Summary

Generative AI can enhance team ideation through collaborative interaction, but success requires deliberate preparation. Workshop facilitators must account for varied experience levels with AI and structure activities to leverage the tool's strengths without overwhelming participants. Four key preparation strategies ensure participants can engage effectively: building familiarity through warmup activities, providing context-rich documents for upload, creating custom AI instances with specific knowledge, and offering adaptable sample prompts.

Effective AI workshop preparation recognizes that participants bring different skill levels and comfort levels with LLMs. The goal is to remove barriers to participation and provide scaffolding that helps both novices and experienced users contribute meaningfully to collaborative ideation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Warmup activities build confidence** — dedicated activities like AI-based icebreakers help participants become comfortable with LLM interfaces early, reducing hesitation during main ideation work.
- **Pre-prepared context documents accelerate work** — uploading company descriptions, personas, research data, and constraints before the workshop saves time and ensures consistent context across participants.
- **Custom AI instances provide powerful constraints** — creating specialized AI assistants (GPTs, Gems, or Claude Projects) pre-trained on team context, guidelines, and constraints produces more aligned outputs without additional participant effort.
- **Sample prompts serve as starter kits** — providing adaptable prompts following the CAREful framework (Context, Ask, Rules, Examples) helps participants overcome hesitation and demonstrates effective prompting techniques.
- **Familiarity matters more than tool variety** — encouraging all participants to use the same LLM prevents distraction and allows troubleshooting to focus on prompting, not tool differences.

## Quotes

> "Well-crafted warmups help groups become familiar with the session's tools. Whether this is a shared document, a digital whiteboard, or Sharpies and sticky notes, getting the tools into people's hands is meant to remove any hesitations or access obstacles that participants may have."

> "Part of good prompting is providing adequate context. Sample prompts help with this, but it's often much more effective to prepare context-providing documents before the workshop."

> "Your sample prompts are meant to be a starter kit, intended for adaptation and abandonment as people get going. However, they can serve as instructive demonstrations of useful prompting and steer people in a helpful direction for the workshop."
