# Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions in User Research

## Summary

Open-ended and closed questions serve different purposes in user research and elicit different types of responses. Open-ended questions encourage exploration and allow participants to share details and context, revealing unexpected insights and mental models. Closed questions restrict responses to limited options and are useful for gathering clarifying details or quantitative data. A key principle is using open-ended questions as the foundation of research (following the funnel technique) before narrowing with specific closed questions. Closed questions can inadvertently lead participants by revealing what the researcher is interested in, so they should be constructed carefully.

## Key Takeaways

- **Open-ended questions enable discovery** — they allow participants to explore topics freely and reveal unexpected motivations, mental models, and concerns that researchers may not have anticipated.
- **Closed questions are easier to answer and analyze** — participants can respond quickly with limited options, making surveys easier to complete and responses simpler to quantify statistically.
- **Use the funnel technique** — begin research sessions with broad, open-ended questions before introducing specific, closed questions to avoid priming participants toward particular answers.
- **Leading closed questions bias responses** — questions beginning with "did," "was," or "is" often suggest an expected answer, whereas open-ended questions starting with "how" or "what" remain neutral.
- **Question construction matters greatly** — minor wording changes significantly affect what people share; asking "How do you normally...?" instead of "Do you normally...?" produces richer responses.
- **Probing questions extend open-ended inquiry** — phrases like "Tell me more about that" and "Why do you think that?" help researchers dig deeper into initial responses.

## Quotes

> Open-ended questions allow participants to give a free-form text answer.

> The greatest benefit of open-ended questions is that they allow you to find more than you anticipate. You don't know what you don't know. People may share motivations you didn't expect and mention behaviors and concerns you knew nothing about.

> Closed questions stop the conversation. If an interviewer or usability-test facilitator were to ask only closed questions, the conversation would be stilted and surface-level.
