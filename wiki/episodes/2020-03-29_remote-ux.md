# Remote UX Work: Guidelines and Resources

## Summary

This comprehensive guide addresses transitioning three types of common UX activities to remote formats: user research, facilitating and presenting, and collaboration and brainstorming. The article provides practical tips for each activity type, recommends specific tools matched to purposes, and shares general guidelines based on Nielsen Norman Group's experience as a 100% remote company. Benefits of remote work include reduced expenses, increased inclusiveness, and participant convenience.

## Key Takeaways

- **Remote Research Setup** — practice technology beforehand, recruit additional users as a safety net against technical failures, plan for technology issues with backup plans, provide clear instructions especially for complex tools, and adjust consent forms for remote recordings.
- **Moderated vs Unmoderated** — moderated remote research allows follow-up questions and probing while unmoderated sessions are faster but provide less detailed data; both are preferable to no research at all.
- **Remote Facilitating Essentials** — turn on camera to establish rapport, plan for relationship building and connection via icebreakers, create ground rules to manage communication challenges, assign homework to practice tool use, and adapt workshop structures to accommodate digital format.
- **Synchronous vs Asynchronous Collaboration** — mix both methods to shorten meeting length; use prestructured shared documents for asynchronous contributions then meet to discuss; structure activities to ensure full participation.
- **Tool Selection Principle** — favor familiar tools already known by the team over specialized new tools to avoid intimidation and learning curves; think creatively about using existing tools for new purposes.
- **Complexity Management** — keep tools few and familiar; don't make participants switch between multiple tools; ensure technology does not become the focus; ask for technical help when facilitating complex sessions.
- **Practice Pays Off** — even when familiar with tools, practice sessions with colleagues help surface issues; host dry runs and pilot sessions before launching; get technical assistance for first-time complex sessions.

## Quotes

> In these cases, remote sessions can offer immense value in maintaining the flow of insights and ideas.

> Did I mention practice? Get familiar with the technology before logging in to present your research recommendations or lead a remote brainstorming session.

> Assume technology challenges will happen, and don’t panic when they do. Have a backup plan ready, such as a phone dial-in in addition to a web link for user interviews and, whenever possible, use a platform that doesn’t require participants to download anything to join the session.
