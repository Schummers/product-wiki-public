# User-Centered Intranet Redesign: Set Up for Success in 11 Steps

## Summary

Successfully redesigning an intranet requires extensive foundational work before design begins, based on analysis of hundreds of high-performing intranet projects over 20 years. The early phase involves four main areas: assembling a team with clear leadership, understanding current state through research, aligning on vision with stakeholders, and securing resources. Eleven specific steps guide this process: appoint a leader, assemble the team, identify the redesign catalyst, conduct user research and measurement, map the organization's digital ecosystem, involve stakeholders, align on vision, allocate resources, create user-centered artifacts, plan systems and operations, and advertise early. The catalyst (merger, technology update, rebranding, or complaints) influences which steps will be harder or easier. Success depends on treating the intranet as serving organizational goals while building excitement and involving people early to overcome change aversion.

## Key Takeaways

- **Leadership and clear ownership prevent diffusion of responsibility** — appointing an official intranet leader who manages vision, team coordination, stakeholder relationships, and decision-making is essential for maintaining focus.
- **80% of award-winning intranets hire external agencies** — outside perspectives and specialized skills fill gaps, though long-term partnerships that extend beyond launch are more effective than one-off engagements.
- **Research should precede vision-setting** — understanding current intranet performance, employee needs, existing systems, and organizational goals must inform—not follow—the vision statement.
- **Four reference points guide research** — job roles and relationships, tasks related to business objectives, current system performance, and opportunities for improvement provide essential early-stage knowledge.
- **Digital ecosystem mapping prevents siloed tools** — inventorying all intranets, extranets, and tools employees use helps teams identify opportunities for consolidation or replacement.
- **User-centered artifacts keep distributed teams focused** — journey maps, personas, top tasks, service blueprints, and jobs-to-be-done keep part-time team members aligned and support iterative design.

## Quotes

> A successful intranet-redesign project begins well before sketching, feature selection, or coding.

> The leader is responsible for distilling many perspectives from upper management and stakeholders from around the organization, making the most of tools in the digital workplace and keeping the team focused.

> Once the vision is decided upon, look for opportunities to socialize the idea of a new intranet across the organization early on. Since people are change averse, get them used to the idea of a redesign.
