# Setup of an Eyetracking Study

## Summary

Eyetracking equipment records where and how long users look at content, producing three types of visualizations: gazeplots (individual fixations), gaze replays (video of eye movement), and heatmaps (aggregate fixation data across participants). The article details the physical lab setup, equipment requirements (tracker, monitors, PC, supporting materials), participant positioning, and practical considerations for running reliable eyetracking studies. It emphasizes the importance of equipment calibration, pilot testing, and setup time to overcome technical challenges.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three visualization types serve different purposes** — Gazeplots and gaze replays require 8-12 participants for qualitative insights; heatmaps require approximately 39 participants to show aggregate patterns of where users focus on a page.

- **Equipment calibration is essential and participant-specific** — Every participant must be calibrated individually because eye and face shape differ; loss of calibration during sessions ruins data, requiring real-time monitoring and recalibration.

- **Lab setup requires separate monitors for participant and facilitator** — The facilitator's monitor displays real-time gaze data (red dots and lines), allowing live monitoring of calibration and preventing data loss from participant movement.

- **Room environment affects tracking accuracy** — Position the eyetracker away from direct overhead lighting; use fixed chairs for participants (to prevent calibration drift from movement) and rolling chairs for facilitators to adjust equipment without disturbing participants.

- **Task delivery methods impact data quality** — Referring to printed task sheets causes participants to look away from the tracker; when possible, deliver tasks verbally or through the eyetracking software itself to maintain continuous tracking.

- **Plan for technical difficulties and dedicate setup time** — Expect equipment challenges; run 1-2 pilot days before data collection; dedicate 1-2 full days just to setup and equipment testing before pilots begin.

## Quotes

> Traditional eyetracking tools are complex, delicate systems. You'll want plenty of time to think through and experiment with your study setup.

> In an eyetracking study, the tracker has to be calibrated for each participant. Every individual has a different eye shape, face shape, and height.

> Compared to 2006, eyetracking tools have certainly improved the calibration process and they've gotten better at hiding the eyetracking mechanisms in the eyetracker (thanks largely to smaller cameras).

## Concepts

- [[Eye Tracking]] — A method using specialized equipment to measure eye fixations and saccades, producing data about visual attention patterns on digital and physical interfaces.

- [[User Testing Setup]] — Eyetracking requires precise physical setup: participant positioning, monitor arrangement, equipment calibration, and environmental controls to collect valid data.

- [[Research Equipment]] — Desktop eyetracking systems integrate specialized cameras, calibration algorithms, and software to track gaze; setup details (monitor height, lighting, distance) directly affect data quality.

- [[Data Visualization]] — Eyetracking produces multiple visualization formats (gazeplots, replays, heatmaps) suitable for different analysis goals and audience sizes.
