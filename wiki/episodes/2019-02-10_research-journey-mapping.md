# How to Conduct Research for Customer Journey-Mapping

## Summary

This article discusses which research methods are most appropriate for collecting data to create accurate customer journey maps. Journey maps must be grounded in reality rather than stakeholder assumptions, requiring direct observation and conversation with users across all phases of their journey. The article recommends a multipronged qualitative research approach combined with quantitative data to create compelling, evidence-based journey maps that drive organizational change.

## Key Takeaways

- **Research is essential before creating journey maps** — Assumption-based maps lack credibility and risk driving poor design decisions; research provides the data needed to create compelling maps that drive real change.
- **Start with existing organizational data** — Before conducting new research, audit internal sources like past focus groups, support call logs, analytics, and satisfaction scores to understand what is already known about the customer journey.
- **Use multiple qualitative methods** — Combine interviews, field studies, diary studies, and competitive analysis to understand users' thoughts, feelings, and motivations across all journey phases.
- **Interviews capture stories and context** — One-on-one conversations uncover first-hand experiences, frustrations, and needs; in-person interviews with visual mapping help participants accurately recall steps.
- **Field studies verify actual behavior** — Observing real usage in natural environments reveals blind spots and differences between what people say they do and what they actually do.
- **Supplement qualitative research with quantitative data** — Use surveys, analytics, satisfaction metrics, and social sentiment analysis to add credibility and demonstrate frequency/magnitude of discovered issues.

## Quotes

> While stakeholders do hold valuable knowledge about different areas of the customer journey, most of them do not have a broad enough perspective of the customer journey, nor a deep enough perspective of users needs at each stage, to be able to piece together a realistic, comprehensive view.

> Because what people say they do is not always what they actually do, it's best to couple interviews with additional qualitative methods, such as field studies.

> Take a look around your organization: Is there any existing data that might prove useful, or at least help you shape your research plan?

- [[Journey Mapping]] — Research-based method for visualizing steps customers take to accomplish goals, requiring direct user observation and data synthesis across multiple phases.
- [[Research Methods]] — Qualitative techniques including interviews, field studies, diary studies, and competitive analysis combined for comprehensive customer journey understanding.
- [[Qualitative Research]] — Emphasis on unstructured observation and conversation to understand user emotions, motivations, and actual behaviors rather than just reported preferences.
