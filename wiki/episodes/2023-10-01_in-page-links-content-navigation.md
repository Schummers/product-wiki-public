# In-Page Links for Content Navigation

## Summary

Research on user perception and behavior with in-page links (also called jump links or anchor links) shows that user familiarity with this pattern has grown significantly. Most users now recognize and appreciate in-page links for skipping to relevant sections, though the links must be clearly labeled and styled to signal their behavior. The article provides guidance on when to use in-page links versus alternatives like accordions or tabs, and emphasizes that content structure should drive design pattern selection.

## Key Takeaways

- **Increasing Familiarity** — Most study participants were familiar with in-page links regardless of age, showing that the pattern has become widely understood; only 2 of 11 participants showed no engagement.
- **Clear Labeling Essential** — Users must be able to tell whether a link stays on the current page or leads elsewhere; labels like "Table of Contents" or "On This Page" help clarify in-page link behavior.
- **Proper Styling Signifies Clickability** — In-page links must be styled as links (colored, underlined) to be recognizable; links without sufficient visual signifiers are overlooked in testing.
- **Skip Irrelevant Content** — Users appreciate in-page links for bypassing sections they don't need, allowing them to avoid scrolling through screenfuls of irrelevant information.
- **Page Length Consideration** — In-page links are most valuable for long-form content; on shorter pages, a table of contents adds unnecessary length without benefit.
- **Logical Chunking Required** — Content must be naturally divisible into distinct sections to make in-page links useful; pages with cohesive narratives don't benefit from breaking points.

## Concepts

- [[Navigation Design]] — In-page links are an effective navigation pattern that users have become familiar with, but they require clear styling and labeling to be effective.
- [[Content Strategy]] — The decision to use in-page links should be driven by content structure first; logical chunking of information is a prerequisite for making the pattern useful.
- [[Information Architecture]] — In-page links provide an overview of page content that helps users form mental models and assess whether a page meets their information needs.
