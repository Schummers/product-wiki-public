# We Can Do Better on Mobile: Designing for the Medium

## Summary

Mobile devices are not just smaller desktops; they have unique characteristics — touch and gesture input, sensors, cameras, and diverse contexts of use — that create opportunities for innovative experiences. Many current mobile designs are merely scaled-down versions of desktop designs, missing chances to leverage mobile-specific capabilities. Good mobile design should prioritize mobile-relevant information, integrate device features, and innovate by creating experiences impossible or difficult on desktop.

## Key Takeaways

- **Information prioritization** — mobile users care most about location-based, time-sensitive, emergency, and phone-number information; design should make this mobile-relevant content easy to find over less critical information.
- **Integration of capabilities** — leverage built-in mobile features like cameras, GPS, biometrics, and touch input to enable unique workflows and remove friction (e.g., barcode scanning for pharmacy refills instead of entering long numbers).
- **Innovation in design** — create experiences that take advantage of portability and context; simple changes like location-based reminders instead of time-based ones are possible only on mobile.
- **Deep design innovation** — rethink entire business processes for mobile, not just UI; new models like mobile-only insurance for individual drone flights with real-time location data show what's possible.
- **Mobile as its own medium** — stop thinking of mobile as a constrained version of web; the power of mobile devices in diverse contexts creates opportunities for entirely new solutions.
