# Startup Studio Playbook: Entrepreneurs-in-Residence and CEOs

## Summary

This chapter addresses the fundamental challenge in studio building: finding and developing the right entrepreneurs-in-residence (EIRs) and startup CEOs. An effective EIR combines entrepreneurial passion and risk-taking with the flexibility to shift focus among conflicting priorities across multiple ventures. The chapter outlines best practices for EIR selection, onboarding, and management, emphasizing that studios must build sustainable CEO development systems to scale beyond handpicked founders.

The chapter describes profiling ideal CEO personas based on desired startup characteristics, assessing candidates on track record, leadership style, market network, and coachability, and attracting talent through personal networks, studio brand presence, meetup participation, community building, and programs like Hackers-in-Residence. It emphasizes training programs covering startup know-how, ideation and validation, sales and growth, investor relations, project management, and company management.

## Key Takeaways

- **Ideal EIR Profile** — EIRs are part entrepreneur, part manager, able to discover market needs via lean methods, direct core team toward viable products, sell and generate traction, and raise follow-on funding; they balance entrepreneurial passion with flexibility to shift focus among multiple initiatives.

- **Candidate Assessment on Five Factors** — Evaluate on previous track record, leadership style, network in the specific market, coachability, and willingness to work across multiple ventures with eventual release of mature companies; juniors require training investment while veterans demand compelling compensation.

- **Recruitment Scalability** — Personal networks are limited and non-scalable; studios must build strong brand presence, speak at meetups, build community, and experiment with programs like Hackers-in-Residence to attract a pipeline of EIR candidates beyond the founder's immediate circle.

- **Comprehensive Training Programs** — Studios must provide CEO development covering generic startup know-how (idea to exit), ideation and validation skills, revenue-generating abilities (sales and growth hacking), investor relations, project and people management, and legal/regulatory/financial company management.

- **Regular Management and Knowledge Sharing** — Studio leaders maintain one-on-ones with EIRs to check progress, ensure neither overload nor boredom, participate in seed fundraising meetings, and facilitate knowledge sharing among the EIR cohort through structured programs.

- **Equity Compensation Model** — Studios typically take 30-80% of equity (average 50%) reflecting their provision of idea, infrastructure, capital, and resources; CEO equity can be rebalanced during Series A to keep CEOs motivated and make new investors comfortable, diluting studio and fund shares.

## Concepts

- [[Entrepreneur in Residence]] — EIRs are essential to studio scaling; they combine entrepreneurial drive with flexibility to move between ventures, discover market needs via lean validation, lead teams, generate sales traction, and navigate fundraising; studios recruit on track record, leadership style, market network, and coachability.

- [[Founder Recruitment]] — Studios attract EIRs through personal networks, brand presence, meetup participation, community building, and specialized programs like Hackers-in-Residence; the challenge is sourcing beyond early circles and convincing senior entrepreneurs to commit despite lack of prior studio results.

- [[Team Scaling]] — To scale beyond handpicked founders, studios need sustainable CEO development systems covering startup know-how, ideation and validation, sales and growth, investor relations, project management, and company management; high-quality online resources and streamlined onboarding are foundational.

- [[Studio Culture]] — EIR management requires regular one-on-ones, calibrated challenge levels (neither overload nor boredom), participation in fundraising, and facilitated knowledge sharing; these practices signal that the studio cares about individual success and motivates tenure despite eventual company releases.

- [[Studio Leadership]] — The equity model (studios taking 30-80%, average 50%) reflects initial capital and infrastructure provision; rebalancing during Series A keeps CEOs motivated and attracts new investors; studio leaders must mentor and nurture entrepreneur candidates while managing competing portfolio priorities.
