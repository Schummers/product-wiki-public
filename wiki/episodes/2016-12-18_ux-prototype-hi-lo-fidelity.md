# UX Prototypes: Low Fidelity vs. High Fidelity

## Summary

A user interface prototype is a hypothesis, a candidate design solution that must be tested with actual users. Prototypes range across multiple dimensions: single-page or multipage, hand-sketched or realistic, and interactive or static. The article explains the trade-offs between high-fidelity prototypes (clickable, realistic visuals) and low-fidelity prototypes (static, requiring real-time manipulation by a facilitator). Testing prototypes is essential because it uncovers design flaws before they reach production, when fixes become exponentially more expensive.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prototype testing is critical** — ripping up a prototype is cheap; ripping up production code is very expensive. Testing prototypes early reveals issues and informs iteration before costly implementation.
- **High-fidelity prototypes enable realistic system response** — interactive prototypes eliminate delays and allow designers to focus on observation rather than managing prototype mechanics, plus they reduce human error.
- **High-fidelity prototypes support complex testing** — they allow evaluation of specific UI components, graphical affordances, page hierarchy, and engagement while making designs appear real enough for realistic user behavior.
- **Low-fidelity prototypes accelerate design exploration** — they require less preparation time, allow design changes between sessions, and reduce pressure on users who may feel less obligated to succeed with incomplete-looking interfaces.
- **Low-fidelity prototypes reduce designer attachment** — sketchy designs feel less finished, making teams more willing to discard them if testing reveals poor usability compared to polished high-fidelity work.
- **Fidelity varies independently across dimensions** — prototypes can be high-fidelity in interactivity but low in visuals, or vice versa. Choose the appropriate level based on project goals, timeline, and what specifically needs testing.

## Quotes

> Ripping up code is very expensive. Ripping up a prototype is not, especially if it's just a piece of paper.

> Testing final products is uninformed and risky. Enlightened teams create prototypes, have users test them, and iterate the design until it's good enough.

> Even with the right tool, building an interactive prototype can be time consuming: you have to get all the click targets right, and make the system respond only when the user interacts with a clickable target.

## Concepts

- [[Prototyping]] — High and low-fidelity prototypes serve as testing mechanisms before production with different trade-offs in time, realism, and error reduction; sketchy, static prototypes particularly accelerate design exploration, reduce designer attachment, allow design changes between sessions, and encourage teams to discard them if testing reveals issues since they feel less finished.
- [[Usability Testing]] — testing prototypes reveals design flaws, user interactions, and necessary iterations; this is presented as essential to quality outcomes.
- [[Iterative Design]] — prototypes enable fast iteration, design changes between sessions, and continuous refinement based on user feedback before implementation.
- [[High-Fidelity Prototypes]] — interactive, realistic prototypes eliminate delays, reduce human error, support complex UI testing, and make designs appear real enough to elicit realistic user behavior.
