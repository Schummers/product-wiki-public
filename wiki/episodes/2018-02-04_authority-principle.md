# The Authority Principle

## Summary

The authority principle describes people's tendency to comply with authority figures and to perceive their opinions as more reliable than their own. This psychological heuristic, studied famously in Milgram's experiments, stems from our learned reliance on authority figures as sources of trustworthy information. While this principle can be used ethically in UX design to increase credibility and user confidence, designers must be careful to use authentic authority rather than deceptive tactics.

## Key Takeaways

- **Definition** — the authority principle refers to people's tendency to comply with those in positions of authority such as doctors, lawyers, professors, and experts, based on the belief that authority figures possess greater wisdom and judgment.
- **Milgram experiment** — research showed that 65% of participants would administer dangerous electric shocks to another person if directed to do so by a scientist in authority, demonstrating the power of authority over moral judgment.
- **UX applications** — designers can increase credibility by using photos of authority figures, symbols of authority, logos of reputable organizations, and expert endorsements.
- **Ethical requirements** — using authority for persuasion is only acceptable when honest and helpful; endorsements from unrelated experts or false authority claims violate ethical standards.
- **Business impact** — empowered customers who trust authority signals become more confident in their decisions and more likely to be paying customers.

- [[Cognitive Psychology]] — grounding UX decisions in psychological principles like reliance on authority figures as a decision-making shortcut.
- [[Authority Heuristic]] — people's use of authority as a mental shortcut to make decisions more efficiently without extensive research.
