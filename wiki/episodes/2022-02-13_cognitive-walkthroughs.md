# Evaluate Interface Learnability with Cognitive Walkthroughs

## Summary

A cognitive walkthrough is a usability-inspection method evaluating interface learnability from a new user's perspective without involving actual users. Unlike heuristic evaluations (which assess general usability against guidelines), cognitive walkthroughs focus specifically on learnability by having a cross-functional team walk through task steps and answer prescribed questions about potential user challenges. The methodology was developed by Clayton Lewis and colleagues in 1990 for walk-up-and-use interfaces like kiosks and ATMs. Modern streamlined versions, adapted by Cathleen Wharton and colleagues, apply the technique to any interface type. Walkthroughs take place in workshop settings and identify aspects that could challenge new users.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four analysis questions drive cognitive walkthroughs** — At each step in a task flow, evaluators answer: (1) Will users try to achieve the right result? (2) Will users notice the correct action is available? (3) Will users associate the action with the desired result? (4) Will users see progress toward their goal after the action?
- **Rooted in cognitive science and learning theory** — The methodology stems from the CE+ theoretical model describing how people learn interfaces through exploration and problem solving; it evaluates how learnable interfaces are through this lens.
- **Best suited for complex, unfamiliar workflows** — Cognitive walkthroughs excel at evaluating systems with complex, new, or unfamiliar workflows where users lack existing mental models; they're overkill for standard web patterns everyone knows.
- **Step-by-step process uncovers user perspective issues** — Facilitators walk through tasks and stop at discrete steps; the group discusses questions to identify potential user confusion points and failure modes.
- **Workshop setting includes cross-functional teams** — Participants (UX specialists, product owners, engineers, domain experts) collectively assess interface from a new user's perspective; one facilitator drives the process and one recorder documents findings.
- **Early-stage evaluation catches fundamental problems** — Best used during development on conceptual prototypes to uncover design problems hindering learnability; paired with usability testing, walkthroughs provide comprehensive evaluation without costly formal studies.

## Concepts

- [[Cognitive Walkthroughs]] — A usability-inspection method where cross-functional teams walk through task steps, answering four prescribed questions to identify interface aspects challenging for new users.
- [[Learnability]] — Cognitive walkthroughs specifically evaluate how learnable interfaces are for new users without prior knowledge; focuses on exploration and problem-solving capabilities rather than general usability.
- [[Heuristic Evaluation]] — Cognitive walkthroughs are one of several inspection methods (alongside heuristic evaluations and expert reviews) using reviewer expertise to identify problems without involving actual users.
- [[User Interface]] — The methodology identifies design problems hindering learnability; best applied to complex systems with unfamiliar workflows; less effective for standard, ubiquitous design patterns.
