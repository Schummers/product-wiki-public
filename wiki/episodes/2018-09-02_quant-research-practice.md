# Quantitative UX Research in Practice

## Summary

A survey of 429 UX professionals examined how teams use quantitative research to inform design decisions. While 71% perform quantitative research at least occasionally, most struggle with challenges including participant recruitment, cost concerns, skill gaps, and interpretation difficulties. Analytics dominates as the most commonly used quantitative method, though it's often collected without meaningful application to design. Only 24% of respondents evaluate success using both quantitative and qualitative data, representing an ideal but rarely achieved practice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Majority use quantitative methods occasionally** — 44% report doing quantitative research "sometimes" and 27% report at least one quantitative study per project, though results likely reflect sample bias toward NN/g-familiar practitioners.
- **Analytics most popular methodology** — analytics, qualitative usability testing, and interviews are equally tied as most common methods; analytics dominance may reflect collection without meaningful use in many cases.
- **Recruitment as primary challenge** — 37% cited difficulty recruiting large sample sizes as the most common obstacle, often due to gatekeeper blocking access to users in enterprise settings.
- **Knowledge gaps significant barriers** — lack of knowledge about quantitative methods, data analysis, and their value ranked highly among challenges; quantitative research intimidates many UX professionals despite declining costs.
- **Ideal vs. reality in success metrics** — ideal practice uses both quantitative and qualitative data to judge success; only 24% follow this approach, while many rely on either one alone or on executive/stakeholder satisfaction.

## Quotes

> UX is sometimes perceived as a "soft" science. Often, that's due to our field's reliance on qualitative research and observations.

> In an ideal world, success would be evaluated by both qualitative and quantitative data — observations and measured results.

> Only 2 respondents out of 429 reported performing at least one quantitative study per project and having no significant concerns. The primary takeaway here seems to be that **almost everyone struggles with quantitative research in some way**— even those who reported doing quantitative research frequently.

## Concepts

- [[Quantitative Research]] — numerical methods for gathering data at scale; includes analytics, A/B testing, surveys, and quantitative usability testing that produce statistical insights about user behavior.
- [[Research Methods]] — the techniques and approaches UX professionals use to gather insights; practitioners employ diverse methods depending on questions asked, budgets, timelines, and expertise available.
- [[UX Metrics]] — measurable indicators of system performance and user satisfaction; effective metrics programs connect quantitative findings to actionable design improvements.
