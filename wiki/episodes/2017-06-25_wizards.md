# Wizards: Definition and Design Recommendations

## Summary

A wizard is a multi-step process that guides users through information input in a prescribed order, with subsequent steps depending on previous answers. Wizards simplify complex processes by showing less information at a time, reducing cognitive load and errors. They work best for novice users or infrequent tasks. However, wizards have disadvantages: they require more clicks than single-page forms for repeated use, can block access to needed information, limit user control, may not be gracefully interruptible, and complicate information comparison across steps. The article provides eight design recommendations including clear step communication, sequential enforcement, and state saving.

## Key Takeaways

- **Use wizards for novice or infrequent tasks** — Wizards excel at guiding users unfamiliar with a domain through complex processes. Avoid them for frequent, expert tasks where users will find the step-by-step approach tedious.

- **Communicate the process clearly** — Display a list or diagram of all steps, highlight the current step, and provide descriptive labels. Users need to understand the scope of the process and their position within it.

- **Enforce sequential completion** — Do not allow users to skip ahead or jump to earlier steps before completing prerequisites. A clear linear order minimizes decision-making and cognitive load.

- **Label buttons descriptively** — Use labels that indicate the actual next step (e.g., Choose Fabric) instead of generic Next; this maintains context and information scent throughout the flow.

- **Allow saving and resuming** — Enable users to pause, save their progress, and return later. Interruptions happen, and users should not lose their work or have to restart.

- **Make steps self-sufficient** — Each step should provide all necessary information or controls; avoid requiring users to reference data from previous steps or elsewhere in the application.

- **Display help adjacent to steps** — Help text should appear beside the wizard, never obscure it. Users need to apply explanations directly to the fields in view.

- **Reuse previous values as defaults** — When users employ the same wizard repeatedly, pre-fill with their prior selections to reduce redundant data entry.

## Quotes

> A wizard is a step-by-step process that allows users to input information in a prescribed order and in which subsequent steps may depend on information entered in previous ones.

> Wizards allow the computer to control the flow of the dialogue, but this limitation of users' freedom can be liberating in cases where people don't care about their choices or don't know enough to make a decision.

> If you expect that some of your users will perform it repeatedly, consider offering them another faster alternative for inputting their data.
