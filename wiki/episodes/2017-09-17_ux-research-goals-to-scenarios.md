# From Research Goals to Usability-Testing Scenarios: A 7-Step Method

## Summary

Planning effective usability tests requires a structured approach that moves from broad user tasks and organizational concerns to specific scenarios and success criteria. This seven-step method helps teams identify what to test, prioritize competing concerns, and write clear scenarios that guide test sessions. Steps include: identifying top user tasks, discovering organizational concerns, grouping and prioritizing issues, creating problem statements, defining research goals, listing participant activities and behaviors, and finally writing user scenarios. The method creates shared understanding among stakeholders and generates artifacts that guide research over time.

## Key Takeaways

- **Start with top user tasks and organizational concerns** — identify what users must do and what worries stakeholders most; these become the foundation for prioritization.
- **Group and prioritize issues collaboratively** — involve stakeholders in ranking issues by importance to both users and the organization, building consensus and buy-in.
- **Problem statements connect issues to research goals** — condensing issues into clear problem statements helps everyone focus on what's important and suggests measurable success metrics.
- **Activities and behaviors ground abstract goals** — break research goals into observable user activities and behaviors so facilitators know what to watch for during sessions.
- **Scenarios should describe outcomes, not interface operations** — write scenarios that motivate users toward goals ("Show me how you'd set up this device") rather than prescribing interface steps.

## Quotes

> It's important to involve stakeholders in these planning steps. After conducting user studies, researchers can sometimes encounter resistance to the findings: someone says the researcher recruited the wrong participants or tested the wrong elements. Going through part of the test-planning process as a team ensures that everyone understands and supports the research goals, priorities, and methods.

> Nonetheless, this method can untangle big and complicated systems, tame and educate a room full of stakeholders having competing priorities, generate a prioritized list of research problems to work on over time, help organize the test plan, the facilitator script, and observer study guide, generate usability metrics, and help you think through the logistics and contingencies for your usability sessions.

> Focus scenarios on outcomes your users would be motivated by.

## Concepts

- [[User Research]] — systematic investigation of user needs, behaviors, and reactions; effective research starts with clear research goals rooted in user tasks and organizational concerns.
- [[Usability Testing]] — empirical evaluation of designs through observed user interactions; test success depends on clear scenarios and well-defined success criteria.
- [[Test Planning]] — structured planning processes that align stakeholders, prioritize testing focus, and generate research goals and scenarios that guide sessions.
