# How Many Participants for Quantitative Usability Studies: A Summary of Sample-Size Recommendations

## Summary

Determining the correct sample size for quantitative usability studies is critical to collecting reliable data without wasting research budget. The 40-participant guideline comes from statistical calculations assuming a user population over 500, a 15% margin of error, and 95% confidence level. However, depending on acceptable risk tolerance and margin of error, fewer participants may suffice. For continuous metrics like task time or satisfaction, variability estimates affect sample size differently. The 40-participant recommendation works for many quantitative studies, but practitioners should understand the assumptions behind it and adjust based on their specific research goals.

## Key Takeaways

- **40-Participant Guideline** — For quantitative studies estimating binary metrics (success/conversion rates) with 15% margin of error and 95% confidence level, 40 participants provide reasonably trustworthy predictions of population behavior.
- **Assumptions Matter** — The 40-participant number assumes a large user population (500+), a 15% margin of error tolerance, and 95% confidence level; different assumptions yield different recommendations.
- **Lower Risk Tolerance Costs More** — Moving from 15% to 20% margin of error and 95% to 90% confidence level drops the requirement from 40 to 28 users; accepting more risk reduces study costs.
- **Continuous Metrics Require More Data** — For continuous metrics (task time, satisfaction), sample size depends on population variability; using Nielsen's estimate of 52% standard deviation relative to the mean typically requires 47 users for 95% confidence and 15% margin of error.
- **Practical Flexibility** — Teams on tight budgets can start with 20-25 participants, analyze results to calculate margins of error, and add more participants if precision is insufficient; this approach requires rapid analysis to maintain study validity.

## Quotes

> In most cases, we recommend 40 participants for quantitative studies.

> If you test with too few, your results may not be statistically reliable. If you test with too many, you're essentially throwing your money away.

> The 40-participant recommendation comes from a calculation. That calculation estimates the minimum number of users needed to produce a reasonable prediction of your population behavior based on one study.

## Concepts

- [[Quantitative Research]] — Quantitative research in UX collects numerical metrics (success rates, task time, satisfaction) to measure the scale or priority of design problems, benchmark experiences, and compare design alternatives experimentally.
- [[Sample Size]] — Sample size in quantitative studies is determined by acceptable margin of error, confidence level, and the metric type (binary or continuous); the appropriate size balances statistical reliability against research budget.
- [[Statistical Significance]] — Statistical significance indicates whether observed differences are real or due to random chance; understanding confidence levels (95% or 90%), margins of error (15% or 20%), and study design is essential for interpreting quantitative findings.
