# Conducting Usability Testing with Real Users' Real Data

## Summary

When conducting usability testing, participants may need to provide personal information to complete realistic tasks. This raises privacy concerns, as users are often hesitant to share personal data with unknown websites. The challenge is balancing the need for realistic testing conditions with respect for user privacy and comfort. Research shows that qualitative testing often benefits from using real user data, as it reveals more diverse interface issues and increases user engagement with tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Consider necessity first** — do not automatically require real data. In quantitative testing, fake but realistic information usually suffices. In qualitative testing, assess on a case-by-case basis whether real data provides benefits.
- **Real data reveals diverse issues** — when users enter their own information, variations in names, passwords, and data entry reveal more usability problems than when all participants use identical fake information.
- **Obtain informed consent** — if participants must provide personal information, especially sensitive data like financial or health information, confirm their willingness during recruiting with clear explanations of how the data will be used and protected.
- **Provide alternatives** — when users are hesitant, offer reasonable workarounds such as gift cards instead of requiring personal credit cards, or budget-based scenarios instead of real financial data.
- **Protect privacy throughout** — blur personal information in highlight videos and reports, clear browser data in front of participants after sessions, and let participants take or shred any printed materials containing their information.
- **Real data strengthens results** — qualitative testing produces more robust findings when participants use realistic or real data, revealing insights about form success, feedback, and information handling that dummy data cannot capture.

## Quotes

> Watching users type their own information can reveal more diverse issues in the interface than if all users enter the same fake information.

> Results from qualitative usability testing will be more robust with realistic user data than with fake data.

> Ensure that the information remains private. In highlight videos, blur personal information. In reports, blur user data in screenshots or examples.
