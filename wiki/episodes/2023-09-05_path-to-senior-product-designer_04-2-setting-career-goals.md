# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 2. Setting Career Goals

## Summary

Part I of *The Path to Senior Product Designer* opens with a foundational principle from Stephen Covey: "Begin with the end in mind." This chapter equips readers with structured exercises to align career decisions with personal values, long-term goals, and individual circumstances. Through personal fit evaluation, values comparison, visualization, job offer comparison, coin-flip decision catalysts, and the ABZ framework, designers can evaluate different career paths—individual contributor, people manager, specialization, product management, freelancer, or business owner—and choose the one most aligned with their aspirations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Personal fit evaluation** — Score potential career options against your values (impact, work-life balance, financial outcome, learning, reputation, leadership, etc.) weighted by importance to determine which path aligns with what matters to you.
- **Values comparison** — A technique the author got from a career coach: write your personal values on one sheet and the outcomes of a potential career change on another, then compare to see how closely the new role echoes what you care about.
- **Visualization** — A technique the author learned from an entrepreneur friend: imagine yourself in a few years having succeeded at your chosen career path and picture your day: who you interact with, what work you do, where you are, and how you feel. Validate your assumptions against reality.
- **Job offer comparison** — When choosing between multiple offers, score them the same way; the chapter reproduces the criteria Joel Califa, VP of design at Heap, used during his own job hunt: "getting things done," "quality of life," "growth," "compensation," "satisfying work," and "future success," with sub-criteria under each.
- **Coin flip catalyst** — Use a coin toss as a psychological trigger, not a decision-maker. The emotional response when the coin lands (relief or doubt) reveals your true preference.
- **ABZ framework** — Attributed to Shaan Puri on the My First Million podcast: know where you are (A), where you want to be (Z), and focus on the immediate next step (B) to avoid overwhelm.

## Quotes

> Effectiveness does not depend solely on how much effort we expend, but on whether or not the effort we expend is in the right jungle.

> You don't have to know every single step between A and Z; you only need to focus on B, the next step.

> In addition to making the decision in the first place, it also helped me after the decision was made when we hit a bump in the road. When we had to make a significant change to the business, I found myself questioning if I still wanted to be part of it. Revisiting the results of this exercise helped me to see that despite the change, the outcomes still aligned with my values.

## Concepts

- [[Career Growth Plan]] — The chapter presents a set of exercises (personal fit evaluation, values comparison, visualization, job offer comparison, coin flip catalyst, ABZ framework) for deciding your next step and mapping long-term direction.
- [[UX Career]] — The chapter discusses multiple career paths a designer can pursue (individual contributor, people manager, specialization, product management, freelancer, business owner) and provides tools to evaluate which fits your values and goals.
- [[Decision Making]] — Techniques the chapter borrows and presents as aids, none to be relied on alone: scoring values against options, comparing life outcomes, a coin flip used as a catalyst rather than a decider, and the ABZ framework for progressive goal-setting.
