# Crafting Product-Specific Design Principles to Support Better Decision Making

## Summary

Design principles are value statements that describe the most important goals a product or service should deliver to users, used to frame design decisions and resolve tradeoffs. Unlike general usability guidelines or visual-design principles, product design principles are specific to the product being designed. They reduce subjective debates by anchoring decisions to shared values. Effective design principles take a stand on conflicting values (prioritizing one over another), inspire empathy for users, are concise and memorable, and do not conflict with one another. The article provides a four-step process for creating design principles: identify core values, consider user impact, identify common tradeoffs, and write, compare, and iterate. These principles should be published, referenced regularly, and used to justify design decisions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design principles resolve tradeoffs, not define features** — When faced with conflicting goals (minimalism vs. discoverability, power users vs. casual users), design principles clarify which value takes priority. They are not directions for designing UI elements, but strategic value statements.
- **Good principles take a stand on which value matters** — Ambiguous principles create interpretation problems. If two values regularly conflict, explicitly state which one should win or specify contexts where one is more important. This eliminates lengthy, unneeded debates.
- **Design principles must inspire empathy** — Include why the value matters to users, not just what it is. This helps designers keep users at heart of decisions and makes principles more meaningful than generic statements.
- **Keep principles concise and memorable** — Design principles are not essays. Short, memorable principles are easily referenced and remembered. Limit to 5-8 maximum; 10 or more are largely forgotten. Involve content specialists to make them particularly memorable.
- **Principles guide justification, not eliminate judgment** — Don't just say "we're doing it this way because I say so." Explain that "we're doing it this way because principle #3 tips the balance." Use principles to justify tradeoff decisions and build team confidence in the choice.

## Quotes

> One useful device to guide designers to make the right tradeoff decisions are product-specific design principles.

> When faced with tradeoffs like these, designers need to choose which goal to sacrifice in favor of another.

> Design principles help to keep important values front and center in the design process. When successfully composed and used, design principles ensure consistency in decision making across designers and teams, removing the need to debate simple tradeoffs and letting designers worry about complex problems.
