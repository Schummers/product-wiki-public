# Solving Product Design Exercises: 3.4. Improving primary health care

## Summary

This chapter applies the design framework to a complex healthcare system problem: making primary care more accessible in the face of US workforce shortages and inequitable distribution. After systematically mapping patient needs and exploring four possible product directions using an impact/effort matrix, Dashinsky selects group appointments as the highest-impact, most innovative solution. The design creates a white-label platform that allows clinics to organize remote group appointments for patients with common, preventable conditions (diabetes, back pain, mental health, etc.), reducing both patient wait times and GP workload while leveraging telehealth to lower participation barriers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four ideas across different leverage points** — Family doctor marketplaces and on-demand chat have low impact because they already exist; appointment scheduling improvements affect only administrative efficiency; group appointments (solution #3) are novel and high-impact because they let one GP educate multiple patients simultaneously on preventable conditions, multiplying time effectiveness.
- **Data-driven recommendations personalize engagement** — The app suggests appointments based on three signals: GP advice during face-to-face visits, existing patient conditions in the clinic's records, and calculated risk factors (e.g., obesity + age + hypertension = diabetes risk), making each patient see only relevant appointments rather than a generic list.
- **Remote participation lowers commitment barriers** — Allowing patients to join via smartphone instead of traveling to the clinic reduces friction and increases attendance for preventive care that might otherwise be skipped due to time constraints or transportation challenges, directly addressing the workforce shortage by multiplying each GP's reach.
- **Common preventable conditions as the focus** — Prescription renewal, check-ups, back pain, mental health, diabetes, and vaccination are the most common clinic visits, and most are preventable through education and early intervention, making group appointments a natural fit for these high-volume conditions.
- **Clinic preference for payment processing** — Since the platform is white-labeled for clinics, payment can flow seamlessly through the clinic's existing billing relationship rather than requiring users to enter new payment information, reducing friction at a sensitive step.
- **Long-term outcomes trump short-term engagement** — Success metrics start with sign-up and attendance, but the real proof is downstream: do patients with preventive education actually have lower disease incidence? Do they spend less on healthcare? Do clinics reduce per-patient costs without sacrificing quality?

## Quotes

> Primary care plays a key role in public health. General practitioners are the gatekeepers of medicine and serve as first tier support in the case of an existing condition or prevention of future diseases.

> I will make the assumption that doctors explain the same things about the same conditions to different people multiple times a week. This process could be optimised by grouping these patients, connecting them with a doctor who will talk about the condition, and having a group discussion afterwards.

> Around 10% of family physicians in the US give patients the option to share their appointments with people who have similar health issues. What we're going to build is a white label product that allows clinics to organise group appointments for their patients and start practicing this concept.

## Concepts

- [[Design Exercise]] — The chapter demonstrates all seven steps: establishing the business goal (improve access, save costs), identifying patient and GP personas, mapping patient context (anxiety, diverse locations, urgent and preventive needs), brainstorming four distinct product approaches, using an impact/effort matrix to prioritize, designing the shared appointment flow, and defining both user engagement and long-term health outcome metrics.
- [[Persona]] — The author segments both patients (by reason for visit and risk profile) and GPs, recognizing that time is the most expensive clinic resource and that different patient archetypes (new, preventive, chronic condition) benefit from different engagement approaches.
- [[Problem Framing]] — Rather than asking "What features does a clinic need?", Dashinsky reframes the core problem as "How can we let one GP serve more patients effectively on preventable conditions?", leading to group appointments as a novel multiplier rather than incremental feature improvements.
- [[Context of Use]] — Patients initiate care from home, work, or on-the-go, often when they feel unwell and are anxious or uncertain, and must travel to clinic (adding time and risk of catching other illnesses). Remote group appointments address this by reducing travel burden and anxiety through peer learning.
- [[Ideation]] — The chapter lists four structurally different solutions (marketplace, on-demand chat, group appointments, appointment scheduling) that tackle different parts of the problem space, showing how to diverge before converging on the best direction.
- [[Prioritization]] — Using an impact/effort matrix, Dashinsky weighs novelty and market potential against feasibility, choosing group appointments over existing solutions with moderate impact or incremental improvements, and betting that a first-mover white-label platform can capture the market before competitors.
