# What Parallax Lacks

## Summary

This article examines the usability of parallax scrolling, a design trend that creates depth through layers moving at different speeds. Despite winning design awards and gaining recent renewed popularity, user testing reveals parallax creates more problems than benefits. Users ignore parallax effects while scrolling quickly to find content, animations can load slowly leaving blank screens, and the effects receive indifferent reactions from average users despite praise from designers. The article presents four primary problems and recommendations for limited, restrained use when appropriate.

## Key Takeaways

- **Parallax content is difficult to control and easy to miss** — Goal-oriented users scroll quickly scanning for keywords, missing parallax animations. Slow-loading parallax causes blank screens; too-fast animations can scroll off before users finish reading. Content presented solely through parallax may be overlooked entirely.

- **Motion overload causes dizziness and reduced readability** — Excessive parallax animating many text elements makes reading difficult and can cause vertigo, nausea, and dizziness (prompting Apple's iOS 7 "Reduce Motion" option). Users with motion sensitivity or vestibular disorders are particularly affected.

- **Parallax effects are easily missed or ignored** — On mobile, hands can block animations. Users have learned to ignore motion (associating it with ads) and may deliberately disregard parallax. Most importantly, parallax is not impressive to average users; they focus on content, not animation.

- **Users cannot deliberately control parallax timing** — When animations are tied to scroll speed, users who scroll too quickly miss content while users who scroll slowly are forced to wait for animations. Reanimating objects on scroll-up (after scrolling down then back up) becomes tedious when users are searching for information.

## Quotes

> The parallax animation effect is created by two or more layers of an interface moving at different speeds or in different directions in order to produce an impression of depth.

> "The vast majority of people are focused on content, and so the animation effects are an afterthought, if they're noticed at all."

> "Unfortunately, parallax effects come with some costs. Designers need to know what their risks and benefits are."

## Concepts

- [[Animation]] — Animations can support understanding or create distraction/harm; parallax represents a cautionary case where visual appeal to designers doesn't translate to user value.

- [[Visual Design]] — Aesthetic choices like parallax must be evaluated against user goals; beautiful effects that interfere with task completion or create accessibility issues are poor UX choices.

- [[Parallax Scrolling]] — A specific animation technique creating depth impression through differential layer movement; most effective in leisure-browsing contexts without specific user goals, not for task-focused or information-heavy pages.
