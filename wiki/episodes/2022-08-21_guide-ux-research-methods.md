# A Guide to Using User-Experience Research Methods

## Summary

This article presents a framework for understanding and selecting among 20 popular UX research methods by mapping them across two dimensions: attitudinal versus behavioral research (what people say versus what people do) and qualitative versus quantitative research (why or how to fix a problem versus how many or how much). The framework helps researchers recognize that no single method answers all questions and that different methods are appropriate for different research objectives. Attitudinal-qualitative research includes interviews and focus groups that explore motivations and preferences; attitudinal-quantitative research includes surveys that measure sentiment at scale. Behavioral-qualitative research includes observations and field studies that reveal actual practices; behavioral-quantitative research includes analytics and usability testing that measure performance and efficiency. Strategic research plans combine methods across quadrants to answer complex business questions comprehensively.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two-dimensional framework** — Research methods vary across attitudinal vs. behavioral (what people say versus do) and qualitative vs. quantitative (why/how versus how much/many); understanding these dimensions helps select appropriate methods for different questions.
- **Attitudinal-qualitative methods** — Interviews and focus groups explore what people think, believe, and prefer; useful for understanding mental models, needs, and values but not for predicting actual behavior.
- **Attitudinal-quantitative methods** — Surveys measure sentiment, preferences, and opinions at scale; useful for quantifying attitudes across larger populations but subject to social desirability bias.
- **Behavioral-qualitative methods** — Observational research, contextual inquiry, and field studies reveal what people actually do and why; provides rich understanding of real-world practices and contexts.
- **Behavioral-quantitative methods** — Usability testing, A/B testing, and analytics measure performance, efficiency, and user behavior; provides objective data about what works and what doesn't.
- **Comprehensive strategy** — Most research questions benefit from combining methods across quadrants; for example, user interviews to understand needs, usability testing to validate solutions, and analytics to measure real-world impact.

## Concepts

- [[Research Methods]] — Twenty popular research methods map across dimensions of attitudinal versus behavioral and qualitative versus quantitative, helping researchers select appropriate methods for different objectives.
- [[Qualitative Research]] — Methods like interviews, observations, and field studies provide deep understanding of how and why people behave, revealing rich context and unexpected patterns.
- [[Quantitative Research]] — Methods like surveys and analytics measure how many and how much, providing scale and statistical confidence but losing contextual depth.
- [[Attitudinal vs. Behavioral]] — Attitudinal research captures what people say they think and do; behavioral research reveals what people actually do, which often differs from stated intentions.
- [[Research Strategy]] — Effective research plans combine multiple methods across all quadrants to answer complex questions comprehensively, reducing bias and providing multiple perspectives.
