# An Error Messages Scoring Rubric

## Summary

The authors present a practical scoring rubric for evaluating error messages across three dimensions: visibility, communication, and efficiency. Each dimension contains four specific guidelines, scored from 1 (poor) to 4 (excellent), with scores averaged to produce a letter grade (A through D). The rubric makes the abstract guidelines from their earlier error-message guidelines article actionable and measurable, allowing teams to assess error-message quality objectively, identify improvements, and prioritize UX work. Three real-world examples demonstrate how the rubric works in practice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three dimensions structure error-message quality** — visibility ensures messages are noticed and presented appropriately to user impact, communication ensures clarity and usefulness, and efficiency focuses on preventing errors and enabling quick recovery.
- **Specific, measurable criteria improve evaluation** — using detailed rubric criteria reduces decision-making burden and helps teams avoid groupthink by allowing independent evaluation before averaging scores.
- **A letter-grade scale communicates severity** — scoring ranges (A: 3.3–4.0, B: 2.5–3.2, C: 1.6–2.4, D: 1.5 or less) provide a familiar framework that helps executives and teams prioritize UX debt.
- **Real examples reveal common patterns** — low-scoring messages like Craigslist's fail primarily on communication and efficiency, medium-scoring ones like Google Flights struggle with visibility, and high-scoring ones like J.Crew excel across all dimensions.
- **Team evaluation catches more issues** — having multiple evaluators independently score messages and then averaging their scores reduces individual bias and produces more reliable assessments.
