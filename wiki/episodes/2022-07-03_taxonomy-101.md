# Taxonomy 101: Definition, Best Practices, and How It Complements Other IA Work

## Summary

A taxonomy is a controlled vocabulary—a closed, hierarchical list of terms used to describe and classify content, enabling effective retrieval. Unlike navigation (user-visible) and information architecture (underlying site structure), taxonomies are backstage metadata tools. The article distinguishes four IA organization models: navigation, IA structure, taxonomies, and content models. It also differentiates taxonomies from related structures like thesauri (adding synonymy and associative relationships) and ontologies (supporting multiple relationship types). The article provides ten steps for building taxonomies, emphasizing user research, existing standards, and ongoing governance.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four IA models serve different purposes** — Navigation is user-visible; IA structure is the full backstage map; taxonomies describe concepts for content retrieval; content models define content types and relationships.
- **Taxonomy supports multiple uses** — Making content connections, enabling faceted navigation, powering search suggestions and refinements, all behind the scenes.
- **Thesauri add synonymy** — A thesaurus includes preferred terms, nonpreferred synonyms, associative relationships, and equivalence relationships; most "taxonomies" in practice are actually thesauri.
- **Ontologies are more complex** — Supporting many relationship types beyond parent-child hierarchies, ontologies are used for complex knowledge domains; less common for UX practitioners than taxonomies.
- **Build systematically** — Inventory content first, check for existing standards, identify concepts from research and analytics, evaluate terms, decide on preferred variants, build relationships, review, apply, and maintain.
- **User research drives success** — Use card sorting, interviews, search logs, and usability testing to identify concepts users care about rather than inventing taxonomy branches that won't benefit users.
- **Governance is ongoing** — Set up processes to add, rename, merge, or remove terms and spot-check tagging consistency; without maintenance, taxonomies become outdated and less useful.

## Quotes

> Taxonomy: A closed list of acceptable terms that are arranged hierarchically and are used to describe and classify content.

> It is okay to start small and that a taxonomy need not be a multi-year project — simply having a small set of topics that you assign consistently to your content in the background is a fine start!

> Whereas the IA structure is a map of how our content is organized, a taxonomy is a map of concepts we use to describe our content and of how all those concepts relate to one another.

## Concepts

- [[Information Architecture]] — Taxonomies are backstage IA structures and formal metadata systems that organize concepts to support content discovery and retrieval through faceted navigation, search suggestions, and related-content recommendations, ensuring consistent logical classification by controlling which terms content creators can use and separating logical precision from user mental models.
