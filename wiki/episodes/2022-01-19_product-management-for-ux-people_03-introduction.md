# Product Management for UX People: Introduction

## Summary

The introduction opens the book with the question a UX practitioner asks the first time a product manager appears in their meeting: "Why is a product manager telling me what to do?" Crumlish notes that what a product manager is "depends on who the person is, where the job is, and how the role is being executed," and that the "correct" relationship between product and UX likewise "depends." He then tells his own story rather than laying out a general model: a little over ten years before writing, as a staff interaction designer at Yahoo and curator of its design pattern library, he attended a workshop on product management for user experience designers taught by Jeff Lash and Chris Baum at the Information Architecture Summit, alongside his boss Erin Malone, then a senior director of UED.

The chapter uses Yahoo as its one worked example. There, UED reported to "the product org," and the product and engineering organizations were, in his telling, "locked in an endless battle for ascendancy." Crumlish contrasts this with his own earlier career in the 1990s freelance, agency and consulting web, where he built client websites that "were not products." A second moment he singles out is the day Larry Cornett moved from VP of User Experience Design for the Yahoo Search product to VP of Product for the same team: "You can do that!?" The introduction closes on the questions the book will answer, not on answers: whether you have to switch teams to get ahead or influence product strategy, or whether product and UX can join forces as "superfriends."

## Key Takeaways

- **The book's framing question** — Crumlish sets out to answer what UX practitioners need to know about product, and refuses a general answer on how product and UX should relate: asked what the correct relationship is, his reply is "It depends." He promises specific examples instead, "thinly disguised if need be, or generalized if it's a ubiquitous pattern."
- **What "product" means in the book** — A note flags that product managers use *product* on its own as shorthand for *product management*, a deliberate choice not to lean into the ambiguous *management* part, and also to denote product thinking in general, covering product design, development and marketing.
- **Yahoo as the formative context** — At Yahoo, UED reported to the product org and had to accommodate product management's demands even on a platform design team sitting inside the tech org. Crumlish reports being vetoed for one job by a product manager who worried he was "too senior" and would not be satisfied producing mocks from that manager's wireframes.
- **A workshop he names its teachers of** — The product management workshop that started his interest was taught by Jeff Lash and Chris Baum; he attended it with Erin Malone, with whom he later co-authored a book on social experience design.
- **Larry Cornett's move as the proof of possibility** — Cornett going from VP of User Experience Design to VP of Product for the same Yahoo Search team was, for Crumlish, a revelation that the move existed at all. He says it was a few more years before he followed.

## Quotes

> "So this idea that we were making a product, and that product people governed our work, made a big impression on me, and Jeff and Chris's workshop provided a great opportunity to learn more about the habits and drives of product people."

> Another moment that made a huge impression on me was the day Larry Cornett went from being the VP of User Experience Design for the Yahoo Search product to being the VP of Product for that same team.

> Did I become one of the oppressors? Do you have to switch teams to get ahead or to influence product strategy? Or can product and UX join forces as "superfriends"?

## Concepts

- [[Product Management]] — The chapter refuses a fixed definition, saying what a product manager is depends on the person, the job and how the role is executed, and introduces the book's shorthand use of *product* for product management and for product thinking in general.
- [[UX Career]] — Crumlish narrates his own path from the 1990s agency web to interaction design at Yahoo to product, and cites Larry Cornett's move from VP of UED to VP of Product for the same team as the moment that showed him the path existed.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — The Yahoo example is one of structural tension: product and engineering "locked in an endless battle for ascendancy," with UED reporting into product and accommodating its demands.
