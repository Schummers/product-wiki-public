# Scanning Patterns on the Web Are Optimized for the Current Task

## Summary

Building on the research of Russian psychologist Alfred Yarbus, this article presents eyetracking evidence showing that users' gaze patterns on web pages are directly shaped by their current task. The same page viewed with different goals will produce dramatically different scanning patterns. Users apply an "efficient scanning" strategy where they focus exclusively on information relevant to their task and ignore everything else. This has significant implications for how designers should structure page layouts and prioritize information, as well as how researchers should construct realistic tasks for usability testing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Task-driven eye movement** — Users' gaze patterns are optimized for their current goal; the same page will be scanned entirely differently depending on whether a user seeks destination names, prices, images, ratings, or other information.
- **Efficient scanning ignores irrelevance** — Users ruthlessly focus only on information needed to complete their task, exhibiting minimal fixations on content unrelated to their goal, demonstrating the principle of least effort.
- **Design consistency supports efficient scanning** — Predictable layouts, consistent positioning of key information, bold typography for primary elements, and adequate white space allow users to develop optimal scanning algorithms quickly.
- **Layer cake scanning pattern** — Users scan horizontally across headings and subheadings but skip detailed text below them when headings provide sufficient information, resembling stacked horizontal sheets.
- **Task construction is critical for valid eyetracking** — Realistic, task-based studies yield meaningful findings, while nonrealistic tasks produce misleading heatmaps that can lead to harmful design decisions.
- **List-page design guidelines** — Consistent positioning, short recognizable text, large bold typography, surrounding white space, comparison tables, consistent photo types, and clear visual differentiation (especially for ratings) all support efficient user scanning.

## Quotes

> Tasks greatly impact user behavior on the web and therefore drastically change the outcome of eyetracking gazeplots and heatmaps.

> Predictable, unambiguous patterns help users get to an optimal scanning algorithm fast and allow them to easily focus on the essential, while skipping those content elements that are unnecessary for their goals.

> Watching the user's full eyetracking session, not just studying gaze plots, is the best way to uncover meaningful insights.
