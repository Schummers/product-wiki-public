# Mental Models for Intelligent Assistants

## Summary

This article reports on a two-week diary study examining how users of intelligent assistants like Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant form mental models of these systems. The research explores how both frequent users and new users understand and expect to interact with intelligent assistants, revealing three primary mental models: IA as interface to the web/smart home, IA as handy helper, and IA as repository of knowledge. The findings highlight that users quickly learn the limitations of these systems and are unlikely to try previously failed tasks again, even when the system improves.

## Key Takeaways

- **Users develop one of three primary mental models** — Most frequent users view their IA as an interface to other systems, some see it as a handy helper, and others view it as a repository of all knowledge; these models shape usage patterns and expectations.
- **Users have limited and stable IA usage patterns** — Despite the numerous advertised skills available, users engage in a fairly limited set of simple activities like weather checks, playing music, and setting reminders, with little expansion over time.
- **Users quickly discover and internalize system limitations** — When users try to expand their assistant's use and encounter failures, they become more aware of limitations and less likely to attempt similar tasks in the future.
- **New users struggle with the same limitations as frequent users** — Issues like inability to understand varied input formulations, inability to answer questions, wrong answers, and lack of multistep command support frustrate both user groups.
- **Mental models form through experience, not marketing** — Users' understanding of what their assistant can do is shaped by what they actually try and what succeeds or fails, not by advertised features or capabilities.
- **Once convinced a system cannot do something, users don't return** — A critical design challenge is that users won't come back to try improved solutions later; poor initial performance prevents future adoption of enhanced features.

## Quotes

> Once users decide that the assistant cannot do something, they are unlikely to try it again very soon. They tend to quickly learn the limitations of a system and then use the system around these limitations (or sometimes stop using it at all).

> The challenge becomes to teach users how these systems are changing and improving over time.

> New users are most fragile. Their mental models shape further usage, so it is imperative that we help them be successful and expand their horizons. As soon as people discover that a system is not able to perform a certain task, they stop trying.
