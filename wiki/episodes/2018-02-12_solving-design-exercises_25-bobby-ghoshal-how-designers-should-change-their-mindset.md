# Solving Product Design Exercises: Bobby Ghoshal: how designers should change their mindset

## Summary

This chapter features an interview with Bobby Ghoshal, co-founder of Candid Co and co-host of the High Resolution video series, who has interviewed design leaders at companies like Airbnb, Spotify, and Slack. Ghoshal argues that designers must evolve their mindset from "user experience designers" focused only on product problems to "customer experience designers" who understand business, frame the entire customer journey, and influence decisions across marketing, operations, and support. He contends that most people never become customers due to poor pricing, positioning, marketing, operations, and support—all design problems—and that the next generation of design leaders must spend time away from their desks building diplomatic bridges with non-product teams to drive business buy-in for design investments.

## Key Takeaways

- **Business Awareness — Critical Skill** — designers with business sensibilities are primed to own the future of the industry; most designers focus too much on specialized tools like visual design and motion design rather than understanding business.
- **The Customer Journey Matters More Than Product** — 90-95% of prospective customers never experience the product due to poor pricing, reviews, branding, marketing, operations, and customer service; these are all design problems.
- **Cross-Functional Influence** — designers need to build diplomatic relationships with teams in marketing, finance, operations, and support, and should spend bulk time understanding business strategy, margins, conversion metrics, and customer support.
- **Mindset Shift From UX to CX** — evolving from "user experience designer" to "customer experience designer" is a small shift that will change how companies and customers see your organization and yield higher value transfer.

## Quotes

> The long road to design-relevance at your company is paved with bricks of business awareness; designers need to understand business now.

> We forget *(or don't realize)* that most people who consider our product **never actually become a customer** and in turn don't experience our product.

> The next decade of design leaders will need to recognize the importance of building influence in their companies by spending a bulk of their time away from their desk and building diplomatic bridges with teams that are directly affecting the entire customer experience and not just with teams affecting the in-product user experience.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — Designers aspiring to influence their industry must shift from specializing in product design tools to understanding business and building cross-functional relationships.
- [[Business Impact]] — Understanding business strategy, margins, and conversion metrics across all channels is essential to drive design relevance and secure investment in design.
- [[Product Thinking]] — Designers should frame the entire customer journey, not just the in-product experience, to address the 90-95% of prospective customers who never experience the product.
- [[Design Presentation]] — Designers must develop skills in communicating business strategy, influence, and diplomatic relationship-building with teams outside product design.
