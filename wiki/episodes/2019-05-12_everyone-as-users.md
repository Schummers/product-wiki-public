# 'Our Users Are Everyone': Designing Mass-Market Products for Large User Audiences

## Summary

Designing for "everyone" actually results in less usable experiences because it prevents clear understanding of who users are and what they need. Even mass-market products require user segmentation to identify meaningful distinctions between user types. Frequency of use is a useful starting point for segmentation; frequent and infrequent users have fundamentally different needs, expertise levels, and motivations. Research studies with unfiltered participant pools risk ending up with participants who are all similar, missing opportunities to understand diverse user needs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Designing for anyone is like packing for anywhere** — Without understanding destination, activities, and conditions, you cannot pack appropriately; similarly, without defining users and their contexts, design cannot meet real needs.

- **Testing with anyone misses important audiences** — An unrestricted research study may recruit 9 commuters and 1 tourist when both are target users; this misses insights about tourists' unique challenges and needs.

- **Segment by behavior, not just demographics** — Age and income don't reveal how people interact with your product; group by actual behavior patterns, frequency of use, goals, and domain knowledge.

- **Frequency of use reveals important differences** — Frequent users need efficient workflows and familiarity; infrequent users need clear guidance and memory aids; the same interface cannot equally serve both without segmentation.

- **Use segmentation in research and design** — Create personas for each user type, recruit proportional participants from each segment, and observe how different groups behave differently with the same design.

## Quotes

> "Avoiding any definitions of the target audience actually leads to a less usable experience for most people."

> "Often, frequent users behave differently than occasional users. But these important differences are easily overlooked if you think only about how 'everyone' acts."

> "By dividing your audience into distinct groups of frequent and infrequent users, you can begin to detect important patterns and trends."

## Concepts

- [[Persona]] — Even mass-market products need personas representing distinct user segments based on behavior and frequency of use (not demographics) to guide research and design decisions, since different user types have different expertise, motivation, and needs.


- [[Usability Testing]] — Research must include appropriate numbers of participants from each important user segment to capture diverse perspectives and needs.

- [[Audience Analysis]] — Large audiences are not homogeneous; identifying and serving meaningful user segments produces more usable designs than trying to serve undifferentiated "everyone."

- [[Research Methods]] — Defining user segments before research ensures data collection includes diverse perspectives and avoids recruitment bias toward one segment.
