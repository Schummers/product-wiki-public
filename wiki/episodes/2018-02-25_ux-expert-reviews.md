# UX Expert Reviews

## Summary

Expert reviews are usability inspection methods in which a UX professional with deep knowledge of usability principles and extensive research experience examines a design to identify problems. Unlike heuristic evaluations that reference established guidelines, expert reviews apply broader expertise and past experience. Expert reviews work best when the reviewer is external to the design team, allowing fresh perspective and unbiased feedback. They complement usability testing by catching issues before user research and by identifying subtle problems that may not emerge in small qualitative studies.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fresh perspective requirement** — expert reviewers should be external to the design team to provide unbiased feedback and spot obvious issues that team members overlook after staring at the same design too long.
- **Core components** — a thorough expert review includes lists of strengths and problems with severity ratings, recommendations grounded in usability principles, and examples of best practices addressing similar issues.
- **Complementary method** — expert reviews find different issues than usability testing; reviews catch minor inconsistencies and violations of best practices, while testing reveals problems specific to the target audience.
- **Timing in design cycle** — expert reviews can be conducted at any stage if a prototype exists, but should happen before major redesign projects and iteratively during the creative phase when changes are still flexible.
- **Problem documentation** — usability issues must be accompanied by clear explanations grounded in UX principles or research, never just subjective opinions, to make findings actionable and avoid designer-reviewer opinion wars.

## Quotes

> If you design in a vacuum and never see how the target audience interacts with your design, you don't build the kind of UX expertise that's needed to review interfaces.

> The reason people are bad at proofreading their own writing is because their mind sees what they intended to write, not what actually makes it onto the page! The same is true of our own designs: we understand what the design is intended to do, and so we can't see clearly whether it truly accomplishes that goal.

> The types of usability issues found in an expert review vary from those that would be uncovered during a usability study, which is why **combining these methods results in the best overall design.** An expert review can identify minor issues that would be difficult to observe or measure in a small qualitative study: inconsistencies in font usage throughout the interface, a color that doesn't follow brand guidelines, or the use of center-aligned text instead of left-aligned.

## Concepts

- [[Heuristic Evaluation]] — systematically assessing designs against established usability heuristics and best practices to uncover violations, employing expert analysis as a complement to user testing for comprehensive evaluation of design quality.
- [[Design Process]] — using expert reviews iteratively throughout design creation to catch and fix issues before development.
