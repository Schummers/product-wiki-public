# Solving Product Design Exercises: 3.5. Improving the ATM experience

## Summary

This chapter presents a worked example of a complete design exercise solution within the book's framework. The author demonstrates how to improve the ATM experience by following a structured seven-step approach: establishing goals, defining the audience, understanding context and needs, generating ideas, prioritizing solutions, designing the solution, and measuring success.

The proposed solution transforms ATMs from interactive terminals into physical fulfillment points, moving requests to mobile apps to reduce friction, queues, and accessibility barriers while maintaining security and expanding the service to underserved populations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Seven-step framework** — The approach consists of: understand the goal (Why), define the audience (Who), understand customer context and needs (When/Where), list ideas (What), prioritize and choose (Prioritization), solve, and measure success (How).
- **Audience segmentation** — Key underserved groups include senior citizens with low tech-savviness, tourists and immigrants, teenagers, and people with disabilities who face higher barriers to using traditional ATMs.
- **Mobile-first redesign** — The solution moves ATM requests to a mobile app where users pre-request cash withdrawals, deposits, or statement printing, then scan a QR code at the ATM to complete the transaction, eliminating card friction and queue time.
- **Accessibility as byproduct** — Leveraging mobile phones' built-in accessibility features (voice-over, font scaling) automatically makes the ATM experience more accessible without requiring specialized hardware.
- **Success metrics** — Measure adoption through churn rate, reductions in queues and PIN reset requests, decrease in card fraud, and adoption rate among people with disabilities.

## Quotes

> Even though the ATM is a great piece of technology, the experience of using it has barely changed since it was invented.

> Most banks already have mobile apps that allow their customers to perform financial operations. Requesting operations with ATMs could be part of these apps.

> Mobile operation systems have built-in support of accessibility features such as voice-over and increase of font size. Thus, I believe that this solution will automatically make the ATM experience more accessible, just by allowing customers to perform requests via their smartphone instead of using an ATM's screen and buttons.

## Concepts

- [[Design Exercise]] — A worked example demonstrating how a candidate can solve a realistic design problem by applying a structured problem-solving framework with explicit steps for research, ideation, and validation.
- [[Problem Framing]] — Techniques for narrowing a broad problem statement (improve ATM experience) with two clarifying questions: rethink how people bank or fix the machine as it exists today, and which type of machine (bank-owned street or branch ATMs rather than third-party ones).
- [[Prioritization]] — Using an impact/effort matrix to evaluate multiple design ideas and select the one with greatest potential benefit (in this case, converting ATMs to fulfillment-only points).
- [[Product Thinking]] — Connecting design decisions to business goals (increased revenue, loyalty) and customer outcomes (reduced queues, increased accessibility, lower fraud risk).
- [[UX Metrics]] — Five specific metrics to validate the design solution: churn rate, queue reduction, PIN reset decrease, card fraud reduction, and disability adoption rate.
