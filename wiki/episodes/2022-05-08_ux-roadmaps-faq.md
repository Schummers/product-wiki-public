# UX Roadmaps: Who, When, and How Much Time?

## Summary

A UX roadmap is a strategic living document that aligns, prioritizes, and communicates a UX team's future work. This article answers frequently asked questions about roadmaps based on Nielsen Norman's training course. It distinguishes roadmaps (strategic, vision-oriented) from project plans (execution-focused). Three types of roadmaps exist: product roadmaps (all organizational problems), field roadmaps (all UX problems across disciplines), and specialty roadmaps (problems within one UX area). Roadmaps are created at key moments (new initiatives, crises, leadership changes, annual planning) and take less than one week for most practitioners. Field roadmaps are most common; they enable cross-discipline alignment and educate stakeholders about user-centered design. Roadmaps should be updated monthly and presented with storytelling and progressive disclosure.

## Key Takeaways

- **Roadmaps are strategic and vision-oriented; project plans are tactical and execution-focused** — roadmaps capture problems to solve, plans capture task details.
- **Field roadmaps are most common** because they coordinate work across UX disciplines without restricting scope to a single product.
- **Roadmaps take less than a week for most practitioners** — typically created during roadmapping workshops lasting a few hours to a few days.
- **Create roadmaps at key moments** — new initiatives, crises, leadership changes, or annual planning.
- **Update roadmaps monthly** with a recurring meeting to keep the artifact current and create organizational alignment.
- **Present roadmaps with storytelling** using progressive disclosure (Now, Next, Future columns) rather than sharing the full document at once.
