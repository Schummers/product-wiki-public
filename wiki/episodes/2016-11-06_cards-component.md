# Cards: UI-Component Definition

## Summary

A card is a UI component that groups related pieces of information into a self-contained, clickable unit resembling a playing card in size and appearance. Cards present summaries with linked entry points to detailed information on separate pages, using visual elements like borders, background color, and subtle drop shadows to establish grouping and signal clickability. They excel at handling heterogeneous content types where items vary significantly in length and composition, making them particularly useful for dashboards and aggregation sites.

Cards are not a revolutionary new concept but a revival of the 1990s hypertext card (or deck-of-cards) model adapted for contemporary multi-card layouts on responsive screens. They leverage cognitive psychology principles, particularly the common-regions principle, where items contained within a boundary are perceived as grouped even when proximity would suggest otherwise.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cards group and summarize content** — A card chunks several related pieces of information into one digestible unit, typically including multiple media types, with an entry point to fuller details elsewhere.
- **Four key properties** — Cards are used for grouping information, present summaries with links to details, resemble physical cards with visual boundaries, and allow flexible layouts with variable heights and fixed widths.
- **Better for browsing than searching** — Multiple cards are less scannable than vertical lists because positioning isn't predictable; cards are appropriate for browsing collections but problematic when users search for specific items.
- **Unsuitable for comparing items** — Card layouts lack consistent visual treatment between items, making side-by-side comparison difficult; irregular layouts force users to look back and forth repeatedly when comparing options.
- **Ideal for heterogeneous content** — Cards excel when displaying different content types together on one page, where not all items need the same information structure; they're less necessary for homogeneous lists.
- **Fitts' Law and touch zones** — Making entire cards clickable creates a larger touch target than individual links, substantially improving usability on both touchscreen and mouse-based devices.

## Quotes

> A container for a few short, related pieces of information. It roughly resembles a playing card in size and shape, and is intended as a linked, short representation of a conceptual unit.

> Cards are often associated with flat design 2.0: they retain some aspects of the aesthetic of flat design, but reintroduce visual depth as a signifierof clickability.

> A single card will typically include a few different types of media, such as an image, a title, a synopsis, sharing icons, or a call-to-action button — all associated with the same concept.

## Concepts

- [[Design Patterns]] — Cards represent a modern revival of the classic deck-of-cards hypertext model, adapted for multi-card responsive layouts.
- [[Design System]] — Cards combine visual design elements (borders, background color, shadows) with interaction affordances (clickability) to create usable, grouped content containers.
- [[Visual Design]] — The principle of common regions allows cards to establish visual grouping more effectively than negative space alone, improving content organization.
- [[Information Design]] — Cards chunk related information into self-contained units and use visual boundaries to indicate grouping, enabling efficient communication of complex content through distinct, digestible pieces.
