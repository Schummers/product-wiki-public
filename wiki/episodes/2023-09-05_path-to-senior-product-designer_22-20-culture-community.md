# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 20. Culture (Community)

## Summary

This chapter, from Part IV on acting on your growth plan, addresses how to align with and shape organizational culture. Culture—defined by shared values, beliefs, behaviors, and attitudes—characterizes a work environment and shapes how employees interact. The chapter distinguishes between abstract company values (like Microsoft's integrity) and behavior-driven ones (like Meta's move-fast philosophy), then provides a framework for improving alignment with those values. It also explores how individual designers can contribute to culture through rituals, interest groups, and initiatives, either by recognizing existing cultural alignment or by initiating new activities that reshape the organization.

## Key Takeaways

- **Company values determine expected behavior** — understanding your company's values reveals what behaviors will be rewarded (e.g., Amazon's customer-centric mission shapes how complaints escalate directly to executives), and explicit awareness of this alignment helps you succeed within the organization.
- **The values alignment framework uses a three-filter table** — prioritize values by checking which ones the company cares about, which matter personally to you, and which advance your growth plan, then identify competencies, existing projects, and new initiatives that align with the highest-priority values.
- **Existing work may already demonstrate cultural alignment** — many projects you're already doing might align with company values, giving you immediate wins to communicate in performance reviews without requiring new effort.
- **Individual designers shape culture through initiatives and rituals** — you can contribute by initiating or owning rituals (design critiques, huddles, celebrations) or by starting cultural initiatives (volunteer groups, running clubs, donation programs) that either stay team-scoped or expand company-wide.
- **Interest groups provide dual benefits** — participating in or creating company interest groups improves your work experience and visibility while helping you build relationships, with particular value at larger companies where such groups are more established.

## Quotes

> Understanding a company's values can give you an understanding of what type of behavior will be valued. Alignment with a company's values will help you to succeed within the organization.

> Company culture isn't just top-down. It is also shaped by employees.

> Participating in these groups could positively impact your overall experience at work, but it could also improve your visibility (more about this in the Getting Promoted chapter) and help you to build relationships.
