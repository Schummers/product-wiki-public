# The 5 Steps to Service Blueprinting

## Summary

This article presents a five-step framework for creating service blueprints, diagrams that visualize how organizations deliver user experiences through processes and people. Service blueprints extend beyond customer journey maps by adding employee actions (frontstage and backstage), support processes, and physical evidence. The five steps are: (1) Find Support by building a crossdisciplinary team and establishing leadership backing, (2) Define the Goal by choosing scope and identifying the customer scenario to blueprint, (3) Gather Research from both customers and employees using methods like interviews and observation, (4) Map the Blueprint by documenting customer actions and corresponding employee actions, support processes, and evidence, and (5) Refine and Distribute by creating a high-fidelity version for stakeholder communication. The article emphasizes that successful blueprinting is collaborative, research-based, and focused; blueprints should visualize one service scenario, include time and quality metrics, and remain living documents that evolve as understanding deepens.

## Key Takeaways

- **Collaborative Creation Is Essential** — Service blueprinting should be done with a crossdisciplinary team; the process of bringing people together and visualizing otherwise abstract infrastructure drives alignment and organizational change.
- **Start With Stakeholder Support** — Before blueprinting, secure backing from managers, executives, or clients; having support ensures that findings will be considered and acted upon.
- **Customer Research Establishes Baseline** — Begin with customer-journey mapping to understand customer actions and touchpoints; this becomes the foundation layer of the service blueprint.
- **Employee Research Reveals Reality** — Employee interviews, observation, and contextual inquiry reveal how work actually gets done (often different from how it's supposed to happen); employees provide essential data about frontstage and backstage actions.
- **Focus on One Service at a Time** — Blueprinting one service or scenario in depth is more useful than attempting to capture multiple services; focusing creates manageable scope and actionable insights.
- **Include Time and Quality Metrics** — Annotating blueprints with time (how long each step takes) and quality measures (where the service succeeds or fails) provides context and helps identify critical moments and pain points.
- **Iterate and Distribute** — Blueprints should evolve as understanding improves; low-fidelity drafts created in workshops evolve into high-fidelity versions for broader stakeholder distribution and communication.

## Quotes

> "Service blueprints are diagrams that visualize organizational processes in order to optimize how a business delivers a user experience."

> "Similar to journey mapping, service blueprinting should be the result of a collaborative process informed by well-defined goals and built on research."

> "A service blueprint should be created from primary data sources (i.e., employee's accounts of their workflows, observations of employee's performing their work, or work-log sheets)."

## Concepts

- [[Service Blueprinting]] — A visualization diagram showing customer actions, employee frontstage and backstage actions, support processes, and physical evidence; used to understand and improve service delivery.
- [[Service Design]] — The practice of designing entire services (not just products); involves understanding and improving all touchpoints, channels, and organizational processes that deliver the service.
- [[Business Process]] — The internal workflows, steps, and procedures an organization uses to deliver products or services; blueprinting makes these processes visible and identifies improvement opportunities.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Getting diverse teams (customer-facing employees, backend operations, management) aligned on how service is delivered and where problems exist; blueprinting facilitates this alignment through visualization.
- [[Journey Mapping]] — A research and visualization technique for understanding customer experiences over time; service blueprinting builds on journey mapping by adding organizational perspectives and processes.
