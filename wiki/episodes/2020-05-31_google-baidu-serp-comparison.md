# How Search Engines Shape Gaze Patterns During Information Seeking: Google vs. Baidu

## Summary

Eyetracking studies comparing Google and Baidu SERPs reveal that search engine design shapes user gaze patterns in fundamental ways. Google's pinball pattern (nonlinear bouncing between organic results and SERP features) was extremely rare on Baidu (1 of 60+ searches), where most participants used sequential, linear scanning. The difference stems from content relevance: Google's knowledge panels contain task-relevant information, while Baidu's right rail contains ads and unrelated searches that users learn to ignore (banner blindness). Both search engines promote their own properties, but Google's integration feels organic while Baidu's feels forced. When Baidu's promoted content is task-relevant, users do engage with it. The findings demonstrate that design choices have long-term effects on user behavior—users develop mental models of page areas based on repeated exposure, and changing behavior requires relevant, high-quality content.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design shapes search behavior patterns** — pinball scanning emerged on Google due to relevant SERP features, while Baidu's linear patterns reflect low-relevance right-rail content that users ignore.
- **Relevance determines engagement with sidebar content** — Google's knowledge panels capture attention because they answer users' questions; Baidu's sidebar ads and unrelated searches create banner blindness.
- **Mental models form from repeated experience** — users develop expectations about page areas; when those areas consistently show irrelevant content, they train themselves to ignore them.
- **Promoted content must serve user tasks** — Baidu's approach works when promoted content answers current queries but fails when it appears ad-like or task-irrelevant.
- **Changing behavior requires consistent quality** — switching users from ignoring an area to engaging with it requires reliably relevant, high-quality content, and takes time despite efforts.
- **Relevance beats prominence for content promotion** — task-relevant content captures attention even when not prominently placed; irrelevant content is ignored even in prominent positions.

## Quotes

> Information-seeking behaviors shift alongside design changes: new designs can trigger new behaviors and eyetracking patterns, particularly when they are frequently encountered (like with Google and Baidu SERPs).

> Thus, it's okay to promote your content on the SERP, as long as it is relevant to the user's task.

> if people have been trained (by your old bad design) to ignore certain areas of your page design, they won't even see the new and better information you place there
