# Your AI UX Intern: Meet Ari

## Summary

This article uses a persona called Ari to help UX practitioners think about AI tools in human terms. Ari represents an extremely knowledgeable intern who is fast but lacks common sense, emotional understanding, and contextual awareness. The article outlines Ari's skills (knowledge, speed, ideation) and critical limitations (inability to observe users, tendency to hallucinate or create false information, eagerness to please without true comprehension). The framework emphasizes that AI works best as an assistant under heavy supervision rather than as an autonomous specialist, particularly in UX research where empathy and contextual judgment are essential.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treat AI as an intern, not a mentor** — AI has broad knowledge but lacks the judgment and contextual awareness needed to work independently; human oversight is essential.
- **Verify all AI outputs** — AI can hallucinate, regurgitate bad advice, and make up false sources, so critical review by humans is necessary before sharing any AI-generated content.
- **AI can't observe or understand users** — AI lacks empathy and contextual awareness, making it unsuitable for evaluating whether interfaces actually serve user needs or understanding the nuances of user behavior.
- **Provide context and specific instructions** — AI works best when given detailed, step-by-step guidance and relevant organizational context rather than open-ended projects.
- **Use AI for drafts, not final products** — AI should generate first drafts of deliverables and provide starting points, not replace human expertise and judgment in UX work.

## Quotes

> We've long recommended working with AI tools like you might work with an intern — providing lots of instructions, guidance, oversight, and correction.

> Unfortunately, that speed and educational background doesn't always translate to common sense. Ari **lacks some critical cognitive abilities,** including:

- Sentience or consciousness
- Deep emotional understanding
- Contextual awareness

> If you consider the qualities that make someone an ideal UX professional, it's clear that this lack of empathy and contextual understanding is a major problem. Ari has a long way to go before they'll be capable of our work without heavy supervision.
