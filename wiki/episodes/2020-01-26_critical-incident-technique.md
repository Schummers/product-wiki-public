# The Critical Incident Technique in UX

## Summary

The Critical Incident Technique (CIT) is a systematic research method where participants recall and describe specific incidents when a behavior or action had a notable impact on an outcome. Formally introduced by John Flanagan in 1954, CIT has become popular in UX and human-computer interaction research for gathering detailed qualitative information about critical system requirements and user experiences. The method can be applied through interviews, focus groups, or surveys. While CIT quickly uncovers significant issues and captures information about rare or extreme events, it relies on memory recall and tends to emphasize extreme rather than typical usage.

## Key Takeaways

- **Structured incident recall** — The critical incident technique asks participants to describe specific times when an action or event had a significant impact on a particular outcome, creating a focused method for gathering rich qualitative data.

- **Better than generic example questions** — Critical incident questions are more effective than open example questions because they focus participants on meaningful, consequential events rather than whatever happens to come to mind first.

- **Captures rare events** — CIT is valuable for identifying uncommon but important incidents that observational research might miss. Participants can recall incidents spanning years, providing temporal range beyond what direct observation allows.

- **Emphasizes important issues** — CIT tends to surface more significant issues compared to other methods that often collect numerous low-importance problems. Significant events are more memorable and easier to recall than minor incidents.

- **Memory and recall limitations** — The technique relies on fallible memory, meaning details can be lost or critical incidents forgotten. Recall can be challenging and even stressful for some participants.

- **Not representative of typical usage** — CIT tends to capture extreme or notable events rather than normal usage patterns. Small usability issues and everyday interactions are rarely mentioned.

## Quotes

> The critical incident technique (CIT) is a research method in which the research participant is asked to recall and describe a time when a behavior, action, or occurrence impacted (either positively or negatively) a specified outcome (for example, the accomplishment of a given task).

> Generally speaking, when the researcher seeks critical incidents, the participant is asked for events that demonstrate both positive and negative effects on the outcome. These questions are usually separate, as in the example interview above. However, in some cases, the researcher may ask for a positive or negative case at the same time and allow the participant to choose which incident to begin with. When asked separately, it is typical to begin with asking for positive incidents in order to begin constructively.

> You will always learn more about how people use existing interfaces and what their pain points are if you carry out observational research, such as contextual inquiry or usability testing.
