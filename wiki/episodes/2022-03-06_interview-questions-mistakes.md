# 6 Mistakes When Crafting Interview Questions

## Summary

This article identifies six common mistakes made when drafting interview questions for user research. The author emphasizes that questions suitable for questionnaires are often inappropriate for user interviews, and poorly constructed questions can lead to inaccurate data. The mistakes range from starting interviews with closed questions that belong in screener surveys, to asking only about typical behaviors, hypothetical questions, leading questions, compound questions, and ambiguous questions. Each mistake is explained with examples and recommendations for how to improve question design.

## Key Takeaways

- **Starting with closed questions** instead of open-ended questions limits participants' ability to share stories and prevents discovery of unexpected insights.
- **Asking only about typical behavior** rather than specific examples yields unreliable information because people's perceptions of typical behavior often diverge from their actual behavior.
- **Hypothetical questions** should be avoided because people are poor at predicting their own future behavior, even though they will provide confident answers.
- **Leading questions with "because"** prime participants toward particular responses and should be replaced with neutral prompts like "What made you decide...?"
- **Compound questions** that ask multiple things at once exceed working memory capacity and should be broken into separate questions with follow-ups.
- **Ambiguous questions** that are too broad cause participants to misinterpret them; questions should be specific enough to prevent confusion.

## Quotes

> Questions that might be fit for a questionnaire are not always appropriate for a user interview.

> The other issue with asking closed questions instead of open-ended ones is that you don't learn things that you didn't think to ask!

> It's much better to ask about specific examples than to ask them to describe what they think is typical.
