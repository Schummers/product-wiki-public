# The Case for Design Disposables

## Summary

Designers often treat all artifacts as deliverables meant to be seen and judged, causing them to invest polish into early explorations. This "deliverable trap" slows the design process and increases attachment to unproven ideas. Design disposables—artifacts created to help designers think rather than to deliver to others—serve a different purpose: they exist to get ideas out quickly so they can be tested and rejected without friction. The sunk-cost fallacy makes it hard to throw away polished work. AI tools compound this problem by speeding up artifact creation without accelerating the thinking that should accompany it; the ease of generation can lead designers to skip the learning that happens during creation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Disposables vs. Deliverables** — Disposables help you think and are allowed to be wrong; deliverables communicate to others and deserve care; the difference is not the artifact but the intention behind it.
- **Exploration Should Be Cheap** — Early-stage exploration requires permission to be rough and wrong; making things polished before testing ideas increases attachment and slows iteration; disposables enable lower-cost exploration.
- **Sunk-Cost Fallacy Slows Design** — Once significant time is invested, designers defend flawed ideas rather than discard them; keeping explorations rough reduces sunk cost and frees designers to question ideas critically.
- **AI Changes the Equation** — AI accelerates artifact generation but removes friction that facilitates learning; when Claude generates a task flow, you get output without the thinking that would occur during manual creation.
- **Ask Before You Make** — Ask yourself: is this for me (to think) or for someone else (to understand)? Answering that question determines how much polish is appropriate.

## Quotes

> Exploration is supposed to be cheap.

> The difference between a disposable and a deliverable isn't always the artifact itself. It's the intention behind it.

> When an AI generates the artifact for you, you can skip straight to having an output without going through the process of making it, and if you're not careful, you can also skip the learning part.

## Concepts

- [[Ideation]] — Effective design exploration requires permission to be rough, wrong, and quick; high-fidelity early artifacts increase sunk-cost attachment and reduce iteration frequency.
- [[Deliverables vs. Disposables]] — Artifacts serve two purposes: to help designers think (disposables) or to communicate to others (deliverables); conflating these roles wastes time and slows progress.
- [[Design Process]] — Successful design processes distinguish between exploration and delivery phases; exploration benefits from low-cost iteration and low attachment; delivery requires care and polish.
