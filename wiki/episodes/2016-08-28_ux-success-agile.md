# Top 10 Tips for UX Success From Agile Practitioners

## Summary

Survey of 125 Agile practitioners from the USA and Singapore revealed ten practices that contribute to successful integration of UX into Agile workflows. Key themes include conducting discovery work before sprints, working one to two sprints ahead in design, building collaborative and trust-based team culture, and treating methodology itself as iterative. Respondents emphasize that UX involvement requires securing stakeholder engagement, clear role definition, and team education to succeed in Agile environments.

## Key Takeaways

- **Invest in release planning and discovery upfront** — Spending time early to plan releases, map stories, and gather user research reduces wasted effort later and helps teams prioritize effectively throughout the project lifecycle.
- **Design should run ahead of development** — UX teams working one to two sprints ahead allows time for thorough research, testing, and design iteration before development needs designs, reducing the pressure to ship incomplete work.
- **Build collaborative culture** — Soft skills and healthy collaboration are fundamental; teams use design-thinking techniques like ideation, whiteboard sessions, and journey mapping to break down silos and build shared understanding.
- **Treat design as iterative, not perfect** — Low-fidelity prototypes (sketches, wireframes) allow teams to test ideas quickly, fail fast, and gather feedback before investing heavily, catching design flaws early rather than after coding.
- **Daily standups keep teams synchronized** — Time-boxed 15-minute scrum meetings help keep everyone updated on progress and impediments, ensuring UX-related activities remain visible and synchronized with development.
- **Make user research a team event** — Involving developers, product owners, and stakeholders in usability testing sessions and debriefs influences decisions based on data rather than opinion, building shared context across disciplines.

## Quotes

> Place more effort in planning, design, and specification.

> Research and design should stay at least one sprint ahead. Give yourself time to do thorough user research and test your designs.

> Close collaboration with other roles in the team has [helped us] arrive at agreements sooner in the process.
