# Demand Accuracy in Your AI Tools: Lessons from Baymard Institute

## Summary

Most AI-powered UX tools lack transparency about accuracy and limitations. When tested, they often underperform: GPT-4's 2023 UX audit accuracy was 20%; more recent tools achieve 50–70%. But even 70% accuracy is dangerous—seven good recommendations and three bad ones, with no way to distinguish them. Baymard Institute's UX-Ray prioritizes accuracy over feature breadth: it checks only 154 guidelines (about 20–25% of Baymard's 700+) with 95%+ accuracy by separating classification (where AI excels) from analysis (where human-defined rules determine if a pattern is good or bad). Designers must demand accuracy metrics, ask about limitations, and understand guardrails before deploying AI tools.

## Key Takeaways

- **Most AI Tools Lack Accountability** — Vendors rarely publish accuracy rates or disclose limitations; without transparency, adoption is risky.
- **High Accuracy Requires Guardrails** — Baymard achieved 95%+ accuracy by using AI for pattern classification only, not for evaluative judgment, which relies on human-defined rules.
- **70% Accuracy Isn't Safe** — Three bad recommendations out of ten can actively harm conversion and experience; teams can't distinguish good from bad without additional review.
- **Small Details Have Massive Impact** — Replacing carousel dots with thumbnails improved conversion 1% ($millions for Fortune 500); an AI tool suggesting the opposite would cause real harm.
- **Separate Classification from Analysis** — Machine learning excels at identifying design patterns; LLMs fail at determining whether those patterns are problematic in context.
- **Hold Vendors Accountable** — Ask for accuracy metrics, understand limitations, identify where the tool breaks, and verify guardrails are in place before deployment.

## Quotes

> The vast majority of tools don't even publish an accuracy rate. So, we can't even discuss whether it's high enough.

> For example, consider that a heuristic-evaluation tool presents you with 10 suggestions to improve the experience. Seven of them are good recommendations… but three of them aren't. They may actively make your experience worse and decrease conversion. But you can't tell which recommendations are the good ones.

> When you ask LLMs to do that, that's where things go off the rails. That's just where accuracy plummets dramatically.

## Concepts

- [[AI Tool Evaluation]] — Evaluating AI tools requires rigorous accuracy testing; vendors must disclose limitations and performance metrics before deployment.
- [[UX Audit]] — Automated heuristic evaluation can support human expertise but cannot replace it; hybrid approaches combining AI classification with human judgment minimize risk.
- [[Accuracy and Reliability]] — Accuracy thresholds differ by context; 95% may be necessary for recommendations affecting conversion; lower thresholds expose users to false positives.
- [[Tool Selection]] — Selecting AI tools means asking hard questions about accuracy, limitations, and guardrails; transparency and rigor distinguish responsible vendors from overhyped ones.
- [[AI Limitations]] — AI excels at pattern recognition but fails at contextual judgment; understanding these boundaries is essential to responsible deployment.
