# Three Key SERP Features: Featured Snippets, People Also Ask, and Knowledge Panels

## Summary

SERP features are specialized design elements on search engine results pages that have become increasingly important for user experience. Based on eyetracking and usability tests conducted from 2016 to 2019, this article examines three major SERP features: featured snippets (excerpts that summarize answers to queries), People Also Ask (accordions with related questions), and knowledge panels (information cards about entities). These features significantly influence how users search by directing attention, helping them modify queries, providing quick answers, and determining whether users click through to full websites or end their search on the SERP itself.

## Key Takeaways

- **SERP features act as signposts** — they help users quickly verify that results are for the entity they are interested in, particularly when accompanied by images or video thumbnails.
- **Pinball scanning patterns replace linear scanning** — users no longer scan SERPs predictably from top to bottom; instead, they bounce between SERP features and organic results based on visual emphasis and expected benefit.
- **Low interaction cost with high expected benefit** — SERP features are attractive because they provide answers without requiring an extra click, page load, or leaving the current view.
- **Post-click viewing occurs frequently** — users sometimes continue examining knowledge panels even after clicking a result, returning to the SERP to read more information while waiting for the next page to load.
- **Modifying queries becomes easier** — Google presents expanded, natural-language interpretations of queries through SERP features, allowing users to explore alternatives without formulating their own searches.
- **In-site search should incorporate SERP features** — since users are conditioned by Google's design, internal site search should incorporate similar SERP-feature designs to meet user expectations.

## Quotes

> Specifically, users' mental models for how search and search features work will be shaped by their experience with Google (or with the dominant search engine in their country), so your own site-search features should cater to the expectations created by the big search engines.

> SERP features involve little interaction cost, because they don't require an extra click, reading lengthy content, or leaving the current view.

> It's like browsing the grocery aisles to find similar products. People don't have to think up new ways of searching themselves; they can simply pick from the computer-generated alternatives, which is much easier to do.
