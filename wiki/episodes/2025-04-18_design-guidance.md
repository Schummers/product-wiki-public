# Design Guidance: Principles, Patterns, Heuristics, and Team Charters

## Summary

Design teams rely on multiple forms of guidance to maintain consistency, advocate for usability, and improve efficiency. This article defines four distinct types: design principles (value statements guiding decision-making), usability heuristics (research-backed best practices for assessing interface usability), design patterns (standardized, reusable solutions for common design problems), and team charters (shared agreements on how teams work together). Each type serves a unique purpose and occupies a different level in a hierarchy of design guidance, together creating a structured approach to design.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design principles align teams and guide decision-making** — high-level value statements describing most important product goals for users; help teams resolve design debates, ensure consistency across products, and make decisions aligned to shared vision; most effective when memorable, distinctive, reflective of brand values, and both general and actionable.
- **Usability heuristics provide universal, research-backed assessment frameworks** — broad rules of thumb for evaluating interface usability (e.g., visibility of system status, user control and freedom, consistency); applicable to any interface regardless of organization; help teams catch common usability problems early and create shared vocabulary for discussing issues.
- **Design patterns solve specific design problems with standardized solutions** — reusable UI components and predefined solutions for common situations (pagination, breadcrumb navigation, form validation, accordions, progress indicators); more tactical than principles; create consistency across product parts and reduce design and development time.
- **Team charters establish shared agreements on collaboration and decision-making** — clarify team purpose, roles, goals, and values; define how team makes decisions (e.g., "Disagreements resolved by revisiting user goals"), communication protocols, and feedback norms; improve efficiency, align priorities, and provide reference point when processes break down.
- **Four types of guidance occupy a hierarchy and function together** — design principles sit atop guiding philosophy; heuristics provide research-backed assessment; patterns offer concrete tactical solutions; charters govern team dynamics; together they create structured design approach at multiple levels.

## Quotes

> Design principles are value statements that describe the most important goals that a product or service should deliver for users and are used to frame design decisions.

> Usability heuristics are broad rules of thumb for guiding design decisions. They can be used to evaluate usability and identify issues in a design.

> Design patterns are standardized, reusable solutions for common design problems.

- [[Heuristic Evaluation]] — applies research-backed best practices as a framework for assessing interface usability independent of organization-specific principles.
