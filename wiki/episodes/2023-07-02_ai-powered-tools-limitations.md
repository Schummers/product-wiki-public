# AI-Powered Tools for UX Research in 2023: Issues and Limitations

## Summary

The authors evaluate four AI-powered research tools claiming to automate analysis of qualitative user research, finding significant gaps between marketing claims and actual capabilities. The tools cannot process video, make vague recommendations, lack context awareness, lack proper citation practices, have usability problems, and cannot meaningfully reduce bias despite claims. Two types exist: insight generators (analysis only) and collaborators (accepting some context). While future iterations may improve, researchers should demand demos, validate findings carefully, and resist tools claiming to fully analyze usability testing from transcripts alone.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI tools cannot meaningfully analyze usability testing** — all tested tools were text-based and could not process video or visual input; transcript-only analysis misses critical context about user actions, microinteractions, and whether design elements truly exist.
- **Context limitations produce unhelpful insights** — insight generators cannot accept study goals, previous research findings, participant backgrounds, or task lists; resulting priorities lack relevance and insights are often uninformative or obvious.
- **Vague recommendations reduce actionability** — AI-generated insights frequently use generic language ("make information easily accessible") without specifying what changed or how implementation should proceed.
- **Lack of citation prevents verification** — tools fail to attribute findings to specific sessions or timestamps, preventing researchers from verifying whether recommendations come from actual data or hallucinated insights, which is particularly risky for research-based decisions.
- **Current usability and reliability are poor** — evaluators encountered outages, errors, and unstable performance requiring customer-support intervention, slowing rather than accelerating analysis.
- **AI bias claims are false** — while AI might reduce confirmation bias in some ways, systems can involve systematic, statistical, and human biases; claiming tools are bias-free misunderstands the technology.

## Quotes

> "If these claims sound too good to be true, it's because they are."

> "Do not trust AI tools that claim to be able to analyze usability-testing sessions by transcripts alone."

> "AI systems and applications can involve biases at 3 levels: systematic, statistical and computational, and human biases."
