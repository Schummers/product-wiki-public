# Venture Studios Demystified: Studio Financing

## Summary

Part 3b of the book covers financial decisions studios must make; this chapter addresses how studios raise funding for their own operations, distinct from any investment fund they may manage. Studios face a unique challenge in capital raising: as a relatively new asset class that differs from traditional VC structures, they must convince investors unfamiliar with the model to back their operating entities. The chapter examines the typical investor profile for studio operating companies and the capital ranges observed across the industry.

## Key Takeaways

- **New Asset Class Barrier** — because studios are a new asset class unlike traditional VC structures, studio leaders struggle to convince investors to fund their operating entities, a challenge amplified in less mature entrepreneurial ecosystems.
- **Target Investor Profile** — operating entities attract high net worth individuals and family offices who seek direct exposure to early-stage ventures, accept the lack of exit guarantees, and can be patient with returns over 5–7 years.
- **Capital Requirements** — studios typically raise $10M–20M for operating company funding, with a small number of outlier studios reaching $50M.
- **Dual LP Pools** — for dual-entity studios, the operating company and investment fund are financed from separate LP bases; the fund typically commands a larger, more diverse investor base than the operating entity.
- **Market Maturation** — as studios demonstrate consistent venture returns and maintain high initial ownership, participation from traditional LPs at the operating company level is expected to increase.
