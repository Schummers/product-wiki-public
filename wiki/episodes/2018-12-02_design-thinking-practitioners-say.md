# What Is Design Thinking, Really? (What Practitioners Say)

## Summary

This article presents findings from Nielsen Norman Group's research into how UX and design professionals define design thinking. Through 71 in-person intercepts and 16 remote interviews across multiple cities, the authors surveyed 87 participants with diverse backgrounds. The research reveals that practitioners hold varied mental models of design thinking, ranging from viewing it as a specific process to understanding it as a flexible mindset or dynamic toolkit. The findings demonstrate that while there is no single definition of design thinking, there is unexpected convergence in how professionals conceptualize it.

## Key Takeaways

- **Characteristic-based understanding dominates** — 62% of respondents associated design thinking with characteristic-like attributes such as "iterative," "user-centered," and "collaborative." This tangible, consistent language appears easiest to teach and grasp compared to more complex jargon.

- **Three mental model progressions exist** — Practitioners define design thinking as (1) a problem-solving process, (2) a mindset shift, or (3) a flexible toolkit. The authors hypothesize these represent stages of expertise, with novices viewing it as a prescribed process and experts treating it as an adaptable approach.

- **Lack of single definition creates friction** — While flexibility in meaning encourages exploration, multiple definitions of design thinking lead to disagreement, misunderstanding, and misaligned outcomes within teams and organizations.

- **Establish shared definitions within teams** — Organizations should create explicit working definitions of design thinking for their specific context rather than relying on industry norms, clarifying what the concept means, what it does not mean, and what outcomes are expected.

- **Measurement depends on definition** — Measuring the effectiveness of design thinking is dependent on the specific definition adopted. What counts as design thinking varies by organizational context.
