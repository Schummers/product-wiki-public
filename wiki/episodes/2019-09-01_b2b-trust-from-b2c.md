# What B2B Designers Can Learn from B2C About Building Trust

## Summary

B2B sites often ignore usability standards that apply to B2C e-commerce, assuming business users have different needs. However, B2B customers bring expectations from B2C sites they use and form impressions based on Jakob's Law. B2B purchasing involves unique constraints (high switching costs, complex decisions requiring stakeholder approval) that make trust-building essential. The article provides recommendations across three areas: transparent pricing strategies, sales contact processes that minimize anxiety, and content strategies that help multiple internal decision-makers understand product value.

## Key Takeaways

- **Jakob's Law applies to B2B** — Business professionals who use B2B sites also use B2C sites and form expectations from the majority of sites they visit; B2B teams should apply proven UX principles rather than assuming different rules apply.

- **Show prices; hiding them signals untrustworthiness** — Hiding pricing increases interaction cost, creates sticker shock, and gives the impression the company is hiding something; showing starting prices or pricing ranges anchors expectations and builds transparency.

- **Clearly explain customization and pricing models upfront** — Display pricing tiers in comparison tables with consistent feature ordering; explain what can be customized and what cannot; manage expectations early to avoid overpromising.

- **Address switching costs directly in messaging** — Promote services or offers (migration assistance, fee coverage) that reduce financial and interaction costs of switching from competitors; include this on FAQ or pricing pages.

- **Provide information upfront before requiring sales contact** — Answer basic questions on How It Works pages, FAQs, and forums without login walls; building reciprocal trust through value first makes users more comfortable contacting sales.

- **Multiple stakeholders require education and "what-if" preparation** — Buyers must educate colleagues and defend decisions; provide case studies, testimonials, product demos, and free trials to address concerns and enable internal advocacy.

## Quotes

> A widespread misconception with business–to–business (B2B) sites is that they are immune to rules and usability standards that apply to ecommerce on business–to–consumer (B2C) sites.

> Clear copy builds trust because it tends to be perceived as honest and transparent, while convoluted writing is often received with skepticism.

> By incorporating these guidelines, you can help users eliminate doubt and bolster trust. A great B2B site must be as simple, clear, and understandable as any great B2C site.

## Concepts

- [[User Trust]] — B2B sites build trust through transparency (pricing, customization, policies), providing value upfront, and demonstrating they understand customer concerns and risks.

- [[B2B UX]] — B2B design must address purchasing complexity, multiple stakeholders, high switching costs, and internal selling; usability principles from B2C apply and improve outcomes.

- [[Pricing Transparency]] — Showing prices anchors expectations, addressing key customer anxiety; hiding prices backfires by increasing perceived costs, suggesting unaffordability, and appearing deceptive.

- [[Customer Experience]] — B2B success requires clear, accessible product explanations, case studies proving value, multiple contact channels, and acknowledgment of what customers must do internally to justify purchases.
