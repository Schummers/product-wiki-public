# UX Mapping Methods: Study Guide

## Summary

This study guide compiles resources on UX mapping methods, which are visualization techniques for understanding user experiences and aligning stakeholders. The guide covers five major mapping approaches: journey maps, service blueprints, UX roadmaps, mapping methods for design exploration, and techniques for synthesizing research. Each method serves different purposes, from capturing the current or future state of a user journey to planning product development timelines or exploring design possibilities. The resource is organized to guide practitioners from foundational concepts through deeper expertise with each technique.

## Key Takeaways

- **Journey maps visualize user experiences** — Journey maps compile user actions into a timeline, add thoughts and emotions to create a narrative, then distill and polish into a visualization that aligns stakeholders and identifies optimization opportunities.
- **Service blueprints connect touchpoints to operations** — Blueprints extend journey mapping by visualizing relationships between service components (people, props, processes) tied to customer journey touchpoints. They are ideal for omnichannel or cross-functional experiences.
- **UX roadmaps align future vision** — Roadmaps act as strategic artifacts that prioritize and communicate a UX team's future work and problems to solve. They serve as a single source of truth for what teams will work on.
- **Design exploration methods support ideation** — Scenario mapping and cognitive mapping help teams think about personas interacting with products and visualize people's mental models for processes or concepts.
- **Synthesis techniques organize insights** — Affinity diagrams and empathy maps help teams collaboratively sort research findings and create shared understanding of user needs.

## Quotes

> A journey map is a visualization of the process that a person goes through in order to accomplish a goal.

> A service blueprint is a diagram that visualizes the relationships between different service components — people, props (physical or digital evidence), and processes — that are directly tied to touchpoints in a specific customer journey.
