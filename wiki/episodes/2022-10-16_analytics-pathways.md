# Understanding User Pathways in Analytics

## Summary

Understanding user journeys—how users move through websites and apps to achieve their goals—is central to UX practice. Analytics tools offer pathways data visualized as Sankey diagrams, which show common sequences of pages or screens users visit and the relative traffic volume between them. However, analytics pathways reveal trends across many users rather than individual journeys, and cannot provide the rich qualitative context (user goals, expectations, thoughts, feelings) that journey mapping requires.

Sankey diagrams display aggregated traffic data showing the most common first, second, and subsequent steps users take. While valuable for identifying patterns, this aggregation masks the diversity of user intentions and cannot tell designers why users take specific paths or whether abandonment represents success or failure. The article provides practical techniques for extracting actionable insights from analytics pathways through filtering, focusing on key touchpoints, starting analysis from desired outcomes, and supplementing with qualitative research. Effective interpretation requires segmenting by user type, comparing related links to actual paths, and investigating hub pages where users repeatedly return.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pathways data shows trends, not individual journeys** — Sankey diagrams aggregate diverse users with different goals and intentions, so identical paths may represent very different user experiences.
- **Analytics cannot show user intent** — two users taking identical paths may have completely different motivations; one abandonment may represent satisfaction (good abandonment), another frustration.
- **Filter data into meaningful chunks** — focus analysis on single nodes, specific user segments (mobile vs. desktop), or key goals to make pathways interpretable.
- **Investigate key touchpoints methodically** — review entry pages, second-step patterns for anomalies, and hub pages where users repeatedly return, which may indicate engagement or frustration.
- **Start analysis from desired outcomes** — begin with key goals (conversions, feature use) and work backward through pathways to understand how users discover and navigate toward goals.

## Quotes

> "A Sankey diagram is made up of nodes (usually corresponding to pages, screens, or use of a feature) connected through links that indicate how people move from one node to the next. The width of the link indicates the amount of traffic that moves between those nodes."

> "They show trends rather than individual-user's movement through the site."

> "We have no reliable way to segment this traffic by what the user was interested in. Two people with the exact same behavior may have had wildly different experiences."

## Concepts

- [[Analytics]] — pathways data reveals common user flows and bottlenecks but must be supplemented by qualitative research to understand intent; analytics alone cannot explain why users behave as they do.
- [[Customer Journey]] — individual journeys differ from aggregated pathways; qualitative research, interviews, and observation provide the rich context analytics cannot, including user goals and emotional states.
- [[Data Analysis]] — effective pathways analysis requires filtering to key pages and user segments, investigating anomalies, and comparing related links to actual traffic patterns.
- [[Behavioral Patterns]] — hub pages, pogo-sticking, and abandonment patterns require qualitative context to interpret; analytics reveals what users do but not why they do it.
