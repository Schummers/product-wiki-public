# How Much Time Does It Take to Create a Journey Map?

## Summary

Journey mapping is regarded as beneficial for aligning stakeholders and identifying experience gaps, but practitioners perceive it as time- and resource-intensive. A survey of 343 practitioners examined time investment across the 5 key steps of journey mapping: building buy-in, collecting internal stakeholder data, conducting external research, synthesizing insights, and creating the artifact. Mean total time invested was 73.8 hours (just under 2 full weeks), with significant variation based on organizational context, company size, and methodological approach.

## Key Takeaways

- **Average investment: ~74 hours** — Mean time across all steps totals 73.8 hours; external research (20.6 hours) and synthesis (16.4 hours) consumed the most time, while securing resources (8.9 hours) consumed the least.
- **In-house teams invest more overall** — In-house practitioners reported 79.8 hours versus 71.7 hours for agency practitioners, though agencies spent more time creating the actual artifact, suggesting different workflow structures.
- **Larger organizations invest more time** — Practitioners from companies with 500+ employees reported 87 hours versus 64.9 hours for smaller companies, reflecting formalized approval processes and stakeholder engagement requirements.
- **Hypothesis-first approach does not save time** — Teams using hypothesis-first approaches (skipping initial research) reported spending as much or more time overall than research-first teams, suggesting upfront research investment provides efficiency gains.
- **All organizations invest in buy-in** — Both large and small companies reported substantial time on securing buy-in and resources, indicating that UX maturity level is not highly correlated with company size.
