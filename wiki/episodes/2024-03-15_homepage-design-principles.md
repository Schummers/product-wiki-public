# Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles

## Summary

This article outlines five fundamental principles for designing effective homepages that capture user attention and guide them toward their goals. The principles are grounded in decades of usability research and user behavior, establishing that effective homepages ensure easy access, communicate clearly who the organization is and what they do, reveal content through examples, prompt actions and navigation, and maintain simplicity. The durability of these principles derives from the fact that they are rooted in fundamental human behavior and cognitive principles that remain stable over time.

## Key Takeaways

- **Easy access** — homepages must be easily accessible through implicit links (like logos) and explicit links (labeled "Home"), and they should have predictable, memorable URLs that users can guess.
- **Clear communication** — the homepage should immediately convey who the organization is and what it does through prominent logos, taglines, and unique value propositions that distinguish from competitors.
- **Content revelation** — the most important content should appear above the fold to drive scrolling, and specific examples of site offerings are more effective than generic category labels in encouraging exploration.
- **Action prompts** — clear, descriptive link labels with high information scent help users understand what they'll get by clicking, and primary navigation should be prominently placed with clear visual hierarchy.
- **Simplicity** — homepages should use standard, predictable designs that align with user expectations, minimize motion and animation, load quickly, and avoid popup windows unless legally required.
- **Consistency with guidelines** — most 113 guidelines identified in 2001 remain valid today because usability principles are stable and grounded in fundamental human behavior.

## Quotes

> While it can be tempting to experiment with creative layouts on your homepage, people spend most of their time on other sites and prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know.

> The best way to welcome users is by providing a clear explanation of your site's offerings and a distinct starting point for navigation.
