# Preparation Tactics for a Tough UX Job Market

## Summary

Navigating a tough UX job market requires different strategies depending on whether you are currently employed, a student, or someone transitioning into UX. The article provides specific tactics for each group. Currently employed practitioners should demonstrate value, avoid leaving too quickly, achieve notable outcomes, and maintain updated portfolios while building financial reserves. Students and recent graduates should cultivate professor relationships, attend industry events, connect with alumni, and emphasize their strengths. Career changers should gain real-world experience through freelance projects, reframe their resume around UX value, and attempt UX pivots at their current employer before seeking external opportunities.

## Key Takeaways

- **For employed practitioners** — Deliver results your manager values, clarify priorities, give disliked jobs at least 1-2 years if you can achieve something notable, document projects, and build 6-9 months of living expenses as financial resilience.
- **Job hopping has perception costs** — Frequently changing jobs is perceived as signaling poor collaboration skills, though perceptions may slowly change as younger generations assume leadership; give jobs time to develop.
- **For students and graduates** — Develop professor relationships, attend university career events, connect with alumni, prioritize building strength in one area over dabbling, and ask professors to keep you in mind for opportunities.
- **For career changers** — Work on freelance UX projects to generate real-world portfolio material (hypothetical redesigns are nearly worthless), reframe resume around UX value, and attempt UX pivots at current employers before external job searches.
- **Real-world project experience matters** — Portfolio must demonstrate ability to confront technology constraints, collaborate with skeptics, and learn from real users; this is critical for career changers.
- **Start transitions at current employer when possible** — It's easier to transition into UX where you have relationships and resources than convincing unknown hiring managers; explore design-adjacent opportunities if no formal UX team exists.

## Quotes

> It's relatively easy to bask in the glow of a sunny UX job market and its plentiful opportunities. However, coping with a challenging job market is the true test of endurance for the UX professional.

> You want to convey a positive outcome: delivering measurable quantitative improvements on benchmarks (e.g., customer satisfaction or time on task) or being qualitatively recognized for your unique and valuable contributions with a promotion or award.

> While the future is difficult to predict, a tough UX job market won't last forever. New technology, tools, and ideas will continue to reshape the UX profession.
