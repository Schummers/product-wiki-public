# The Hawthorne Effect or Observer Bias in User Research

## Summary

Observer bias, also called the Hawthorne effect, is the tendency for people to change their behavior when they know they are being observed. This phenomenon appears across all user research methods from field studies to surveys and affects validity. Researchers can mitigate observer bias through specific strategies tailored to each research type: building rapport in field studies, designing natural scenarios and setting clear expectations in usability tests, using indirect questioning in surveys, and maintaining participant engagement in diary studies. While perfect elimination is impossible, awareness and intentional study design can minimize bias impact and lead to more authentic insights.

## Key Takeaways

- **Bias occurs in all research methods** — The Hawthorne effect and social-desirability bias appear across field studies, usability testing, surveys, and diary studies, affecting how truthfully participants behave.
- **Don't make participants feel judged** — In field studies, judgment-free environments help participants return to natural behaviors; even researcher presence affects behavior less if participants feel comfortable.
- **Design natural, realistic scenarios** — Task scenarios that feel familiar rather than novel reduce the pressure participants feel to perform well for the researcher.
- **Set clear expectations about testing** — Tell participants the design is being tested, not them; that honest feedback is valued; and they can proceed however they would normally act.
- **Indirect questioning yields better data** — In surveys, indirect questions about related behaviors often reveal views better than direct questions, especially on sensitive topics.
- **Explain purpose and share updates** — In diary studies, helping participants understand why their responses matter and showing them how feedback led to change increases honest participation.

## Quotes

> The Hawthorne effect is also widely used to promote desirable behaviors –- for example, to encourage compliance with handwashing by telling people they are being watched in the bathrooms and to increase road safety by displaying dummy CCTV cameras.

> At the same time, people quite quickly return to their default routine activities and behaviors if they feel comfortable and do not mind the researcher's presence.

> We're not testing you and how much you know about X topic. We're testing the design.

## Concepts

- [[Observer Bias]] — The phenomenon where people modify their behavior when aware of observation, requiring researchers to design studies that minimize awareness or build authentic relationships.
- [[Research Methods]] — Various approaches for conducting user research including field studies, usability tests, surveys, and diary studies, each with distinct observer-bias mitigation strategies.
- [[Study Design]] — The intentional structuring of research to minimize participant awareness, build comfort, use natural scenarios, and encourage authentic responses.
