# The Return of the UX Generalist

## Summary

The article argues that while UX specialization dominated the past decade, artificial intelligence is reversing this trend toward narrowly defined roles. As AI tools increasingly handle specialized technical tasks, the value proposition shifts toward generalist professionals who can think strategically across disciplines, orchestrate AI-supported tools, and understand how different UX areas interconnect. The authors illustrate this trend with a case study of Landor, a brand-transformation company, and provide four strategies for professionals to prepare for this transition.

## Key Takeaways

- **Specialization is being disrupted by AI** — over the past decade, UX became highly specialized with dedicated researchers, designers, content strategists, and visual designers; AI is now democratizing expertise by allowing individuals to accomplish tasks that previously required collaboration across specialized roles.
- **Generalists will have vastly more job opportunities** — low-UX-maturity companies represent the majority of the market and will increasingly need some UX support to compete; these companies cannot afford specialists and will favor AI-augmented generalists for their efficiency and adaptability.
- **Generalist roles will shift toward strategic thinking** — as AI handles technical execution like wireframing, visual design, and data analysis, the unique human value moves to facilitation, organizational navigation, business acumen, and connecting disparate insights to strategic outcomes.
- **AI accelerates skill development across disciplines** — generalists can now learn visual design, research methodologies, or information architecture faster because AI provides immediate feedback, multiple design variations to study, and context-aware explanations of principles and decisions.
- **Four key preparation strategies sustain career value** — developing a learning mindset, becoming fluent in AI collaboration, focusing on transferable skills (critical thinking, facilitation, strategic decision-making), and expanding into adjacent fields (systems thinking, business strategy, front-end development).

## Quotes

> Increasingly, **individuals can accomplish tasks that previously required collaboration with several experts in different areas.**

> **True generalist thinking involves a fundamentally different approach** to understanding and solving problems.

> As genAI technology becomes more reliable and better integrated into UX work, this trend will accelerate.
