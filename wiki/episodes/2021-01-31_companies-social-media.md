# Companies on Social Media: 6 Types of User Interactions with Business

## Summary

Social media is a key component of the omnichannel user experience. Based on research with 23 participants, six core interaction types emerge when users engage with companies on social media: discover (finding interesting content), research (understanding products), engage (interacting with content), purchase (social selling or commerce), support (getting help), and promote (sharing experiences). Users may enter social media via advertisements or active searches and can move fluidly between these interaction types. Social media often serves as an entry point to other channels rather than the primary interaction channel.

## Key Takeaways

- **Discover: users find companies through ads and hashtag searches** — users passively discover products through suggested posts and ads, and actively search using hashtags to find companies and content.
- **Research: users seek practical information and inspiration** — social media supports research activities where users want easy-to-consume content with clear access to more comprehensive information and styling inspiration.
- **Engage: users interact through participation and sharing** — users engage by entering giveaways, commenting, liking, sharing posts, or visiting company websites through social links.
- **Purchase: two methods exist for social buying** — social selling directs users to website purchases, while social commerce enables end-to-end purchasing within social platforms.
- **Support: users seek faster response than traditional channels** — frustrated users contact companies via social media believing it's faster than email; some post publicly to shame companies into responding.
- **Promote: users share experiences when deeply satisfied** — promotion reflects deep engagement; users tag brands, send sales to friends, and become advocates when they trust the company.
