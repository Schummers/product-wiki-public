# Groupthink in UX Work

## Summary

Groupthink is a psychological phenomenon where well-intentioned team members conform to majority opinion and make suboptimal decisions for conformity's sake, avoiding dissent to maintain harmony. In UX contexts, groupthink often emerges during ideation sessions, workshops, focus groups, when following design trends, and in virtual environments. The article identifies symptoms (collective rationalization, illusion of unanimity, self-censorship, direct pressure on dissenters) and provides specific prevention strategies for each common scenario where groupthink occurs in UX teams.

## Key Takeaways

- **Definition and UX relevance** — Groupthink propels cohesive teams to prioritize consensus over consideration of alternatives; it occurs when dissenting ideas are avoided or ignored due to desire for unanimity.
- **Symptoms in UX contexts** — Common symptoms include collective rationalization of warnings, illusion of unanimity from silence, self-censorship of dissenting views, and direct pressure on dissenters to conform.
- **Prevent through group norms and solo ideation** — During ideation, communicate group norms that value all ideas, explicitly encourage disagreement, and have members ideate solo first before sharing to the group.
- **Workshop facilitation prevents groupthink** — Facilitators should introduce groupthink awareness, include solo and group activities using diverge-converge technique, and use dot voting to democratize decision-making rather than having facilitators decide.
- **Focus group recruitment and warmup matter** — Recruit diverse participants to avoid homogeneous agreement; use warmup activities to help participants feel comfortable speaking up; experienced facilitators are critical.
- **Evaluate trends with user testing** — When considering design trends, test them with your actual users rather than assuming competitor adoption means it's right for you; involve multiple stakeholders in trend evaluation.
- **Virtual settings exacerbate groupthink** — Rotate meeting hosts, use anonymous follow-up activities, and establish purpose for each communication channel to prevent virtual-meeting groupthink.

## Quotes

> "Groupthink occurs when highly cohesive teams lose sight of individual thoughts and opinions."

> "Groups benefit from hearing diverse perspectives. For that to happen, group members have to feel comfortable in sharing their thoughts."

## Concepts

- [[Team Collaboration]] — Highly cohesive teams are prone to groupthink; while collaboration is valuable, collaboration that suppresses dissent undermines decision quality, so teams must actively manage dynamics and cultivate psychological safety to balance harmony with healthy disagreement and diverse perspectives.
- [[Decision Making]] — Groupthink leads to suboptimal decisions; structured approaches (dot voting, diverge-converge) and explicit invitation for dissent improve decision quality.
- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — Skilled facilitation that introduces awareness of groupthink, manages participation, and uses structured techniques prevents conformity-driven decision-making.
