# Collaboration in the Omnichannel Experience

## Summary

Collaboration is the fifth key element of high-quality omnichannel experiences, allowing users to leverage multiple devices or channels together to achieve goals more efficiently. Unlike consistency, optimization for context, seamlessness, and orchestration which are required for good omnichannel experiences, collaboration is optional but valuable when available. Collaboration can reduce interaction cost by letting helper devices (like a mobile phone scanning a QR code to unlock a desktop) streamline complex workflows, or it can enrich interactions by providing new capabilities (like taking a photo with a phone to insert into a presentation on a laptop). Examples include WeChat's QR code login, Dropbox's app installation flow, TurboTax's video chat support, Keynote's photo insertion, smart appliances that pair with mobile apps, and Netflix's cross-device content selection.

## Key Takeaways

- **Collaboration uses helper and target channels together** — A target channel is where the main task occurs, and a helper channel provides resources to reduce effort on the target channel; for example, WeChat phone app helps you log into desktop WeChat via QR code.
- **Collaboration reduces interaction cost** — By borrowing resources from a helper device (like a camera or existing login), users can accomplish complex tasks with less mental and physical effort on the target device.
- **Collaboration enriches and adds capabilities** — Beyond reducing effort, collaboration can add entirely new capabilities; for example, Keynote on Mac can use an iPhone camera to insert photos that would be tedious to capture otherwise.
- **Context and paired devices enable collaboration** — Smart appliances, streaming services, and productivity tools provide natural opportunities for collaboration when devices are physically nearby or logically paired.
- **Collaboration is optional but valuable** — Unlike other omnichannel components, collaboration enhances experiences but isn't required for basic usability; it shines when addressing specific pain points or enabling new interactions.
- **Cross-ecosystem collaboration is rare** — Drag-and-drop and file sharing between Microsoft and Google apps remains inconsistent and confusing; adoption requires most apps to support the pattern.

## Quotes

> Collaboration is when an experience allows for two or more normally disparate devices or channels to be used together to achieve a goal.

> Of all 5 omnichannel components, collaboration is the only one that should not be considered a requirement for a high-quality omnichannel experience. Although collaboration often creates valuable and enriching touchpoints, the lack of collaborative interactions in a journey is not detrimental to the basic experience.

> Interaction cost is the sum of efforts — mental and physical — that the users must deploy in interacting with a site in order to reach their goals.
