# Recruiting and Screening Candidates for User Research Projects

## Summary

Recruiting qualified participants is essential to research quality, but participant recruitment faces many challenges including bias toward certain participant types, professional testers who may game studies, and self-selection bias. Screening surveys help identify participants representative of the target audience and exclude poor fits. The article outlines eight steps for effective recruiting: defining eligibility criteria, constructing screeners, choosing a recruitment strategy, adapting the strategy to the target, handling multiple user segments, reviewing both qualified and borderline candidates, and conducting screening interviews for highly specific needs. It also provides a detailed comparison of five recruiting methods: professional recruiters, automated platforms, internal user panels, online forums, and intercept studies.

## Key Takeaways

- **Define explicit eligibility criteria** — Consider both demographics and user goals relevant to the product. Avoid overly restrictive criteria that eliminate good candidates (like excluding anyone who has participated in research recently).
- **Design screeners to prevent gaming** — Use open-ended or multiple-choice questions with distractor options to avoid telegraphing the study's intent. Place critical elimination criteria early to avoid wasting respondents' time.
- **Choose the right recruiting method** — Professional recruiters work best for specialized needs, automated platforms for general audiences, internal panels for power users, online forums for niche interests, and intercepts for visitors or task-specific users.
- **Avoid professional tester bias** — Remote-testing platforms often include "professional testers" who participate for income. Vet screening-survey answers for reasonableness and consider combining recruitment methods.
- **Review near-matches carefully** — Some disqualified candidates may still be acceptable. For example, someone owning both Android and iPhone might have been screened out despite being qualified.
- **Screen for diversity** — Ensure sample includes diverse genders, ages, ethnicities, abilities, and tech skills. Prioritize relevant characteristics like low digital skills or accessibility needs to support inclusive design.

## Quotes

> Recruiting participants for research studies is a difficult task: you have to attract interested participants, schedule times to meet for the study, remind them to come to the study, and then hope that they do, in fact, remember to come to their scheduled session.

> To be clear, the intent behind screening surveys is to alleviate some of the manual work of vetting candidates, but some effort is still required (whether by the researcher or a professional recruiter) to ensure that quality candidates get selected.

> No matter who your users are, you must screen research participants to ensure you are using your research time and budget wisely. After all, your design decisions will only as good as your data is.
