# Venture Studios Demystified: Process

## Summary

This chapter describes the systematic processes studios use to generate, test, and launch ventures at scale. Drawing from design thinking principles of rapid prototyping and iteration, studios apply a standardized three-step process: brainstorming and early idea development, customer validation through user research and marketing testing, and building and testing early prototypes. Studios achieve significantly higher success rates than traditional startups, with 60% of studio-founded companies reaching Series A compared to 42% for non-studio-founded companies. Process variation emerges through differences in stage gates, evaluation criteria, funnel efficiency, and companies launched per year, with most studios launching fewer than 4 ideas annually.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design Thinking Foundation** — Studios leverage rapid prototyping, testing, and fail-fast principles to minimize costs and extend runway. They kill unsuccessful ideas early in research stages and heavily scrutinize advanced ideas against short-term evaluation windows.
- **Three-Step Process** — Studios follow a consistent process: (1) brainstorming and early idea development, (2) customer validation through user research and marketing testing (click-through rates, customer LOIs), and (3) building and testing early prototypes.
- **Studio Funnel Efficiency** — The ideation funnel varies significantly, requiring 30 to 107 top-level ideas to launch a single company. More efficient funnels reduce per-idea time investment and lower individual idea costs.
- **Time to Market** — Studios typically require 6-18 months to Seed stage and an additional 12 months to Series A (aligned with GSSN average of 11 months to Seed and 15 months to Series A). Time-to-launch directly affects timelines for subsequent rounds and eventual exits.
- **Launch Cadence and Volume** — 73% of studios aim to launch at most 4 companies annually, while 13% target 4-6 companies and 13% launch 6-12 companies per year. Launch volume may reflect process efficiency differences or varying bars for launch-readiness.
- **Superior Success Rate** — Companies founded out of studios show a 30% higher conversion rate from Seed to Series A (72% vs 42%), demonstrating the effectiveness of systematic ideation and de-risking processes.

## Quotes

> Studios tend to broadly follow the same ideation and development process built on design thinking. The foundation of the design thinking process is simple: rapidly prototype, test, and fail fast. Given that studios need to fund and support ideas for several years before seeing a single exit, minimizing costs to extend funding runway is critical.

> Many ideas are killed in the earliest research stages, and advanced ideas are heavily scrutinized against shortterm evaluation periods. It's no surprise then that studios are leveraging the design thinking process, as rapid iteration to test ideas is key in ensuring studios move nimbly without sinking too much funding into any one failing idea.

> Companies founded out of studios have a 30% higher conversion rate between Seed and Series A (72% vs 42%) (Disrupting the Venture Landscape, 2020).

## Concepts

- [[Venture Studio]] — This chapter details the systematic processes studios use to generate and launch ventures at scale, achieving superior success rates compared to traditional startup formation.
- [[Stage Gate]] — Studios implement gates where ideas must pass evaluation criteria (customer LOIs, click-through rate targets, IC review) to advance or receive additional funding.
- [[Idea Validation]] — Customer validation is the second step of the three-step process, involving user research and marketing testing to validate customer demand before prototype development.
- [[Minimum Viable Product]] — The third step involves building and testing early prototypes to further validate product concepts before full company launch.
- [[Studio Funnel]] — The ideation funnel converts 30 to 107 top-level ideas into 1 launched company, with significant variation across studios in funnel efficiency reflecting differences in process, quality bar, and team capabilities.
