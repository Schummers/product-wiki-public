# A Lesson on Top Tasks from Hurricane Beryl

## Summary

This article illustrates the critical importance of identifying and supporting top tasks through CenterPoint Energy's website failures during Hurricane Beryl. When the hurricane knocked out power for 2.5 million homes, users urgently needed to know when their power would return, yet the utility's website made this top task extremely difficult. The article examines the company's UX problems and contrasts them with Duke Energy's superior outage map, demonstrating why organizations serving essential services must prioritize top tasks even when they're infrequently used in normal conditions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Top tasks are must-have capabilities** — top tasks are activities that users must be able to do with a product; if users cannot do them, the experience has failed.
- **Crisis elevates dormant top tasks** — tasks that appear low-priority under normal conditions can suddenly become critical during emergencies, requiring organizations to anticipate and prepare.
- **Findability compounds problems** — even when CenterPoint deployed an outage map, requiring users to scroll past metrics and explanatory text before finding the link made the top task harder.
- **Cognitive load matters in crisis** — presenting multiple outdated maps alongside the current one, jargon-filled status labels, and vague alerts overwhelmed users who needed quick, actionable information.
- **Content clarity prevents abandonment** — Duke Energy's map proved that clear search fields, simple language, visible restoration times, and update frequency build user trust and support top task completion.
