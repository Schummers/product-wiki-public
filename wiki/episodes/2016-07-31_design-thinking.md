# Design Thinking 101

## Summary

Design thinking is an ideology and accompanying process that asserts a hands-on, user-centric approach to problem solving leads to innovation and competitive advantage. The design-thinking process comprises six phases within three larger buckets: understand (empathize), explore (define, ideate), and materialize (prototype, test, implement). The process is iterative and cyclical rather than strictly linear; teams often return to earlier phases after prototyping and testing to refine understanding. Design thinking achieves multiple advantages: it is user-centered and data-driven, leverages collective expertise, and encourages exploration of multiple solution avenues. The critical sixth phase—implement—is often forgotten but is essential; design thinking is not magic and requires actual execution to transform ideas into real solutions that improve users' lives. The term was formalized in the 1990s by David Kelley and Tim Brown of IDEO.

## Key Takeaways

- **Six iterative phases** — Empathize (research and observation), define (identify problems and opportunities), ideate (generate creative solutions), prototype (build tactile representations), test (get user feedback), implement (execute the vision).
- **Three larger flows** — The process moves through understanding, exploring, and materializing rather than following a strictly linear path.
- **Iteration is essential** — Phases should be repeated as needed; teams often revisit empathy and define phases after prototyping reveals new learning.
- **Implementation is crucial** — The sixth phase—actually implementing the solution—is the most important but most often forgotten; design thinking requires real execution, not just ideation.
- **Flexibility and adaptation** — Use the framework as scaffolding, not as a rigid recipe; adapt it based on team needs, expertise, and the specific problem.

## Quotes

> Design thinking is an ideology that asserts that a hands-on, user-centric approach to problem solving can lead to innovation, and innovation can lead to differentiation and a competitive advantage.

> This is the most important part of design thinking, but it is the one most often forgotten. As Don Norman preaches, "we need more design doing." Design thinking does not free you from the actual design doing. It's not magic.

> Jakob Nielsen says "a wonderful interface solving the wrong problem will fail." Design thinking unfetters creative energies and focuses them on the right problem.

## Concepts

- [[Design Thinking]] — Design thinking is a formalized framework that combines user research, ideation, prototyping, and testing to solve problems systematically.
- [[Design Process]] — The six-phase design thinking process (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test, implement) provides structure while remaining iterative and flexible.
- [[User Research]] — Design thinking begins with empathy and understanding; research and observation of real users ground all subsequent decisions.
- [[Innovation]] — Design thinking encourages exploration of multiple solutions and focuses on solving real user needs, not imaginary ones.
- [[Problem Solving]] — Design thinking starts by clearly defining the problem before jumping to solutions; this ensures effort is focused on the right challenges.
