# Solving Product Design Exercises: Chapter 5: Tasks list

## Summary

The chapter is what its title says: "A collated list of all tasks mentioned in the book", followed by a block of additional tasks. It carries no prose of its own beyond that one line. The tasks span fintech, transportation, healthcare, e-commerce, music streaming, travel, real estate and media; some are a single sentence, others open with a paragraph of market context or a named persona, and several close with an explicit constraint line.

## Key Takeaways

- **Fintech and commerce variety** — Tasks include redesigning financial systems (ATM experience, MetroCard), marketplace hiring flows (LinkedIn), e-commerce user targeting (Amazon), and standalone platform briefs (restaurant reservations, a new revenue driver for Airbnb).
- **Underserved audience angle** — Several tasks are framed as picking a group a product serves badly and designing for it: an under-served Spotify audience, a target user Amazon.com does not appeal to, parents with children on Spotify, and a Gen-X bachelor recently diagnosed with early signs of obesity.
- **Systems and complexity** — Tasks extend beyond single-screen design to full platform thinking: artist management systems, sales funnel dashboards, healthcare access systems, and multi-party workflows (group vacation planning, roommate matching).
- **Constraints are written into the prompt** — Several tasks carry an explicit constraint line, all of them about available technology ("existing mobile capabilities of iOS and Android," "available on the market today," "viable for the market within six months," a Mac desktop app); others set the target as a business metric instead (improve Pinterest's retention, create a new revenue driver for Airbnb). The chapter states the constraints without commenting on what they are for.
- **Accessibility and inclusion** — Two tasks name a group the mainstream product does not serve: an alarm clock for the blind, and an ATM machine for children.

## Concepts

- [[Design Exercise]] — A collated list of 30+ open-ended design prompts, the ones used earlier in the book plus additional ones, several carrying an explicit technology constraint or a business-metric target.
- [[Problem Framing]] — Tasks demonstrate how to frame problems across diverse domains (fintech, healthcare, transportation, social, real estate) with contextual details like market size, user demographics, behavioral trends, and technology constraints.
- [[Product Thinking]] — Exercise prompts require connecting user problems to business metrics (retention improvement, new revenue drivers, underserved segments) and considering systemic impacts beyond single features.
- [[Ideation]] — The chapter provides a structured library of diverse problem spaces to generate and practice ideation across different scales: single-screen flows, platforms, ecosystems, and policy-adjacent solutions.
- [[Portfolio]] — Supplies the raw briefs a candidate can work through; the chapter itself only lists them, the instruction to use them as portfolio material is made earlier in the book.
