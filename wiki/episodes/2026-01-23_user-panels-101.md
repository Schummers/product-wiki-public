# User Panels 101

## Summary

A user panel is a curated group of opted-in customers or target users who agree to future research contact. Functioning as research infrastructure rather than a mere list, panels connect fragmented recruiting efforts across teams and increase UX maturity. Building a panel requires intentional planning: recruiting, organizing, contacting, incentivizing, re-engaging, and establishing governance. Internal panels speed up recruiting, reduce costs, and deepen customer relationships but require ongoing management and come with tradeoffs—existing customers won't reflect new-user perspectives. Many organizations use hybrid approaches, combining panels with external recruiting for diversity and reach.

## Key Takeaways

- **Panel Functions as Infrastructure** — Panels connect fragmented recruiting and serve as a living asset, moving research from isolated projects to steady organizational decision-making.
- **Three Major Benefits** — Panels save time (no-show rates drop ~20%), save money (external recruiting is expensive), and build lasting customer relationships.
- **Two Primary Panel Types** — Customer panels leverage existing users (high trust, context-rich) but risk overfamiliarity; target-user panels offer fresh perspectives but are harder to recruit.
- **Six-Step Building Process** — Recruit, organize/segment, contact/schedule, incentivize/engage, re-engage/manage, and establish governance; the last step ensures privacy, consistency, and sustainability.
- **Tool Variety Supports Flexibility** — Purpose-built research platforms offer study management; CRM systems can structure profiles; general tools like spreadsheets are inexpensive but risky at scale.
- **Hybrid Approach Is Standard** — Internal panels provide speed and familiarity; external recruiting adds diversity; many teams use both simultaneously.

## Concepts

- [[User Research]] — Panels represent foundational infrastructure for scaling research across organizations; they support continuous, systematic user engagement.
- [[Recruitment and Sampling]] — Panels enable efficient, consistent recruiting by maintaining a curated pool; hybrid approaches combine panel speed with external recruiting's diversity.
- [[UX Maturity]] — Panel maturity ranges from informal spreadsheets (low maturity) to structured systems with automation and governance (high maturity); panels themselves signal organizational investment in research.
- [[Research Operations]] — Panels require operational systems: segmentation, contact management, participation tracking, incentive distribution, and governance to sustain over time.
- [[User Engagement]] — Panels foster long-term customer engagement, building relationships beyond single studies; re-engagement practices prevent fatigue while maintaining participation.
