# In User Research, Don't Stop at "Yes" or "No"

## Summary

Research conducted solely to validate pre-made design decisions is less valuable than no research at all, because it pressures researchers to gloss over issues and focus only on affirmative findings. Binary yes-or-no validation misses nuance and prevents teams from making well-informed decisions. The article argues that "yes" and "no" answers are unreliable (participants often want to please researchers or lack context to make realistic predictions), that attitudinal data alone must be enhanced with behavioral data, and that binary results provide no actionable insight into *why* something succeeded or failed. Effective research captures nuance, identifies both strengths and weaknesses, and creates an iteration mindset that delivers real value.

## Key Takeaways

- **Binary Validation is Unreliable** — Participants often give positive responses to avoid disappointing researchers or because framing omits tradeoffs; "I think people would use it" is not data but speculation, revealing only that the participant themselves wouldn't use it.
- **Words Don't Predict Behavior** — What people say they will do is heavily influenced by factors they won't anticipate in the moment; attitudinal data alone cannot be trusted to predict real-world behavior.
- **Behavioral Data is Essential** — Prototype testing and observing actual user behavior reveal whether stated preferences hold up in practice and uncover edge cases and real-world situations where design assumptions fail.
- **Validation Bias Flattens Insights** — When research is framed as validation, even behavioral data gets flattened into simple pass/fail rather than capturing the rich nuance of where and how users succeed or struggle.
- **Nuance Enables Iteration** — Understanding *how* a design succeeded and simultaneously *how* it failed creates a complete picture; teams that can learn from both successes and failures make better decisions and iterate faster.

## Quotes

> Demanding that user research validate existing decisions is worse than doing no research at all because coming back with anything but a "yes, this solution is great" puts delivery deadlines at risk.

> The response "I think people would use it" is a common indicator that the participant is trying to spare your feelings.

> What people say is not what they do.
