# Why Does a Design Look Good?

## Summary

Good design is not accidental; it results from intentional application of visual-design principles. This article dissects three real-world examples to demonstrate how specific design choices create attractive and usable interfaces. By analyzing Medium.com, Ritual.com, and Spotify, the article shows how grid alignment, typography systems, color palettes, visual hierarchy, imagery, and consistency work together to make designs feel cohesive, professional, and visually appealing. The underlying principle is that aesthetic appeal and usability are intertwined, achieved through deliberate decisions guided by a visual-design system.

## Key Takeaways

- **Alignment to a grid creates visual structure** — Consistent alignment to grid lines makes designs look crisp and helps content be easier to scan; establish a column grid and ensure alignment is consistent across pages.
- **Limited color palettes unify the design** — Refined color palettes with 2-3 carefully chosen colors provide focus and create stronger brand representation; avoid primary CMYK colors which can look unsophisticated.
- **Typographic variance with purpose** — Use different type styles from the same font family (bold, italic, small caps) consistently for the same purpose across all pages to differentiate content types without disrupting cohesion.
- **Generous leading increases readability** — Increasing line spacing beyond the default makes text feel more open and less dense; consider adding 4-6px extra leading to paragraph text for better readability.
- **Visual hierarchy directs attention** — Use size, color, and placement intentionally to make important elements stand out first; limit font treatments to a maximum of two different styles per headline.
- **Consistency in visual systems creates polish** — Define clear visual rules for spacing, typography, and padding, then apply them consistently throughout the design; spacing and color differentiate groupings and establish relationships.

## Quotes

> Visual details like fonts, colors, and alignment create a usable experience and express brand traits such as friendliness or reliability.

> While too much variety can disrupt consistency and make a design look haphazard, Medium's variants are applied consistently and with purpose.

> Each decision in a design should be made with intention, ideally backed up by a visual-design system.
