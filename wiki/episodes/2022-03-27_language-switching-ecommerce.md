# 6 Tips for Improving Language Switchers on Ecommerce Sites

## Summary

International customers often hesitate to shop on foreign-language sites even when they can read the language. To serve global audiences, ecommerce sites must provide language and country options. This article presents six evidence-based tips for designing effective language switchers based on research with Chinese and other international shoppers. The tips cover automatic language detection, placement of switchers, presentation methods, and flexibility in allowing users to change language, country, and currency independently.

## Key Takeaways

- **Default to users' browser language settings** to avoid requiring extra steps; if automatic detection is unreliable, ask users to select rather than guess incorrectly.
- **Place language switchers in top corners** on desktop (left or right) where users expect utility features to be located.
- **Mobile sites need language switchers above the fold or in navigation** since the top-right corner is occupied by account or cart links on small screens.
- **Display language names in their own language** (e.g., "Español" not "Spanish") because speakers will recognize their language's name.
- **Use multiple symbols together** (flag, currency symbol, language name) to make switchers more discoverable and recognizable.
- **Allow independent changes of language, country, and currency** so users can view information in one language while shopping from another country.
