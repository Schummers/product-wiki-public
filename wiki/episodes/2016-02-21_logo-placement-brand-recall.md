# Website Logo Placement for Maximum Brand Recall

## Summary

Logo placement in the top left corner is a longstanding web convention, based on research showing that left-to-right language readers attend to the left side first. Despite the appeal of breaking conventions to appear unique, a study of 128 users comparing left vs. right logo placement found that left-aligned logos produced 89% higher brand recall than right-aligned logos. Users did not perceive right-aligned logos as more stylish or unique; in fact, they rated nearly identically to left-aligned versions on subjective qualities. Breaking this convention risks losing users' attention without gaining differentiation benefits.

## Key Takeaways

- **Left logo placement significantly improves brand recall** — an average of 39% of users recalled the brand name with left-aligned logos vs. 21% with right-aligned logos; this 89% lift is among the largest effects seen in UX metrics research.
- **Unusual logo positions do not create perceived uniqueness** — users rated right-aligned and left-aligned logos identically on subjective dimensions like uniqueness, stylishness, welcoming nature, and purchase intent.
- **Web conventions are based on proven usability** — left placement aligns with how readers naturally scan pages and where they expect to find orientation landmarks.
- **Legibility matters more than position** — even position cannot overcome poor legibility; difficult-to-decipher logo script dramatically reduces brand recall regardless of placement.
- **People rely on patterns for sense-making** — users are creatures of habit who depend on familiar patterns; breaking conventions risks losing attention altogether.
- **Right-to-left script users may prefer mirrored layouts** — the research was conducted on left-to-right language readers; RTL users may benefit from right-aligned logos, though this was not tested.

## Quotes

> Placement definitely does matter, at least if you want users to actually remember your brand.

> People are attracted to novelty, but we're also creatures of habit who rely on patterns to make sense of the world. When you break these patterns, you risk losing people's attention altogether.

> People were significantly more likely to remember the name of a hotel when the logo had been positioned in the top left corner of a website, compared to in the top right corner.
