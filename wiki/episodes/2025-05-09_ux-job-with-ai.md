# Why I'm Not Worried About My UX Job in the Era of AI

## Summary

This reflection on UX career resilience in the era of AI argues that UX and design methods are tools for building better products, not ends in themselves. The author traces 20 years of tool changes in UX research (from stopwatches to remote testing platforms to AI transcription) to show that UX jobs have repeatedly adapted as tools evolved. AI is just another tool to evaluate against the core mission: building better products more efficiently. New tools typically prompt new research methods (as mobile prompted customer-journey mapping); designers will need methods for studying and evaluating AI-based interactions. The irreplaceable human skills are understanding people, asking right questions, and judging AI-generated outputs against that understanding.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX methods and tools have constantly changed** — from manual stopwatches to automated platforms, from in-person to remote testing, from manual notes to AI transcription; with each tool shift, UX jobs and techniques adapted; AI is the latest in a long succession of tool changes requiring adaptation.
- **AI is just another tool, not an existential threat** — evaluate AI against the core mission: building better products efficiently; if AI can find 85% of usability issues with fewer resources than human testing, use it; the question is not whether AI replaces UX but how it augments the work.
- **New technologies prompt new research methods** — customer-journey mapping emerged with mobile; similarly, generative UIs and AI-based interactions require new methods to evaluate whether they're good; designers will need to compare new methods to established approaches and validate their reliability.
- **Irreplaceable human skills focus on problem-framing and judgment** — someone must define problems clearly enough for AI to address them, ask right questions, judge whether AI outputs are good, and decide which issues are most critical; these skills will be necessary regardless of tool changes.
- **Deep understanding of human behavior matters more than specific methods** — individual skills like conducting usability tests may be automated; but skills for understanding people, asking right questions, and applying analytical thinking carry through all changes and will continue to be valuable.

## Quotes

> UX and design methods are just tools for building better products.

> With each change in available tools, our jobs and techniques had to adapt accordingly.

> If your focus is on understanding people and designing usable interfaces, there will always be work for you.
