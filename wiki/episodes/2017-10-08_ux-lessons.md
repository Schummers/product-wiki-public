# UX Lessons I Wish I Learned in School

## Summary

A reflection from a 20-year UX career about lessons learned that academic training did not provide. The author emphasizes that good user experience is about people, not technology, and highlights the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical success in influencing organizational change. Key lessons address iteration over perfection, balancing user needs with business goals, failing early through testing, advocating for UX leadership, earning trust incrementally, and building team empathy across disciplines.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pragmatism over perfection** — there is no perfect user interface; embrace incremental improvement and iterative refinement rather than trying to solve all problems at once.
- **Balance user and business needs** — focusing exclusively on optimal user experience while ignoring business goals leads to failure; design recommendations must balance both to keep organizations viable.
- **Fail early and together** — testing too late in the process leaves no time for adjustments; establish a regular testing cadence and involve team members in research to build buy-in.
- **Proactive leadership** — move beyond being reactive to design requests; shape product vision, influence strategic discussions, and use data to evangelize UX across the organization.
- **Earn trust incrementally** — depending on organizational UX maturity, systemic change takes years; demonstrate value with small, visible wins and let teams experience new approaches firsthand.
- **Empathy for all stakeholders** — show empathy toward colleagues and team members, not just users; understand different perspectives, resolve conflicts through compromise, and collaborate to build cohesive teams.
