# A Guide to Service-Blueprinting Workshops

## Summary

Service blueprints help organizations create shared understanding of experiences they provide. Creating blueprints collaboratively in workshops forces holistic thinking and informs project planning, maximizing impact. An effective service-blueprinting workshop requires extensive preparation (establishing core team, collecting research, creating agenda, selecting tools, setting roles), active facilitation during the workshop (introductions, research review, mapping key elements, tracking questions, playback, prioritizing fail points), and meaningful follow-up (acting on items, layering quantitative data, refining and sharing the blueprint).

## Key Takeaways

- **Before workshop: Prepare thoroughly** — Assemble interdisciplinary team of 4-6 stakeholders with power to act. Define specific scenario and customer perspective. Collect and synthesize internal and external research. Create realistic agenda with timing. Select tools (sticky notes and posters for in-person; familiar digital tools for virtual). Assign roles and responsibilities.
- **Workshop core activities** — Facilitate introductions and icebreakers. Allow time for research review (10-15 minutes of reading). Use diverge-and-converge technique for mapping key elements (10 minutes individual generation, then group discussion). Track questions using parking lot. Conduct brief playback of discussions.
- **Fail-point prioritization** — Visualize service holistically to identify service gaps, weaknesses, and pain points. Use dot voting to prioritize most important fail points. Document ideas for addressing fail points using new-color sticky notes or parking lot.
- **Expectations setting** — Set clear expectations that the workshop will be interactive, all members will contribute, mapping covers most important interactions (not comprehensive list), knowledge gaps are expected, and blueprint will not leave workshop complete or polished.
- **After workshop: Refine and share** — Follow up on action items and answer questions. Layer in quantitative data supporting qualitative findings. Clean up blueprint for shareability. Add flow lines, emotional indicators, clear language. Test with someone unfamiliar to ensure clarity.
- **Facilitation ratio** — Maintain 1 facilitator per 12 attendees. For virtual workshops, assign backup facilitator for technical issues.

## Concepts

- [[Service Design]] — Service blueprinting workshops create holistic understanding of experiences from both customer and service-delivery perspectives, revealing how all touchpoints and backend processes work together.
- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — Effective facilitation requires pre-workshop preparation, active guidance during the workshop using structured techniques, and post-workshop follow-up to refine and implement findings.
- [[Customer Journey]] — Service blueprints map customer actions and emotions alongside organizational actions and support processes, revealing fail points and opportunities across entire customer journeys.
