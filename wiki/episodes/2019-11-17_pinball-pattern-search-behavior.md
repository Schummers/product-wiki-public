# Complex Search-Results Pages Change Search Behavior: The Pinball Pattern

## Summary

Modern search engine results pages (SERPs) have evolved from simple lists of 10 blue links to complex layouts featuring knowledge panels, featured snippets, images, videos, and interactive elements. This visual complexity has fundamentally changed how users scan results, shifting from linear sequential scanning to a nonlinear "pinball pattern" where attention bounces between various salient elements. A meta-analysis of 471 queries from 2017-2019 eyetracking studies reveals that the first position now receives only 28% of clicks compared to 51% in 2006, with wider distribution of attention across lower-ranked results.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pinball pattern defined** — users scan SERPs nonlinearly, bouncing around between visual elements and keywords rather than moving sequentially down the list; driven by visual weight and distribution of compelling elements.
- **SERP features get attention** — featured snippets, knowledge panels, and interactive elements received looks in 74% of cases; visual complexity redistributes attention away from top organic results.
- **Lower positions receive more visibility** — while first position declined from 51% to 28% of clicks, the 6th position now receives looks in 36% of cases; 10-20% click chance for positions 1-5, 40-80% chance of receiving a look.
- **Task type affects search behavior** — for simple fact-finding tasks, users focus heavily on top results; for complex research-based tasks, users willing to examine lower results, with 20% of selections below the fold.
- **First page still dominates** — despite changes, users only clicked past page one in 2% of queries; appearing on first SERP page remains critical for visibility.
- **Glances build awareness** — even without clicks, seeing your site name provides awareness and increases familiarity, bolstering chances for future searches.

## Quotes

> In a pinball pattern, the user scans a results page in a highly nonlinear path, bouncing around between results and SERP features.

> The continuously evolving layout of the SERP is shaping how people search. Each new feature affects the distribution of users' attention on the page.

> users spent an average of 5.7 seconds considering results before they made their first selection (with a 95% confidence interval from 4.9 to 6.5 seconds).
