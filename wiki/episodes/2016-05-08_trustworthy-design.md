# Trustworthiness in Web Design: 4 Credibility Factors

## Summary

Four factors that influenced trustworthiness in 1999 continue to influence users today: design quality, upfront disclosure, comprehensive and current content, and connection to the rest of the web. Research across Western and Asian cultures found identical basic factors, demonstrating the durability of usability guidelines. While design trends and patterns change, human behavior remains stable. Users perceive trust through visual design quality, clear navigation with meaningful labels, absence of errors, and appropriate color and imagery. Upfront disclosure builds transparency; comprehensive content demonstrates expertise; connection to external sources (reviews, social media) provides credibility through third-party validation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design quality signals competence** — Well-organized navigation, appropriate color schemes, quality imagery, and absence of typos/broken links all communicate professionalism and trustworthiness.
- **Site organization and labels** — Meaningful, unambiguous navigation labels indicate the company understands user needs and mental models; unclear labels frustrate users and damage trust.
- **Visual design sets expectations** — Color schemes affect perceived value (corporate, budget, luxury); young adults perceive flat design as more professional; adequate white space indicates organization.
- **Upfront disclosure builds transparency** — Display contact information prominently, document base costs, state additional fees and charges, link to policies; omitting information signals the company has something to hide.
- **Avoid login walls and gated content** — Asking for personal information before providing value breaches trust; even if technically providing value, prominent signup forms create perception of gates, degrading trust.
- **Comprehensive, correct content** — Thorough, relevant content demonstrates expertise and commitment; display services across full range with supporting evidence (photos, testimonials, data); avoid appearing to cater only to premium segments.
- **Connection to external sources** — Users trust third-party sources (review sites, social media, news outlets) more than company-sponsored content; provide links to external reviews rather than relying on in-house testimonials.
- **Avoid gaps in representation** — Missing content about certain offerings or customer segments suggests the company undervalues those lines of business, damaging credibility.

## Quotes

> Basic factors used to weigh site trustworthiness were the same, regardless of location and culture.

> Although design patterns and trends change over time, human behavior does not. Users' priorities and methods of evaluation are the same today as they were 17 years ago, even though the web itself has vastly evolved.

> Due to the sheer amount of social media and review sites available, people have learned to trust these external sources more than company-sponsored content.
