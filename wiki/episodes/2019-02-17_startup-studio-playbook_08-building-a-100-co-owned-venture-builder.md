# Startup Studio Playbook: Building a 100% co-owned venture builder

## Summary

This chapter documents Lab.Coop's transformation from a failing venture (Brickflow) into a thriving co-owned venture builder. When Brickflow—a content recommendation startup—ran out of funding despite growth, the team chose to keep working together rather than disperse. The founders, Peter Langmár and Tamás, proposed founding Lab.Coop, a new entity that would start as a development agency to generate cash flow, then gradually shift toward building portfolio companies. The chapter reveals how Lab.Coop solved the core challenge facing many bootstrapped studios: how to retain and motivate exceptional talent without VC funding. The answer: radical transparency through co-ownership, holacracy-based governance, and a peer-driven compensation system (Badge-Based Compensation Scheme) that aligns incentives and distributes authority across the team rather than concentrating it in founders' hands.

## Key Takeaways

- **Co-ownership closes the motivation gap** — when cash is scarce, traditional employee compensation (fixed salary) cannot compete with VC-backed competitors; Lab.Coop solved this by making every team member a co-owner with equity that grows based on tenure, complexity of work, and skill; this financial alignment, combined with influence over high-level decisions, motivated developers to join a fragile startup instead of established companies.

- **Holacracy enables self-management at scale** — Lab.Coop built an organizational "operating system" based on holacracy, distributing authority across team members rather than concentrating power in a hierarchy; transparent rules, peer-to-peer tension resolution, and role-based domains replace traditional management; this structure reduced the usual founder overhead of direct supervision while keeping accountability clear.

- **Transparent, formula-based salaries resolve wage disputes** — compensation is derived from transparent formulas (inspired by Buffer and similar companies) that regulate salary, common stock, and preferred stock, removing the need for opaque individual negotiations; the Badge-Based Compensation Scheme allows peers to recognize skills, propose new badges for emerging competencies, and map badge sets to compensation tiers, creating a bottom-up system for wage evolution.

- **Starting as an agency buys runway and attracts talent** — when VC was unavailable, Lab.Coop accepted client services work (Chain.Reaction agency), using revenue to fund internal projects and co-founded ventures (Green Fox Academy coding school, SmartWare.tech hardware accelerator); this de-risked the venture-building business and proved the business model before scaling; the hybrid model turned a negative (no VC money) into a strength (lower cash burn, real revenue).

- **Trust and transparency replace long contracts** — because Lab.Coop was founded on trust and transparency (founders disclosed all operations, decisions were peer-driven), the team did not need lengthy founder-employee agreements; everyone understood they were taking equal risks and had equal voice; this cultural foundation made co-ownership and holacracy workable rather than chaotic.

## Concepts

- [[Equity Ownership]] — Lab.Coop distributes equity continuously based on a formula tracking tenure, work complexity, and peer-recognized skills (Badge-Based Compensation Scheme); equity is not a one-time grant but evolves through a peer-driven system where any partner can propose badge valuations based on internal or market wage tensions; this approach aligns financial incentives across the entire team and eliminates the motivation gap between founders and employees that plagues startups without VC.

- [[Studio Culture]] — Lab.Coop's culture is built on foundational trust and radical transparency: all operations are disclosed, decisions are peer-driven through holacracy, and authority is distributed rather than hierarchical; this cultural foundation enables co-ownership and self-management to work without chaos; newcomers require education, but self-selection (attracted by the model) and peer pressure (tight-knit team) maintain culture as the organization scales from 7 to 35 people.

- [[Team Scaling]] — as Lab.Coop grew from 7 to 35 people (including portfolio companies), the organizational operating system based on holacracy allowed authority to scale without creating a bloated management layer; domains are split among team members, anyone can propose rule changes, and only the domain owner implements changes they judge beneficial; this peer-to-peer model distributes decision-making and prevents founder bottlenecks.

- [[Venture Studio]] — Lab.Coop operates a hybrid model: agency work (Chain.Reaction) generates cash flow, while internal projects and co-founded ventures (Green Fox Academy, SmartWare.tech) explore the studio vision; this structure de-risks venture building by proving the business model before scaling; the founders accept that startups fail, so building revenue-generating businesses first (school, accelerator) provides a stable base.

- [[Studio Structure]] — holacracy replaces traditional hierarchy with transparent rules and peer-to-peer tension resolution; CEO power is balanced by the organizational constitution; compensation is driven by transparent formulas and badge proposals; this structure keeps the organization responsive and prevents the usual founder-employee disconnect, enabling co-ownership to function as a motivational force.
