# Writing an Effective Guide for a UX Interview

## Summary

Semistructured interviews are a core discovery method in user research, combining a predefined structure with the flexibility to follow up on unexpected responses. An interview guide is essential to conducting these interviews effectively, helping researchers avoid leading questions and ensure consistent coverage of research topics across participants. Without one, interviews risk compromising data validity. This article provides seven practical steps for constructing an effective interview guide, from defining research questions to pilot testing the guide with real participants before fieldwork begins.

## Key Takeaways

- **Structure enables flexibility** — An interview guide allows interviewers to ask questions in any order and omit or add questions as needed, unlike a rigid script, while still maintaining focus on research objectives.
- **Start with research questions** — Before writing interview questions, clarify what you want to learn from users; these research questions shape the direction of the entire interview guide.
- **Broaden closed questions into open-ended ones** — Converting yes/no questions into broader, narrative prompts like "Tell me about..." encourages participants to share stories and unanticipated details rather than constrained responses.
- **Prepare probing and follow-up questions** — Include follow-up questions for each main question to gather additional detail and clarification, such as "Where were you when this happened?" or "Tell me why you did that?"
- **Arrange questions logically** — Order questions in a way that builds rapport and follows a natural narrative; warmup questions should come first, and reflective questions later to avoid overwhelming participants early.
- **Pilot before fieldwork** — Testing the guide with one or two participants reveals which questions need rewording, whether timing is realistic, and whether the question order works, allowing for fixes before data collection begins.
