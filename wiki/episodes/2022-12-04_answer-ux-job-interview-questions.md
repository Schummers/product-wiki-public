# How to Answer UX Job Interview Questions

## Summary

UX job interviews commonly use behavioral and situational questions to evaluate candidates. Behavioral questions ask candidates to discuss past experiences, grounded in the principle of behavioral consistency — how people acted in the past predicts future performance. Situational questions present hypothetical scenarios to test problem-solving ability and job knowledge. The article provides frameworks (STAR and METEOR) to structure compelling answers, along with strategies for practicing interview skills through mock interviews and storytelling techniques.

## Key Takeaways

- **Behavioral consistency principle** — Past behavior is a reliable predictor of future job performance, making behavioral and situational questions effective hiring tools used by 80% and 63% of UX teams respectively.
- **STAR framework** — Structure behavioral answers with Situation, Tasks, Actions, and Results, keeping each section brief, specific, and quantifiable to demonstrate concrete accomplishments.
- **METEOR framework** — Answer situational questions by Musing (taking time to think), Enquiring (asking clarifying questions), Theorizing (speculating based on facts), Endeavoring (describing your approach), describing Outcomes, and providing a Recap.
- **Self-awareness over perfection** — Candidates should share experiences where they stumbled and learned, demonstrating self-awareness and growth, which managers value more than unrealistic perfection.
- **Storytelling matters** — Research shows candidates who answer with stories receive better hiring recommendations, but most candidates use stories only 23% of the time; mock interviews with practice help build this skill.
- **Preparation through practice** — Mock interviews with trusted colleagues, recorded and transcribed, followed by restructuring answers to fit STAR/METEOR frameworks, significantly improve interview performance and future recall.

- [[Situational Interviewing]] — Situational questions present hypothetical scenarios to evaluate candidates' problem-solving ability, job knowledge, and how they align with organizational expectations.
