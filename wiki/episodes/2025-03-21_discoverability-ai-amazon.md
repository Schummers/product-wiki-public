# Discoverability of AI Features: Learn from Amazon's Mistakes

## Summary

AI features, even when valuable, often fail because users don't discover them. Discoverability—users noticing features without prior awareness—differs from findability (being able to find features when looking). Qualitative testing at Amazon identified severe discoverability issues across three AI features: Rufus chat (hidden in nonstandard location with unfamiliar label and ambiguous icon), search-bar AI prompts (invisible amid regular suggestions, violating mental models of search), and product Q&A chat (buried at page bottom amid information density). Once directed to features, users found them valuable but admitted they would never have discovered them independently. Successful AI feature discovery requires standard placement, plain language, and careful mental-model alignment.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI features lack discoverability even when placed visibly** — Users don't expect AI features and often miss them despite prominent placement; participants who found features valuable after guidance had never noticed or considered using them independently.
- **Nonstandard placement violates mental models and expectations** — Rufus button in upper left violated chat-placement expectations (lower right); search-prompt features violated mental models where search finds products, not answers.
- **Unfamiliar labels and ambiguous icons create confusion** — The name "Rufus" meant nothing to users; the sparkles-plus-chat-bubbles icon was not understood; standard design patterns and plain language would improve discoverability.
- **Information density obscures even well-placed features** — Amazon's busy product pages made embedded AI Q&A invisible at page bottom; contrast this with TripAdvisor's visually separated AI assistant section, which was immediately discoverable.
- **Mental models change slowly, but generational shifts are visible** — Younger users (Gen Z in the study) intuitively looked for AI near search bars, suggesting that repeated exposure gradually shifts expectations; standardization across multiple products accelerates mental-model evolution.
- **Research is essential to identify discoverability problems** — Exploratory research confirms feature value and optimal placement; evaluative usability testing reveals discoverability issues before launch, preventing costly post-deployment failures.

## Quotes

> When a feature has good findability, users can easily find it if they look for it.

> When a feature has good discoverability, users notice, recognize, and understand it even when they were not previously aware of its existence.

> Mental models are rigid but not unchangeable. As AI-powered search features become more prevalent, users may gradually expect AI chat or prompt suggestions to be integrated into search.
