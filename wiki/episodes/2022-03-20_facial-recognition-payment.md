# Revisiting Facial-Recognition Payment: Old Problems Still Lingering

## Summary

This article reports on a 2022 study of facial-recognition payment (FRP) in Beijing, comparing findings with a 2019 study. Despite three years of technological advancement and increased adoption (495 million Chinese users in 2021), the same usability problems persist. Users appreciate the speed of FRP but remain concerned about security. The study identifies key issues, including poor mental models of how the technology works, confusing and inconsistent secondary authentication, missing payment information on confirmation screens, and lack of maximum-payment-limit disclosure. The research demonstrates how erroneous user mental models can survive even when the technology improves, if the design doesn't actively help users construct accurate models.

## Key Takeaways

- **Users prefer speed and convenience but distrust security** — even experienced users worry about unauthorized charges despite the technology's actual security measures.
- **Incorrect mental models persist when not actively corrected** — users believed FRP compared faces to stored photos, not realizing it worked via feature recognition that handled partial obscurement like masks.
- **Inconsistent secondary validation confuses users** — when the secondary-authentication step (entering phone number) appeared sometimes but not others, users questioned whether payment completed.
- **Payment confirmation screens lack transparency** — users should see the exact amount paid, which account was charged, and the maximum payment limit.
- **Better onboarding can increase user acceptance** — brief explanations alongside advertising (e.g., via QR code) help users understand technology capabilities and alleviate security concerns.
- **Maintenance of interaction consistency is critical** — when systems behave differently, explanations must accompany the variance or users lose confidence.
