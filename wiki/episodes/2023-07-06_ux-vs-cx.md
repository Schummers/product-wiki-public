# User Experience vs. Customer Experience: What's The Difference?

## Summary

While the terms UX and CX are essentially equivalent when properly interpreted, they emerged from different eras and focus on different scopes of experience. The article distinguishes three levels of experience design: interaction level (single user-product interaction), journey level (end-to-end process using multiple channels to complete a goal), and relationship level (lifetime experience across all interactions). Organizations often fail when designing interactions in isolation without considering how they connect into journeys or relationships. Both terminology frameworks are valuable if teams use them consistently and understand that excellent experience requires attention to all three levels.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three experience scopes require different design approaches** — interaction level focuses on single-task completion using specific design patterns, journey level requires cross-channel consistency and seamless transitions, and relationship level demands organizational alignment and anticipating customer needs over time.
- **Terminology matters less than shared understanding** — whether teams use "UX" or "CX" is less important than agreeing on terminology and understanding the different scopes of experience they're designing for.
- **Isolated interaction design creates journey-level failures** — even flawless single interactions break down when woven into realistic journeys; for example, a claims workflow might function perfectly in testing but conflict with confirmation-email messaging.
- **Poor interactions can eliminate customers despite good strategy** — relationship-level vision requires flawless execution at the interaction level; confusing UI copy or rude support interactions destroy customer relationships despite grand strategic plans.
- **All three levels are equally important** — the article explicitly rejects hierarchies suggesting one level matters more than others; each requires different expertise and attention.

## Quotes

> Whether you use the newer term “customer experience” or prefer the older “user experience,” the point to remember is: there are multiple levels of experience and each is equally important in delivering a good experience to your users.

> You and your team use these terms consistently so that you minimize friction and misunderstanding

Designing the experience should not only take place at the interaction level.

> While it is important to design the longitudinal experience, it’s equally important to design its components.

## Concepts

- [[Customer Experience]] — the broadest scope of experience encompassing all interactions and journeys across a customer's lifetime relationship with an organization.
- [[Experience Design Scope]] — three levels (interaction, journey, relationship) represent different temporal and breadth scopes requiring distinct design approaches and expertise.
- [[Journey Mapping]] — understanding multi-channel journeys requires mapping and optimizing the end-to-end process customers follow to accomplish goals.
