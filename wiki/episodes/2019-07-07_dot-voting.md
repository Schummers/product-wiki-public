# Dot Voting: A Simple Decision-Making and Prioritizing Technique in UX

## Summary

Dot voting is a democratic, straightforward method for prioritizing items and making group decisions. Participants receive a fixed number of "dot" tokens to place on alternatives, with the distribution revealing group preference as a heat map. The process is simple: specify voting constraints and purpose, participants vote quietly without lobbying, outcomes are calculated, and ties are broken via revoting if needed. Dot voting prevents the HIPPO (highest-paid person's opinion) effect and enables equal participation. Adaptations address weaknesses like group think and persuasion: giving voters research time, making voting digital or anonymous, dictating voting order, ranking dots for weighted votes, and color-coding dots for different criteria. Dot voting is especially useful when decisions need quick convergence, discussion involves friction, diverse perspectives are needed but underheard, or large option sets must be narrowed.

## Key Takeaways

- **Democratic and Efficient** — dot voting gives all participants equal voice and prevents HIPPO bias; it enables quick convergence and creates a shared artifact (the vote distribution) for communication.
- **Customizable Constraints Prevent Pitfalls** — give voters research time beforehand, use digital voting to hide influence, dictate voting order (junior first), rank dots for weighted votes, and color-code dots by criteria to refine the method for your context.
- **Narrow Large Sets Quickly** — use dot voting to focus discussion on prioritized options within large sets, assess insights to explore further, rank design ideas, or gather information before prioritization matrices.
- **Tie-Breaking Requires Revoting** — if top options tie, reissue votes to participants but restrict voting to only the tied top candidates for quick resolution.

## Concepts

- [[Dot Voting]] — a participatory decision-making technique using tokens to democratically rank alternatives; prevents bias, enables equal voice, and creates a visual heat map of group consensus.
- [[Decision Making]] — dot voting supports group consensus by making voting visible and equal; adaptations like digital voting, research time, and voting order further improve decision quality and reduce groupthink.
- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — dot voting is a scalable, flexible workshop technique adaptable to many contexts; variations (ranking, color-coding, order manipulation) allow facilitators to tailor the method to specific group dynamics and goals.
