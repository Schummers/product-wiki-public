# Solving Product Design Exercises: Justin Maxwell: advice for designers who want to become founders

## Summary

This chapter features Justin Maxwell, Chief Design Officer of Smith.ai and former designer at Apple, Google, and Sony, who also founded Mint.com. He provides advice for employee designers aspiring to start their own companies, organizing skills into three categories: discipline, people skills, and big-picture thinking. Maxwell emphasizes that aspiring founders should practice building organizational discipline through habits, develop people skills including negotiation and communication, and cultivate big-picture thinking grounded in user need and principled business decisions rather than cosmetic appeal—all while learning to embrace the chaos of entrepreneurship.

## Key Takeaways

- **Discipline as Organizational Alignment** — discipline is foundational for founders; practice building habits through B.J. Fogg's "Tiny Habits," and develop the ability to align organizations to constraints and objectives independently, covering prioritization, organization, and focus.
- **People Skills: Facilitation Through Others** — negotiation is the most frequently used skill in founder life; develop active listening, critical inquiry, hiring and interviewing, sales, and pitching; unreliable communication is an immediate liability.
- **Big-Picture Thinking Beats Cosmetics** — product design must satisfy user need before cosmetic appeal, and company leaders must choose words carefully, spend dollars wisely, triage product decisions deliberately, and seek opportunities aligned with stated goals.
- **Learning Through Influence** — while an employee, practice negotiation (Stuart Diamond's "Getting More"), presentation and storytelling to align vision, and build resilience from sales rejection and hiring processes to drop ego in future interactions.
- **Embrace Chaos** — founders must get comfortable with operating in chaotic conditions; perfect adherence to principles is unrealistic.

## Quotes

> There are 1,000 things, but ultimately they roll up to three main categories: discipline, people skills, and big-picture thinking.

> Big-picture thinking as a company leader means you choose words carefully, spend dollars wisely, triage product decisions deliberately, and seek opportunities that get you closer to the goals you have set.

> get comfortable with chaos.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — Designers preparing to found companies should develop discipline, people skills (negotiation, communication, hiring), and big-picture thinking while employees, building resilience and organizational habits before autonomy.
- [[Business Impact]] — Founders must align organizational decisions to explicit OKRs, constraints, and objectives; cosmetic design appeal is secondary to understanding user need and principled business strategy.
- [[Product Thinking]] — Big-picture thinking requires focusing product decisions on satisfying user need first, triage based on stated goals, and avoiding the distraction of shiny features or trends.
- [[Design Hiring]] — Active listening, critical inquiry, and hiring and interviewing skills are learnable and strengthened through practice in employee roles; rejection and hiring failures build the resilience needed for founder life.
