# AI Features Must Solve Real User Problems

## Summary

This article addresses a critical UX principle that remains true in the age of generative AI: products, features, and services must solve users' actual problems. Many companies have rushed into implementing AI features without considering whether they genuinely address user needs, resulting in useless AI chatbots and poorly thought-out features. The article uses historical parallels (Flash plugins) and contemporary examples (LinkedIn's AI-generated questions, Instagram's AI chat integrated into search) to illustrate the dangers of implementing technology for its own sake rather than as a solution to real user problems.

## Key Takeaways

- **Technology for its own sake fails** — Implementing cool new technology without considering whether it solves user problems rarely works well; examples include Flash plugins that were widely used despite poor usability, and recently LinkedIn's AI-powered questions and Instagram's AI chat.
- **AI has incredible potential** — AI can solve genuine user pain points, as demonstrated by well-designed features like Adobe Lightroom's AI object-removal tool, which makes previously difficult or time-consuming tasks accessible to average users.
- **Communicating AI value is critical** — Designers must contextualize AI features so they make sense to users; technical jargon and feature descriptions don't compel users, but benefit-focused language does.
- **Lead with benefits, follow with features** — When promoting AI features, start with how they will improve users' lives rather than technical specifications like token context windows.
- **AI requires thoughtful design** — Generative AI is not magic; it doesn't eliminate the need for research, user-centered design, and deep understanding of users' needs and mental models.
- **One bad AI experience discourages future adoption** — A single negative experience with an AI feature can make users avoid trying them again, so designers must ensure features are thoughtfully designed and ready before launch.

## Quotes

> While it's substantially changing what and how we design, some things stay the same. One important UX principle is still true: **Whether they're AI-driven or not, products, features, and services must solve users' problems .**

> We haven't learned our lesson about chasing after shiny new technology. Today, we're see lots of companies that rushed into implementing useless AI features and chatbots.

> The only thing worse than an unnecessary AI chat is an unnecessary AI chat that blocks a useful feature like search.
