# How to Run Surveys at Every Stage of the Design Cycle

## Summary

Surveys are valuable research tools but are frequently misused by practitioners who assume they are quick and easy. Where you are in the design cycle determines which survey types are most appropriate. The design cycle has four phases (Discover, Explore, Test, and Listen), and different survey types serve distinct purposes at each stage. Discover surveys uncover user insights; Explore surveys inform design direction through competitor analysis and statistical personas; Test surveys measure perceived usability; Listen surveys track sentiment and problems. Strategic survey selection based on research goals is essential for effective user research.

## Key Takeaways

- **Discovery surveys and diary-study surveys** gather background information and longitudinal user behaviors during the Discover phase, often using qualitative approaches to uncover insights.
- **Competitor surveys and statistical-persona surveys** during Explore phase inform design decisions by understanding competitor strengths/weaknesses and quantifying user segments statistically.
- **Post-task and post-test surveys** (SEQ and SUS) during Test phase measure usability of prototypes, with established benchmarks enabling meaningful interpretation of results.
- **NPS, CSAT, and CES during Listen phase** monitor user sentiment and experience to detect problems early, though NPS alone should never be the only usability metric.
- **Custom listening surveys** deployed at key moments through intercept popups can track metrics longitudinally to assess design changes and catch emerging problems.
