# Solving Product Design Exercises: Answer structure

## Summary

This chapter introduces the 5W1H framework for solving product design exercises. It establishes that solving a design exercise mirrors the real product-building process, divided into understanding what you're building, coming up with a solution, and measuring success. The 5W1H technique (Five Ws and How) provides a structured approach: Why (goal), Who (audience), When and Where (context), What (product), and How (measurement).

## Key Takeaways

- **5W1H Framework** — Dashinsky bases his design exercise framework on the well-established 5W1H technique used in journalism, research, and product building, with six key questions that must be answered to solve an exercise.
- **Process Demonstration** — The goal during an interview exercise is to demonstrate how you answer these six questions and follow a structured problem-solving process.
- **Variable Task Inputs** — Design exercise prompts already include some 5W1H elements (Who, What, Why) but typically omit How (measurement); more guidance in the prompt means more solution development time.
- **Question-Answer Mapping** — The task itself provides partial answers to some 5W1H questions, and the candidate must identify which questions are answered and which remain to be explored.

## Quotes

> Solving a design exercise is basically designing a product in an extremely limited time and presenting your thinking process and the result.

> 5W1H is a technique used in journalism, research and police investigations for information gathering and problem solving. It is also used in product building: Whitney Hess relies on it during UX research,[11] Lewis Lin used it in his CIRCLES Method™[12] to help product managers to prepare for interviews.

> Your job during the interview exercise is to demonstrate how you answer these questions and follow a process.
