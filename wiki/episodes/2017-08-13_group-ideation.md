# Troubleshooting Group Ideation: 10 Fixes for More and Better UX Ideas

## Summary

Group ideation can be a powerful tool for generating diverse ideas and building team buy-in, yet it often fails due to common group-dynamics problems. This article identifies the primary pitfalls that derail group ideation sessions—including groupthink, unequal contributions, hierarchy intimidation, and distractions—and provides practical fixes for each. The author emphasizes that while individual ideation has value, excluding group work means teams miss opportunities for diverse perspectives and collaborative problem-solving.

## Key Takeaways

- **66% of UX professionals believe group ideation produces higher-quality ideas** — yet group sessions often fail because of group dynamics, not because the method itself is flawed.
- **Groupthink causes teams to fixate on the first idea** — fix this by explicitly setting aside initial suggestions and generating a wider diversity of options before settling on solutions.
- **Unequal contributions exclude valuable perspectives** — emphasize quantity over quality, warm up participants, and create low-pressure environments where quieter team members feel safe contributing.
- **Hierarchy intimidation silences dissenting views** — consider excluding high-ranking members from ideation sessions, or hold pre-sessions where junior staff can share ideas in a safer space first.
- **Structured techniques improve outcomes** — use parallel design (solo ideation followed by group sharing), parking lots for off-topic ideas, and facilitators trained to manage group dynamics.

## Quotes

> 66% of UX professionals feel that working in groups is better for producing high-quality design ideas. In particular, the inclusion of a variety of job roles in the UX ideation process results in useful, diverse ideas, and in buy-in from teammates.

> Group ideation gets a bad reputation — not for any inherent faults it has as a creative approach, but because it so often goes wrong. It's messy, challenging, and sometimes emotional, but repairing your group ideation problems is worth the effort.

> When 100% of your ideation is being done alone, your team misses out on big opportunities.
