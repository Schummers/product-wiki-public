# Different Information-Seeking Tasks: Behavior Patterns and User Expectations

## Summary

Research on 498 critical incidents and 471 search instances identified three distinct online information-seeking behaviors: Acquire (finding facts), Compare/Choose (evaluating options), and Understand (learning about topics). These tasks have different characteristics: Acquire tasks are less memorable and shorter, Compare/Choose and Understand tasks require more effort and are better remembered. User expectations differ significantly by task type: Acquire users want fast, direct answers in simple language; Compare/Choose users want comprehensive information from multiple perspectives with comparison tools; Understand users want organized, centralized content with fewer ads. Supporting complex tasks effectively is crucial because users form stronger impressions from research-intensive experiences than from simple fact-finding tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three distinct information-seeking behaviors exist with different characteristics** — Acquire (facts), Compare/Choose (evaluation), and Understand (learning) each require different search patterns, cognitive effort, and satisfaction levels.
- **Acquire tasks are less memorable** — 57% of Acquire incidents were reported within one week compared to 33% for other types, suggesting simple fact-finding is less cognitively demanding and less memorable.
- **Research tasks receive more user effort** — people spend significantly more time on search-engine result pages before clicking for Research (Compare/Choose and Understand) tasks than for Acquire tasks (p<0.01).
- **Acquire users prioritize speed and directness** — they want fast processes, few clicks, direct answers, and plain language, making featured snippets and knowledge panels especially valuable.
- **Compare/Choose users need multiple perspectives and comparison tools** — they want comprehensive information from multiple sources, key information upfront, and comparison tables to evaluate options.
- **Understand users value organization and fewer distractions** — they want clear, well-organized content with bullet points and subheadings, centralized information in one place, and ads in expected locations.

## Quotes
