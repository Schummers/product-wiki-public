# Exploring the Boundaries of Unmanned Restaurant Services: Irreplaceable Human Services Are Still Needed

## Summary

This case study of an unmanned restaurant in Beijing, combined with research on facial-recognition payments in China, examines the promise and limitations of fully automated restaurant services. While unmanned systems offer convenience and time savings, they create new problems: insufficient customization, lack of multi-user support, kitchen synchronization failures, limited payment methods, and forced subscriptions. Despite automation, human help remains irreplaceable for accessibility assistance, personalized questions, and the social warmth of formal dining experiences. The article concludes that unmanned restaurants are not yet truly unmanned.

## Key Takeaways

- **System design must sync with operations** — Failures in ordering (out-of-stock items) create extra waiter interactions instead of reducing them; thoughtful service design ensures orders are correctly read and fulfilled.
- **Customization is essential** — Many systems cannot handle special requests like less sugar or salt; without customization options, customers must rely on waiters, defeating automation's purpose.
- **Party ordering remains unsolved** — Single QR codes for tables force groups to pass phones around or have one person order for everyone; only some restaurants solved this successfully.
- **Payment methods matter** — Systems supporting only one payment method (WeChat Pay) force users to call waiters; payment flexibility reduces unnecessary human intervention.
- **Accessibility cannot be automated** — Customers in wheelchairs, elderly users, and those with special needs require waiter assistance; robots cannot flexibly respond to diverse accessibility issues.
- **Human warmth is valuable** — Beyond efficiency, waiters provide personalized recommendations, formal atmosphere, and care that robots cannot replicate; dining is not purely transactional.
- **Force-subscribe is a deceptive pattern** — Requiring account subscriptions before ordering annoys users; providing great service creates natural subscription interest without coercion.

## Quotes

> Unmanned restaurants are popular not only in Beijing. According to a report published by Nayuki, a popular milk-tea chain with more than 800 stores all over China, in 2020, 71% of their in-store orders were placed via WeChat and Alipay mini programs.

> Still, less human help does not necessarily translate into a good user experience.

> If the waiter helps place an order, I would feel embarrassed when she is standing next to me. I might feel pressured to tell her what I want quickly. It will be more comfortable to scan a QR code and self-order.

## Concepts

- [[Service Design]] — Automating surface interactions without redesigning underlying operations (kitchen synchronization, inventory management) creates new problems instead of solving them.
- [[Mobile Design]] — QR code ordering via mobile apps enables automation but requires careful interface design to handle parties, customization, and payment flexibility.
- [[User Experience]] — Efficiency alone does not define good UX; accessibility, personalization, and human connection remain valuable and irreplaceable in service contexts.
