# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 11. Communicating Design (Communication)

## Summary

Part IV of this book shifts focus to acting on growth plans. This chapter teaches designers how to present designs effectively to diverse audiences—PMs, developers, organizational stakeholders, and external parties—each with different priorities and concerns. The chapter presents a flexible four-step presentation framework and emphasizes the importance of articulating design reasoning with robust evidence rather than relying on gut feeling or visual taste.

## Key Takeaways

- **Different stakeholders, different priorities** — PMs evaluate designs against business metrics, ease of development, and measurability; developers assess implementation complexity; stakeholders and executives have varied objectives. Presenting effectively requires understanding what matters to each audience.
- **Four-step presentation framework** — Explain why you're there and state your ask clearly; provide project context (problem, timeline, scope, readiness level, constraints, blindspots, competitive analysis); present the solution using the Five Ws and How; suggest next steps and reiterate your ask. Adapt this structure based on your audience and context.
- **Articulate reasoning with evidence** — Replace "I think" with "We know," "Data shows," or "According to research." Robust reasons include data and analytics, research, visual design principles, business objectives, technical feasibility, project context, and ethical or legal considerations. Different people respond to different types of evidence, so adjust your approach by audience.
- **5W1H framework for presenting solutions** — Adapted from the author's first book, Solving Product Design Exercises, and based on the Five Ws and How technique: why are we building this? Who are we building it for? When and where will it be used? What should we build? How can we measure it? This structured approach covers all essential information without overwhelming the audience.
- **Reasoning improves decision quality** — Articulating reasoning challenges your own decisions, builds trust with collaborators, and removes unnecessary feedback cycles by grounding decisions in evidence rather than preference.

## Quotes

> "During their studies, designers normally present their designs to other designers. When we begin working in a company, we're suddenly required to present to people without a design background and, more importantly, with completely different goals and objectives in mind."

> "This sort of reasoning is harder to disagree with but also, in most cases, it leads to better solutions."

> "Try to check if the, "I think/believe that …," can be replaced with, "We know that …," "Data shows that …," or, "According to research …""

## Concepts

- [[Design Presentation]] — Framework for presenting designs to multiple audiences with different priorities; structured approach using context, solution walkthrough, and next steps tailored by audience.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Understanding the distinct concerns of PMs (business metrics), developers (implementation complexity), internal stakeholders (progress), and external parties (investors, clients, vendors).
- [[Design Advocacy]] — Articulating design decisions with evidence-based reasoning drawn from data, research, business objectives, technical constraints, and ethical considerations rather than preference or visual taste.
