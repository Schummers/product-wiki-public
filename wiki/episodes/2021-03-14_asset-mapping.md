# Asset Mapping for Experience Consistency

## Summary

An asset map is a visual tool that catalogs all screens, emails, notifications, and other interface elements users encounter across channels when completing a workflow. Asset maps help teams identify inconsistencies in core functionality, workflows, data, tone of voice, and visual design across channels. While related to customer-journey maps, asset maps are more focused on the UI and visual presentation rather than user context and emotions. They are most useful for auditing smaller, well-defined workflows that fall within a single organization's control, making them ideal for teams seeking to improve consistency before or alongside implementing broader experience design.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prioritize functional consistency first** — Before worrying about look and feel, audit whether core functionality works the same across channels; users cannot accomplish goals if the functionality differs, regardless of visual consistency.
- **Workflow and process consistency matters** — While device capabilities may streamline certain steps, the overall process should remain consistent across channels so users experience no surprises when switching channels mid-task.
- **Asset maps complement journey maps** — Asset maps focus on what users see and interact with, while journey maps focus on user thoughts and feelings; combining both approaches provides complete understanding.
- **Use asset maps for smaller, defined workflows** — Asset maps work best for bounded tasks like purchasing a product or checking in for a flight, not sprawling journeys with many external information sources or actors.
- **Involve crossfunctional partners early** — Share the map with channel-dedicated teams and other departments to discuss inconsistencies before proposing solutions; this builds buy-in and breaks down organizational silos.
- **Prioritize high-impact inconsistencies** — Focus on fixing inconsistencies that provide the most value with the least effort; functional inconsistencies take longer to fix but matter most to users.

## Quotes

> An asset map provides a high-level, chronological method of displaying and organizing all the screens and elements users encounter across channels when completing a workflow or journey to assess its consistency.

> If core functionality must differ for business or strategic reasons from one channel to another, don't mislead users. Be upfront about it through content and help users recover.

> Asset maps can also expose unnecessary redundancies, such as multiple emails that could be consolidated into one, areas where users lack critical information, or disparate functionality from one channel to another.
