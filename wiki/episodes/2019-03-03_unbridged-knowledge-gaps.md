# Unbridged Knowledge Gaps Hurt UX

## Summary

This article examines how websites and applications often fail to support users' complex research and decision-making tasks due to insufficient information or information presented in unhelpful formats. A diary study revealed that 21% of online activities are research-based, yet users frequently struggle to complete these tasks because sites fail to anticipate knowledge gaps or support information synthesis. The article demonstrates how missing information derails tasks and provides examples of sites that effectively support information synthesis.

## Key Takeaways

- **Anticipate and fill user knowledge gaps** — Sites must understand what users don't know and provide the information needed to complete research tasks; missing information derails tasks or drives users elsewhere.
- **Provide information needed for decision-making** — Users need comprehensive context to make informed decisions; a seat selection interface should show views, not just seat maps.
- **Support information synthesis and comparison** — Rather than simply providing individual product details, sites should structure information to support comparing alternatives side-by-side with consistent attributes.
- **Use tools to reduce mental effort** — Calculators, interactive comparisons, and integrated tools that combine related information reduce cognitive load and external-memory requirements.
- **Create self-sufficient experiences** — Users prefer one-stop platforms where they can complete tasks without opening additional tabs or consulting external sources.
- **Connect related information visually** — Present information in formats that support scanning, comparison, and decision-making rather than long unformatted paragraphs.

## Quotes

> When users **struggled to complete their research tasks, it was often due to a website or application's failure** to support those activities **.**

> It is not enough just to put information on your site and let people deal with it on their own. That approach is a fundamental UX failure because it puts an unnecessary burden on users' organization, memory, research, and synthesis skills.

> Bring together the right information, in the right format, at the right time. Only then will you be the one-stop-shop users prefer and recommend to their friends.

## Concepts

- [[Information Architecture]] — Organizing and presenting information to support complex synthesis tasks and comparisons across multiple attributes or alternatives.
- [[Content Strategy]] — Identifying what users need to know for decision-making and providing sufficient information in well-organized, accessible formats.
- [[User Research]] — Observational research revealing gaps between what users need to know and what sites actually provide to support complex decision-making.
- [[Task Support]] — Design patterns and tools that reduce cognitive burden and external-memory requirements for research and comparison tasks.
