# Optimizing for Context in the Omnichannel User Experience

## Summary

Omnichannel experiences must optimize for context, balancing consistency with channel-specific design. Context comprises users' tasks, when and where they complete them, which devices they use at different journey stages, and each device's strengths. Desktop suits complex tasks and detailed content; mobile excels at microtasks and leverages location/camera; tablets emphasize media consumption. Successful omnichannel design maintains core consistency while optimizing experiences for each device's capabilities and use context.

## Key Takeaways

- **Context of use has four elements** — understand users' most important tasks, when/where they complete them, which devices they use at each journey stage, and each channel's strengths.
- **Desktop suits complex tasks and rich content** — large screens, keyboards, and mice enable multitasking, productivity, and detailed decision-making; content should be rich and scannable.
- **Mobile prioritizes microtasks and location context** — portable phones support quick information access, geolocation-based services, and urgent actions; content should be layered, consumable, and location-aware.
- **Tablet emphasizes media consumption and immersion** — large touch screens suit reading, videos, and activities with minimal text input; design should be immersive and accommodate wet/messy hands.
- **Leverage device-specific capabilities** — geolocation for store finders, camera for photo input, SMS for urgent alerts, Touch ID for biometric auth; integration of native features streamlines experiences.
- **Provide layered information on mobile** — collapse secondary details, prioritize gist first, use accordions; small screens and interrupted sessions demand condensed, scannable content.
- **Optimize vs. consistency is a balance** — core functionality should be consistent across channels, but presentation and available features should optimize for device and context; this compromise ensures learnability and usability.

## Quotes

> Users interact with organizations through many channels, including the web, email, mobile and tablet applications, kiosks, online chat, telephone, and by visiting physical locations. Regardless of the channel, people expect companies to provide a usable experience. For this reason, it's important to understand the channel's role in the customer journey and design for that channel's context while creating the omnichannel user experience (sometimes referred to as cross-channel user experience).

> In a world where customers use multiple channels and devices to interact with organizations, understanding each device's and channel's role in a customer's journey can help organizations incorporate helpful and usable context-specific elements necessary to create an exceptional user experience.

> The goal is to provide core consistency, but make compromises to optimize the experience where it is appropriate.
