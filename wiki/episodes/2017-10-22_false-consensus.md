# You Are Not the User: The False-Consensus Effect

## Summary

The false-consensus effect is a cognitive bias where people assume others share their beliefs and will make similar choices in given contexts. This bias is deeply ingrained in human nature and is particularly dangerous in design, where designers and developers assume users think like them. The article explains the psychological basis of this bias and argues that testing with real target users is the only way to overcome it, both for practitioners building products and for researchers interpreting study findings.

## Key Takeaways

- **False-consensus effect** — people overestimate how many others share their choices, values, and beliefs; they assume anyone who differs is rare, deviant, or unusual.
- **Availability bias** — humans generalize from the few examples they have (ourselves and maybe colleagues), a cognitive shortcut that is natural but often wrong for design decisions.
- **Testing is mandatory** — the only way to overcome this bias is to test with real target users, not colleagues or people like yourself; assumptions without user data are almost always wrong.
- **Researchers are vulnerable too** — UX researchers also fall prey to these biases when interpreting data; heuristics and prior knowledge can blind teams to what actual users experience.
- **Investigate, don't validate** — avoid using research to validate existing assumptions; instead investigate actual user behavior, especially when even slight doubt exists about design choices.
