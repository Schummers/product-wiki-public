# UX-Roadmapping Workshops: Agenda + Activities

## Summary

This article provides a detailed guide for conducting UX roadmapping workshops that create alignment around a team's future work and problems to solve. A UX roadmap must be created collaboratively to be effective; the workshop occurs during steps 3 and 4 of the six-step roadmapping process (creating themes and prioritizing themes), after pre-work and goal-setting. The article details a 3-hour workshop agenda covering introduction, readout, theme distillation, affinity diagramming, and prioritization, plus an alternative approach of holding two sequential workshops. Success depends on clear logistics, defined participant roles, realistic timelines, and ensuring all participants understand the workshop goals and expected outputs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Collaborative Creation Essential** — UX roadmaps must be created with key stakeholders and team members to be effective; a solo-created roadmap will likely remain unused.
- **Workshop Timing** — Conduct the workshop during steps 3 (creating themes) and 4 (prioritizing themes) of the roadmapping process; pre-work establishes goals and gathers inputs, post-work visualizes and shares the roadmap.
- **Workshop Duration** — Plan for 2 to 4 hours depending on scope; specialty roadmaps with small teams require less time, while high-level product roadmaps with more people require more time.
- **Pre-Workshop Preparation** — Success requires identifying purpose, choosing scope, gathering relevant research inputs, assigning roles, planning logistics, and setting clear expectations about workshop outcomes.
- **Theme Distillation Process** — Use small groups reading inputs, individual inventory creation, group convergence on low-fidelity themes, affinity diagramming across groups, and theme expansion with defined descriptors.
- **Structured Prioritization** — Establish 4-8 standardized, agreed-upon criteria (mix of user and business objectives), have members rate individually, average scores, and discuss results for alignment.
- **Two-Workshop Alternative** — When roadmapping is new to an organization or burden is high, hold a preliminary workshop (1.5 hours) for goal-setting and input collection, then a primary workshop (2.5 hours) for theme creation and prioritization.

## Quotes

> A UX roadmap is a strategic, living artifact that aligns, prioritizes, and communicates a UX team's future work and the problems it needs to solve.

> Roadmaps must be created collaboratively in order to be effective. Otherwise, regardless of how well-constructed the roadmap is, it may stay unused.

> The roadmap produced in the workshop does not need to be a high-fidelity — keep it low-fidelity in the workshop then make it high-fidelity after, to distribute.

## Concepts

- [[UX Roadmap]] — Strategic artifact aligning and prioritizing a team's future work; must be created collaboratively and updated regularly to remain useful.
- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — Conducting effective workshops requires careful agenda design, role assignment, clear logistics, and understanding of facilitation techniques like affinity diagramming.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Workshops build alignment by surfacing different perspectives, establishing shared criteria for decisions, and ensuring all voices are heard before prioritization.
- [[Prioritization]] — Structured prioritization using standardized criteria and scoring scales ensures objectivity and buy-in compared to unstructured debate.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Including diverse stakeholders (leadership, cross-functional peers, customer representatives) in workshops ensures their priorities are considered and they remain invested in the roadmap.
