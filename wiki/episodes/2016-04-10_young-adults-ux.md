# Young Adults/Millennials as Web Users (Ages 18–25)

## Summary

Based on comprehensive research across 7 countries involving 91 young adults and multiple methodologies (usability testing, naturalistic recordings, diary studies, surveys), this article challenges common stereotypes about Millennials as web users. Young adults exhibit distinct behavioral patterns and preferences compared to teenagers and older adults. They are digital natives comfortable with technology, but contrary to popular belief, they are error-prone and impulsive in their interactions. They are skeptical of information online, sensitive to tone, and use browser tabs intensively for page parking (information-seeking strategy) as well as parallel browsing. No significant international differences were found among young adult participants in different countries.

## Key Takeaways

- **Multitasking is linear, not simultaneous** — Young adults engage in multiple activities in parallel but perform them sequentially, often losing context and suffering efficiency losses from rapid context-switching.
- **Intensive browser-tab use** — Young adults rely heavily on page parking (opening multiple pages in tabs to support a single task) and parallel browsing (using one tab per task), more than older adults.
- **Confidence leads to error-proneness** — Young adults are extremely confident in their ability to navigate interfaces but are also error-prone, often clicking before thinking and rarely blaming themselves for usability issues.
- **Interactivity must serve a purpose** — Unlike teenagers, young adults only appreciate interactive features (games, quizzes) when they serve their current task; gratuitous interactivity feels insulting.
- **Skepticism and evidence demands** — Young adults are more skeptical of information online than teenagers, demand more evidence to support claims, and are sensitive to tone (dislike condescension or sites trying too hard to be cool).
- **Global consistency** — No major behavioral differences emerged across 7 countries studied, suggesting that design guidelines for young adults hold globally.

## Quotes

> Most of them are digital natives, meaning they grew up with access to digital communications technology. They are a critically important user group: many of them are studying for degrees, or beginning careers.

> Young adults are much more skeptical of the information presented on websites. They demand more evidence to support claims than teenagers do.

> Compared to older users, young adults tend to be extremely confident in their own ability to navigate digital interfaces, even when encountering radically new design patterns.
