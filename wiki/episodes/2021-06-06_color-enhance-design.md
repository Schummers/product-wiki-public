# Using Color to Enhance Your Design

## Summary

Color is a powerful design tool that sets brand tone, influences perception, draws attention, affects emotion, and impacts usability. The color wheel organizes primary (yellow, red, blue), secondary (orange, purple, green), and tertiary colors to show relationships. Color theory provides harmonies like analogous, complementary, triadic, split-complementary, and monochromatic combinations that work well together. While cultural color meanings exist (red for stop, green for go), they vary by culture and should be validated with user testing. Effective color palette creation involves choosing a harmony, limiting to three colors, following brand guidelines, applying the 60-30-10 rule for distribution, maintaining consistency, and testing with users to ensure legibility, accessibility, and intended usability effects.

## Key Takeaways

- **Color Harmonies Provide Templates** — Analogous, complementary, triadic, split-complementary, and monochromatic harmonies are building blocks for color palettes; start with two or three colors before experimenting with complex schemes.
- **Cultural Context Affects Color Meaning** — Color interpretation varies by culture (red means money in China but green in USA); color blindness affects perception; assume meanings won't work globally without user testing.
- **60-30-10 Distribution Rule** — Use dominant color in 60% of design, secondary in 30%, accent in 10% to create balance and prevent chaos; typically dominant and secondary are neutral with accent colors drawing attention.
- **Limit Palette to Three Colors** — Fewer colors reinforce visual hierarchy and reduce cognitive load; many colors compete for attention and create distraction similar to overwhelming product displays.
- **Consistency Across Interface** — Maintain consistent color use across screens (same color for calls to action everywhere, warnings always the same color) to help users understand color's meaning.
- **Test Color Effectiveness** — Conduct usability testing to verify colors work as intended; check legibility, accessibility, and contrast; gray buttons may appear disabled; colors can vibrate and become unreadable.
- **Iteration and Refinement** — Color palette design requires iteration; draw inspiration from existing palettes, experiment by switching colors in and out, and understand why certain palettes appeal to you.

## Quotes

> Color is one of the most important and influential tools a designer has. In designs, it can set the brand tone and influence its image, draw users' attention, affect their emotions, and increase usability.

> Colors are how our eyes perceive different lightwave lengths.

> The choice of a color palette and its application to a design are not mere happy accidents. It takes iteration and careful application to really benefit and take full advantage of color in a UI.

## Concepts

- [[Color Theory]] — Framework for understanding color harmony, relationships on the color wheel, and combinations that work well together.
- [[Color Palette]] — Curated set of colors selected for a project or brand; creation follows harmony principles, limitation to three colors, and brand guideline compliance.
- [[Visual Hierarchy]] — Color use creates visual hierarchy by drawing attention to important elements and creating balance through 60-30-10 distribution rule.
- [[Brand Design]] — Color palettes must follow established brand guidelines to create consistent, strong brand experience across products and interfaces.
- [[Accessibility]] — Color use must be tested for legibility, contrast ratios, and color-blindness compatibility to ensure design works for all users.
