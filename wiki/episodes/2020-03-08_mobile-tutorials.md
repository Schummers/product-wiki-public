# Mobile Tutorials: Wasted Effort or Efficiency Boost?

## Summary

This quantitative usability study tested 70 users with 4 mobile apps using deck-of-cards tutorials (instructional screens shown on first launch) to measure their impact on user performance and perception. The study compared outcomes between users who engaged with tutorials and those who skipped them, examining task success, perceived ease of use, and task completion time. The findings challenge the assumption that tutorials significantly improve user experience for simple mobile applications.

## Key Takeaways

- **Comparable Success Rates** — both users who read tutorials (91% success) and those who skipped them (94% success) achieved nearly identical task success rates, showing no significant performance advantage from tutorials.
- **Perceived Difficulty Increased** — users who read tutorials rated tasks as more difficult (4.92 on a 7-point scale) compared to those who skipped them (5.49), suggesting tutorials may increase perceived app complexity rather than reduce it.
- **No Speed Advantage** — reading tutorials did not make users faster at completing described tasks, with nearly identical task-completion times between groups (93.49 vs 85.17 seconds).
- **Tutorial Skip Bias** — many participants skip tutorials when downloading apps, and when users do read them, they derive no tangible performance benefit.
- **Design-First Approach** — tutorials take significant time and effort to design and develop; this effort would be better spent making the UI easy and intuitive to reduce the need for tutorials.
- **Interactive Alternatives** — the article recommends considering interactive methods like contextual help instead of formal upfront tutorials.

## Quotes

> whether people viewed the tutorial or not, they were still very successful at completing the primary task.

> participants who read tutorials perceived tasks (which were explained in the tutorials) as more difficult than those who skipped tutorials.

> **Tutorials take time and effort to design and develop, and those would be better spent on making the UI easy**to use and thus alleviating the need for a tutorial in the first place.

## Concepts

- [[Onboarding]] — reveals that formal deck-of-cards tutorials may not effectively onboard users to simple mobile apps and may even harm perceived usability.
