# Privacy Policies and Terms of Use: 5 Common Mistakes

## Summary

Policy pages (privacy policies, terms of service, end-user licensing agreements) often fail users because they are written in complex legalese, poorly formatted, and difficult to navigate. This article presents findings from usability research on policy pages, identifying five common mistakes: (1) unreadable language that is too vague or complex, (2) lack of high-level summaries, (3) poor formatting, (4) lack of functional navigation, and (5) information not located where users expect. Users feel policies are "for lawyers" when written in jargon, appreciate bulleted summaries and plain-language versions, and expect policy links in footers and settings. Well-designed policies reassure users and build trust, while poorly designed ones erode confidence and suggest companies are hiding something.

## Key Takeaways

- **Write in plain language with translations of legal terms** — Avoid vague, overly complex legalese that users perceive as hiding information. Provide plain-language summaries or "translations" of legal sections. Use specific examples of how policies affect users rather than broad, vague statements about data collection or usage.
- **Provide high-level summaries at the top** — Include an overview written in plain language covering what the policy pertains to, who it's intended for, and key points. Add update dates and effective dates. Summarize recent changes (not just when changes were made). Users expect this context at the start.
- **Format for web readability** — Use minimum 14pt font, bold important phrases, use sentence case (not ALL CAPS), avoid long unbroken paragraphs, and ensure pages render well on mobile. Poor formatting signals the policy was "carelessly put together" or the company is hiding something.
- **Create functional navigation with a table of contents** — Include a detailed, clickable table of contents or left-rail navigation so users can find specific sections. Functional navigation makes users feel the policy is transparent even if they don't read all of it.
- **Place policies in expected locations** — Include policy links in the footer of every page. Also link to specific policy sections in Settings/Preferences and near relevant claims or features (e.g., notification settings should link to notification privacy policy). Redundant links are helpful for policy pages.
