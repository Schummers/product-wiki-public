# Why Field-Study Sessions Go Wrong: 5 Common Problems

## Summary

Field studies are a valuable UX research method in which researchers observe and interview participants in their natural environment while they engage in everyday activities and routines. However, practitioners commonly encounter obstacles that compromise the quality of insights. This article identifies five frequent problems that can derail field studies and provides recommendations to avoid them.

Field study sessions often fail due to poor planning around session duration, allowing sessions to become complaint sessions rather than behavioral observation, inadvertently transforming individual sessions into group observations, creating skewed relationship dynamics that alter normal behavior, and relying on abstract descriptions instead of concrete observed behaviors. Sessions that are too long cause participant fatigue; sessions that shift focus from observation to problem documentation collect wishes rather than behaviors; group sessions compromise the depth of individual context; skewed host-guest dynamics in home environments alter typical behaviors; and abstract descriptions ("we usually…") miss the specific details that inform design. Each problem has practical solutions grounded in clear research setup, skilled facilitation, and probing questions that anchor participants in concrete instances.

## Key Takeaways

- **Avoid sessions longer than 2 hours** — remote sessions especially suffer from attention fatigue; quality of observation and analysis matter more than the number of tasks observed.
- **Clarify the session purpose to prevent complaint sessions** — explain upfront that the goal is understanding tasks and practices, not documenting all problems; acknowledge complaints and redirect focus to habitual behaviors.
- **Keep sessions one-on-one** — observing one person provides immersive context; avoid group sessions, which lack the depth needed to understand daily life and work practices.
- **Manage host-guest dynamics in home environments** — accept offers to ease participants' social stress (e.g., water), but redirect focus to the task; aware facilitators notice and prevent behavior alteration from hospitality.
- **Probe for concrete behaviors over abstractions** — look for "in general…" phrases and steer participants toward specific instances; ask "How did you do this today?" rather than "How do you usually…?"

## Concepts

- [[Field Studies]] — conducted in natural environments, field studies reveal distractions, workarounds, and interruptions that lab settings cannot; effectiveness depends on proper session length, one-on-one observation, and managing relationship dynamics.
- [[Research Methods]] — field study quality deteriorates with poor planning; researchers must clarify purpose upfront, set expectations around observation (not complaint-collecting), and maintain neutral, observing postures.
- [[Observation Techniques]] — effective observation includes noticing abstract descriptions and probing for concrete instances; asking "Tell me about this morning" yields more detail than "How do you usually spend mornings?"
- [[Participant Behavior]] — natural behavior emerges over time as participants acclimate to researchers; host-guest dynamics in private spaces, focus on problems rather than practices, and group pressure all bias behavior.
