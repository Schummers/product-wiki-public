# Minimize Design Risk by Focusing on Outcomes Not Features

## Summary

One of the biggest traps in product planning is focusing on outputs (what to build) before clearly defining outcomes (the problem being solved). Many apps and websites fail because they solve problems nobody cares about. An output is a product or service created; an outcome is the problem solved. The distinction parallels the classic marketing lesson: features are product attributes; benefits are what customers want. A wonderful interface for the wrong features will fail, no matter how well-designed. The article proposes five steps for ensuring valuable outputs: state the problem clearly and who it solves for; gather user data proving the problem exists; define core competencies that create competitive advantage; set concrete goals with clear success criteria; and test multiple ideas with users to identify winning solutions. Teams should resist the temptation to jump to solutions and instead invest time understanding the problem, as time spent on discovery prevents wasted resources building the wrong things.

## Key Takeaways

- **Define outcome before output** — An outcome is the problem solved; an output is the product created. Define the outcome first to avoid building the wrong things.
- **Avoid output-first mentality** — Focusing on what to build before understanding why leads to unproven assumptions and risky decisions; any good problem-solver gathers evidence first.
- **State the problem clearly** — Work with teams to develop shared understanding of the problem being solved, for whom, and how it helps the business.
- **Gather user data** — Verify problems exist through research; avoid making major decisions based on gut feel and cognitive biases; vet solutions against real user needs.
- **Define core competencies** — Focus on what the organization is good at and what creates sustainable competitive advantage; avoid chasing trendy or peripheral features.

## Quotes

> One of the biggest traps in product planning is focusing on outputs over outcomes — that is, discussing what to build before clearly defining its purpose.

> A feature is something a product or service offers, whereas a benefit is what customers actually want. One of the oldest lessons in marketing and persuasive writing is that you sell more by emphasizing a product's benefits instead of its features.

> Resist the temptation to lead with the features in a UX design project. Know the customer problem before deciding how to solve it.

## Concepts

- [[Product Strategy]] — Strategic product decisions must be rooted in clear problem definition and evidence, not assumptions; outcomes drive strategy more than features.
- [[User Research]] — Understanding real user needs requires research and empathy; assumed user needs often lead to solutions nobody wants.
- [[Problem Framing]] — Clear problem definition is the foundation of successful design; problems must be stated before solutions are proposed.
- [[Design Process]] — User-centered design begins with problem discovery; research methods identify real needs before ideation and prototyping.
- [[Business Impact]] — Both user and business goals must be considered; successful outcomes solve real user problems while supporting business metrics.
