# Solving Product Design Exercises: Why I wrote this book

## Summary

The author, a design leader at WeWork, describes his motivation for writing the book based on experience recruiting designers. Through interviews with dozens of candidates, he observed that many lacked understanding of design's business impact and role beyond aesthetics. The chapter names four reasons for this gap, the first of them the Dribbblisation of Design, and explains how public sharing of design exercises led to the book as a resource to help the design community bridge these gaps.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three career stages for designers** — Julie Zhuo's framework outlines progression from Craft & Execution (mastering design tools and aesthetics), to Product Thinking (understanding business outcomes and designing toward them), to Influencing Skills (communication and collaboration).
- **Hiring gap reveals education gap** — Many candidates failed interviews because they could not connect design to business strategy, revealing that design schools and employers are not teaching the necessary skills.
- **"Dribbblisation of Design"** — Design communities like Dribbble and Behance celebrate visual work, creating a widespread misconception that Product Design equals visual design, which misdirects both designers and organizations.
- **Four reasons for the gap** — The chapter names four: the Dribbblisation of Design (above); a lack of communication between businesses and the design community, since the top 1% of companies do not share what they expect or how they work; different expectations behind the same Product Designer title, so two roles can carry the same job description; and a lack of educational resources, design schools not teaching the appropriate skills, online courses not going deep enough, and companies not sharing their processes or case studies.
- **Consequences across the industry** — Junior designers over-invest in visual skills instead of problem-solving portfolios; mid-level designers get stuck in visual-only roles; companies miss the value designers could deliver on business problems.

## Quotes

> Strong product thinking means that you understand what a good outcome is and how to design an experience that would lead to those good outcomes.

> At the end of the day, businesses exist to generate profits and they will value anyone who will help them to achieve their business goals.

> During interviews designers are often asked to do something they have never been taught.
