# Remote Design Work: Top Challenges

## Summary

Based on interviews with 17 remote workers and surveys of 306 UX practitioners, this article identifies three major challenges in remote design work, all related to communication. Designers struggle with receiving timely feedback on designs (overcome through tool selection and recurring feedback schedules), replicating informal hallway conversations (solved through recurring team meetings, social space at meeting starts, and full-day working sessions), and maintaining clear project direction (addressed through single documentation sources, clear handoffs with video discussion, and frequent check-ins). 70% of survey respondents began remote work due to the 2020 pandemic.

## Key Takeaways

- **Feedback tools and processes enable asynchronous design review** — tools like Figma, InVision, or Google Drive with commenting features reduce friction; recurring feedback schedules help stakeholders make it a routine.
- **Single tool minimizes feedback fragmentation** — requiring team members to navigate multiple feedback channels (PDF, Figma, Slack) causes delays and missed participation.
- **Recurring team collaboration time replaces hallway conversations** — scheduling hour-long recurring meetings for collaborative work, ideation, and design review helps maintain connection and flow of ideas.
- **Social space at meeting starts builds relationships** — allocating 5-10 minutes at the start of meetings for informal discussion (showing desk items, answering open questions) compensates for lost casual office interactions.
- **Single source of truth prevents miscommunication** — centralizing requirements, documentation, and designs in one location (backlog, intranet, cloud storage) enables self-service access and reduces context loss.
- **Video discussion at handoff prevents misinterpretation** — handing off designs via video discussion instead of email alone prevents misunderstandings about interactions, business importance, and developer expectations.

## Quotes

> "For collaborative UX teams, it can be hard not to be in a shared workspace where we can use a wall, post-it notes, and have ongoing discussion with each other."

> "Since I work across time zones, the biggest challenge is to stay connected and to maintain a flow of ideas without every member receding to his own silo."

> "Don't get stuck in your UX cave for too long. Make sure to check in with your team frequently for feedback on ideas and designs throughout your projects."
