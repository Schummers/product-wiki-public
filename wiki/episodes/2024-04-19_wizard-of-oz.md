# The Wizard of Oz Method in UX

## Summary

The Wizard of Oz method is a moderated research technique where users interact with an interface that appears autonomous but is partially or fully controlled by a human operator. Named after the novel where the "wizard" is an ordinary person behind a curtain, this method enables teams to test complex technologies at low cost before full development. The method is particularly valuable for testing conversational UIs, recommendation engines, and real-time information systems. The article provides five implementation steps: creating a prototype, deciding response methods, developing a study protocol, selecting and preparing the wizard, and piloting the study.

## Key Takeaways

- **Method definition** — users interact with a seemingly autonomous interface while a hidden "wizard" provides system responses, enabling cost-effective testing of complex technologies without building them.
- **Appropriate contexts** — useful for testing conversational UIs, learning algorithms that recommend content, and interfaces that look up real-time information.
- **Response methods** — wizards can use closed method (choosing from preset responses), open method (creating responses in-session), or hybrid method (combination of both).
- **Study protocol** — must document roles, initial questions, which design elements the wizard controls, response options, and guidelines for improvisation.
- **Preparation importance** — wizard should understand product concept, responses, and prototyping software; piloting ensures smooth sessions and identifies needed responses.

## Quotes

> The Wizard of Oz method is a moderated research method in which a user interacts with an interface that appears to be autonomous but is (fully or partially) controlled by a human.

> The Wizard-of-Oz method lowers investment risk into complex, costly technologies (such as generative AI): it provides early insights into their desirability, utility, and usability before companies spend money building them.

> Preparation and planning are key. Make a plan, involve engineers, and pilot your study for the best results.
