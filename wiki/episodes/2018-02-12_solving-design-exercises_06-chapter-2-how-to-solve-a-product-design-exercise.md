# Solving Product Design Exercises: Chapter 2: How to solve a product design exercise

## Summary

This chapter establishes the foundational principle that solving design exercises requires following a deliberate process rather than jumping to visual solutions. The chapter provides three critical tips for success: clarifying deliverables and expectations with the interviewer, asking questions and making explicit assumptions when information is incomplete, and maintaining critical awareness of solutions' pros and cons. The author emphasizes that process and structured thinking are what separate effective problem-solving from failed attempts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Process over aesthetics** — Designers are tempted to sketch immediately, but this leads to building the wrong product. Analyzing the problem, asking questions, and understanding the audience before visual design is crucial for successful outcomes.
- **Clarify expectations upfront** — Understanding deliverable type (sketch, prototype, presentation), fidelity level (wireframe or high-fidelity), presentation format (phone, video, in-person), and whether presenting is required prevents misalignment between candidate and interviewer.
- **Ask questions and make assumptions** — Great designers ask clarifying questions to understand goals, audience, and needs rather than silently implementing. When information is missing, make explicit assumptions backed by reasoning rather than guessing.
- **Assumptions are tested** — Operating with incomplete information and making sound assumptions is a core skill tested in interviews. An assumption is a claim backed by little or no data that is needed to build a successful product.
- **Think critically about trade-offs** — Always be aware of "why" behind decisions and be ready to discuss both pros and cons. This demonstrates critical thinking and prevents defensive attachment to ideas. For example, a chatbot feature might reduce friction but require significant NLP engineering effort and human support for edge cases.

## Quotes

> As designers we tend to think visually, so we're tempted to jump right into the final solution and start sketching right away. In both your actual job and in interviews, this approach will most likely lead you to building the wrong product for your audience.

> The single most important thing you should remember while solving exercises is to follow a process. Analysing the problem, asking questions, and evaluating and understanding the audience before doing any visual work is crucial for a successful outcome.

> Great designers don't just receive a task and quietly implement it. One of the most important skills of great designers is asking the right questions and making sure they have all the information needed to build the right product for the right audience.
