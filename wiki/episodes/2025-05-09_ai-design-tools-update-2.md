# AI Design Tools Are Marginally Better: Status Update

## Summary

This follow-up to a 2024 status assessment finds that AI-powered design tools have improved modestly between April 2024 and May 2025 but remain far from the promised capabilities. Narrow-scope AI features are most useful: Figma's layer renaming, content rewriting, and asset finding; Khroma Color for palette generation; Midjourney for placeholder images. Conversely, broader tools for wireframe and prototype generation still lack sophistication to handle real-world context, design-system integration, and complex requirements. Prompt-length limitations and insufficient contextual awareness prevent broader AI tools from replacing human designers. The article demonstrates that narrow-scope AI excels where it eliminates repetitive tasks but fails where nuanced judgment and contextual understanding are required.

## Key Takeaways

- **Narrow-scope AI features deliver practical value** — focused tools for specific repetitive tasks (rename layers, rewrite copy, find assets, generate color palettes, create placeholder images) are genuinely adopted by designers and save meaningful time without requiring extensive guidance.
- **Broad wireframe and prototype generation remains impractical** — tools claiming to generate full designs lack sophistication to balance design, business, and user needs; produce generic layouts with poor information hierarchy; generate minor variations rather than meaningful alternatives.
- **Design system integration is critically missing** — most designers work with established design systems; AI-generated prototypes using random design elements provide no value; current tools cannot pull from design systems to create cohesive designs across contexts.
- **Prompt-length limitations severely restrict AI utility** — 500-character prompts cannot convey sufficient context for contextual design decisions; longer prompts would require more model tokens; even Figma's First Draft feature discourages long prompts through UI design (small text box).
- **Human designers remain irreplaceable** — no current AI tool can replicate 85% of a designer's work despite marketing claims; design requires balancing multiple competing requirements, understanding real-world context, and making nuanced decisions that generative AI cannot yet handle.

## Quotes

> As of May 2025, their usefulness has improved, but we're still nowhere near the AI-powered design tools we've been promised, nor are design professionals yet in danger of being replaced by AI.

> Unlike a human designer who can understand and adjust to a variety of contexts and needs, most genAI tools lack the sophistication to balance all the requirements of a design.

> Despite Uizard's claims, no AI on the market can replicate a fraction of what a human UX designer can create.
