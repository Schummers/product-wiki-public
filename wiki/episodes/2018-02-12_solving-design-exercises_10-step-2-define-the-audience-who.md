# Solving Product Design Exercises: Step 2: Define the audience (Who)

## Summary

This chapter details Step 2 of the design exercise framework, focusing on understanding the target audience. Dashinsky emphasizes that not understanding the audience is a fundamental reason products fail, citing Paul Graham's principle that startups die from not making something users want. The chapter explains how to choose a primary audience and characterize it using demographic and contextual factors, then provides examples from Spotify and other products to show how audience differentiation leads to better solutions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Audience Selection as Critical** — Understanding the target audience is crucial because building without true audience understanding risks creating something users do not want, which is a primary reason products fail.
- **High-Level Audience Categories** — Identify people with significantly different motivations for using the product (e.g., Spotify has listeners, artists, and business owners) and select one primary audience from the two largest segments.
- **Audience Characterization Dimensions** — Describe the audience using multiple classification approaches: age, gender, location, occupation, and mobility patterns; these dimensions influence product design significantly.
- **Audience Segmentation Within Primary Group** — Create subgroups within the primary audience based on different needs (e.g., Spotify listeners by age or activity); this segmentation reveals opportunities for targeted features.
- **User vs. Customer Distinction** — Recognize that in B2B2C solutions, the product's users and the customer who buys it may be different (e.g., patients vs. clinics), which should be mentioned during presentation.

## Quotes

> After defining your vision you should **understand** **who are you building a product for**.

> By not truly understanding your target audience, you're risking building something users don't want. That's why this step is crucial.

> Pick one of the two biggest audiences, so you will have enough scope for coming up with ideas of how to serve them.
