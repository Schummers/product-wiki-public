# Hierarchy of Trust: The 5 Experiential Levels of Commitment

## Summary

Like Maslow's hierarchy of needs, user-site relationships progress through five levels of commitment, each building on lower levels. Starting from no trust, users climb through: (1) baseline relevance and credibility, (2) interest and preference, (3) trust with personal information, (4) trust with sensitive/financial information, and (5) willingness to commit to ongoing relationships. Sites fail when they demand higher-level commitments (e.g., registration or payment) without first establishing lower-level trust. Successful persuasion requires matching site demands to users' current trust levels and progressively building trust through each stage.

## Key Takeaways

- **Five levels build on each other** — baseline trust, interest, personal information trust, financial trust, and commitment to ongoing relationships. Each higher level requires all lower levels to be satisfied first.
- **Skepticism is strong by default** — users start in the sand below the pyramid with no trust established. Sites must actively induce climbing through the levels.
- **Align demands with trust level** — do not ask for email, phone number, or payment before establishing that the site is relevant, credible, and preferable to alternatives.
- **Login walls skip critical levels** — requiring registration before users understand what the site offers violates levels 1 and 2, causing abandonment before trust builds.
- **Use low-friction trust signals early** — descriptive taglines, social proof, representative images, and free browsing help users bridge early levels without demanding personal data.
- **Exploration builds confidence** — allowing users to browse and search freely before asking for commitment gives them a sense of control and confidence in the system.

## Quotes

> Much like Maslow's pyramid of needs, we can define a pyramid of trust. In relationships (whether between two people or between a site and a user), individuals must have basic trust needs met before being able to progress to more substantial interactions.

> The site's requests and the users' trust needs must be in equilibrium: Don't make demands at higher levels of commitment until you've addressed all the trust needs at the inferior levels.

> Skepticism is strong by default. While this skepticism can sometimes be overcome with the help of external factors such as word-of-mouth recommendations or reviews, the site itself must also work to gain users' trust by smoothly fulfilling users' needs at all levels of commitment.
