## Summary

A comprehensive, curated study guide of 150+ essential NN/g articles and videos on web UX organized into 13 topic areas: general web-usage theory, viewing and eyetracking behavior, web interaction design patterns, content strategy, form design, links, navigation, search, cross-platform design, visual design, design trends (minimalism, flat design, dark mode, glassmorphism, neobrutalism), and e-commerce. The guide emphasizes foundational theories (information foraging, information scent, scanning patterns like F-shaped and layer-cake) and core principles before diving into specific patterns and components.

Key themes across sections: convention and standardization are essential for discoverability and trust; users scan rather than read, so content must be front-loaded with keywords; information scent guides navigation decisions; common UI patterns (accordions, tabs, carousels, modals) solve recurring problems but introduce specific usability tradeoffs; responsive design requires thoughtful adaptation across devices rather than mobile-first prescriptions; visual design trends must be evaluated for usability impact, not adopted for novelty alone.

## Key Takeaways

- **Foundational theories inform all web design decisions** — Information foraging and information scent explain how users navigate; scanning patterns (F-shaped, layer-cake, lawn-mower) reveal how people interact with different content types; standardization and convention are essential because they align with mental models users bring from other websites.
- **Users scan, not read; front-load information** — Users read only ~20% of page text; they scan selectively based on task; place keywords at the start of headings, links, and bullets; avoid placing critical information after secondary content or hiding it below the fold (the fold still matters for discoverability).
- **Every UI pattern solves a problem but introduces tradeoffs** — Accordions hide content but may obscure information; carousels hide content but have low discoverability; modals interrupt workflow; infinite scrolling removes pagination cues; each pattern's use must be justified by context, not adopted for minimalism or trend appeal.
- **Cross-platform design requires device-aware adaptation, not mobile-first dogma** — Users complete important or complex tasks on larger screens; mobile-first design that disperses content across too many pages hurts desktop usability; breakpoints and responsive layouts should adapt based on content hierarchy and task, not screen size alone.
- **Visual design trends (minimalism, flat design, dark mode, neobrutalism, glassmorphism) have usability implications** — Evaluate trends through a usability lens: flat design lacks clickability cues; high contrast text improves scannability; minimalism can reduce visual noise but also reduce affordances; adopt trends only when they serve user goals, not aesthetics alone.
