# UX Strategy: Study Guide

## Summary

This study guide provides an overview of UX strategy, defined as a plan of actions designed to reach an improved future state of an organization's user experience over an established time period. The guide organizes UX strategy into three primary components: vision (aspirational statements of intent), goals and measurement (metrics and KPIs connecting UX improvements to business outcomes), and plan (specific objectives and steps to achieve goals). The article serves as an index to Nielsen Norman's extensive content on these topics, including resources on creating vision statements, setting objectives and key results (OKRs), conducting UX benchmarking, creating roadmaps, and prioritization methods. The framework emphasizes that UX strategy bridges aspirational vision with measurable business impact through systematic planning.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX strategy has three components** — vision defines the intended future state, goals with metrics connect UX work to business outcomes, and plans outline specific steps and objectives.
- **Vision must be research-based** — an aspirational vision of the product's ideal form and user value must be grounded in research and viable in the market, not merely aspirational.
- **Goals require measurement** — connecting UX improvements directly to business KPIs ensures UX work is strategically aligned and progress is trackable.
- **Roadmaps translate strategy into action** — UX roadmaps visualize how problems will be solved immediately, in the near future, and far future, helping teams maintain strategic direction.
- **Themes focus roadmaps** — theme-based roadmaps (centered on high-level opportunities) are more effective than feature-focused roadmaps for communicating experience strategy.
- **Prioritization depends on context** — the best prioritization method varies by project context, team culture, and success criteria, requiring thoughtful selection.
