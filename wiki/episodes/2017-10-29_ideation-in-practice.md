# Ideation in Practice: How Effective UX Teams Generate Ideas

## Summary

A survey of 257 UX professionals revealed that only 15% feel their ideation process is very effective, with 59% rating it merely somewhat effective. Key challenges include insufficient time, lack of managerial support, inadequate user research, and group dynamics issues. Teams with very effective ideation spend less time on ideation (77% use under 3 hours) but structure it better, involve groups more (collaborative > individual), conduct research early, use written ideation techniques, and employ formal ideation methods rather than unstructured discussion.

## Key Takeaways

- **Effectiveness is rare** — only 15% perceive their process as very effective; effectiveness depends on approach and structure, not time spent.
- **Group ideation beats individual** — teams rating themselves very effective collaborate more and rely less on individual ideation; 66% of respondents agree group ideation generates higher-quality ideas.
- **Timing matters** — 71% of very effective teams ideate after research but before prototyping, compared to only 48% of somewhat effective teams; earlier ideation produces more usable divergent solutions.
- **Written techniques are key** — those with very effective processes use significantly more written ideation (brainwriting, etc.) compared to ineffective teams, reducing groupthink and hierarchy bias.
- **Structured beats open-ended** — 95% of ineffective teams rely on unstructured discussion; formal ideation techniques (brainstorming, mind mapping, etc.) significantly improve outcomes.
- **Managerial support is critical** — 15% cite lack of management support as primary barrier; without top-level clout to pull people from other teams and allocate time, ideation suffers.
