# The Anchoring Principle

## Summary

This article explores anchoring bias, a cognitive heuristic where people rely heavily on an initial piece of information (the anchor) to make decisions. Research by Tversky and Kahneman demonstrates that a small change in how information is presented significantly influences estimates and choices. The article presents practical applications of anchoring for UX design, showing how well-chosen anchors can set expectations, reduce cognitive load, and improve perceived value.

## Key Takeaways

- **Initial information disproportionately influences judgment** — In the classic Tversky and Kahneman study, reversing a mathematical expression's order (1×2×3×4×5×6×7×8 versus 8×7×6×5×4×3×2×1) produced median estimates differing by more than 4x, demonstrating that leading numbers anchor subsequent estimates.

- **Default values set expectations** — Well-chosen numerical defaults minimize interaction cost and serve as anchors for understanding normal versus exceptional values, helping novice users calibrate their understanding of what constitutes appropriate input.

- **Suggested values improve decision outcomes** — Research by Behave.org showed that Oxfam donation forms with suggested values (versus simple "Donate now" buttons) achieved 23% more completions by anchoring donors' expectations and reducing decision burden.

- **Original prices anchor perceived value** — Displaying strikethrough original prices alongside discounted prices anchors customers' perception of true value higher than showing only the discounted price, affecting purchase decisions.

- **Time estimates anchor satisfaction** — Accurate (or slightly conservative) process duration estimates set appropriate expectations; if experiences exceed the estimate, users feel cheated and dissatisfied.

## Quotes

> "Anchoring (or focalism) bias refers to the tendency to rely on a single piece of information or aspect of an event (the "anchor") to inform decision making."

> Small numbers at the beginning signaled a small result, and large ones coaxed a large estimate.

> "Good anchors help users set their expectation for what's normal or exceptional, lower the cognitive cost of decision making, and can even increase the perceived value of a product."

## Concepts

- [[Anchoring Bias]] — A judgment heuristic where initial information disproportionately influences decision-making, applicable in UX through defaults, suggested values, and pricing displays.

- [[Judgment Heuristics]] — Mental shortcuts that expedite decision-making in the absence of information or time, including anchoring, framing, and priming.

- [[Default Values]] — Pre-selected values in forms and systems that serve as anchors, reducing cognitive load while setting expectations for appropriate input ranges.
