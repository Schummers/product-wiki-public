# How and Why to Include Users in UX Workshops

## Summary

The article explores when and how to include real users in UX workshops across five different types: discovery, empathy, design, prioritization, and critique workshops. Including users in workshops can provide significant value by bringing direct user perspectives into team discussions, though it requires careful planning around recruitment, logistics, and managing potential pitfalls. The article provides detailed agendas and recommendations for each workshop type, emphasizing that users' contributions should be balanced with other expertise and that teams must educate themselves to avoid blindly implementing user suggestions.

## Key Takeaways

- **User inclusion varies by workshop type** — discovery, empathy, design, prioritization, and critique workshops each have distinct benefits from including real users, and the level of participation should be tailored to the workshop's goals.
- **Recruitment matters significantly** — prioritize recruiting users who have participated in previous research sessions and are known to be articulate contributors rather than randomly selecting participants.
- **Follow the user blindly risk** — teams can mitigate the risk of implementing all user suggestions by educating participants in advance about the difference between user needs and design solutions, and by scheduling critical discussions after users leave.
- **User proxies can substitute** — when including real users isn't feasible, user researchers, customer-support representatives, or sales representatives can serve as valuable proxies who advocate for user needs based on their deep knowledge.
- **Bias management is essential** — careful attention to recruitment balance, clear communication about feedback expectations, and neutral questioning techniques are necessary to prevent biasing users' contributions.

## Quotes

> "Sometimes, this means including user advocates such as UX researchers. Sometimes, however, it is advantageous to invite real users to the workshop. Doing so can make you or your stakeholders feel vulnerable, but the perks of having the user's voice in the room can be immense."

> "While it is sometimes appropriate to invite a user to critique a concept in a critique workshop, an alternative, more common arrangement is to ask the user to provide their perspective about that concept, perhaps by using it directly, so that the team can later use this information in the actual critique."

> "A successful UX workshop will have a persistent focus on the user. One way to ensure that is by inviting users to participate directly. While users' contributions to the workshop should be taken with a grain of salt and always balanced by other perspective, they will allow the team to build empathy and provide visibility to users' needs and pain points."
