# Solving Product Design Exercises: Step 3: Understand the customer's context and needs (When and Where)

## Summary

This chapter covers Step 3 of the framework, which focuses on understanding when, where, and how customers experience problems. Dashinsky provides questions to explore the user context (physical location, trigger events, time constraints, platforms, emotional state) and introduces techniques for identifying customer needs. The chapter teaches the user stories technique and customer journey mapping as tools to understand deeper motivations and pain points that should inform the solution design.

## Key Takeaways

- **Context Variables** — Understanding user context requires exploring where they physically are, trigger events causing the need, available time, specific platforms, and emotional states; these conditions influence design decisions like hands-free navigation or dark interfaces.
- **High-Level Motivation Discovery** — Identify the customer's primary motivation for solving the problem (e.g., freelancers want to be in control of their business), then dig deeper to understand how they achieve that goal.
- **User Stories Technique** — Use the template "As a [role], I want [goal/desire] so that [benefit]" to explore the connection between user context and needs; this technique helps uncover the real motivation behind desired behaviors.
- **Needs Hierarchy** — Break down high-level motivation into specific sub-needs (e.g., financial monitoring, work planning, business growth) that reveal multiple solution angles.
- **Customer Journey Mapping** — Map the current user journey to identify pain points and suboptimal experiences that can be transformed into product opportunities during later ideation steps.

## Quotes

> Once you have defined your audience, the next step is to understand **when and where they experience this problem, and how you can solve it**.

> The *goal/desire* is what the user wants to achieve with your product. The *benefit* is the reason they want to achieve this goal — the real motivation for performing it.

> Another technique for generating ideas is mapping out the current customer journey and identifying any problems that can be transformed into opportunities later on.
