# Venture Studios Demystified: Equity Ownership

## Summary

Part 4 of Venture Studios Demystified examines how equity dilution and ownership stakes directly impact a studio's financial returns. The chapter models various dilution scenarios to show that initial ownership percentage is critical to studio viability, with scenarios ranging from 30% pre-seed down to 8% by Series D, and explores how different raise sizes and timelines affect long-term cash balance.

## Key Takeaways

- **Dilution impact on returns** — A 10% increase in round sizes across all funding stages reduces studio exit returns from $22M to $19M per company, resulting in a $15M lower cash balance by year 10 (17% reduction); a 20% increase drops exits to $16M, creating a $28M gap (31% reduction).
- **Ownership floor for viability** — Studios without a separate fund require at least 10% founding equity to justify the model; at 8% (the break-even point), dilution to 1% by Series D generates only $3.6M per exit, just covering ongoing costs with no investor returns.
- **Competitive pressure on equity splits** — As studio competition intensifies, founding equity ownership will likely decline toward a 10% floor, driven by competition for top-tier EIRs and founders, with established studios maintaining equity premiums above industry averages.
- **Cap table sensitivity** — The model tracks ownership through a cap table for each funding round from pre-seed through Series D, showing that ownership percentages at each stage (19% at Seed, 15% at Series A, 12% at Series B, 9% at Series C, 8% at Series D) are critical variables in determining exit returns.

## Quotes

> "Ultimately, as the number of studios increases and competition for top tier EIRs and founders increases, studios will need to determine equity splits that are in line with industry averages and competitive relative to their own internal offering."

> "The break-even point for a studio without a fund is 8%. At 8% and without a fund, studio ownership will be diluted down to 1% by Series D. At exit, that means each company only pulls in $3.6M for the studio."

> "At 10% founding equity, the studio will have enough money to operate, but not enough to provide returns to investors. This makes the model unsustainable."
