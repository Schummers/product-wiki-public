# How to Create a UX-Vision Statement

## Summary

A UX vision is an aspirational, future-state view of an experience people will have with a product, service, team, or organization. Documented visions help teams articulate UX goals and prioritize UX efforts; without strong shared vision, team members cannot articulate their work's purpose or value. Vision statements differ from mission statements: mission statements capture who we are and how we provide value now, while vision statements describe what we want to become. Nielsen Norman Group presents a three-stage collaborative process for creating vision statements: gathering research to understand current state, envisioning through collaborative workshops, and planning activities to bridge the gap between current and desired futures.

## Key Takeaways

- **Vision and mission statements serve different purposes** — Mission statements answer "What collective value do we generate now?" while vision statements answer "What do we want to become in n years?"—both inform UX strategy and should be checked against goals.
- **Two types of visions address different organizational needs** — Product visions describe impact and value a product will provide to users; team visions describe value a team provides to the company or impact on products, useful during rapid growth or cultural shifts.
- **Stage I: Gather research to understand current state** — Before ideation, collect or conduct research understanding current UX perception; methods include stakeholder interviews (understanding processes and obstacles), usability testing (if no research exists), and competitive analysis.
- **Stage II: Envision through collaborative workshops** — Teams share research findings, brainstorm and prioritize future-state attributes using colored dots (agree, disagree, surprising, essential), and craft vision statements from top attributes.
- **Stage III: Plan resources and activities to realize vision** — Using "Requisite Roundup" methodology, teams brainstorm required processes, people, tools/tech, and governance to bridge the gap between current and desired future states.
- **Communication is crucial for vision momentum** — Visions must be shared with engaging artifacts (documents, videos, vision boards, prototypes) to build momentum, hold teams accountable, and engage broader organization.

## Quotes

> "A documented vision helps teams articulate their UX goals and identify and prioritize UX efforts. Without a strong, shared vision, team members may not be able to articulate the purpose of their work or the value that work provides to the greater organization."

> "Because of the group activities and discussions involved, a strong facilitator is necessary to drive the process."

> "This is a crucial step to hold the team accountable and engaging others with the vision. Even when created within a strategic and collaborative process such as the one outlined in this article, visions will fail if they are not shared and visible."

## Concepts

- [[Vision Statements]] — Aspirational future-state descriptions of products, services, teams, or organizations; created through collaborative three-stage processes involving research, group envisioning, and planning.
- [[Business Strategy]] — Vision statements inform overall UX strategy and should be checked against goals and objectives; they guide resource allocation and help teams determine which UX initiatives to prioritize.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Collaborative vision-creation processes (research sharing, dot-coding exercises, affinity diagramming, dot voting) help entire teams rally around and believe in the final vision output.
- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — Creating visions requires skilled facilitators to guide group activities, ensure research is understood, facilitate brainstorming, and synthesize inputs into coherent vision statements.
