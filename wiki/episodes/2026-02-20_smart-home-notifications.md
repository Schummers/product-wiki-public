# Designing Useful Smart Home Notifications

## Summary

Smart-home notifications inform users about system status and invite interaction, but poor design causes notification fatigue. NN/g identified three notification types—reactive (immediate attention needed), proactive (upcoming events, prepare), and optimization (efficiency suggestions)—each with different timing, relevance, and channel needs. Seven design principles make notifications useful: timely delivery (instant for reactive, advanced for proactive, contextual for optimization), relevant to user priorities and automatically expiring when resolved, specific (answering what, why, when without requiring app access), appropriately intense (visual/audio cues matching urgency), right frequency (avoiding fatigue through thresholds and grouping), delivered via right channel (push for urgent, in-app for nonurgent), and adaptable to changing contexts with snooze options.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three Notification Types, Different Urgencies** — Reactive (immediate action needed), Proactive (upcoming, prepare), Optimization (efficiency suggestions); each requires different timing and channel.
- **Principle #1: Timely Delivery** — Reactive: instantly, in real-time; Proactive: advanced but not so early users forget; Optimization: when relevant to current use.
- **Principle #2: Relevant to Priorities** — By default, send alerts for core purpose only; allow customization; auto-expire when resolved; flag when issues resolve themselves.
- **Principle #3: Specific, Not Vague** — Answer user's key questions (what, why, when) without app access; "Person detected at front door" beats "Motion detected."
- **Principle #4: Right Intensity** — Match urgency with visual/audio cues; red and strong alerts for critical, yellow for moderate, subtle for low-priority; consistency across devices.
- **Principle #5: Avoid Fatigue Through Frequency** — Allow event thresholds (notify at 20% battery, not 50%), event-type filtering (people only, not bugs), and repeat intervals; separate discrete events from ongoing conditions.

## Quotes

> Notifications are central to how smart home technologies inform users about the system's status and invite interaction. Well-designed notifications build trust and help users stay in control.

> The goal is not to communicate more but to communicate better. Notifications that disregard user goals and priorities — whether through excessive volume, irrelevant alerts, or unsolicited marketing messages — may cause fatigue, frustration, and disengagement.

> Smart-home notifications shape how people perceive their devices' usefulness. Well-designed alerts don't just inform people about important events; they also foster trust and confidence in the device, while demonstrating respect for the user's time and attention.

## Concepts

- [[Notification Design]] — Seven principles ensure notifications remain useful: timing, relevance, specificity, intensity, frequency, channel, and adaptability prevent fatigue while keeping users informed.
- [[Smart Devices]] — As multiple manufacturers' devices integrate, consistent notification patterns across ecosystems become essential to usability and trust.
- [[Mobile and Tablet Design]] — Smart-home apps deliver notifications across push, in-app, email, SMS, and on-device channels; designers must choose channels matching urgency.
- [[Information Architecture]] — Organizing notifications by type, urgency, and impact helps users understand what requires immediate action and what can wait.
- [[Customization]] — Users need granular control over notification types, thresholds, channels, and timing; allowing customization prevents abandonment of entire notification systems.
