# Communicating Changes Throughout the Buyer's Journey: A COVID-19 Case Study

## Summary

The COVID-19 pandemic created unprecedented disruptions to e-commerce operations, forcing retailers to adapt quickly to new shipping delays, inventory shortages, and purchasing limits. This article examines how retailers communicated these operational changes to customers across multiple touchpoints—from the homepage and product pages through checkout and email—to manage expectations and maintain trust. The case study demonstrates best practices for emergency communication across the entire customer journey, showing how different retailers (Adidas, Best Buy, Target, Chewy, and others) handled transparency about delays, curbside pickup, and order status updates.

## Key Takeaways

- **Communicate shipping delays early** — Customers should learn about potential delays before reaching checkout, not after they've committed time to shopping. Display messaging prominently on homepages and product pages so users can make informed purchasing decisions upfront.
- **Use high-contrast banners for critical announcements** — Important COVID-related notifications require visual weight to stand out. High-contrast backgrounds, bold headlines, and iconography help ensure these messages are noticed rather than dismissed as banner blindness or confused with ads.
- **Include fallback communication at checkout** — Some users miss homepage banners due to task focus. Always reiterate important operational changes on the shopping cart or checkout pages as a safety net to catch users who missed earlier notifications.
- **Layer communication by product category** — When only certain products are affected (e.g., cleaning supplies with high demand), provide general sitewide messaging plus targeted banners on affected category and product pages to help users understand which items are impacted.
- **Explain new services like curbside pickup** — Contactless pickup was unfamiliar to most users. Retailers should advertise and explain the basics on the homepage and checkout, then provide detailed step-by-step instructions via email and in-app notifications to prepare customers for the physical interaction.
- **Update automated transactional emails** — Automated order confirmations, shipment notifications, and pickup emails must reflect current operational changes. These messages are often the primary reference customers keep, so they should reinforce delays and new procedures rather than outdated information.
