# Using AI for UX Work: Study Guide

## Summary

This study guide compiles resources addressing how AI can assist UX work without replacing it. Organized into eight sections—integrating AI into workflows (using AI as assistant, not guide), career strategy and evolving roles, design, research, writing, service design, ideation and workshops, and the current state of AI-UX work—the guide clarifies that AI doesn't guarantee better or faster work but can enhance specific workflows when used strategically. Key insight: think of yourself as a strategist leveraging AI as gloves to expand what you can accomplish, not as replacing your judgment.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI assists specific tasks better than entire workflows** — Handing complete workflows to AI hasn't proved productive; effective integration uses AI to enhance existing work by thinking of yourself as strategist with AI "gloves."
- **UX roles are evolving but fundamental skills endure** — Tool changes have always happened; what remains valuable is understanding people, asking right questions, developing taste, and strategic thinking—skills AI cannot replace.
- **Writing is AI's most powerful current value-add for UX** — AI has exceptional writing abilities, which is likely the strongest value it can add to UX work; however, over-reliance erodes unique style and voice.
- **Research needs real user data, not AI simulations** — AI cannot replace research with real people; while synthetic users and digital twins show promise for supplementing research, they're far from replacing actual user insights.
- **Design tools aren't ready for primetime** — Most AI design tools fail to meaningfully support core design workflows; narrowly scoped tools showing promise, but not ready to replace designers.
- **Test AI selectively for inefficiencies and bottlenecks** — Rather than forcing AI into every task or resisting it outright, identify specific inefficiencies and automation opportunities, then test thoughtfully.

## Quotes

> "Should you use AI in your work? Yes. The question is no longer if it can be helpful, but when? And how?"

> "Does AI guarantee better, more efficient work? No."

> "If you don't want to work for the robot, make the robot work for you."

- [[UX Career]] — Shows how UX roles evolve as AI advances; professionals remain valuable through taste, strategic thinking, understanding people, and ability to synthesize research—skills AI cannot replicate.
- [[Workflow Automation]] — Recommends identifying bottlenecks and repetitive tasks where custom GPTs and reusable prompts pay off; one-off uses often waste more time than they save.
- [[Future of UX]] — Positions AI as enabling generalists who understand multiple disciplines and strategic thinking; discernment and taste become more valuable as AI enables more people to produce work.
