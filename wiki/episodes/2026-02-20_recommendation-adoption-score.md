# Tracking Adoption of Research Recommendations: The Recommendation-Adoption Score

## Summary

The Recommendation-Adoption Score (RAS) tracks research breakage—the gap between recommendations and actual change. Each recommendation is assigned a status (Adopted, Committed, Communicated, Canceled) and a user-value rating based on impact. RAS = (actual user value ÷ total possible user value) × 100, scoring from 0–100. Interpreting ranges: Poor (0–29) means teams ignore research; Fair (30–54) shows some movement but insufficient momentum; Good (55–79) indicates majority adoption; Great (80–100) is rare territory. RAS treats recommendations as inventory: each unit has an owner, a clear definition, and a measurable status. Tracking a rolling 12-month window reveals trends; awareness of RAS alone starts conversations about adoption and makes invisible value visible, actionable, and measurable.

## Key Takeaways

- **RAS Treats Recommendations as Inventory** — Each recommendation needs clear description, owner, definition of done, and tied-back evidence; fuzzy recommendations are already at high breakage risk.
- **Four Status Categories** — Adopted (shipped, verified), Committed (scoped, resourced, on roadmap), Communicated (delivered, not yet on path), Canceled (rejected, deferred, no longer relevant).
- **User-Value Ratings by Impact** — Adopted: 3 (high), 2 (medium), 1 (low); Committed: 0.66 regardless of impact; Communicated: 0; Canceled: excluded from calculation.
- **RAS Ranges Guide Interpretation** — Poor (0–29): research ignored; Fair (30–54): some movement; Good (55–79): majority adopted; Great (80–100): teams act on almost everything.
- **Trends Matter More Than Single Scores** — Scores naturally fluctuate as new research enters as Communicated (lowering RAS) and moves to Committed/Adopted (raising it); upward trends indicate healthy adoption.
- **Rolling 12-Month Window Is Standard** — Long enough to account for implementation time, short enough to stay relevant; adjust window based on organizational velocity (6–18 months possible).

## Quotes

> Each recommendation represents time, energy, and money. Each one is a unit of value that either reaches the user intact or is lost.

> Simply tracking adoption started conversations that had never happened before. Suddenly, product teams knew their adoption rates, executives could see the trend, and researchers finally had proof they could use to push for change.

> A single RAS score is useful, but a trend over time is far more telling.

## Concepts

- [[Research Operations]] — RAS is an operational system for tracking and improving research adoption; it requires clear ownership, governance, and regular review to sustain.
- [[Analytics]] — RAS provides a quantifiable, interpretable metric for research impact; trending RAS separates temporary setbacks from systemic problems.
- [[Recommendation Management]] — Clear recommendation structure (definition, owner, status, value rating) enables tracking, accountability, and visibility into where value breaks down.
- [[Research Impact]] — RAS measures system performance, not individual researcher capability; low adoption signals collaboration problems, not research failure; inward reflection ensures rigor.
- [[Change Management]] — RAS awareness drives cultural change; visible metrics create accountability and conversations between research and product teams, improving adoption over time.
