# Treemaps: Data Visualization of Complex Hierarchies

## Summary

Treemaps are a data-visualization technique using nested rectangles proportional to data values to display hierarchical datasets. A large rectangle represents a branch; subdivided rectangles represent nodes within that branch. Treemaps work well for showing two dimensions of data (size via area, categorical or quantitative via color) and identifying top-performing items within categories. However, they have significant limitations: area is not preattentively perceived, making precise comparisons difficult; they work poorly for balanced trees; and they can visually overwhelm with many items. Bar charts or scatter plots often serve the same purpose more effectively.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treemaps efficiently display hierarchical data with two dimensions** — Rectangle size encodes a positive quantitative value; color encodes a categorical or second quantitative value, enabling quick perception of large contributors within categories.

- **Area perception is non-preattentive, making comparisons difficult** — Unlike length or position, area cannot be grasped instantly; treemaps require interaction (hovering, tooltips) for precise value comparison, limiting their utility for decision-making tasks.

- **Use single-color intensity for quantitative values, multiple colors only for categories** — Humans lack an inherent ordering for colors; varying intensity of a single color represents quantitative ranges; different colors work only for categorical distinctions.

- **Accessibility requires color-blind consideration** — Avoid red/green combinations; use color-blind-safe palettes; provide text labels or hover details as secondary signals for data values encoded in color.

- **Visual separation and high-contrast text improve readability** — Distinct borders around high-level categories, high-contrast labels, visually distinctive hover states, and detail overlays help users navigate complex treemaps.

- **Alternatives (bar charts, scatter plots) often better serve hierarchical data** — Consider whether simpler visualizations match your users' top tasks; sorted bar charts enable quick value ranking; scatter plots more effectively display two quantitative variables.

## Quotes

> Treemaps are visualizations for hierarchical data. They are made of a series of nested rectangles of sizes proportional to the corresponding data value.

> Although treemaps can convey overall relationships in a large data set, they are not suited for tasks involving precise comparisons.

> While treemaps can be useful for visualizing certain types of complex, hierarchical data sets, they often hard to interpret.

## Concepts

- [[Data Visualization]] — Treemaps are effective for hierarchical data and add visual variety to dashboards but have cognitive limitations and consume space; visualization choice should match user tasks and cognitive processes, with density calibrated to user needs and may require interaction for meaningful interpretation.

- [[Hierarchical Data]] — Treemaps specifically display hierarchy using spatial nesting; they fail when data is flat (equivalent to pie charts) or when trees are balanced (items similar in size).


- [[Accessibility]] — Data visualizations using color require accommodations for color-blind users; treemaps should employ redundant signals (text labels, patterns, hover details) alongside color encoding.
