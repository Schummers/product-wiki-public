# The 6 Steps to Roadmapping

## Summary

This article outlines a six-step process for creating and maintaining effective UX roadmaps. The process begins with establishing clear goals and identifying stakeholders, moves through gathering inputs from existing artifacts and research, creating themes by clustering problems, prioritizing themes using established criteria, visualizing and sharing the roadmap, and finally, revisiting and updating regularly. Each step is essential for creating roadmaps that stakeholders trust and that actually guide team work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Establish goals first** — Define the primary purpose of the roadmap and determine scope (product, field, or specialty level) and identify the core team and stakeholders who should be involved.
- **Gather diverse inputs** — Collect data from existing artifacts (previous roadmaps, journey maps, service blueprints) and research (qualitative/quantitative studies, support logs, surveys) to inform problem identification.
- **Create themes through clustering** — Group candidate problems into clusters using affinity diagramming, then organize each cluster into a polished theme with beneficiary, need, and business objective components.
- **Prioritize using criteria** — Establish 4-8 prioritization factors (both user-oriented and business-oriented), create a scoring scale, score each theme, and force rank to assign priorities across time horizons.
- **Visualize appropriately** — Create roadmap fidelity matching audience and goal; internal planning can be low-fidelity while stakeholder-facing roadmaps need higher fidelity with context and version tracking.
- **Revisit regularly** — Establish routine cadence (at minimum monthly) to move completed themes, update progress, add new themes, and maintain the roadmap as a living document while saving versions.

## Quotes

> A [UX roadmap](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-roadmaps/) is a strategic, living artifact that aligns, prioritizes, and communicates a UX team's future work and the problems it needs to solve.

> Roadmaps are successful when they make realistic promises, value functionality over pretty visuals, or are strategic documents instead of feature-specific release plans.

> Don't be afraid to adapt this process to your needs: make it a workshop, create a hypothesis roadmap on your own then vet it with peers, or do one step a week, over the course of a month.

## Concepts

- [[Roadmapping]] — a structured six-step approach to creating effective UX roadmaps that align teams, prioritize work, and communicate strategy to stakeholders.
- [[Prioritization]] — using established criteria and scoring frameworks to rank roadmap themes from high to low priority, balancing user needs and business objectives.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — involving the right people at each stage of roadmapping, securing buy-in and support, and communicating roadmaps appropriately to different audiences based on their interests and roles.
