# Imagery in Newsletters: Make Your Marketing Emails Visual But Don't Sacrifice Usability

## Summary

This article documents a significant shift in newsletter design toward visual communication, driven by users' expectations for high-quality imagery that matches what they see on the web. While large, relevant imagery improves user reception and engagement with marketing emails, many newsletters take the visual trend to an extreme by creating flyer-style emails where an entire message is embedded as a single graphic. This approach sacrifices usability for aesthetics: legibility suffers from poor contrast and stylistic fonts, typographic hierarchy disappears when text is not properly prioritized, content discoverability declines due to excessive negative space on mobile, preheader text becomes useless when the entire message is an image, and clickability signifiers vanish, leaving users uncertain what, if anything, is interactive. Effective visual newsletters require balancing aesthetic appeal with interaction design principles, including prominent calls-to-action, clear links, appropriate click-ability cues, and text-based content that supports email client functionality.

## Key Takeaways

- **Large, Relevant Imagery Improves Reception** — Users expect high-quality, large-scale imagery in emails that directly relates to the message content; generic stock photography and small thumbnail images feel outdated and clutter the message without adding value.
- **Flyer-Style Emails Sacrifice Usability** — Single-graphic emails that embed all text within an image create serious usability problems including poor legibility, lack of typographic hierarchy, and inability for email clients to render preheader text or access content on mobile.
- **Legibility Issues in Visual Emails** — Flyer-style designs prioritize visual appeal over readability; poor contrast, decorative fonts, and imagery that obscures text make messages difficult to parse, especially for users with vision impairments or viewing on small screens.
- **Missing Typographic Hierarchy** — Text-heavy images without hierarchy (e.g., all text in bold) overwhelm users; prioritizing information through varied font weights, sizes, and emphasis is essential for scannable, usable layouts.
- **Mobile Discovery Problems** — Excessive negative space and large graphics force mobile users to scroll extensively before encountering content; important information should be immediately visible, not buried below a large hero image.
- **Clickability Must Be Obvious** — Flyer-style emails must include prominent buttons, visual link indicators (color, underline), and clear labels for calls-to-action; users should not have to guess or experiment to find interactive elements.

## Quotes

> "Our recent research indicated that newsletter recipients expect the same high-quality imagery that they encounter on the web. They strongly prefer images that can be seen at a larger scale and show detail very clearly."

> "In comparison, dense, multicolumn emails filled with tiny images were considered "cluttered" and "outdated" by users in our most recent study."

> "It is not enough to design a beautiful flyer that looks great on a printed page. This format does not translate directly to email; it needs to be tweaked to make it useful as a user interface."

## Concepts

- [[Email Design]] — Creating marketing and transactional messages that are readable, scannable, and support interaction across diverse email clients and devices; differs significantly from web design due to client limitations.
- [[Visual Design]] — The aesthetic use of images, color, typography, and layout; in emails, visual appeal must be balanced against legibility and functional requirements like email client compatibility.
- [[Typography]] — Typography combines readability (achieved through sufficient contrast, readable font sizes, and lack of visual obstruction) with hierarchical design through font choice, size, weight, and spacing to guide users to important information; in email where resolution and rendering vary widely, hierarchy is often flattened by making all text equally prominent.
- [[Call to Action]] — The directive that tells users what to do next; must be visually prominent and clearly interactive; in flyer-style emails, CTAs buried in images are often missed or invisible.
