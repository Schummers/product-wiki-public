# How UX Professionals Collaborate on Deliverables

## Summary

This article examines how UX professionals collaborate when creating deliverables, drawing from a survey of 86 UX professionals in various roles. The research shows that 82% collaborate on deliverables (52% often, 30% sometimes), making collaboration five times more common than solitary work. Key reasons for collaboration include generating multiple design ideas early through divergent thinking, catching mistakes before stakeholder presentation via the four-eyes principle, and distributing workload across team members. The article identifies primary collaborators (developers, product managers, other UX professionals) and effective collaboration methods (design workshops, informal review sessions, joint data analysis).

## Key Takeaways

- **Collaboration Is Standard Practice** — 82% of UX professionals collaborate on deliverables, making it five times more common than solitary work; collaboration is not an exception but the norm.
- **Divergent Thinking Benefits** — Early collaboration on multiple design alternatives before converging on one direction saves effort, leads to deeper testing insights, and avoids bias by incorporating diverse perspectives from different disciplines.
- **Four Eyes Principle** — Showing deliverables to colleagues before presenting to stakeholders helps catch biases, mistakes, and blind spots (such as missed user scenarios); this cheaper approach prevents wasting user testing on problems colleagues can identify.
- **Design Workshops Drive Ideation** — In-person whiteboard sessions and sketching charrettes effectively engage cross-functional teams (developers, stakeholders, designers, product management) in early-stage ideation in a dynamic, memorable manner.
- **Multiple Evaluators Find More Issues** — The evaluator effect shows that different usability evaluators using the same method detect different problems; more UX experts reviewing research data uncover more findings and insights.
- **Strategic Collaborators** — Primary collaborators are engineers/developers (technical feasibility), other UX professionals (fresh perspective and discipline knowledge), and product/business stakeholders (requirements and goals); clients typically provide feedback during review rather than early creation.

## Concepts

- [[Team Collaboration]] — emphasizes that 82% of UX professionals collaborate on deliverables with developers, product managers, and other UX professionals; collaboration is the dominant practice for creating design and research artifacts.
- [[Ideation]] — describes divergent thinking as a key collaboration driver; teams generate multiple design alternatives early before converging on a single refined direction, avoiding bias and improving outcomes.
- [[Four Eyes Principle]] — explains the practice of showing unfinished deliverables to colleagues to catch biases, mistakes, and blind spots before presenting to wider audiences or conducting expensive user testing.
- [[Design Workshops]] — identifies in-person workshops and sketching charrettes as effective methods for cross-functional ideation, enabling developers, stakeholders, and designers to contribute early ideas in a collaborative environment.
- [[Cross-Functional Teams]] — describes how collaboration brings together developers, product managers, business analysts, designers, and executives; each role contributes distinct expertise to refine deliverables and ensure feasibility.
