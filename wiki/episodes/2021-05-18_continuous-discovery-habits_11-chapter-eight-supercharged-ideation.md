# Continuous Discovery Habits: Chapter Eight: Supercharged Ideation

## Summary

Part 3 shifts focus to generating and evaluating solutions. This chapter challenges the popular belief that brainstorming is the most effective way to generate ideas. Drawing on decades of creativity research, the chapter shows that individuals generating ideas alone outperform brainstorming groups in quantity, diversity, and originality of ideas. The chapter explains why groups suffer from social loafing, conformity bias, production blocking, and downward norm setting, yet feel they are more productive (the "illusion of group productivity"). The solution is a hybrid approach: individuals ideate alone, then share and discuss with the group, then ideate alone again, repeating this cycle until 15-20 ideas are generated. Teams then evaluate ideas by filtering for those that actually address the target opportunity and dot-voting to select three finalists for further testing.

## Key Takeaways

- **Quantity predicts quality in ideation** — Fluency (number of ideas) correlates with flexibility and originality; most original ideas emerge late in ideation, not early, so teams must push past first instincts.
- **Individuals ideate better than groups, but groups evaluate better** — Alternate individual ideation (which avoids social loafing and conformity) with group sharing (which sparks new ideas through exposure to others' work); never ideate as a group in real time.
- **Take breaks and leverage incubation** — Spread ideation across time and places; sleep on ideas to let your brain work in the background; look for inspiration in analogous products from unrelated industries.
- **Filter before dot-voting** — Remove ideas that don't address your target opportunity, then dot-vote with three votes per person to select the top three ideas, ensuring all team members are excited about at least one finalist.
- **Push for diversity, not variations** — Avoid riffing on the same idea in multiple ways; deliberately seek categorically different ideas and look beyond your industry for analogues to spark diverse solutions.

## Quotes

> "Creative teams know that quantity is the best predictor of quality."

> "You'll never stumble upon the unexpected if you stick only to the familiar."

> "In other words, as we generate more ideas, the diversity and novelty of those ideas increases. Additionally, the most original ideas tend to be generated toward the end of the ideation session."

## Concepts

- [[Ideation]] — The chapter reframes ideation as a deliberate, structured practice: individuals generate ideas alone in cycles, interrupted by group sharing and breaks, continuing until 15-20 diverse ideas are produced, then evaluating through group dot-voting to select three finalists.
- [[Product Discovery]] — Ideation is the bridge between identifying a target opportunity and testing assumptions; it generates solution candidates for the team to explore, distinguish, and refine.
- [[Decision Making]] — Dot-voting is the evaluation method: a lightweight group decision that uses experience to assess ideas against the target opportunity, setting up a compare-and-contrast mindset where three diverse ideas compete, not one champion emerges.
