# Making Cutting-Edge Technology Approachable: A Case Study of Facial-Recognition Payment in China

## Summary

Facial-recognition payment (FRP) is an emerging payment method in China that requires no device other than a face, in contrast to Apple Pay which requires a compatible iPhone. While convenient for returning users, four critical onboarding mistakes made by Ali Pay caused first-time users to distrust the technology despite its speed: not asking for consent, failing to explain how faces are recognized, not allowing users to choose payment accounts, and not requiring confirmation passwords. These mistakes generated false mental models about security risks, leading to 4 of 5 participants preferring QR-code scanning despite experiencing FRP. Better onboarding techniques—asking for explicit consent, explaining the technology, allowing customization, and providing clear confirmation screens—can help users understand and trust new technologies, particularly when they challenge existing expectations.

## Key Takeaways

- **Convenience alone is insufficient** — despite FRP's speed advantage, 4 out of 5 participants preferred existing QR-code scanning due to security concerns, demonstrating that perceived trust outweighs efficiency gains.
- **Consent must be explicitly requested** — Ali Pay added consent to user agreements but didn't notify users; participants felt unauthorized, not understanding that consent had already been obtained in terms they didn't see.
- **Onboarding must explain how technology works** — participants incorrectly believed FRP registration happened during their first use rather than using existing ID photos; they feared anyone with their phone number could register and steal their money.
- **Custom mental models overcome false mental models gradually** — providing information about how facial recognition works helps users develop accurate understanding and reduces suspicion about security risks.
- **Allow user choice in defaults** — Ali Pay forced users into specific payment accounts without choice, violating expectations formed by QR-code scanning which did allow account selection.
- **Handholding is necessary for new technologies** — even sophisticated technologies require carefully introduced explanations so users develop correct mental models and feel in control, as users did when JD unmanned stores invited pre-checkout face registration.

## Quotes

> "The key to avoiding these problems is a little extra handholding — carefully introducing the technology in a simple way, so that users can understand it and feel in control."

> "Belief overrules facts, to drive opinion."

> "While Ali Pay may have faith in the security of the technology and a good understanding of how it works, new users do not. They need to be convinced."
