# Your New UX Habit: Establishing Baselines for Impact

## Summary

This article explains why establishing baselines before any UX work begins is critical for demonstrating value. A baseline is an initial benchmark of a product's performance against a meaningful standard, providing a "before" measurement that makes "after" measurements meaningful. The article outlines how UX teams often undermine their own value by measuring too late, and provides best practices for choosing relevant metrics, tracking single metrics, prioritizing behavioral over attitudinal measures, and documenting methodology alongside results.

## Key Takeaways

- **A baseline is essential for demonstrating impact** — Without a "before" measurement, an "after" measurement is just a number; you cannot determine whether your work made a difference. A task-completion rate of 67% after a redesign only proves value if you know it was 48% before.
- **Avoid choosing metrics for convenience** — Teams commonly select easy-to-collect metrics like page views rather than behavior-linked measures like task-completion or error rates. Test any candidate metric by asking: if this number improves, does that mean users have a better experience?
- **Track one metric, not many** — Picking one well-chosen metric is far more convincing than assembling a collection after results come in, which creates opportunity for cherry-picking and undermines trust.
- **Prioritize behavioral metrics** — Behavioral metrics (completion rates, error rates, dropoff) from analytics are more defensible than attitudinal metrics (perceived ease, satisfaction) from surveys because they show what actually happened, not just how users felt.
- **Document your method alongside the number** — A baseline without documentation isn't a baseline. Record how, who, when, and what you measured so that post-launch comparison uses a comparable method.
- **Share the baseline with stakeholders before building** — When stakeholders understand the baseline upfront, post-launch conversations focus on completing a story rather than making a case for UX's impact.

## Quotes

> "the most common ways UX teams undermine their own value isn't by doing poor work, but by measuring too late."

> "A clear baseline makes later results meaningful. If a task-completion rate was 48% before the redesign and 67% after, that 19% increase proves your effect (provided that it is, indeed, statistically significant)."

> "if this number improves, does that mean users have a better experience with the thing we changed? If the answer requires multiple inferential steps, the metric is probably too indirect."

## Concepts

- [[Benchmarking]] — establishes the starting point for measuring design impact by comparing before and after measurements against a meaningful standard.
- [[UX Metrics]] — requires choosing relevant, behavior-linked metrics that directly reflect the user experience being changed and tracking them consistently from baseline through post-launch to prove whether UX work created meaningful change.
