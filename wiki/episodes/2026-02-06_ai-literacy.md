# How AI Literacy Shapes GenAI Use

## Summary

AI literacy is a multidimensional skill involving two distinct capabilities: prompt fluency (expressing intent and constraints) and output literacy (evaluating, verifying, and checking results). These dimensions develop independently; some users are fluent prompts but trust output uncritically, while others are skeptical but rarely engage. NN/g identified four user types: AI novices (low on both), naive power users (high prompt fluency, low output literacy), skeptical abstainers (high output literacy, low prompting), and AI experts (high on both). The knowledge–receptivity paradox shows that lower AI understanding predicts higher receptivity (users see AI as "magical"), explaining why adoption and critical thinking don't always align. Designers must support both dimensions through UI strategies: prompt refinement, lightweight controls, and visible verify actions.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI Literacy Is Multidimensional** — Prompt fluency and output literacy develop independently; frequent AI use doesn't guarantee critical evaluation skills.
- **Four User Types Emerge** — AI novices lack confidence; naive power users prompt well but accept outputs uncritically; skeptical abstainers evaluate well but rarely engage; experts balance both.
- **Knowledge–Receptivity Paradox** — Lower AI conceptual knowledge predicts higher receptivity; users with less understanding are more likely to see AI as "magical" and adopt enthusiastically.
- **Prompt Fluency Grows Quickly** — Prompt-fluent users include context, constraints, and multiple questions; they build on prior conversations and understand how AI builds on context.
- **Output Literacy Lags Behind Prompting** — Expert users check results, seek sources, and verify details; novices accept polished outputs without scrutiny, missing errors and gaps.
- **Design Must Support Both Dimensions** — Provide clickable prompt suggestions, quick refinements, lightweight controls for constraints, and visible verify actions to support users across literacy levels.

## Concepts

- [[AI Literacy]] — AI literacy requires prompt fluency and output literacy; neither dimension alone ensures effective AI use; design must address both to serve diverse user types.
- [[Mental Model]] — Users conflate genAI with search engines, expecting different interaction paradigms; education and design must align interface behavior with user expectations.
- [[Prompting Techniques]] — Prompt fluency can be taught; longer, context-rich prompts with clear constraints yield better outputs; prompt suggestions and examples guide novices.
- [[Information Evaluation]] — Output literacy requires behavioral skills: verifying details, seeking sources, crosschecking, and treating confident-sounding answers as provisional when stakes are high.
- [[Inclusive Design]] — Supporting varied literacy levels through progressive disclosure, visible guidance, and adaptive interfaces helps novices while preserving efficiency for experts.
