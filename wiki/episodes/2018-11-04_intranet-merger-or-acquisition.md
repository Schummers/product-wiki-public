# Intranet Design After a Merger or Acquisition

## Summary

This article provides guidance on designing and redesigning intranets during mergers and acquisitions. Mergers often create conditions that lead to excellent intranet redesigns because multiple experienced teams collaborate, existing designs provide ideas to learn from, clear management direction exists, and intranet teams gain autonomy. However, there are critical mistakes to avoid, such as hiding evidence of the old organization without explanation, leaving everything unchanged, hastily choosing one intranet over another, or imposing identical rules for internal and external websites. The article emphasizes treating employees as humans with real anxiety, confusion, and excitement.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mergers create favorable conditions for intranet excellence** — Multiple experienced teams, existing designs to learn from, clear management goals, and autonomous intranet teams combine to produce better designs than might be created under normal circumstances.

- **Don't remove features and content without explaining why** — Hiding evidence of the old organization causes confusion and demotivation; employees need transparency about what has changed and why to feel secure.

- **Avoid leaving everything unchanged or hastily choosing one intranet** — Implicitly keeping multiple intranets or choosing the wrong one leads to duplication, lost productivity, and messages that the company is disorganized or that one group was devalued.

- **Create a two-stage strategy: day-one and long-term solutions** — An immediate interim solution addresses urgent needs while longer-term research and design create the ideal final intranet, reducing pressure and allowing quality work.

- **Separate internal and external branding needs** — Rules for public-facing websites may not apply internally; employees need freedom to reference deprecated brand names in parentheses to maintain recognition and comfort.

- **Design for three employee emotional states: anxiety, confusion, and excitement** — Successful post-merger intranets acknowledge that employees are anxious about job security, confused about what has changed, and potentially excited about new opportunities.

## Concepts

- [[Intranet Design]] — internal websites that serve as the primary communication and information hub for employees, requiring particular attention to organizational changes and employee needs during mergers.

- [[Information Architecture]] — organizing and integrating information from multiple organizations into a coherent structure that employees can navigate and understand.

- [[Change Management]] — helping employees understand and adapt to organizational changes by providing clear communication and maintaining transparency about what has changed and why.

- [[Organizational Culture]] — how intranet design must reflect and support new organizational structures, hierarchies, and ways of working post-merger.

- [[User Research]] — the critical importance of understanding employee mental models, concerns, and information needs during organizational upheaval to design effective intranets.
