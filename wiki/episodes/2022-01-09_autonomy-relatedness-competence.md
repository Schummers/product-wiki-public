# Autonomy, Relatedness, and Competence in UX Design

## Summary

Nielsen Norman Group explores self-determination theory, developed by psychologists Richard Ryan and Edward Deci, which identifies three basic psychological needs that drive human motivation and well-being: autonomy, relatedness, and competence. People are motivated by experiences that meet these needs, and they experience greater psychological well-being when all three are satisfied. In UX design, meeting these three needs creates deep delight and motivates users to continue using products and services. While these three needs can occasionally conflict (requiring user testing to determine priorities), well-designed products and services often successfully address all three.

## Key Takeaways

- **Autonomy: users need control over their choices** — Design that incorporates autonomy lets users configure apps, pick customized colors, or view content at their own pace; allowing people to choose how they use an interface leads them to place greater personal value on the product through their investment of time and energy.
- **Relatedness: connection through understanding** — Relatedness develops over longer periods but is influenced by many small interactions; designers can demonstrate understanding by limiting messaging to what users perceive as helpful and relevant, and by facilitating communication between individuals.
- **Competence: users must become proficient** — Supporting competence means helping users master a system, often more effectively through just-in-time help (pull methods) than upfront tutorials; competent use of a system leads to greater satisfaction, safety, efficiency, and positive outcomes.
- **The three needs can conflict and require trade-offs** — Wizards increase competence by guiding users through complex tasks but reduce autonomy by limiting user control; user testing determines whether users prefer surrendering autonomy for competence or struggling more to preserve autonomy.
- **Minecraft and Figma exemplify meeting all three needs** — Open-world video games and collaborative design tools succeed by giving users freedom (autonomy), enabling collaboration (relatedness), and providing helpful resources and forums for learning (competence).
- **Meeting all three needs increases motivation and well-being** — Users will be more motivated to use and enjoy products that accommodate their needs for autonomy, relatedness, and competence, regardless of industry context.

## Concepts

- [[Self-Determination Theory]] — Developed by Ryan and Deci, this theory describes three basic psychological needs (autonomy, relatedness, competence) that drive human motivation and well-being.
- [[User Behavior]] — Meeting users' needs for autonomy, relatedness, and competence increases their motivation to use products and provides deep delight, even when performing tasks they have to do rather than want to do.
- [[Autonomy]] — UX design meets autonomy by allowing users control over choices, customization, and pacing; forcing users into one predetermined way unnecessarily limits autonomy and reduces motivation.
- [[Relatedness]] — Users feel related to products when designers demonstrate understanding through well-timed, relevant messaging and by facilitating communication between individuals.
- [[Competence]] — Supporting competence requires helping users master systems through just-in-time help and instruction at moments of need rather than upfront tutorials that increase cognitive load.
