# Card Sorting: Pushing Users Beyond Terminology Matches

## Summary

In card-sorting studies, participants often group cards based on similar keywords rather than deep consideration of conceptual relationships, a phenomenon called keyword matching. This superficial grouping undermines the research value by not accurately reflecting users' mental models of how items relate to one another. The article provides strategies for preventing keyword matching through careful card labeling (using synonyms, non-parallel structures, detailed descriptions) and facilitation techniques (explaining objectives, encouraging thinking aloud, allowing iterative refinement).

## Key Takeaways

- **Keyword matching is a significant research problem** — participants group cards based on word similarities (e.g., all cards with "Employee") rather than conceptually organizing items by meaning and relationship.
- **Superficial groupings don't reflect mental models** — keyword matching produces vague category labels that don't represent how users actually think about relationships between items.
- **Use synonyms to disrupt keyword patterns** — replacing parallel language (e.g., "Staff Directory" instead of "Employee Directory") forces participants to consider meaning rather than matching words.
- **Non-parallel structures increase cognitive load** — rephrasing cards with different grammatical structures (e.g., "Benefits for Employees" vs. "Employee Benefits") prevents quick scanning and word-matching.
- **In-depth descriptions add contextual meaning** — replacing short labels with descriptive phrases that explain purpose and medium encourages participants to reflect on content meaning.
- **Facilitation techniques encourage deeper thinking** — explaining study objectives, asking participants to think aloud, requesting subcategories, and allowing iterative changes help participants move beyond keyword matching.

## Quotes

> "Keyword matching is a problem because it focuses on superficial similarities rather than conceptual relationships between items."

> "Effective card sorting goes beyond keyword matches. Card labels should encourage users to deeply consider each card and its association with others, which is crucial for the usefulness of your study."

> "Card labels should compel participants to recognize that *Employee Directory*, *Employee Benefits*, and *Employee Online Training* serve distinct purposes, despite all containing the word *Employee*."
