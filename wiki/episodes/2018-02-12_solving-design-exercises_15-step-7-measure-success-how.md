# Solving Product Design Exercises: Step 7: Measure success (How)

## Summary

The final step of the design exercise framework asks candidates to define how they would measure whether their solution succeeded. Dashinsky explains that measuring success involves selecting Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) without necessarily specifying the numeric thresholds—a more limited scope than real-world product work. The chapter provides eight example metrics (task success rate, completion time, engagement, retention, revenue, conversion, user acquisition, net promoter score) and demonstrates how to articulate success criteria for a specific design challenge. It also covers bonus territory: MVP validation strategies and the time allocation for a complete one-hour exercise.

## Key Takeaways

- **Define KPIs without threshold numbers** — For design exercises, candidates need only propose which metrics matter, not the exact numbers that would indicate success; in real product work, product managers or analysts make final calls on these targets.
- **Use relevant metrics for the problem** — A doctor-facing dashboard designed to increase patients served per day should track task completion time and patient satisfaction (NPS); metric selection depends on the specific goal and problem statement.
- **Propose validation experiments** — Bonus points come from suggesting an MVP or experiment to validate the solution before full investment; this demonstrates thoroughness and product thinking.
- **Time allocation across seven steps** — In a one-hour exercise, allocate roughly 3 minutes for goals, 5 minutes for audience, 5 for context, 5 for ideation, 3 for prioritization, 30 for solving, and 3 for measuring success, with 5 minutes reserved for presentation prep.

## Quotes

> How would we know that the solution was successful?

> As a final step, before shipping this product, I'd work with the product manager to understand what KPIs we should measure and what would be considered a success.

> With a real product, the final call on these aspects will be made by a product manager or a data analyst.

## Concepts

- [[UX Metrics]] — Quantifiable indicators such as task success rate, task completion time, engagement, retention, revenue, conversion, user acquisition, and NPS that demonstrate whether a designed solution achieves its intended outcomes.
- [[Design Exercise]] — The closing step of an interview exercise where candidates articulate how they would validate and measure the impact of their proposed solution.
- [[Product Thinking]] — Understanding that a successful design must be paired with measurable business outcomes and showing awareness of the need to collaborate with product managers on data-driven validation.
- [[Business Impact]] — Connecting design decisions to company goals by selecting metrics that reflect both user success (task completion, satisfaction) and business success (revenue, conversion, acquisition).
