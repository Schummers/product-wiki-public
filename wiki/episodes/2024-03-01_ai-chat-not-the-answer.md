# AI Chat Is Not (Always) the Answer

## Summary

Organizations are rushing to integrate AI chatbots into products, driven by ChatGPT's success and stakeholder pressure. However, AI chat is a specific solution suited to particular problems, not a universal fix. Two types exist: universal AI chatbots (like ChatGPT) trained on broad knowledge, and product-specific chatbots embedded in apps with narrower knowledge. Good use cases include customer support, where AI can provide faster responses than humans. Poor examples include LinkedIn's AI-powered follow-up suggestions that add chat functionality without solving real user problems. Before implementing AI chat, organizations should validate three criteria: does it solve a real, widespread user need; does it align with product strategy; and does it scale with advancing technology. Alternative AI implementations—personalization, predictive analytics, strategic automation—often better address user needs than conversational interfaces.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI chat serves specific, not universal, use cases** — appropriate for customer support where speed matters, not for adding functionality for its own sake.
- **Distinguish universal from product-specific AI** — universal chatbots like ChatGPT serve broad purposes; product-specific chatbots answer narrowly scoped questions.
- **Not everything should be a chatbot** — many organizations add chat without identifying real user problems, creating friction instead of value.
- **Validate three criteria before implementing** — does it solve a real user need, align with strategy, and scale with evolving technology.
- **Many alternatives to chat exist** — personalization, predictive analytics, strategic automation, and email automation often solve problems better than conversation.
- **AI doesn't have to be anthropomorphic** — invisible AI powering personalization or analytics adds value without conversational UI.
