# AI Adoption in the Workplace Still Low, 2 Years Later

## Summary

Two years after ChatGPT's release, Pew Research survey data shows that 81% of US workers do not use AI, contradicting optimistic 2023 predictions of rapid workplace transformation. While 55% of workers rarely or never use AI chatbots and 29% haven't even heard of them, those who do use AI (concentrated in tech, data, finance, and information industries) rate its helpfulness highly. Younger workers adopt AI more than older cohorts, reflecting a historic pattern of early-adopter demographics. Most AI usage remains simple—information seeking, editing, and drafting—not sophisticated analysis or coding. Design teams must account for low baseline familiarity: not all users will be experienced with AI, and many still need basic onboarding to understand AI capabilities relevant to their workflows.

## Key Takeaways

- **Most American workers are not using AI for work** — 81% of surveyed workers reported little or no AI use; 55% rarely or never use AI chatbots; 29% haven't heard of popular AI products, showing adoption lags far behind hype predictions.
- **AI adoption is concentrated in digitally focused occupations** — Workers in data processing, information technology, finance, and insurance adopt AI more; only 9% of non-users report employer restrictions, indicating adoption is driven by perceived value rather than barriers.
- **Younger workers adopt AI more and use it more sophisticatedly** — 73% of AI adopters were under 50; workers aged 18–29 most frequently use AI chatbots; younger adopters also use AI for ideation and coding, not just information seeking.
- **Most AI usage involves simple, sequential tasks** — 57% use AI for information seeking, 52% for editing, 47% for drafting; fewer use it for ideation (35%), analysis/coding (27%), or media creation (21%).
- **Perception of AI usefulness increases with usage frequency** — Frequent users (at least monthly) rate AI helpfulness at 54%, while infrequent users rate it at 25%; mental-model shifts and competence building take time.
- **You are likely more AI-familiar than your users** — Designers often work in tech environments and socialize with similar people, creating false-consensus bias; assuming users share your AI familiarity can lead to poor design decisions.

## Quotes
