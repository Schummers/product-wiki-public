# What a UX Career Looks Like Today

## Summary

Based on responses from over 700 UX professionals, this article presents the second edition of the User Experience Careers report, comparing findings with the first edition from 2013. The research reveals what has remained constant (career satisfaction, role diversity, industry distribution, soft-skills emphasis) and what has changed (global expansion, specialization analysis, increased mobile and AI work). The article also addresses career transitions and provides advice for practitioners new to the field.

## Key Takeaways

- **Career Satisfaction Stable** — UX practitioners rate career satisfaction identically to 2013 (5.4 on 1-7 scale); satisfaction stems from enjoying the process, seeing work impact, receiving recognition, and having growth opportunities.
- **Job Titles Remain Diverse** — 2019 survey respondents report 134 unique UX job titles (slightly down from 210 in 2013), showing the field still lacks job-title standardization.
- **Soft Skills Critical** — soft skills remain the most important for gaining and succeeding in UX roles; while hard skills are desirable, hiring managers and practitioners prioritize soft skills as core requirements.
- **UX Field Globalization** — UX has emerged as a field in many more countries; 2019 study received responses from 65 countries versus 38 in 2013, with increased non-English speaking country representation.
- **Specialization Analysis New** — the report newly analyzes differences between researchers (focused on UX research), designers (broad responsibilities, design-related backgrounds), and generalists.
- **Designer Profile** — UX designers have broad responsibilities from prototyping to collaboration; tend to have design-related education; often have front-end coding skills; most desired skill is data analysis.
- **Researcher Profile** — UX researchers focus specifically on research rather than design or IA; strong communication skills in writing and public speaking; less likely to have front-end coding; most desired skill is visual design.
- **Career Transition Possible** — people can successfully transition into UX from other fields; can slowly start doing UX within existing organization; relevant experience from other fields is an asset.
- **Advice for New Practitioners** — keep learning and stay curious, respect others, be confident despite impostor syndrome, and take charge of your own career progression.
