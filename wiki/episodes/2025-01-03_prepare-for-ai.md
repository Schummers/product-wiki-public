# Redefine Your Design Skills to Prepare for AI

## Summary

Generative AI is significantly impacting job markets and the design profession. Demand for automation-prone jobs fell 21% within eight months of ChatGPT's release. Designers must navigate these disruptions by redefining their skills, focusing on strategic thinking while offloading tactical tasks to AI, maintaining critical scrutiny of AI outputs, designing for both human users and AI agents, embracing team augmentation, and addressing unequal effects to prioritize human well-being. The article presents five principles for staying relevant as AI transforms what designers do and how they collaborate.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI affects tasks through automation and augmentation** — Automation makes tasks more efficient, while augmentation enables designers to perform tasks previously outside their expertise; both increase productivity and competition.
- **Embrace strategic work, outsource tactical tasks** — Focus on high-impact strategic activities like defining product vision while delegating predictable tactical work like mockup generation to AI, but avoid automating strategic decisions.
- **Balance trust with scrutiny** — AI systems reflect biases in their training data and algorithms; designers must question AI outputs and validate them against real user research to avoid perpetuating inequities.
- **Design for dual audiences** — Future systems must serve both human users and AI agents making decisions on users' behalf; this requires rethinking not just interfaces but the broader dynamics of service delivery.
- **Team augmentation creates functional overlap** — AI enables crossfunctional team members to contribute to domains outside their expertise, potentially redrawing organizational boundaries and enabling radical interdisciplinary collaboration.
- **Address unequal effects for equity** — Technological shifts risk exacerbating existing inequalities; designers must embed equity and inclusivity into practice from the start.

## Quotes

> Automation doesn't mean that tasks will be completely delegated to AI. Rather, it enables designers to work efficiently by combining AI capabilities with human oversight.

> To stay competitive and relevant, designers must be prepared to shift how they think about their skillset.

> By staying grounded in critical thinking, we ensure that AI augments our work without compromising our responsibility to create equitable, inclusive, and meaningful user experiences.
