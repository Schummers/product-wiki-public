# AR-Onboarding Walkthroughs in Mobile Apps

## Summary

Most users are unfamiliar with augmented reality technology; many confuse it with virtual reality and have limited hands-on experience with AR applications. Because AR presents novel interaction patterns that are unfamiliar to most users, onboarding becomes critical to help users understand how to interact with AR features. This article presents findings from a usability study with 11 participants testing AR apps across education, health, fitness, art, history, ecommerce, and entertainment domains, examining how onboarding approaches—including contextual tips and instructional walkthroughs—can improve user success with AR experiences.

The study found that while both contextual tips and instructional walkthroughs can assist users, the effectiveness varies by approach. Static walkthroughs, such as deck-of-cards presentations, often cause cognitive overload and strain working memory, leaving users unable to remember information and requiring them to replay the tutorial. Interactive walkthroughs that allow users to practice AR interactions were significantly more effective, giving users hands-on practice and building confidence. Effective AR walkthroughs should clearly communicate the purpose of the feature, explain how users should handle their device, and provide guidance on environmental preparation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Most users are unfamiliar with AR** — many confuse it with virtual reality and have limited prior experience with AR applications, making onboarding necessary to teach novel interaction patterns.
- **Interactive walkthroughs are more effective than static ones** — users experienced cognitive overload with static presentations like deck-of-cards layouts, but appreciated interactive walkthroughs that let them practice tasks.
- **Clarify the purpose of the AR experience first** — focus initially on high-level purpose (e.g., visualizing furniture in your room) before presenting low-level instructions about specific controls.
- **Explain device handling requirements** — users were often unaware that AR relies on the device camera; clear instructions about holding the phone, angle, height, and orientation prevent confusion and misuse.
- **Provide step-by-step environmental guidance** — users need timely instructions on lighting, surface quality, space size, and safety considerations before launching the AR experience.
- **Combine text, visuals, and audio instructions** — especially when users must hold the device away from their body, audio instructions can supplement written text and help users follow along.

## Concepts

- [[Augmented Reality]] — AR users need clear walkthroughs that combine purpose, device handling, and environmental guidance; interactive walkthroughs are more effective than static presentations.
- [[Cognitive Load]] — static walkthroughs with extensive information cause cognitive overload and strain working memory, forcing users to replay instructions to retain details.
- [[Interactive Walkthroughs]] — allowing users to practice AR tasks in guided walkthroughs improves comprehension and builds confidence compared to passive demonstrations.
- [[Mobile Design]] — AR apps must provide multi-modal instructions (text, visual, audio) tailored to unfamiliar user populations; clarity about device orientation and environmental requirements prevents failure.
