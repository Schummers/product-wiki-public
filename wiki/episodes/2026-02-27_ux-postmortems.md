# Project Postmortems for UX Teams: Learning from Success and Failure

## Summary

Postmortems are a structured method borrowed from engineering to examine completed projects and extract learnings to prevent failures and replicate successes. Unlike sprint retrospectives, which focus on team process, postmortems examine specific project outcomes to understand why a launch succeeded or failed. The article argues that postmortems are underutilized in product development and offers practical guidance on when to conduct them, what should be included, and how to ensure they drive systemic change.

## Key Takeaways

- **Learn from success, not just failure** — Postmortems should examine both successful and failed projects; success often reveals more useful patterns than failure.
- **Timing matters** — Conduct postmortems after sufficient data exists to evaluate success (not too early) but while details are still fresh (not too late).
- **Root-cause analysis** — Use techniques like the five whys to dig past immediate causes to uncover systemic issues that can be fixed across future projects.
- **Actionable deliverables required** — Every postmortem must produce concrete process changes, new requirements, or updated documentation; vague recommendations are ineffective.
- **Own each action** — Assign an owner and deadline to every postmortem action item; without follow-up, postmortems produce no change.
- **Psychological safety essential** — Frame postmortems around system failures, not personal blame, so teams share honestly about what happened.

## Quotes

> "A postmortem is a structured analysis of a completed project that examines what happened, why it happened, and what systemic changes are needed as a result."

> "Success isn't magic. It has causes, and you can identify them."

> "People make mistakes; systems should catch them."
