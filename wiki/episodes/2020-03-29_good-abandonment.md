# Good Abandonment on Search Results Pages

## Summary

This article examines good abandonment, a phenomenon where search-engine users find complete answers through SERP features (snippets, knowledge panels, etc.) without clicking through to actual websites. While generally efficient for users, good abandonment raises questions about accuracy, critical thinking, and information quality assessment. The article explores when good abandonment succeeds and when it fails, then offers strategies for content creators adapting to answer engines.

## Key Takeaways

- **Good Abandonment Definition** — when a user's information need is successfully addressed entirely by the SERP itself with no need to click results or reformulate queries, usually perceived as positive for users seeking quick answers.
- **Answer Engines Transition** — search engines have evolved from simple link providers to answer engines; SERP features like snippets, knowledge panels, and People Also Ask increasingly provide direct answers extracted from sources.
- **Time-Efficiency Benefit** — good abandonment saves users time and effort for simple factual needs; users are satisficers who collect information until sufficiently confident in decisions, then stop rather than exhaustively research.
- **Accuracy Risks** — when snippets extract information out of context or answers are subtly wrong, users relying on featured snippets make incorrect decisions; users often don't read closely enough to catch errors.
- **Credibility Assessment Impossible** — assessing information quality and credibility is difficult when answers are extracted and reformatted by search engines; poor design of sources becomes invisible when only excerpts appear.
- **Reduced Click-Through Competition** — desktop searches end without clicks about 35% of the time (up 9% since 2016), rising to 62% on mobile; visibility in results still builds brand awareness even without clicks.
- **Content Creator Strategies** — provide high-quality answers to common questions; claim and optimize knowledge panels; study voice search queries which tend to prompt how/what-is questions featured in snippets.

## Quotes

> **Good abandonment** is when the user's information need is successfully and entirely addressed by the search-results page, with no need to click on a result or reformulate the query.

> Such an assessment of information quality and credibility is often impossible when the answer is extracted and placed on the SERP. The content has been reformatted by Google, and only a short excerpt of the content is presented, even in a featured snippet.

> This raises an ethical question for designers: We spend so much time making things as easy as possible for users — is there a line we cross? Are we chipping away at people's capacity for critical thinking or judgement?
