# Venture Studios Demystified: Other Assumptions

## Summary

Part 4 documents the key variables and assumptions underlying the financial models. The chapter details the time required to raise successive funding rounds, the sources of ideas within a studio (structured programs, full-time positions, or assigned concepts), team resource constraints, and the typical process timeline. These operational levers directly impact studio efficiency and cash burn, making them critical to model accuracy and real-world studio performance.

## Key Takeaways

- **Accelerated fundraising timelines improve viability** — Studio companies reach Seed in 11 months versus 36 months for traditional startups, and Series A in 14.5 months versus 20 months, enabling faster exits and earlier cash returns to the studio.
- **EIR program outcomes vary widely** — Success rates for EIR-led companies range from 3 of 50 (6%) to 1-4 selected from 200 applicants with all launching; programs typically run 3-6 months and EIRs receive salary but share costs during validation and MVP phases.
- **Team capacity constrains idea throughput** — Each ideating team member can manage 1-3 ideas simultaneously, making team size a direct constraint on how many companies a studio can launch in parallel.
- **Standard process duration is 12 months** — The model assumes a 12-month end-to-end process from concept to spinout, though actual timelines range 6-18 months depending on studio approach and market conditions.
- **Multiple idea paths require different assumptions** — Studios source ideas through structured founder programs, limited full-time EIR positions, or assignment to developed concepts; no single path is financially superior and choice depends on studio philosophy and inputted assumptions.

## Quotes

> "Part of the value of venture studios is that upfront testing and work not only leads to a higher chance of success, but also faster times to exit. GSSN found that the time from zero to Seed for a studio was 11 months, as compared to 36 months for a traditional startup."

> "The second lever, how many succeed in launching a business, varied substantially across the studios. One studio quoted a success rate of 3 out of 50 EIRs in their 3-month program. Another studio claimed to select 1-4 EIRs from a pool of 200, and all would result in launched companies."

> "For internal ideas, team members need to work through customer validation and prototyping. Thus, understanding how many ideas any single team member can manage at a single point in time is critical for studio founders."
