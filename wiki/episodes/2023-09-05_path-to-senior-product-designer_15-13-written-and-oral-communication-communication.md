# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 13. Written and Oral Communication (Communication)

## Summary

This chapter focuses on the mechanics of how to communicate rather than what to communicate (covered in the preceding chapter on design presentations). It addresses two contexts: written communication (collaboration tools, feedback, emails, documentation) and oral communication (design critiques, manager check-ins, presentations). As companies grow and remote work becomes standard, these communication skills become increasingly critical for scaling knowledge and building influence.

## Key Takeaways

- **Practice oral communication at design critique meetings** — The author says presenting weekly at a recurring critique was one of the things with the biggest impact on his own growth; it builds presentation skills, feedback skills, confidence, and oral communication ability, and he calls it the best way he knows of practising them. Solo designers should find a sparring partner or create a mutual weekly critique with another designer, following a simplified presentation structure: explain why you designed it, state what feedback you want, walk through the solution.
- **Record yourself to improve speaking** — Review your own recordings to identify filler words, body language, pace, and intonation; use AI tools like Speeko for real-time feedback; or hire a communications coach on a platform like Fiverr, where the chapter reports prices of $35 to $300. Recording yourself is, the chapter says, the first thing public speaking coaches advise.
- **The Pyramid Principle structures complex messages** — Start with the key idea (answer or conclusion), group supporting arguments, and provide evidence for each. This top-down approach works for presentations, written communication, and emails. You can respond to a specific point using just a number rather than repeating words.
- **Replace qualifiers with facts to build trust** — Instead of "A lot of users can't find X," say "12% of users can't find X." Checking exact numbers provides better context, appears more credible, may change your opinion if the reality differs from assumption, and leads to better business decisions.
- **Writing is design: apply design principles to text** — Create hierarchy by starting with the most important things; communicate maximum information with minimum elements; make text scannable and skimmable; use visual structure (bullet points, numbering); tailor to audience; iterate from structure to fidelity. Use shorter, simpler words; remove unnecessary phrases and words; shorten sentences. Move main messages to the beginning of sentences rather than the end.

## Quotes

> "As companies grow, the knowledge has to be shared. Text is easy to share with a lot of people and, as a result, easier to scale. Therefore, at big companies, designers work more on documentation, written status updates, presentations, etc."

> "The Pyramid Principle is a communication method created by former McKinsey consultant Barbara Minto. It helps organize and articulate complex ideas."

> "All these principles are true for both design and written communication. I'd argue that designers have a better starting point for being effective at written communication than other tech professionals (besides copywriters, of course)."

## Concepts

- [[Design Presentation]] — Oral communication through regular practice at design critique meetings, one-to-one critiques with sparring partners, and recording yourself to identify speaking improvements in pace, filler words, intonation, and body language.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Design critique meetings and mutual feedback sessions structure how teams collaborate and improve together; the simplified critique structure (why you designed it, what feedback you want, walkthrough of solution) mirrors the broader presentation framework.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Written communication skills determine how effectively you reach and influence different stakeholders; clarity, hierarchy, factual evidence, and scannability make messages more credible and actionable across audiences from executives to developers.
