# Translating UX Goals into Analytics Measurement Plans

## Summary

Tracking metrics without first defining user-experience goals produces meaningless data. This article emphasizes starting with high-level goals and working down to behavioral signals and metrics (goal-down approach) rather than starting with what's easy to measure and inferring insights backward. The key is repeatedly asking "Why?" to uncover root goals, then identifying user behaviors that signal goal achievement, and finally designing measurements for those behaviors.

## Key Takeaways

- **Define goals before choosing metrics** — Tracking metrics for their own sake wastes time; first understand what you truly want to know and what actions you'd take with that information.
- **Ask "Why?" repeatedly** — Dig down to root goals using the toddler strategy; video plays matter only if they lead to contact form submissions, which matter only if they drive sales.
- **Think goals, not metrics** — Real goals are never "reduce bounce rate" or "increase page views"; goals describe experiences (engagement, helpfulness, appropriate audience match).
- **Identify behavioral signals** — Determine observable user actions that indicate goal achievement: scrolling depth, widget interaction, photo zooming, return visit patterns.
- **Narrow to strongest signals** — Focus on primary indicators rather than tracking every possible proxy, simplifying measurement and maintaining focus.
- **Measure actual benefits** — For time-saving features, measure actual time-on-task improvement, not adoption rates; track completion rates and error reduction.
- **Focus on user-experience outcomes** — Good design isn't about clicks or page views; measure whether tasks become easier, time decreases, errors reduce, or engagement improves.
- **Connect to business impact** — User-experience improvements drive loyalty and business value; show ROI through performance metrics that demonstrate benefits to users.

## Quotes

> Do not solve the problem that's asked of you. It's almost always the wrong problem.

> If you don't clearly define the goal, it is impossible to determine the best method to track performance or user experience.

> Good design is not about getting more clicks or more page views, so why would we measure success that way?
