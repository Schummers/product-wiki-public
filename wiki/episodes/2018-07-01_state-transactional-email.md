# The State of Transactional Email

## Summary

Transactional emails (purchase confirmations, shipping notifications, delivery alerts, registration updates) have improved significantly over the past decade. Fear of spam has decreased due to advanced spam-blocking tools, and text-only emails are no longer the norm—HTML, images, and emojis are now common. In a recent diary study, transactional emails received an average rating of 5.9 out of 7 for desirable characteristics. However, some companies still fail to make transactional emails a priority, missing opportunities to strengthen customer relationships and encourage future purchases.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prioritize core utility over marketing** — Transactional emails must communicate information quickly, easily, and completely. They should answer all user questions and not require extra work like contacting the company or clicking links unless necessary.
- **Avoid aggressive marketing tactics** — Users quickly become annoyed if emails are cramped with ads or too frequent. Aggressive sales approaches undermine the opportunity to build customer relationships.
- **Optimize for mobile reading** — Users are likely to check email on mobile devices, making emails that work on both small and large screens essential. Mobile impatience means users want quick answers.
- **Leverage preheader text effectively** — The summary text following the subject line should provide valuable information to users in the inbox, not waste space with long subject lines or uninformative text.
- **Maintain email as utilitarian entity** — Design should keep emails succinct and informative, with entertainment or cleverness only included if it doesn't interfere with delivering the core message.
