# User-Ecosystem Thinking: An Anthropologic Approach to Design

## Summary

Traditional UX frameworks imagine users as isolated, intentional actors with 1-to-1 relationships to products. This model misses the reality that products exist within complex ecosystems of interconnected people, relationships, and processes. Drawing on anthropology, Mike Youngblood and Ben Chesluk (authors of *Rethinking Users*) propose user-ecosystem thinking focused on roles and relationships rather than isolated individuals. Personas and user journeys miss how design decisions ripple across a system; a surgical tool affects not just the surgeon but nurses, technicians, and maintenance staff. As AI and smart devices proliferate, the autonomic user emerges—where technology is so intermingled with human identity that distinguishing user from object becomes impossible. Anthropological mindsets of questioning assumptions, mapping power dynamics, and embracing context are increasingly critical for designing resilient, inclusive systems.

## Key Takeaways

- **Users Exist in Ecosystems, Not Isolation** — Traditional models of individual users fail to capture the complex, interconnected relationships and roles within systems; design decisions ripple far beyond the primary intended user.
- **User Archetypes Replace Personas** — Rather than personas based on demographics and personality, archetypes categorize roles and relationships; archetypes emphasize function within a system and how that role interacts with other parts.
- **Context is Core, Not Supplementary** — Anthropology teaches that understanding context isn't just helpful but foundational; what works for one group may not work for others, and systemic effects matter.
- **The Autonomic User Emerges** — With AI and smart devices, users and technology become so intermingled that distinguishing subject from object becomes difficult; users and their tools are becoming cybernetic wholes.
- **Systems Thinking is Essential** — Comfort with how people, technology, and processes fit together is increasingly crucial as teams build interconnected systems where humans collaborate with AI tools.
- **Anthropological Mindset Matters** — Questioning assumptions, mapping power dynamics, tracing how people adapt products unpredictably, and staying curious about long-term systemic effects prevent costly oversights.

## Quotes

> The impact of design decisions ripples far beyond the intended primary user, and the best design considers organic relationships people may have with each other, with their physical spaces, in addition to the digital products they're using, as part of what makes a successful interaction.

> User-ecosystem thinking focuses on interconnected roles and relationships, rather than on users as isolated individuals.
