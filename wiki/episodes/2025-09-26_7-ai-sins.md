# 7 Deadly AI Sins for UX Professionals

## Summary

Drawing on nearly 400 hours of conversations with 3,000+ UX professionals, the author identifies seven temptations that weaken UX practitioners when working with AI. Each sin pairs with a corresponding virtue: outsourcing thinking (ownership), wasted time (automation), lost details (selectivity), isolated ideation (inclusion), naive trust (skepticism), bland taste (originality), and defensive outlook (experimentation). The article argues that while AI is useful, repeatedly succumbing to these temptations erodes professional skills and work quality.

## Key Takeaways

- **Think first, ask AI second** — Outsourcing all thinking to AI creates "brain drain"; use AI as a partner for your own thinking, not a guide replacing your judgment.
- **Automate repetitive workflows, not one-offs** — AI saves time on tasks you do repeatedly (where custom GPTs and reusable prompts pay off); one-off uses waste more time than they save.
- **Engage directly with details when responsible** — When you're accountable for accuracy, engage with all source material; use AI summaries only when you're informed rather than responsible for understanding details.
- **Keep humans in the ideation room** — Collaborative thinking aligns teams and builds commitment; AI can help tee up conversations with structured prompts, but removing humans from ideation reduces buy-in for outputs.
- **Verify outputs skeptically** — AI is weak on niche topics and specific expertise; find trustworthy sources for guidance rather than treating "ChatGPT said" as persuasive evidence.
- **Refine generically bland AI outputs for originality** — Pure AI suggestions fall into predictable patterns; your taste and discernment differentiate valuable work from commoditized, unoriginal output.
- **Experiment thoughtfully on inefficiencies** — Rather than resisting AI outright, identify specific bottlenecks and automation opportunities, then test carefully rather than forcing AI into every task.

## Quotes

> "If AI disappeared tomorrow, could you still confidently do what you do?"

> "What you gain in efficiency, you lose in richness and nuance."
