# Five User Requirements for Online Ads

## Summary

Analysis of 300 open-ended user responses about online advertising, combined with earlier Nielsen Norman Group research, identifies five user requirements for ads: user control, instant gratification, placement, predictability, and relevance. Users complain when ads force them to watch content, delay access to primary content, obstruct important information, behave unpredictably (autoplay), or show irrelevant content. Modal ads, autoplay videos, and intracontent ads violate multiple requirements and rank as most hated. The article provides five practical guidelines for designers and marketers to reduce ad annoyance and build customer relationships.

## Key Takeaways

- **User control matters most** — Users resent ads that force interaction (modal windows, unskippable videos). Ads users can dismiss or skip receive significantly more positive sentiment than mandatory formats.

- **Instant gratification: don't delay content** — Ads that block or delay access to primary content increase cognitive load and frustration. Prevideo ads, modals, and slow-loading ads all violate this principle; users want immediate access to their desired content.

- **Placement affects acceptance** — Right-rail ads and related links don't obstruct content and occupy conventionally ad-designated space. In-content ads that shuffle page layout or cover primary content generate significant annoyance.

- **Predictability reduces surprise** — Autoplay audio/video violates user expectations; users assume they control content playback. Ads in expected locations (right rail) feel predictable; ads appearing mid-article feel jarring.

- **Relevance increases tolerance, but not beyond a point** — Related links and retargeting ads based on user behavior are better-tolerated. However, overly specific retargeting feels creepy and intrusive, especially if targeting the same item repeatedly or ruining surprises.

## Quotes

> This effect is only true to a point. Ads which are "too" relevant tend to become creepy and intrusive, and lose their perceived value.

> The challenge for marketers and advertisers lies in the delicate balance between quantity and quality. It is understandable to attempt to reach as many eyeballs and eardrums as physically possible with a given budget, but our research findings are clear.

> Be polite. Ensure your user consents to seeing the information being presented.
