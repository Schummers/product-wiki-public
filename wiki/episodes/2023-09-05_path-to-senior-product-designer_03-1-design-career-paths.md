# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 1. Design Career Paths

## Summary

This chapter opens Part I by mapping six major design career paths and comparing them in a table of subjective ratings. Dashinsky establishes that individual contributor roles (IC) with staff and principal positions are increasingly viable career progression beyond management, challenging the outdated expectation that management is the only senior path. The chapter covers people management as a separate lateral track rather than promotion at companies like Square and Airbnb; freelance and contractor work, reflecting workforce trends toward flexibility; specialization paths like research or operations; business ownership, which offers the highest impact potential; and product management, which has the highest promotion ceiling. The chapter concludes with what the author calls a subjective comparison table, drawn from his own experience, to help designers choose paths based on their life goals, acknowledging that some paths can be combined.

## Key Takeaways

- **Individual Contributor Viability** — IC career paths with roles like staff and principal designer are becoming more common at large organizations, moving away from the historically inevitable expectation that designers must become managers to advance.
- **Management as Lateral Move** — Major companies like Square and Airbnb explicitly position management as a lateral career move, not a promotion, to avoid incentivizing talented individual contributors into unwanted people-management roles.
- **Contractor Workforce Growth** — The chapter reports that in 2018 half of Google's workers were contractors and that in 2022 flexibility ranked third among job-choice criteria, and relays Naval Ravikant's prediction that the highest-quality work will become available in a gig fashion, taken by "free agents".
- **Specialization Trade-offs** — While specialization (research, motion, operations) allows deeper fulfillment, it constrains options to larger organizations, specific company types, and reduces management opportunity paths.
- **Designer-Founder Advantage** — Designers are well-positioned to be business owners since they understand users' needs, have a grasp of marketing, and know how to build and ship.
- **PM Path Ceiling** — Product manager roles offer the highest organizational promotion ceiling, with director, VP, and Chief Product Officer positions more common than equivalent design leadership positions, making PM transitions advantageous for future business creation.

## Quotes

> A long-term design career as an individual contributor (IC) was considered a failure until recently. At some point, a designer was expected to become a people manager. This was happening due to a lack of senior individual contributor positions.

> In their internal levelling document, Square explicitly says: "Becoming a manager is not a promotion. […] We define a promotion as moving up a level. When an engineer first becomes a manager, they remain in the same level and move laterally to the engineering management track."

> Designers are well-positioned to be business owners since they understand users' needs, have a grasp of marketing, and know how to build and ship.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — The chapter systematically outlines six distinct design career paths (IC, manager, freelancer, specialist, owner, PM) that define the full landscape of designer career progression options.
- [[Career Ladder]] — The chapter's comparison table rates each path on dimensions such as impact, work-life balance, long-term financial outcome and job security, which the author presents as a subjective account of his own experience.
- [[Design Leadership]] — Discussed as both a people-management track and an individual-contributor track; large organizations increasingly support both pathways rather than forcing designers toward management.
- [[Business Impact]] — The chapter frames business ownership as the path with the highest impact potential, and argues that product positions have a higher ceiling than design positions for long-term promotion inside organizations.
- [[Product Management]] — The chapter presents the PM path as the full-time option with the biggest potential for future growth, quoting Intercom's definition of the role and noting that director, VP and CPO positions are more common than their design equivalents.
