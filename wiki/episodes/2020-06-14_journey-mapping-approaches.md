# Journey-Mapping Approaches: 2 Critical Decisions To Make Before You Begin

## Summary

Before conducting a journey-mapping initiative, teams must make two critical decisions that will shape the entire process: whether to map current-state or future-state experience, and whether to use an assumption-first or research-first approach. The current-state/future-state decision determines whether the map documents existing reality or idealized possibilities, while the assumption-first/research-first decision determines when and how data collection occurs. This article explores the benefits and risks of each approach and recommends a hybrid method that leverages both assumption-first workshops for buy-in and subsequent research validation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Choose current-state mapping to identify problems** — Map the reality of today's experience to identify pain points, gaps, and emotional dips in the customer journey. Current-state maps help teams communicate problems to stakeholders and generate persuasive narratives for investment.
- **Choose future-state mapping to envision possibilities** — When reimagining experiences or creating new offerings, future-state maps serve as a North Star. They help teams create shared vision and provide direction for prioritizing features and services that must exist in the ideal experience.
- **Use assumption-first approach for speed and buy-in** — When time or budget is limited, leverage existing organizational knowledge in a workshop setting. This approach quickly builds team alignment and identifies pain points without expensive upfront research, though there is risk of embedding incorrect assumptions.
- **Use research-first approach for rigor** — When stakeholder credibility requires data-backed findings, invest in primary user research before mapping. This ensures the map reflects actual user behavior, though the approach is more time-consuming and expensive.
- **Hybrid approach works best for most teams** — Start with a current-state assumption map to understand existing opportunities and build buy-in. Validate or evolve with user research (interviews, diary studies, customer feedback). Then create a future-state vision once pain points are well understood.

## Quotes

> Journey mapping can be useful activity for bringing teams together to create one shared organization-wide vision for prioritizing design and UX ideas and investments.

> Answering these questions at the outset will focus the scope of the map, set the overall direction for the team's activities, and eliminate snags in collaboration as the process advances.

> For most teams, using a hybrid approach for each decision works well: Create a current-state map first, to understand existing opportunities, and then create a future-state map to envision new ideas in a holistic way.
