# Young Adults Appreciate Flat Design More than Their Parents Do

## Summary

An online survey of 457 users (229 young adults aged 18-25, 228 older adults aged 35+) measured aesthetic ratings of flat vs. traditional designs using a modified Microsoft Desirability Toolkit. Young adults rated four flat-design screenshots significantly higher than older adults, by an average of 0.53 points on a 7-point scale. The single traditional (skeuomorphic) control design received nearly identical ratings from both groups, suggesting flat designs are more polarizing than conventional designs. The difference is statistically significant but modest in practical terms; designers should weigh aesthetic preferences against potential usability trade-offs like reduced click clarity and lower efficiency with flat interfaces.

## Key Takeaways

- **Age is a statistically significant predictor of flat-design preference** — young adults rated flat designs 0.53 points higher on average than older adults (p<0.00001), a highly significant difference.
- **Extreme flat designs show the largest gap** — the minimalist "Lifeaweso.me" design showed a 1.26-point difference, with 71% of older adults calling it boring vs. 59% of young adults calling it professional.
- **Traditional designs show no age difference** — the skeuomorphic control design received nearly identical ratings from both age groups, suggesting flat designs are uniquely polarizing.
- **Aesthetic appeal may not justify usability costs** — a slight increase in attractiveness (0.53 points) may not outweigh potential downsides like reduced efficiency, click uncertainty, and alienation of older users.
- **Content dominates visual style** — subject matter in screenshots influenced ratings as much as or more than design style; design effects are harder to isolate than style preference alone.
- **Further research is needed** — conclusions are based on four flat-design variants from two website builders; a larger sample would better represent the diversity of flat UI approaches.
