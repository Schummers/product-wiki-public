# ChatGPT and Tone: Avoid Sounding Like a Robot

## Summary

ChatGPT and similar AI tools struggle to produce natural-sounding text, often creating exaggerated or unnatural output when asked to adopt specific tones. Research testing common prompting strategies reveals which produce better results. Using a single tone word (e.g., "happy," "formal") causes ChatGPT to latch onto the adjective and exaggerate it unnaturally. Using multiple tone descriptors together works better, preventing over-indexing on individual words and creating more balanced output. The most effective approach is providing existing copy as a stylistic model—feeding ChatGPT sample text of desired tone allows it to mirror that style more naturally than abstract descriptions. Requesting multiple alternatives also increases chances of finding usable output. Human editing remains essential; AI-generated text requires substantial revision to feel authentic. Organizations without existing tone-of-voice guidelines can leverage ChatGPT to analyze sample copy and generate guidelines themselves.

## Key Takeaways

- **Single tone words cause exaggeration** — ChatGPT tends to latch onto individual tone adjectives and overapply them, creating unnatural results.
- **Multiple tone descriptors work better** — using several nuanced tone words together (e.g., "happy, professional, business-casual") prevents over-indexing and balances output.
- **Existing copy is the best model** — providing sample text of desired tone produces more natural results than abstract tone descriptions.
- **Ask for alternatives** — requesting multiple outputs increases likelihood of finding something useful; combining elements from variations produces better results.
- **AI exaggerates certain tones** — outputs often feel forced or cartoony; human editing is essential to tone down exaggeration.
- **Analyze copy to define tone** — use ChatGPT to analyze existing copy and generate tone-of-voice guidelines for future writing.
