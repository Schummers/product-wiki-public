# Data Tables: Four Major User Tasks

## Summary

This article focuses on designing usable data tables for workplace applications where large multivariate datasets must be displayed compactly. Unlike card-based or dashboard presentations, tables excel at scalability and comparison tasks. The article identifies four core user tasks that tables must support: finding records that fit specific criteria, comparing data, viewing/editing/adding single rows, and taking actions on records. For each task, the article provides specific design solutions addressing common usability challenges like disorientation in large tables, the need to compare distant columns, and cluttered action buttons.

## Key Takeaways

- **Tables are superior for comparison and scalability** compared to cards or other formats because adjacent data points are easy to compare without moving eyes or using working memory.
- **The default first column should be a human-readable identifier** rather than an auto-generated ID to enable users to scan and locate records of interest.
- **Freeze headers and first columns** when tables exceed screen size to maintain context as users scroll.
- **Hiding and reordering columns** must be easy and clearly indicate which columns are hidden to support comparison tasks.
- **Non-modal side panels** work better than modals for editing single records because they preserve visibility of related data.
- **Batch actions** (with checkboxes for record selection) are more space-efficient than single-record actions for applying operations to multiple items.

## Quotes

> Tables are not usually winners of a UI beauty contest — they are a utilitarian part of our designs.

> Compared to modular presentations such as card-based displays, tables are space efficient and well suited for comparing records and for detecting patterns in the data.

> The default order of the columns should reflect the importance of the data to the user and related columns should be adjacent.
