# 7 Ways to Analyze a Customer-Journey Map

## Summary

This article provides seven analytical lenses for extracting actionable insights from customer-journey maps. Rather than treating maps as descriptive deliverables alone, this framework guides practitioners in systematic analysis to identify improvement opportunities. The seven approaches cover expectations management, process optimization, friction detection, channel transitions, time evaluation, critical moments, and emotional high points.

## Key Takeaways

- **Expectation Mismatches** — identify points where user expectations are not met by comparing what personas expect based on prior experiences against actual interactions; work backward from pain points to resolve the conflict between expectations and reality.
- **Unnecessary Touchpoints** — eliminate steps that could be streamlined to reduce total interaction cost; look for logical optimizations that remove unneeded steps or add efficiency.
- **Low-Point Friction** — pinpoint where users experience the most pain or friction, represented as dips in emotional journey diagrams; prioritize addressing these low points considering dependencies and constraints (remembering the peak-end rule).
- **High-Friction Transitions** — identify friction appearing at channel or device transitions where the journey breaks down; redesign transitions to bridge gaps rather than forcing users to work harder.
- **Time Evaluation** — annotate journey stages with time durations to assess whether timing is appropriate and whether excessive time correlates with problematic user effort.
- **Moments of Truth** — identify critical make-or-break points where the overall experience might hinge on a single interaction; these moments often show high emotion or strong divergence in user paths.
- **High Points and Successes** — balance problem-focused analysis by identifying where expectations are met or exceeded and where positive emotions occur; amplify or recreate successful experiences elsewhere.

## Quotes

> Journey maps are useful in communicating the general narratives and themes uncovered by longitudinal research done to understand how a customer works toward a goal over time.

> Because of the peak-end rule, the lowest point in a journey will have a particularly ruinous impact on the branding effect of the user experience.

> As the creator of the map, you'll want to identify and call attention to these important elements through visual emphasis and storytelling. As a consumer of the visualization, apply this checklist like lenses through which to view the map in order to find the most actionable insights.
