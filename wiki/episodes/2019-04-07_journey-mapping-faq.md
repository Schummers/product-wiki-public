# Journey Mapping: 9 Frequently Asked Questions

## Summary

Journey mapping is a powerful technique for creating organizational alignment on holistic customer experience. This article addresses common questions about creating and using journey maps, including how many maps to create, how to gain stakeholder buy-in, who should be involved, timing considerations, research approaches, and tool selection. The practice itself, rather than the visualization, is often the most valuable part of the process.

## Key Takeaways

- **Focused maps tell clearer stories** — Maps centered on one persona in one scenario are more effective than trying to capture all users at once; create separate maps for different personas or scenarios.

- **Stakeholder involvement from the start** — Gaining buy-in requires involving stakeholders early to explain value, maintaining their support throughout, and using their knowledge to focus research.

- **Collaborative process matters most** — The act of creating the map together forces conversations and builds shared mental models across teams; this shared vision is more valuable than the artifact.

- **Research informs better maps** — Maps must be based on real data from user research methods like interviews, contextual inquiry, and diary studies, not just internal assumptions.

- **Current and future states both matter** — Mapping both current-state and future-state journeys reveals gaps and opportunities; overlaying emotional journeys highlights areas needing improvement.

## Concepts

- [[Journey Mapping]] — A collaborative technique for visualizing customer experiences across touchpoints and time; practice and alignment matter more than the artifact.

- [[User Research]] — Successful maps require qualitative research to understand real customer behavior, not just internal assumptions.

- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — Journey mapping forces cross-functional teams to share knowledge and agree on how to improve customer experience.

- [[Customer Experience]] — Maps reveal touchpoints where departments own service delivery and where friction or emotions run high.

- [[Design Process]] — Journey mapping helps teams prioritize problems and iterate designs based on validated customer experiences.
