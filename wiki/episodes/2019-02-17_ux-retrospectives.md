# UX Retrospectives

## Summary

This article discusses retrospectives as a team meeting format for reflecting on how team members work together and identifying improvements based on recent work. Retrospectives are typically practiced within Agile processes but benefit any team regardless of methodology. The article provides guidance on planning effective retrospectives, including setting expectations, discussing successes and improvements, creating actionable plans, and following up on action items.

## Key Takeaways

- **Establish clear ground rules** — Retrospectives should avoid blame, focus on team improvement rather than individual shortcomings, and maintain an open mind to new ideas and different perspectives.
- **Discuss accomplishments first** — Begin retrospectives by acknowledging what went well and what helped propel the team forward, recognizing team achievements as a whole rather than individuals.
- **Address improvement areas constructively** — Shift to discussing what needs improvement using inclusive language like "we fell short" rather than "you failed" to maintain psychological safety.
- **Create actionable and realistic plans** — Base action items on retrospective discussion, break down large items into smaller ones, and assign owners and due dates for accountability.
- **Track patterns and follow up** — Review action items from previous retrospectives before starting new ones and check in on progress to demonstrate commitment to improvements.
- **Rotate facilitators and vary formats** — Sharing facilitation roles keeps team members engaged; using varied formats and icebreakers maintains fresh engagement and prevents retrospective fatigue.

## Quotes

> A retrospective is a regular meeting during which the team reflects on how team members work together and considers ways to improve that process based on lessons from recently completed work.

> Make the retrospective a safe space to bring up issues without fear of retaliation. Every team will have areas for improvement, and it should be clear to those in the meeting that bringing up issues for discussion should not be seen as negative.

> By regularly refining your team's processes, retrospectives can become a catalyst for ongoing success.
