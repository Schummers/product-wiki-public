# UX Research Cheat Sheet

## Summary

A comprehensive reference for UX research methods and activities across project phases: Discover (illuminate needs), Explore (understand problem space), Test (validate designs), and Listen (monitor and improve). The article organizes methods by phase, lists the most frequently used activities, and provides guidance for practitioners who lack budget for all approaches. It emphasizes starting research today, conducting it throughout development, and prioritizing qualitative usability testing as the highest-impact single activity.

## Key Takeaways

- **Conduct user research at every stage** — research at the current stage has impact; doing it across all phases maximizes value; limited budgets should front-load early research where it matters most.
- **Discover phase validates and discards assumptions** — field studies, user interviews, diary studies, and competitive analysis uncover whether building the product makes sense before effort is wasted.
- **Explore phase informs design decisions** — personas, journey mapping, card sorting, and prototype feedback help define the problem space and identify best-of-breed components.
- **Test phase validates designs in development** — qualitative usability testing, benchmark testing, and accessibility evaluation ensure systems work well for people who use them.
- **Listen phase identifies patterns and trends** — surveys, analytics review, search log analysis, and FAQ reviews capture ongoing issues and guide future work.
- **Qualitative usability testing is most impactful single activity** — when resources allow only one method, think-aloud testing of existing systems yields maximum improvement potential.
- **Use top methods strategically** — task analysis, requirements gathering, in-person usability studies, and journey mapping are the most frequently used methods; vary methods each cycle for different insights.

## Quotes

> If you can do only one activity and aim to improve an existing system, do qualitative (think-aloud) usability testing, which is the most effective method to improve usability.

> The discovery stage is when you try to illuminate what you don't know and better understand what people need.

> Do user research at whatever stage you're in right now.
