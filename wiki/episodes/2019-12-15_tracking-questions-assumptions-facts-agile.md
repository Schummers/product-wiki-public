# Tracking Research Questions, Assumptions, and Facts in Agile

## Summary

In Agile environments with time and budget constraints, teams frequently make assumptions about users to move forward in development. The danger is that assumptions are later treated as facts when team members forget their shaky foundation. This article presents a knowledge board framework with four phases: questions (what we don't know), assumptions (what we think we know), research (testing assumptions), and facts (validated knowledge). Creating visible documentation of this progression helps Agile teams manage risk by distinguishing known unknowns from unknown unknowns, with three methods for tracking: Kanban boards, project-management software, or text documents.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four-phase knowledge progression** — research questions identify gaps, assumptions make semi-informed guesses, research tests those guesses, facts document validated findings; same statement can progress through all phases.
- **Questions phase** — formulate research questions in backlog-refinement meetings to create shared understanding of knowledge gaps; helps prevent overlooking what you don't know.
- **Assumptions phase** — document guesses and their origins (persona, early research, stakeholder input); weaker assumptions lack empirical backing while stronger ones derive from data; list next research steps.
- **Research phase** — conduct planned research to test assumptions; if wrong, refine and continue testing; if validated, move to facts phase.
- **Facts phase** — confidently answer research questions with factual statements based on collected data; document and revisit throughout product lifecycle to maintain team alignment.
- **Documentation methods** — Kanban board (recommended for visibility), project-management software (integrates with existing process), or text documents (for teams preferring Drive/Sharepoint).

## Quotes

> An assumption is something we take on faith or as a best guess despite a lack of proof.

> The danger with assumptions is that they are often treated the same as facts later in the project, when team members forget that the assumptions have a shaky base.

> Much worse to be hit by the infamous "unknown unknowns," which are the things you never even thought of.
