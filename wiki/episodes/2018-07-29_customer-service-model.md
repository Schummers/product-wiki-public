# Customer-Service Information on Websites: The Hub-and-Spoke Model

## Summary

Customer-service information on websites is often poorly organized with inconsistent labels and unclear hierarchies, forcing users to search through trial and error. A hub-and-spoke model addresses this by creating a main "Customer Service" hub page linking to all service-related information, with optional FAQ and Contact Us pages as secondary hubs. All hub pages should link to each other, and specific service pages (returns, shipping) should link back to hubs. This standardized structure helps users find customer service information efficiently and builds trust in the company.

## Key Takeaways

- **Create a main Customer Service hub** — Always include a high-level customer-service page as a catch-all for all service-related questions, ensuring users can find answers even when unsure which specific link to visit.
- **Use "Customer Service" or "Customer Assistance" labels** — These terms performed well in testing; alternatives like "Help" or "Help Center" caused confusion as users expected them to assist with website navigation rather than provide service policies.
- **Link all hub pages together** — Customer Service, FAQ, and Contact Us pages should crosslink to each other, allowing users to navigate easily between them when their initial choice isn't the right one.
- **Link specific pages back to hubs** — Granular service pages (Returns, Shipping) should include links to hub pages in the body content, not just in global navigation, to help users find related information.
- **Limit hub pages to three types** — Provide only Customer Service, FAQ, and Contact Us hubs; additional hub pages increase confusion about which is the right one to visit.
