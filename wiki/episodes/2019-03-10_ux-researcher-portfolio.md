# Portfolios for UX Researchers: Top 10 Recommendations

## Summary

This article provides guidance for UX researchers creating professional portfolios to showcase their research skills and past work. Unlike design portfolios that emphasize visual output, research portfolios must communicate research methodology, findings, and business impact. The article recommends curating three to five projects, including both study artifacts and communication deliverables, clearly describing context and team, and demonstrating measurable value while maintaining honest communication about constraints.

## Key Takeaways

- **Curate strategically to 3-5 projects** — Include projects with high impact, robust methodology, and recent completion dates; avoid overwhelming readers with every project ever completed.
- **Show how you think and create impact** — Include both methodology artifacts (study plans, tasks, concepts) and communication deliverables (affinity diagrams, storyboards, reports) that demonstrate research value.
- **Clearly describe context and team role** — Explain project goals, duration, participants, and findings in an engaging story; specify your direct responsibilities and team collaborators.
- **Demonstrate breadth of skills** — Show experience with multiple research methodologies (qualitative, quantitative), scales (small guerrilla to large projects), and conditions (independent and team-based).
- **Communicate clearly for external audiences** — Write in plain language without jargon; explain UX concepts so readers outside your industry and company can understand your work's value.
- **Use metrics and quotes to show value** — Highlight measurable outcomes (reduced support calls, improved conversion rates) and user quotes demonstrating design impact.

## Quotes

> What people reading your portfolio really need to know is the types of research you have done, how you approach problems, and how you deliver results.

> Showing insights to someone who is not familiar with your specific project, problem space, or user base is challenging — focus on a few key data points.

> Your portfolio, just like your research skills, will improve with time and effort.
