# Three Methods to Increase User Autonomy in UX Design

## Summary

Autonomy, the ability to use an interface in ways that align with personal preferences and priorities, is recognized as a fundamental human need. Many interfaces force users into narrowly defined workflows that restrict choice and control. This article presents three methods to increase user autonomy: providing customizations (both surface-level like themes and task-related like view options), increasing content scannability so users can choose what to read in detail, and allowing flexible timing and sequences for interactions. The article emphasizes balancing autonomy with usability by avoiding choice overload and providing meaningful defaults.

## Key Takeaways

- **Autonomy is the ability to use an interface aligned with personal preferences** and is considered a fundamental human need.
- **Surface-level customizations** (themes, colors) provide delight and ownership without altering workflow, though users rarely customize them.
- **Task-related customizations** (view options, zoom controls) help users adapt interfaces to meet shifting needs.
- **Scannability gives autonomy** by allowing users to quickly choose what to read; poor scannability forces users into reading everything or leaving.
- **Flexible timing and sequences** respect different user readiness (some want onboarding, others want to jump in immediately).
- **Balance autonomy with usability** by providing a few meaningful options and strong defaults rather than forcing customization.
