# AI-Moderated Interviews: If, When, and How to Use Them

## Summary

NN/g tested two AI interview tools with ten research professionals, evaluating their readiness for unmoderated, voice-based interviews. AI interviewers can collect structured feedback at scale and excel at multilingual interviews and recruitment screening, but they fall short at discovery, semistructured interviews, and nuance. Participants felt heard through AI summarization but craved nonverbal feedback; they experienced awkward pacing, interruptions, overly enthusiastic responses (sycophancy), and privacy concerns. AI interviewers follow scripts rigidly, never adapting, skipping, or reframing questions—capabilities essential to deep discovery. They complement human moderation but cannot replace it for complex, exploratory research requiring judgment and domain knowledge.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI Interviewers Best for Structured Tasks** — Effective for product feedback, recruitment screening, and multilingual interviews; not ready for semistructured discovery where adaptation matters.
- **Rapport Through Summarization, Not Nonverbal Cues** — AI summarization makes participants feel heard, but lack of facial expressions and nodding left them craving nonverbal backchannel feedback.
- **Awkward Conversational Dynamics** — Participants experienced interruptions, long pauses, repetitive questions, and dramatic interview-length variation (13–56 minutes) due to rigid, non-adaptive flow.
- **Sycophancy Undermines Credibility** — AI interviewers praised mundane answers as "fascinating" and "brilliant," feeling fake and disingenuous to experienced participants.
- **Cannot Read the Room** — AI interviewers struggle to recognize when questions are sufficiently explored or when to move on; they lack situational judgment that human interviewers exercise constantly.
- **Privacy Concerns Reduce Openness** — Participants held back sensitive information, doubting AI systems' accountability compared to human interviewers handling insights responsibly.

## Concepts

- [[AI in User Research]] — AI interviews supplement but do not replace human moderation; they excel at structured tasks but lack judgment for discovery and complex contexts.
- [[Interview Methods]] — Structured interviews benefit from AI standardization; semistructured discovery requires human expertise, adaptation, and real-time judgment.
- [[Rapport Building]] — Nonverbal cues, consistency, and authentic responsiveness build rapport; AI summarization helps but cannot fully replace human connection.
- [[Research Limitations]] — AI interviewers' inability to adapt, read nonverbal cues, or exercise judgment makes them unsuitable for exploratory research requiring nuance.
- [[Human-AI Collaboration]] — AI and humans have complementary strengths; AI handles scalable, structured tasks while humans address complex, judgment-intensive work.
