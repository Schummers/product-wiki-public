# Design-Pattern Guidelines: Study Guide

## Summary

This article provides a comprehensive index of Nielsen Norman Group guidelines covering specific UI design patterns across multiple categories: input controls (checkboxes, toggles, steppers), forms and wizards, tooltips and dialogs, icons and indicators, menu design, site navigation, in-page navigation, search, and error handling. Each pattern listing links to detailed articles and videos with practical guidelines. The resource emphasizes that usability guidelines based on human behavior change slowly despite rapid technological change, so designers should understand the core principles behind guidelines rather than dismissing them as outdated. Coverage spans foundational elements like the 10 usability heuristics through specialized patterns like skeleton screens, infinite scrolling, and password-less authentication.

## Key Takeaways

- **Usability guidelines are durable** — guidelines based on human behavior remain valid across technology changes; focus on core principles rather than dismissing guidelines as outdated.
- **Foundation: the 10 usability heuristics** — these provide foundational principles for all interaction design before diving into specific pattern guidelines.
- **Patterns span the full UI spectrum** — input controls, forms, navigation, search, errors, and privacy each have specialized guidelines addressing common usability pitfalls.
- **Multiple formats available** — guidelines appear as articles (detailed reference), videos (visual learning), and checklists (quick validation).
- **Privacy and ethics increasingly important** — design guidelines now address cookie permissions, passwordless authentication, deceptive patterns, and dark UI patterns.
- **Organized by use case** — categories like "when to use checkboxes vs. radio buttons" or "tooltips, dialogs, and instructional overlays" help designers find relevant guidance quickly.

## Quotes

> As you adopt the following guidelines in your own work, consider the core user behaviors and usability principles at play (not just the technology). Don't throw out old findings because of their age.
