# Foldable Smartphones: New Devices, New Opportunities

## Summary

Foldable smartphones represent an emerging device category combining the portability of phones with the larger viewing area of tablets. Two main types exist: fold-out phones (horizontally folding into tablet-sized screens) and flip phones (vertically folding clamshell designs). While foldable market share is currently small (1.6% in 2023), market penetration is expected to reach nearly 5% by 2028. Designers must prepare for these new devices by ensuring apps work across unconventional screen sizes, supporting different interaction modes, and maintaining consistency across screen versions. Foldable phones enable new use cases including multitasking with split screens, larger displays for accessibility, and novel interaction modes like photography from half-folded positions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Two foldable types with different use cases** — Fold-out phones unfold into tablet-sized screens for multitasking and accessibility, while flip phones are compact when closed with smaller cover screens for quick information access.
- **Growing but still small market** — Foldable smartphone market share grew from 0.5% in 2021 to 1.6% in 2023, with projections of 5% by 2028; China leads with 4.1% adoption rate.
- **Larger screens enable new behaviors** — Fold-out phones support multitasking with split-screen views, document editing, and viewing that previously required tablets; flip phones provide smartwatch-like quick access.
- **New interaction modes open opportunities** — Positioning screens at different angles enables typing (90-degree angle mimicking laptops) and half-folded photography modes, creating interaction opportunities unique to foldable devices.
- **Set breakpoints wisely** — Foldable phones have unconventional screen sizes; designers should test apps on popular foldable models and adjust breakpoints if apps struggle on those devices.
- **Maintain consistency across screen modes** — Users may access the same app on multiple screen sizes on one device; inconsistency forces relearning, so maintain consistent experiences across folded and unfolded states.
