# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 8. Design Career Growth Canvas

## Summary

This chapter provides a practical tool for implementing the prioritization work from the previous chapter: a canvas where designers list their top five competencies to develop and specify, for each one, their current level, desired level, concrete action items, next steps, timeline, and a metric for tracking progress. The author frames this canvas as an opportunity to both take control of your own growth and invite your manager into the process. By sharing the plan with your manager, you educate them and your organization about growth planning, increase the likelihood they will support you, and, ideally, set a precedent for formal growth plans in your company. The chapter emphasizes that the plan should be revisited twice a year or whenever context changes, and notes that improving these competencies complements, rather than replaces, company OKRs or KPIs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Canvas structure** — List your top five competencies, then for each specify your current level, your target level, action items you will take, your next step (what you will do in the next few days), a timeline for reaching the target, and a metric to measure success.
- **Metric importance** — Many competency improvements are hard to measure objectively, so regular one-on-ones with your manager are a practical way to get feedback on your progress toward each action item.
- **Manager conversation** — Share your plan with your manager by asking for their input: "Here's what I'd like to improve and here's my plan. What do you think?" This educates them about structured growth planning and puts the responsibility on them to support it.
- **Updating cadence** — Revisit and revise the plan twice a year or whenever context changes: a new career goal, feedback from a manager, or a change in employment status.
- **Organizational impact** — By taking this initiative, you model structured growth planning for your company and create an opportunity for them to formalize growth plans as part of their policy and structure.

## Quotes

> "The advantage of these growth plans is that they allow you to take control of your own growth. Ideally, of course, you'd like the company to be on board and provide you with the right opportunities. But even if they don't, these skills will serve you at any future company or in any new career."

> Tell them: "Here's what I'd like to improve and here's my plan. What do you think?" Remember that it's in their interests (and it's their responsibility) to provide you with growth opportunities.

> "Ideally, keep this plan updated and revise it twice a year or when your context or objectives change (for instance, when you have a new career goal, feedback from a manager, when your employment status changes, etc.)."

## Concepts

- [[Career Growth Plan]] — This chapter is entirely about the design and use of a growth canvas: a structured tool to list your top competencies, define current and desired levels, specify action items and timelines, and identify success metrics for each competency.
- [[UX Career]] — The canvas supports career progression by making your growth intentional and measurable; it helps you stay on track toward your career goal and communicate that goal to your manager.
- [[Design Competencies]] — The canvas transforms the priority list from the previous chapter into a working document that tracks progress on each competency with timelines and metrics.
- [[Mentorship]] — By bringing your manager into the canvas discussion, you create an opportunity for ongoing mentorship and support; your manager's role includes providing feedback on whether you are progressing toward your targets.
- [[Performance Review]] — The canvas provides structure and documentation between formal performance reviews, creating a way to measure progress and discuss development between annual or semi-annual review cycles.
