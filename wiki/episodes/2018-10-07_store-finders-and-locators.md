# Store Finders and Locators

## Summary

This article examines the evolution of store locator design over nearly two decades of research. The study assessed 46 users testing locators across 56 websites, comparing results from four rounds of usability testing conducted since the original store-locator study 18 years prior. While user success rates with locators have dramatically improved from 63% to 97%, the research shows that 40% of users still encounter difficulty despite succeeding at the task. The findings reveal shifts in user behavior, particularly the increased dominance of external mapping tools like Google Maps and the decreased reliance on printed directions due to smartphone adoption.

## Key Takeaways

- **Success rates have improved dramatically but usability still needs work** — User success in finding locations increased from 63% to 97% over 18 years, yet 40% still encounter difficulty, indicating that locators must be easy in addition to functional.

- **Three critical steps must all be easy** — Finding the locator link, using it to locate the desired location, and getting directions from that location are all essential; breakdowns in any step impact overall usability.

- **Search dominance reduces direct site visitation for location finding** — 80% of users go directly to search engines or mapping apps like Google Maps when looking for company locations, rather than visiting company websites first.

- **Geolocation features significantly improve the experience** — The ability to use current location via browser or device substantially streamlines the search process and should be explicitly offered with a discoverable button or link, not hidden behind permission dialogs.

- **External mapping tools provide better experience than custom solutions** — Companies should link to familiar platforms like Google Maps for turn-by-turn directions rather than building proprietary map interfaces, as users already trust and understand these tools.

- **Location finder complexity remains problematic** — The main usability challenge today is that location finders are either too complex with unnecessary features or oversimplified, making it difficult for users to filter results by their specific needs.

## Concepts

- [[E-Commerce]] — how online shopping increasingly requires integration with physical retail locations and how companies must facilitate transitions between digital and physical channels.

- [[Omnichannel Experience]] — users' expectation that they can research products online and purchase in-store, or vice versa, requiring seamless experience across digital and physical touchpoints.

- [[Geolocation]] — how location-based features and current-location detection improve usability by reducing the interaction cost of searching for nearby stores.

- [[Information Architecture]] — the structural design of locator tools, including navigation, labeling, and how to organize location results when stores meet certain criteria like amenities or hours.

- [[Mobile Design]] — the evolution from printed directions to mobile mapping integration, and how smartphone availability changed the role of store-locator features on company websites.
