# Stop Reporting UX Activity and Report Business Outcomes

## Summary

UX teams often lose budget conversations not because their work is invaluable but because they report in the wrong language. The article explains how UX teams make two critical reporting mistakes: presenting effort rather than impact (e.g., "we conducted 24 interviews") and reporting UX-specific metrics (e.g., "SUS improved from 62 to 74") that leadership does not track or understand. The solution is connecting upstream UX metrics to downstream business metrics that leadership cares about—revenue, cost reduction, risk mitigation, speed to market, and retention. The article provides a framework mapping common upstream metrics to business priorities with suggested framing for each.

## Key Takeaways

- **Reporting effort looks like activity without outcomes** — "We conducted 24 interviews and 3 usability studies" tells leadership what the team did, not what changed. This appears to be resources spent without anything to show for it.
- **UX metrics don't speak leadership's language** — A CFO has no frame of reference for SUS scores, and a VP of operations cannot connect a task-completion rate to their quarterly targets. These belong in research reports, not budget conversations.
- **Leaders evaluate investments through five questions** — Does it impact revenue? Reduce cost? Mitigate risk? Improve speed to market? Improve retention or satisfaction? All business decisions flow from one of these priorities.
- **Upstream metrics connect to downstream outcomes** — Upstream metrics (task success rates, error rates, SUS) tell you how design performed; downstream metrics (support contact volume, conversion rates, churn) tell you what the work was worth.
- **The data already exists inside your organization** — Partner with finance, product analytics, support, or marketing to translate before-and-after data from redesigned flows into business language. Even directional data is persuasive when honest.
- **UX teams have always influenced the numbers that matter** — When users struggle to complete key tasks, support tickets get created, opportunities to convert are missed, and customers leave. Now it's time to surface and take credit for that connection.

## Quotes

> "Two different languages are spoken in the same organization, and UX has to ensure it's speaking the right one."

> "UX teams that can't connect their work to business outcomes don't just lose individual budget conversations; they get categorized as cost centers and cut accordingly."

> "The difference comes down to where in the chain you're measuring."

## Concepts

- [[UX Metrics]] — must connect to downstream business outcomes that leadership tracks, such as revenue impact, cost reduction, and retention.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — requires translating UX findings into business language and demonstrating measurable connection between design work and organizational priorities.
- [[Business Impact]] — is proven by showing how UX improvements reduce failure demand, rework costs, legal risks, speed time to market, and improve customer retention.
