# The 10x Method: Becoming a 10x Founder

## Summary

Chapter 1 of The 10x Method establishes that building a 10x company requires becoming a 10x founder first. Through working with 100+ founders, Hexa has identified ten foundational traits: having a long-term vision while executing the first step, balancing conviction with adaptability, executing relentlessly, building routines around weekly and quarterly rhythms, keeping ego low, building strong teams, thinking globally from day one, being mission-driven rather than motivated by quick exits, building resilience through purpose, and leading with kindness. The chapter argues that these traits are learnable skills that compound over time to shape company culture, pace, product development, and team dynamics.

## Key Takeaways

- **Long-term vision with immediate execution** — Have a clear five-year vision as your compass, but ground it in specific execution this week. Think of it as a domino effect where each week's action builds momentum toward the larger goal.
- **Weekly hypothesis testing** — Run each week like a sprint, testing and validating something new. This discipline allows you to learn fast and figure out whether the product works, which channels matter, and how to approach go-to-market.
- **Hands-on founder work** — In early stages, founders must stay deeply involved and in the trenches: building, coding, selling, and learning alongside users. This closeness to the work is a startup's competitive advantage.
- **Operating cadence** — Build strong routines with quarterly OKRs to reset direction and weekly sprints to maintain momentum. This rhythm, tracked consistently and shared transparently, builds real company momentum.
- **Low-ego leadership** — Hire people smarter than yourself, admit mistakes, ask for help, and pivot when needed. Focusing on what works rather than being right builds stronger companies and attracts better talent.
- **Mission over exits** — Be a missionary driven by impact and long-term value creation, not a mercenary chasing quick exits. This purpose fuels resilience when things get hard and attracts team members and investors who believe in the same vision.

## Quotes

> Each week should be like a sprint where you ask yourself: What hypothesis are we testing this week? That discipline (testing and validating something new every week) is what allows you to learn very fast and figure out: Will your product work? What are your first channels? How do you approach your go-to-market?

> The best routine I ever put in place – and stuck to for ten years – was sending an email to the entire team every Sunday evening. A clear, no-frills recap: key metrics, how they moved, and the top priorities for the week ahead. Same format, same discipline, every time. Even when we were 500 or 600 people, I never skipped it, because a company should be run week by week. If we missed our goals, we rolled up our sleeves on Monday and got to work. There were tough weeks, lost clients, but every week was a new shot to bounce back. That ritual was about transparency, intensity, and building a culture of rhythm.

> Ensuring transparency was a big time investment, far more than I've seen at most other companies, but to me, it was worth it. It was how I made people feel part of the journey.

## Concepts

- [[Founder Mindset]] — The ten traits Hexa identified (vision, adaptability, execution, rituals, low ego, team building, global thinking, mission focus, resilience, kindness) are the DNA of a 10x founder and can be learned and developed over time. Purpose connected to the mission is what fuels resilience: missionary founders driven by impact rather than exit timelines keep the energy and clarity to persist through chaos.
- [[Operating Rituals]] — Weekly sprints with hypothesis testing and metric tracking, paired with quarterly OKRs for realignment, create the cadence that drives momentum and enable fast learning in early-stage companies.
- [[Business Strategy]] — Founders must think globally from day one through international hiring, English as internal language, products designed for cross-border scalability, and seeking international design partners rather than optimizing for local markets alone.
