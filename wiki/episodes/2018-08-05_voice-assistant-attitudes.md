# Intelligent Assistants: Creepy, Childish, or a Tool? Users' Attitudes Toward Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri

## Summary

This usability study of 17 frequent users of Alexa, Siri, and Google Assistant in New York City and the San Francisco Bay Area examined how users perceive and interact with voice-based conversational assistants. Findings reveal that users project anthropomorphic qualities onto assistants, have mental models of them as unreliable or unintelligent, and harbor significant trust and privacy concerns despite widespread adoption. Users selectively employ assistants for simple, predictable tasks rather than complex interactions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Anthropomorphic projection** — users assign gendered pronouns, apply human metaphors, and use politeness markers when interacting with assistants, though many recognize this behavior and laugh at themselves.
- **Limited mental models** — users view assistants as similar to young children or people with poor hearing, believing they cannot understand complex questions, lack context preservation, or pick up fine semantic distinctions.
- **Context-dependent usage** — users prefer using assistants only at home or alone, not in public settings, and primarily for simple tasks, entertainment, or when hands are busy rather than for subjective information or opinion-based queries.
- **Language adaptation** — users either speak naturally to assistants or artificially compress queries into keywords to maximize comprehension, sometimes avoiding articles, changing word order, and viewing the assistant as a search interface.
- **Privacy and trust concerns** — major concerns include always-on recording, audio transmission to the cloud, consequences of misunderstandings, unauthorized contact, and smart-home reliability issues.

## Concepts

- [[Voice Assistant]] — conversational AI systems that users interact with through natural language, but whose current limitations lead users to restrict usage to simple, predictable tasks.
- [[AI Chatbots]] — text-based or voice-based interfaces that leverage natural language; users adapt their speech patterns based on perceived assistant capabilities, sometimes speaking unnaturally to improve success rates.
- [[Mental Model]] — users' beliefs about what assistants can do shape their interactions significantly; they view these systems as far less intelligent than true AI, limiting ambitions for complex tasks.
- [[Privacy]] — major barriers to expanded assistant adoption, with users expressing concerns about data collection, security, and the reliability of delegated actions.
