# The Layer-Cake Pattern of Scanning Content on the Web

## Summary

The layer-cake scanning pattern occurs when page information is split into clear headings and subheadings; users fixate mostly on headings with occasional deliberate fixations on body text in between, creating a gaze pattern resembling horizontal stripes (cake layers with frosting). This pattern is one of the most effective ways to scan pages and ensures users find relevant information if present. Users scan rather than read every word because scanning delivers nearly the same information value with less time and effort. Effective layer-cake patterns require visual and content guidelines: visually, subheadings must stand out through size, color, typeface, bolding, or effects without appearing as ads; content-wise, subheadings must be descriptive of their sections, succinct, lead with important words, and be understandable. Pages with mixed content (cards, irregular layouts) extend layer-cake principles by chunking related content, visually distinguishing chunks, and labeling each chunk with clear subheadings. Layer-cake scanning is far more efficient than F-pattern scanning (which occurs with undifferentiated text columns).

## Key Takeaways

- **Layer-Cake Pattern Supports Efficient Scanning** — when headings and subheadings are visually distinct and descriptive, users scan directly to relevant sections, saving time and ensuring relevant information is found.
- **Visual Guidelines for Subheadings** — subheadings must stand out consistently through size, color, typeface, bolding, or combinations thereof; avoid making them so prominent they look like ads or promotions instead of content.
- **Content Guidelines for Subheadings** — subheadings must describe all and only topics in their section, be concise, lead with important words, and use clear language; good subheadings help users skip irrelevant content and understand relevance quickly.
- **Extend Principles to Mixed Layouts** — pages with cards or irregular formats should apply layer-cake principles: group related content, visually distinguish chunks, use spacing to signal relationships, and label chunks with clear subheadings or labels.

## Quotes

> The layer-cake scanning pattern consists of fixations made mostly on the page's headings and subheadings, with deliberate occasional fixations on the (body) text in between.

> Aside from reading every (or almost every) word, the layer-cake pattern is by far the most effective way to scan pages: most of the time, it ensures that users will find the information they are looking for (if it is present on the page).

> Chunking content and assigning clear, descriptive subheadings that stand out from the other text and content on the page increase content usability exponentially because they support the layer-cake scanning pattern, which, unlike its relative, the F-pattern, increases users' efficiency by allowing them to quickly identify the content that is most relevant to their task.
