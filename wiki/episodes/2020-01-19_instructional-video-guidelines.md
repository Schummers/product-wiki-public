# Videos as Instructional Content: User Behaviors and UX Guidelines

## Summary

Users exhibit three distinct behavioral patterns when encountering instructional content: reading text only, skimming text then watching video, or immediately watching video. Video consumption depends on content complexity, familiarity, user motivation, and how daunting the text appears. When provided, videos should supplement rather than replace text, as users have different preferences and video is an inefficient sequential medium. Placement, duration, comprehensiveness, video styling, captions, and the ability to jump to specific content all significantly impact whether videos are discovered and used effectively.

## Key Takeaways

- **Provide both text and video** — Not everyone enjoys videos for learning. If budget allows, include both text and video options so users can choose based on their preferences and the situation. Never make video the sole source of information.

- **Complexity and familiarity drive video use** — Users are more likely to turn to video when content is complex, unfamiliar, or involves long multistep processes. Even users who prefer text may resort to video as a last resort when confused.

- **Strategic placement matters** — Videos at the top of a page should be comprehensive and cover all content. Videos specific to a section should be at the top of that section. Avoid placing videos at the bottom or in right rails where they are frequently overlooked.

- **Keep videos short** — Aim for under 30 seconds for overview videos. For multistep processes, break into several short videos rather than one long one, allowing users to watch only relevant portions and maintain engagement.

- **Include multiple entry points** — Display video length upfront, use illustrative thumbnails that set style expectations, and for long videos include chapters or timestamps so users can jump to relevant content instead of scrubbing through.

- **Include captions and text** — Many users cannot or prefer not to play sound. Captions and incorporated text allow users to follow along without audio and to pause for comprehension.
