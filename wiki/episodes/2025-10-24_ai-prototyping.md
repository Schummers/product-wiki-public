## Summary

AI-powered prototyping tools generate interfaces instantly from natural-language prompts, but evaluation with real design scenarios reveals significant limitations. While these tools can follow instructions to achieve general goals, they lack the sophistication to weigh design tradeoffs and produce thoughtful, high-quality designs without extensive guidance from humans. The evaluation tested AI design tools with varying levels of context: broad text prompts, detailed text prompts, and text plus design artifacts. Results showed that specificity of prompts and access to design references dramatically improved output quality, but even strong outputs missed subtle details like visual hierarchy, spacing, and color contrast that characterize human-designed work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prompt specificity drives output quality** — longer, more detailed prompts with clear design requirements yield significantly better AI-generated results, particularly for code-based prototypes that can resemble human designer work.
- **Design artifacts matter more than words** — providing Figma links or high-fidelity mockups as references produces more accurate designs than text prompts alone, because AI can examine existing patterns and spatial relationships.
- **AI lacks nuance and human judgment** — even with detailed prompts, AI tools miss subtle but important details like visual hierarchy, grouping of related elements, spacing, and color contrast that distinguish thoughtful design from "good from afar, but far from good."
- **AI reflects training data biases** — outputs are biased toward mainstream design conventions and generic visual styles using sans-serif typefaces and minimalistic styling, because AI learns from common patterns in training data.
- **Designers must disambiguate language** — UX language is ambiguous (e.g., "profile page" can mean different things), so designers need to precisely communicate context to prevent AI from misinterpreting requirements.
- **Strong design knowledge is necessary** — AI promises to close skill gaps but works best in the hands of people who already understand design, layout, typography, and component naming well enough to direct it effectively.

## Concepts

- [[Artificial Intelligence]] — AI-powered prototyping tools can rapidly generate interfaces from text prompts but consistently miss subtle design nuances and lack the judgment to weigh tradeoffs, revealing a fundamental gap between automation speed and design quality.
- [[Interaction Design]] — When designing interfaces with AI, specificity matters greatly; detailed prompts and visual references produce better results, but designers must disambiguate ambiguous language like "profile page" to prevent misinterpretation of design intent.
- [[Prototyping]] — AI tools are best suited as rapid exploration and ideation aids for early-stage prototypes, not production-ready design; they efficiently draft layouts and assemble components but require human judgment to refine and contextualize.
- [[Design Process]] — Strong design knowledge is prerequisite, not substitute, for AI prototyping; designers who understand layout, typography, and component naming can direct AI effectively, while the craft of design remains fundamentally about interpreting context and establishing priorities.
