# Design Critiques: Encourage a Positive Culture to Improve Products

## Summary

A design critique is a group conversation aimed at analyzing a design and providing feedback on whether it meets its objectives. Unlike design reviews or subjective judgments, critiques are standalone sessions where presenters share work and critiquers offer informed perspectives. They differ from mere judging by emphasizing collaborative improvement rather than approval.

Effective critiques operate on three core themes: clear scope for the conversation, agreed-upon design objectives, and conversation rather than command-and-control feedback. Design critiques happen at any stage of the design process and should be frequent, crossdisciplinary activities that build team trust and prevent ego-driven damage to projects.

## Key Takeaways

- **Critique vs. Review** — A standalone design critique focuses on improving a particular piece of work through group discussion, while design reviews evaluate designs against heuristics and approve work to move forward.
- **Facilitation importance** — A designated facilitator should manage the conversation, set scope, choose participants, ask reformulating questions to connect feedback to design goals, document discussion, and follow up with action items.
- **Round-robin and quota methods** — Round-robin allows everyone to contribute democratically one by one, while quota-based feedback requests specific numbers of positive and negative comments to balance perspective.
- **Presenter best practices** — Presenters should reiterate objectives, tell the story of their work, present efficiently without over-explaining, and make designs available for extended discussion afterward.
- **Start soon and small** — Begin with current projects rather than waiting for the next one, and dedicate time to regular critique sessions among immediate team members to build critique culture.
- **Avoid pitfalls** — Common mistakes include not agreeing on personas or objectives beforehand, scheduling overly long sessions, taking feedback personally, rushing to solve problems during the critique, and focusing only on negatives.

## Concepts

- [[Design Process]] — Critiques are positioned as a key iterative stage where feedback can be incorporated before a design is final, reducing project costs and timelines.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Critiques build team trust, enable buy-in, and prevent destructive egos by creating transparency and early involvement in design decisions.
- [[Feedback Culture]] — Creating a culture of honest critique takes time and investment but improves product quality by incorporating multiple perspectives into the final design.
- [[Workshop Facilitation]] — A designated facilitator manages critique conversations by setting scope, asking reformulating questions, documenting discussion, and following up with action items to keep discussions productive and aligned to design goals.
