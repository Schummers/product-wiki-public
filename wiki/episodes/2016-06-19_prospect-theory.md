# Prospect Theory and Loss Aversion: How Users Make Decisions

## Summary

Prospect theory explains how people make decisions between different options (prospects) and how they estimate the perceived likelihood of each option, often in biased or incorrect ways. Proposed by psychologists Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky in 1979, the theory reveals that users do not always make logical choices. Instead, they rely on cognitive biases like loss aversion, certainty bias, and the isolation effect when weighing options. Understanding these biases is crucial for UX designers seeking to persuade users to take specific actions, such as purchasing products, making donations, or selecting service levels.

## Key Takeaways

- **Loss aversion** — Losses loom larger than gains in users' minds; people overweight small probabilities to guard against losses and often prefer a smaller certain loss over a potential large loss.
- **Certainty bias** — People overweigh options that are certain and are risk averse for gains; they would rather accept a small but certain reward over a mere chance at a larger gain.
- **Isolation effect** — People disregard elements common to both options when comparing alternatives, focusing only on differentiators, which can lead to inconsistent choices depending on how alternatives are presented.
- **Framing effects** — How information is presented (as a gain or loss) significantly influences user decisions; negatively framed messages prime people to think of possible losses.
- **User experience and loss perception** — Users react more strongly to moments of loss (frustration or confusion) than to things working as expected; small stumbling blocks are remembered far longer than positive experiences.

## Quotes

> Prospect theory: How people choose between different options (or prospects) and how they estimate (many times in a biased or incorrect way) the *perceived* likelihood of each of these options.

> We react more strongly to moments of loss — in the form of frustration or confusion that may occur during an interaction with a website or an app. When everything works as expected, people consider that the norm. But, once anything goes slightly wrong, people balk and remember those bad experiences for much longer.

## Concepts

- [[Cognitive Bias]] — Prospect theory describes systematic deviations from rational decision-making, including loss aversion and certainty bias that influence how users evaluate options.
- [[Behavioral Economics]] — Users experience the pain of losses more strongly than the pleasure of equivalent gains, leading them to take defensive actions and prefer guaranteed outcomes over risky alternatives.
- [[Decision Making]] — Understanding prospect theory helps designers anticipate how users will choose between options and frame choices to align with user goals.
- [[Persuasive Design]] — By understanding biases like loss aversion and certainty, designers can ethically persuade users to take desired actions through better framing and information presentation.
- [[User Behavior]] — Prospect theory explains why users often make seemingly illogical choices and how context, framing, and perception of risk shape their behavior in digital interfaces.
