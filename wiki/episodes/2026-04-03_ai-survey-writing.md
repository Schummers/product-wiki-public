# AI Can Help with Survey Writing, But It Still Requires Human Expertise

## Summary

GenAI tools like ChatGPT and Claude can generate solid survey drafts quickly, but do not replace survey expertise. These tools handle foundational tasks well—generating questions covering multiple dimensions, writing in clear neutral language, grouping related questions—but miss important nuances. GenAI tends to underestimate respondent burden, generate flawed response options, overlook useful response formats, and produce leading tasks in AI-moderated studies. A solid first draft is only a starting point; experienced survey designers must review and refine outputs.

## Key Takeaways

- **GenAI strengths** — Generates questions covering multiple dimensions, writes in clear language, avoids double-barreled wording, groups questions logically, suggests open-ended and closed-ended mix.
- **Respondent burden risks** — GenAI underestimates how effortful surveys feel; it may suggest grid questions (increasing straightlining), too many multiselect options, demographic questions too early.
- **Flawed response options** — GenAI produces options that seem reasonable but have issues: missing "Other," unbalanced scales, custom Likert scales that don't match standard formats.
- **Overlooked formats** — GenAI may not suggest semantic differential scales or rank-order questions when they would fit the research goal.
- **Leading tasks in AI-moderated** — AI-generated study tasks often prime participants unrealistically, making results untrustworthy.

## Quotes

> "A solid first draft is still only a starting point. Although these tools can handle some aspects of survey design surprisingly well, they also show weaknesses in other important areas."

> "AI can support the work, but it still takes an experienced survey designer to tell the difference between a draft that is seemingly good and one that will actually yield useful, trustworthy data."
