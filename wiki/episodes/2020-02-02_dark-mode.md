# Dark Mode vs. Light Mode: Which Is Better?

## Summary

Research indicates that positive contrast polarity (light mode with dark text on light background) generally provides better performance for users with normal or corrected vision in most conditions. The human pupil contracts in brighter light, reducing spherical aberrations and improving focus. However, during nighttime with reduced ambient light and for small font sizes, dark mode can be beneficial. For users with visual impairments, particularly those with cloudy ocular media such as cataracts, dark mode may be preferable. Long-term exposure to light mode may carry costs related to myopia, though research is limited. Designers should provide dark mode as an option without making it the default, especially for applications involving long-form reading.

## Key Takeaways

- **Light mode better for normal vision** — Research shows that light mode with positive contrast polarity produces better visual performance for acuity, proofreading, and glanceable reading tasks in users with normal or corrected vision.

- **Pupil size affects visual acuity** — Light mode causes the pupil to contract, reducing spherical aberrations and increasing depth of field. Users can focus without as much eye strain. Smaller pupils also improve visual clarity without straining to focus.

- **Nighttime and small fonts favor dark mode** — During nighttime with low ambient light and for very small font sizes, dark mode can outperform light mode. During bright daytime conditions, the polarity advantage nearly disappears.

- **Accessibility for cloudy media** — Users with cloudy ocular media (cataracts and similar conditions) benefit from dark mode because less light from the display reduces distortion caused by light scattering.

- **Possible long-term myopia effects** — Limited research suggests sustained light mode exposure may be associated with myopia, but this should not be the primary factor in choosing default display modes.

- **Provide user choice** — Allow users to switch to dark mode even if not the default. Offer dark mode especially in applications designed for long-form reading, keeping the option pervasive across all screens.

## Quotes

> In users with normal vision, light mode leads to better performance most of the time.

> Why is light mode better for performance? These findings are best explained by the fact that, with positive contrast polarity, there is more overall light and so the pupil contracts more. As a result, there are fewer spherical aberrations, greater depth of field, and overall better ability to focus on details without tiring the eyes.

> We strongly recommend that designers allow users to switch to dark mode if they want to — for three reasons: (1) there may be long-term effects associated with light mode; (2) some people with visual impairments will do better with dark mode; and (3) some users simply like dark mode better.
