# Diary Studies: Understanding Long-Term User Behavior and Experiences

## Summary

A diary study is a qualitative user research method where participants self-report their interactions and experiences over extended periods ranging from days to months. Unlike field studies requiring in-person observation or usability testing in controlled environments, diary studies collect contextual, self-reported insights remotely and asynchronously, making them cost-effective for understanding long-term user behavior and repetitive activities. The article provides comprehensive guidance on planning, conducting, and analyzing diary studies, including participant recruitment, data collection tools, communication protocols, and analysis strategies.

## Key Takeaways

- **Research scope** — diary studies are useful for exploring long-term experiences, habits, attitudes, behavioral changes, customer journeys, channel usage, and external collaboration factors that cannot be captured in single-session methods.
- **Data collection methods** — three common approaches are event-based (recording when specific interactions occur), interval-based (reporting at regular intervals), and signal-based (responding to prompted signals).
- **Participant considerations** — sample size should reach saturation (typically 5-50 depending on scope and user-group variance); incentives should reflect time commitment (roughly $40/hour for US participants); recruitment should expect dropouts.
- **Tool selection** — researchers choose among all-in-one apps (expensive but streamlined), survey tools (free but require manual coordination), or messaging apps (informal but easy for participants).
- **Analysis approach** — researchers should revisit original research questions, evaluate behavior evolution over time, examine customer journeys holistically, and synthesize themes for actionable insights.

## Quotes

> A diary study is a qualitative user research method used to collect insights about user behaviors, activities, and experiences over time and in context.

> Saturation refers to a moment in qualitative research when your data becomes repetitive — you hear the same thing again and again.

> For studies longer than a week, think about how you might keep users engaged throughout the length of the study. You can break apart the total incentive and offer smaller installments as participants reach specific milestones (e.g., 3 days of logging), to keep them motivated throughout the duration of the study.
