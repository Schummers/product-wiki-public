# Visual Hierarchy in UX: Definition

## Summary

Visual hierarchy guides users' attention to design elements in order of intended importance. It controls information delivery from system to user and prevents overwhelming visual clutter. Hierarchy is created through three primary techniques: color and contrast, scale, and grouping using proximity and common regions. The squint test (applying blur) can validate whether intended hierarchy is actually conveyed. Effective visual hierarchy enables users to easily understand pages, complete tasks, and gain trust in the design and brand.

## Key Takeaways

- **Visual hierarchy guides attention through importance** — it organizes design elements so users' eyes consume them in order of intended importance, establishing focus on a screen.
- **Color and contrast create primary hierarchy** — the contrast between elements and their background determines hierarchy; saturation, type weight, and visual style differences also signal importance.
- **Scale signals relative importance** — larger elements attract attention and should be reserved for the most important content; designers should limit to three sizes (small, medium, large) for clarity.
- **Grouping through proximity and common regions structures hierarchy** — implicit grouping via whitespace and explicit grouping via borders or backgrounds help users see logical relationships and direct attention.
- **The squint test validates hierarchy** — blurring a design reveals whether groupings and hierarchy work as intended, uncovering unintended emphasis from content choices.
- **Content influences hierarchy perception** — even well-designed templates can have disrupted hierarchy if content (e.g., a news photo with strong colors) dominates in unexpected ways.

## Quotes

> The page's visual hierarchy controls the delivery of information from the system to the end user — it lets users know where to focus their attention.

> The eye is first drawn to the search field not only due to its large size, but also its white color contrasted against the black background.

> Before beginning a design, take a step back from the visuals and define the hierarchy of the content and the key point(s) you want the user to take away.
