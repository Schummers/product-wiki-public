# Laws of UX: 11. Applying Psychological Principles in Design

## Summary

Chapter 11 addresses how design teams integrate psychological principles into their daily decision-making processes. It presents three approaches: building awareness through visibility and show-and-tell, establishing design principles as team guideposts, and creating a framework that connects design principles to psychological laws and actionable rules. The chapter provides a complete methodology for defining design principles through workshop processes (identify team, align, diverge, converge, refine, circulate) and demonstrates how to map principles to laws using examples like Hick's Law and Jakob's Law.

## Key Takeaways

- **Design principles as decision anchors** — establishing a shared set of design principles creates a North Star for teams, eliminating bottlenecks from design gatekeepers and ensuring consistency as team size and decision volume increase.

- **Building awareness through visibility** — displaying psychological principle posters in workspaces and conducting regular show-and-tell sessions develop team understanding and create a shared vocabulary around psychology concepts.

- **Workshop-based principle definition** — a structured process of identifying the team, aligning on success criteria, diverging into ideas, converging on themes, refining, and circulating ensures widespread adoption and team buy-in.

- **Principle-to-law-to-rule framework** — design principles are most effective when connected to psychological laws (the observation) and translated into specific rules that teams can follow, creating a clear three-tier decision framework.

- **Qualities of effective principles** — good design principles are direct and actionable (not truisms), solve real design questions, are opinionated with clear prioritization, and are memorable enough for regular use.

- **Example mapping: "clarity over abundance of choice"** — connected to Hick's Law, this principle generates rules like limiting choices to three items and keeping explanations under 80 characters.

## Quotes

> By being constantly visible to the teams, these posters help to remind them of the various psychological principles that can help guide decisions during the design process.

> Design principles can serve as a North Star: guiding values that embody what good design looks like for the team.

> Good design principles are direct, clear, and actionable, not bland and obvious.

## Concepts

- [[Design Principles]] — provides the framework teams use to ensure consistent decision-making by articulating shared priorities, values, and what good design means within their specific context.
- [[Cognitive Psychology]] — the foundational knowledge about how humans think and behave that informs the design principles teams establish.
- [[Design Consistency]] — the core problem that design principles solve by preventing individual team members from defining good design differently and creating inconsistent output.
- [[Hick's Law]] — demonstrated as an example law connected to the design principle "clarity over abundance of choice," showing how psychology principles guide specific rules like limiting choices.
- [[Jakob's Law]] — demonstrated as an example law connected to "familiarity over novelty," illustrating how to build framework rules around using common design patterns and avoiding flashy animations.
- [[User-Centered Design]] — the overarching goal that design principles support: the chapter's premise is that designers who understand how humans behave create better designs, the way an architect who knows how people experience space creates better buildings.
