# 3 Persona Types: Lightweight, Qualitative, and Statistical

## Summary

Personas are memorable, actionable representations of user archetypes that help teams focus on user needs and motivations. This article outlines three approaches to persona creation, each with distinct tradeoffs. Proto personas leverage existing team knowledge without new research, making them fast but potentially inaccurate. Qualitative personas derive from interviews or usability tests with 5-30 users and offer a sweet spot between effort and accuracy. Statistical personas combine qualitative research with large-scale surveys and statistical clustering, providing population-level insights but requiring expertise and significant resources. The choice of approach should depend on organizational maturity, available resources, and need for rigor.

## Key Takeaways

- **Proto personas require no research but risk embedding assumptions** — These lightweight personas quickly expose the team's implicit assumptions about users and create shared direction without expensive research. However, they often misrepresent real users and can damage team credibility in personas if the assumptions prove wrong.
- **Qualitative personas fit most teams' needs** — Based on 5-30 user interviews, these personas capture real motivations, pain points, and expectations. They require modest time investment, can be conducted parallel to other work, and provide the accuracy needed for design decisions. The limitation is they cannot indicate what percentage of the user base each persona represents.
- **Statistical personas provide population-level insights but require significant resources** — Using large-scale surveys (100+ respondents) and statistical clustering, these personas can represent actual user distributions and support recruitment for future studies. However, they require qualitative research upfront, statistical expertise, and often duplicate effort without generating substantially different results than qualitative personas.
- **Never base personas on demographics alone** — Personas rooted only in demographic or analytics data lack context about user motivations and behavior. Even high-level behavioral data cannot answer the "why" behind actions, making assumption-based design decisions inevitable and often wrong.
- **Personas must be rooted in qualitative understanding** — All effective personas reflect the what and why of user needs. Even statistical personas must begin with qualitative research to inform the survey. The core requirement is empathy-inducing detail about user context and motivations.

## Quotes

> Personas used in UX work are a quick, empathy-inducing shorthand for our users’ context,motivations, needs, and approaches to using our products.

> They are meant to help us focus on what matters most to our users and put ourselves in their shoes when making design decisions.

> All three of these have different pros and cons and will be applicable for different situations.
