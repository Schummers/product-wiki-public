# Get the Responses You Want: Designing Diary Study Entries

## Summary

A diary entry is the core data element in a diary study, representing each participant response to research prompts. How the entry template is structured directly affects data quality and participant effort. The article describes three main types of diary entries: closed entries (multiple choice, rating scales) track behaviors and trends efficiently but don't capture the "why" behind patterns; open-ended entries (free-form text) provide rich qualitative detail and surface unexpected insights but require more participant and analyst effort; multimedia entries (photos, screenshots, video) capture visual context that text cannot convey but introduce technical complexity.

The key to effective diary studies is balancing three factors: participant effort (keeping entries to 5-10 minutes), analysis effort (considering how to code and interpret responses), and research goals (deciding which question types are truly necessary). When these factors conflict, participant effort should take priority, since high-effort entries lead to incomplete responses, burnout, and study dropout. Most effective diary studies combine question types strategically, using closed questions for quantitative tracking, open-ended questions for depth, and multimedia selectively when visual context is essential.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three entry types with different tradeoffs** — Closed entries (rating scales, checkboxes) require minimal effort and yield quantitative data but don't reveal underlying motivations; open-ended entries provide rich qualitative insights but demand more participant effort and time to analyze; multimedia entries capture visual context but add technical complexity.
- **Closed entries reveal what, not why** — Closed questions track adoption rates, frequency, and satisfaction but are "less effective for uncovering the why behind patterns"; pair structured questions with short open-ended follow-ups to understand the reasoning behind behaviors.
- **Participant effort takes priority** — Prioritize reducing participant burden before minimizing analysis effort; high-effort entries lead to incomplete responses, rushed answers, or study dropout, which produces low-quality insights even if analysis would be easier.
- **Keep entries short: 5-10 minutes maximum** — Diary entries submitted repeatedly must be kept brief to prevent fatigue and abandonment; entries longer than 15 minutes significantly increase the risk that participants will skip questions or stop reporting altogether.
- **Mix question types strategically** — Effective diary studies combine closed, open-ended, and (sometimes) multimedia questions based on specific research needs; optional fields for high-effort questions allow participants to skip irrelevant prompts, preventing cumulative burden.
