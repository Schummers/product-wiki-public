# Usability Testing of Icons

## Summary

Icon usability depends on four quality criteria: findability, recognition, information scent, and attractiveness. Different testing methods address each criterion at different stages of product development. Out-of-context tests (showing icons in isolation) work well for recognition and information scent early in design; in-context testing measures findability on live pages. Standard usability testing can reveal issues but often conflates multiple problems. Testing should be tailored to the project phase and iterated incrementally to improve icon-label relationships.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four quality criteria for icons** — findability (can users locate it on the page), recognition (do they understand what it represents), information scent (can they predict what happens when clicked), and attractiveness (is it aesthetically pleasing).
- **Out-of-context testing for early stages** — in conceptualization, show icons in isolation and ask users what they represent and what they expect to happen, without revealing the intended meaning.
- **Time-to-locate testing measures findability** — in-context testing on the full interface, measuring how long it takes to find an icon and tracking first-click accuracy.
- **A/B testing for information scent** — on live sites, test alternative icon designs and measure interaction rates and probing behavior (users clicking and immediately leaving).
- **Avoid priming in task wording** — be careful with language in testing; words used in task descriptions can prime specific interpretations and bias results.
- **Attractiveness testing** — use 1-7 rating scales and comparative selection to evaluate individual icons and icon families for visual appeal.

## Quotes

> The only way to know whether a particular icon will work is to test it with users.

> Users presented with an icon must guess what that icon symbolizes. In some ways, this is the icon version of a Rorschach inkblot test.

> It doesn't matter if they don't know what the object is — we needn't worry about finding an old floppy disc to present to youths as long as they continue to understand that the strange square symbol means Save.
