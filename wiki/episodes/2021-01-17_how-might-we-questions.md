# Using "How Might We" Questions to Ideate on the Right Problems

## Summary

How Might We (HMW) questions are a problem-framing technique borrowed from Procter & Gamble and popularized by IDEO. They are used at the end of discovery research to frame design challenges and prevent teams from defaulting to pet solutions. Well-written HMW questions should be grounded in actual research findings, avoid suggesting solutions, remain broad enough to generate multiple ideas, focus on desired outcomes rather than symptoms, and use positive language. The technique helps teams move from problem discovery to ideation by creating a shared understanding of what needs solving.

## Key Takeaways

- **HMW questions ground ideation in research** — they should be based on actual problems and insights discovered in research, not generic design challenges.
- **HMW questions avoid solution bias** — poor HMW questions embed suggested solutions (e.g., "tell users") which narrows idea generation; good HMW questions remain solution-agnostic.
- **Breadth generates more ideas** — overly narrow HMW questions limit possibilities; broader questions allow for multiple solution approaches, but without losing sight of the core problem.
- **Focus on desired outcomes, not symptoms** — HMW questions should target the root problem users face, not the symptom (e.g., user confidence vs. stopping users from calling).
- **Positive framing encourages creativity** — using positive action verbs (increase, create, enhance) instead of negative ones (reduce, remove, prevent) generates more creative ideas.
- **Team collaboration improves questions** — teams should write and select HMW questions together using a checklist to ensure quality before ideation.
