# Laws of UX: Preface

## Summary

The Preface introduces *Laws of UX*, a resource designed to make psychological principles accessible to designers without formal training in behavioral science. Yablonski created this book after discovering that psychology provided the empirical foundation he needed to justify design decisions when quantitative or qualitative data was unavailable. The book presents psychological principles—not as strict laws but as guidelines grounded in observed patterns of human behavior—that designers can use to build more intuitive, human-centered products.

The second edition adds contemporary context (LLMs, AI-powered image generation, spatial computing) while emphasizing that the psychological principles remain timeless. Yablonski argues that understanding psychology is fundamental for all designers, enabling them to design products that adapt to how people actually perceive and interact with the world rather than forcing users to adapt to the design.

## Key Takeaways

- **Psychology as foundation** — Designers can use psychological principles from cognitive and behavioral psychology to justify design decisions and understand why people behave certain ways, even without quantitative research data.
- **Guidelines over laws** — The principles in this book are guidelines informed by observed patterns of human behavior, not absolute laws to be rigidly followed; they complement rather than replace user research.
- **Human-centered design fundamentals** — Understanding the human "blueprint" for how we perceive and process the world allows designers to create products adapted to people's actual capabilities and limitations, which is the foundation of human-centered design.
- **Accessibility to designers** — The book distills complex psychological concepts into language and examples directly relevant to design practice, making psychology useful for professional and aspiring designers without specialized background knowledge.
- **Timeless relevance** — Although technology and interaction methods constantly evolve, the underlying psychological principles that govern human perception and behavior remain stable across different eras and mediums.

## Quotes

> Psychology, which provides a deeper understanding of the human mind, could be helpful in these circumstances. I quickly became immersed in the rich and expansive field of behavioral and cognitive psychology, and found myself reading through countless research papers and articles in order to find empirical evidence that supported the design decisions I was making.

> As humans, we have an underlying "blueprint" for how we perceive and process the world around us, and the study of psychology helps us decipher this blueprint. Designers can use this knowledge to build more intuitive, human-centered products and experiences.

> This is the fundamental basis of human-centered design, and it's the foundation of this book.

## Concepts

- [[Cognitive Psychology]] — The study of how humans perceive and process information, which the Preface identifies as essential for designers to understand the "blueprint" of human perception and behavior.
- [[User-Centered Design]] — The foundational design approach Yablonski advocates, which adapts products to how people actually perceive and process the world rather than forcing users to adapt to the design.
- [[Design Principles]] — The psychological guidelines presented in this book that help designers make decisions aligned with human behavior patterns and cognitive capabilities.
- [[User Behavior]] — The observable patterns of how people interact with products and systems, which psychology helps explain and which designers can use to inform design decisions.
