# Four Factors in UX Maturity

## Summary

UX maturity describes an organization's capability to consistently apply user-centered design across products and services. The updated UX Maturity Model identifies four high-level factors that shape an organization's maturity level: Strategy (UX leadership and planning), Culture (attitudes toward UX and career development), Process (systematic use of UX methods), and Outcomes (intentional goal-setting and measurement). Organizations at the same maturity stage may look different due to size, priorities, and politics; maturity is not a single score but a multidimensional profile where different factors may be stronger or weaker.

## Key Takeaways

- **Strategy Factor** — Consists of vision (user-centered objectives), planning and prioritization (embedding UX in schedules and processes), and budget allocation; low-maturity organizations rarely mention UX in schedules, while high-maturity organizations prioritize UX work and track quality systematically.
- **Culture Factor** — Encompasses awareness (knowledge of UX across the organization), appreciation and support (respect for UX and C-suite involvement), competency (defined UX skills and career paths), and adaptability (willingness and ability to evolve practices); rigid organizations struggle to adopt and adjust UX approaches.
- **Process Factor** — Includes methods (established use of research and design across the lifecycle), collaboration (cross-functional teamwork), and consistency (shared frameworks and tools); low-maturity organizations use methods haphazardly or in response to specific needs rather than embedding them.
- **Outcomes Factor** — Addresses design impact (success measured by user-centered metrics, not just features) and measurement (tracking and using user-centered goals); low-maturity organizations chase metrics unrelated to user needs.
- **Synergistic Growth** — Rather than optimizing a single factor, organizations should grow all factors in parallel because they reinforce each other; growing all factors together provides better ROI than pushing one factor far ahead of the others.

## Concepts

- [[UX Maturity]] — UX maturity describes an organization's capability to consistently apply user-centered design; it is assessed across four interdependent factors (strategy, culture, process, outcomes) with each factor containing subfactors that together indicate an organization's overall maturity level.
- [[Organizational Culture]] — Culture encompasses organizational awareness of UX value, support for UX practitioners, competency and career development, and adaptability to evolving practices; it is fundamental to sustainable UX maturity.
- [[UX Strategy]] — Strategy includes vision (user-centered organizational objectives), planning and prioritization (embedding UX in development processes), and budget allocation (adequate resources for UX initiatives).
