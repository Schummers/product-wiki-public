# How to Maintain a UX Portfolio Over Time

## Summary

Maintaining an up-to-date UX portfolio is challenging as years pass and project documentation becomes incomplete due to fallible human memory. Rather than an overwhelming annual portfolio overhaul, the article proposes a more manageable approach: a new year's self-assessment that systematically reviews the past year's skills, accomplishments, professional activities, and career development. This approach involves preparing materials, conducting a structured self-assessment across 5 dimensions, and creating a curated presentation with images and notes to serve as future portfolio source material.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prevention through regular capture** — Instead of retrospective scrambling, capture deliverables throughout the year: sketches, whiteboards, research notes, prototypes, presentations, feedback, performance metrics, and photos of physical work.
- **Expansive view of deliverables** — Include not just high-fidelity designs but also wireframes, journey maps, personas, research plans, specifications, presentations, and evidence of professional activities to show full process.
- **Self-assessment structure** — Systematically review skills/tools/abilities, accomplishments, professional activities, career management, and disappointments; each category surfaces different evidence useful for future portfolio storytelling.
- **Curated presentation format** — Create a roughly 30-slide presentation deck with images, speaker notes (3-4 sentences per slide), and context about process, collaborators, and project goals; avoid deep dives into single projects.
- **Legal and ethical obligations** — Remember to anonymize, redact, or omit materials covered by NDAs, employment contracts, or privacy regulations; flag items for later compliance work but don't skip it entirely.
- **Measurement and outcomes focus** — Capture before-state documentation and quantifiable results or KPIs to demonstrate impact; portfolio effectiveness depends on showing not just what was created but whether it achieved goals.

## Quotes

> Incomplete and less persuasive material will result in a low-quality UX portfolio and poor job prospects.

> Your future self will thank you.
