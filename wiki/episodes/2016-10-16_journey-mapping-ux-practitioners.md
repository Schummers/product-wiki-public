# Journey Mapping in Real Life: A Survey of UX Practitioners

## Summary

Survey of 48 UX professionals revealed how journey mapping is used in practice and when it succeeds or fails. Most maps include Zone A (Lens: personas, scenarios) and Zone B (Experience: actions, thoughts, emotions) but omit Zone C (Insights: opportunities, ownership, metrics), the most critical section for driving organizational action. Journey maps fail when lacking clear focus (36%), not grounded in user research data (25%), not shared or used (21%), or lacking organizational trust/buy-in (11%). They succeed when collaborative and shared, lead to action, tightly focused, and produce measurable results. The gap between what makes maps successful and current practice represents a significant opportunity for improvement.

## Key Takeaways

- **Omitted insights zone undermines effectiveness** — Most practitioners exclude opportunities, ownership, and metrics from their maps, yet these elements are essential for transforming maps from narrative into action plan. Without ownership, no accountability exists; without metrics, improvement cannot be measured.
- **Clear, defined focus is critical for success** — 52% of frustrations center on unclear scope and process. Maps become too general and unfocused when purpose and goals are not articulated upfront. Defining specific scenarios and actors sets necessary constraints.
- **Journey maps must be grounded in user research** — 25% of failures result from maps based on assumptions rather than data. Preconceptions and stakeholder assumptions fail to gain traction. Valid maps require research-based narratives that reflect user realities.
- **Collaborative creation drives buy-in and adoption** — 37% of success factors involve creating maps as teams and sharing broadly. Cross-functional participation generates buy-in, ensures multiple perspectives inform the narrative, and spreads understanding across the organization.
- **Maps must lead to action and produce results** — 27% of success depends on actually implementing recommendations. Maps that sit on shelves, unchanged, fail. Successful maps include measurable outcomes that demonstrate ROI and drive implementation decisions.
- **Common goals and vision are primary benefits** — 32% of responses cited shared understanding and alignment as the top benefit. Maps help cross-department consensus and collaborative focus on customer needs rather than departmental silos.
