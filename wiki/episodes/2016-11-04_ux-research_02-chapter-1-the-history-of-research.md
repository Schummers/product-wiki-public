# UX Research: Chapter 1: The History of Research

## Summary

This chapter traces the evolution of research and design from early twentieth-century industrial engineering through the digital age. Beginning with Frank Gilbreth's motion studies and Henry Ford's assembly line innovations, it demonstrates how systematic observation and measurement improved efficiency in manufacturing. The chapter then examines the emergence of human factors engineering, exemplified by the kitchen's Golden Triangle, which applied research to improve everyday life for consumers. It introduces GOMS (Goals, Operators, Methods, Selection rules) and Keystroke-Level Modeling as frameworks for analyzing task performance, and concludes by showing how diverse professional backgrounds—computer science, psychology, ethnography, library science, and design—collectively shape modern UX practice.

## Key Takeaways

- **Frank Gilbreth's motion studies** — Industrial engineer Frank Gilbreth studied how bricklayers, construction workers, and manufacturing employees moved through space, and standardized motions to improve worker productivity and efficiency systematically rather than through guesswork.
- **Human factors and everyday life** — Research evolved beyond manufacturing efficiency to improve consumers' everyday experiences; the kitchen's Golden Triangle (positioning sink, oven, and refrigerator) shows intent to improve individuals' lives on a personal level through human motion studies and physiological understanding.
- **GOMS framework** — Goals, Operators, Methods, and Selection rules measure the intent and process of both physical and digital systems; Goals ask what people want to do, Operators identify available tools and cognitive hurdles, Methods explore how tools accomplish the goal, and Selection rules address the different options available.
- **Keystroke-Level Modeling** — KLM applies Fitts's law to measure the time and efficiency of micro interactions in digital products, allowing designers to estimate task completion times by calculating target size and distance.
- **Diverse backgrounds strengthen UX** — UX practitioners come from computer science, psychology, ethnography, library science, and design; diverse professional backgrounds reduce the risk of stagnation and enable teams to approach problems creatively and efficiently.

## Quotes

> Gilbreth believed that by understanding how people moved in space we could design and engineer more efficient systems.

> Rather than thousands of people with the same professional upbringing and perspective, our mosaic of backgrounds arms UX designers, researchers, and developers with the skills to take on digital interfaces, physical products, and the convergence of the two in everyday life.

## Concepts

- [[Task Analysis]] — GOMS and Keystroke-Level Modeling provide systematic methods for analyzing and measuring how people accomplish tasks, from physical motions to digital keystroke sequences.
- [[Human Factors Engineering]] — The chapter explicitly covers human factors as a field of study that improved design by understanding human physiology, motion, and capabilities in both physical spaces like kitchens and digital systems.
- [[Research Methods]] — Motion studies, GOMS, and KLM represent methods for measuring and improving efficiency and performance that evolved from manufacturing into human-computer interaction.
- [[User-Centered Design]] — The chapter traces the shift from utilitarian, engineer-focused design toward design that considers human needs and emotional responses, recognizing that services must now strike on an emotional level as well as provide utility.
