# Rating Scales in UX Research: Likert or Semantic Differential?

## Summary

Likert scales and semantic differential scales are both common rating-scale methods in UX surveys, but they measure attitudes differently and have distinct limitations. Likert scales ask respondents how much they agree or disagree with statements, measuring agreement on typically 5-point scales. Semantic differential scales ask respondents to rate attitudes on a 7-point continuum between bipolar adjective pairs (e.g., easy-difficult). Likert scales are vulnerable to acquiescence bias (tendency to agree) and social-desirability bias (tendency to report favorably). Semantic differential scales require higher cognitive effort due to unlabeled options but provide cognitive flexibility and don't feel constraining like Likert labels might. Likert scales are more flexible for various applications, while semantic differential is ideal for rating specific attributes. Choosing between them requires understanding research goals and testing both approaches with users.

## Key Takeaways

- **Likert scales measure agreement, semantic differential rates attitudes** — Likert asks how much someone agrees with statements, while semantic differential positions responses on a continuum between opposites.
- **Acquiescence bias affects Likert scales** — people naturally tend to agree with statements, and priming (positive or negative framing) can push responses in particular directions.
- **Social-desirability bias influences both but manageable** — asking for names or identifiers increases this bias; removing identifying information reduces the tendency to report favorably.
- **Semantic differential requires more cognitive effort** — unlabeled options demand abstract thinking about attitudes, though this provides flexibility and doesn't constrain respondents within predefined categories.
- **Likert scales are more versatile** — they work for agreement/disagreement statements better than semantic differential for complex statements (e.g., "We don't begin thinking about solutions until discovery is complete").
- **Test both with users before deploying** — conducting in-person qualitative surveys or A/B testing both versions with your target audience helps determine which works better for your questions and respondents.

## Quotes
