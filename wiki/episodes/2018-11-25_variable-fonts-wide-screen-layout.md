# Variable Fonts and Wide-Screen Layouts: Adopting Data-Driven Progressive Enhancements

## Summary

This article discusses how Nielsen Norman Group updated their website by implementing two major technical changes: adding layouts for wide screens and adopting variable fonts. Both changes are implemented as progressive enhancements that benefit users with particular device types. The authors emphasize that before investing development resources in complex enhancements, teams should review analytics data to determine how many users would benefit and whether the investment is worthwhile.

## Key Takeaways

- **Analytics-driven decisions** — Before implementing costly features, analyze website traffic to determine what proportion of users would benefit. The authors found that 77% of NN/g's visitors use browsers wider than 1100 pixels, justifying investment in wide-screen layouts.

- **Variable fonts solve performance-aesthetics tradeoff** — Variable fonts allow designers to use a single font file while adjusting weight variations in the browser, eliminating the need for multiple font downloads. This improves both visual design quality and page load performance.

- **80% browser support enables implementation** — Variable fonts have achieved 80% browser support for nngroup.com users, with the remaining 20% receiving fallback browser-safe fonts, making the enhancement viable without harming the minority of unsupported users.

- **Prioritize by audience size and impact severity** — When 80% of users benefit from an enhancement while 20% see no change (but don't suffer harm), the enhancement is worthwhile. However, features that impose severe pain on minorities should be avoided even if they benefit the majority.

- **Conservative approach to trends** — The authors acknowledge their selective approach to design trends, adopting only innovations that provide genuine UX value rather than following fashion. Less scrolling and more content for large-screen users represents genuine value.

## Quotes

> "Currently, 80% of sessions on nngroup.com are on browsers that support variable fonts, and we expect this number to increase further soon, when Firefox for Windows will implement its planned support for this feature."

> "Whether it be new technology or extensive breakpoints, look at your own website's analytics before deciding where it is worth investing your design and development resources."

> "The 20% of traffic from browsers that do not support variable fonts will see a fallback, with browser-safe fonts plus one custom font, as our legacy site used to have."

## Concepts

- [[Progressive Enhancement]] — The authors present variable fonts and wide-screen layouts as progressive enhancements that improve the experience for modern browsers without breaking functionality for older ones.

- [[Variable Fonts]] — Single font files that allow weight adjustment in the browser, solving the historic performance versus aesthetics tradeoff by eliminating the need for multiple font-file downloads.

- [[Data-Driven Design]] — The article advocates examining analytics to justify design investments, measuring the proportion of users who would benefit before committing development resources.
