# Venture Studios Demystified: Third-Party Agencies

## Summary

Part 3a of Venture Studios Demystified explores how studios have evolved their approach to engineering and product development. Early venture studio designs typically incorporated full in-house engineering, product, and design teams to build MVPs and prototypes. However, the chapter documents a significant operational shift as many studios have begun outsourcing development work to third-party agencies to reduce costs and operational complexity.

The chapter reveals that studios increasingly view outsourcing as more cost-effective than maintaining large permanent teams, particularly for early-stage venture concepts. This operational change is driven by economics, as personnel costs to maintain engineering teams often exceed what management fees can support, and the difficulty of transitioning technical talent between studio and ventures.

## Key Takeaways

- **Economic Cost of In-House Teams** — Personnel costs for maintaining full engineering, product, and design teams are often too large to be supported by management fees or place significant strain on operating budgets, creating pressure to reduce headcount.
- **Talent Transition Challenges** — Moving technical talent between the studio and its launched ventures is difficult in practice, with studio engineers frequently leaving to join newly established ventures and leaving ventures with significant technical gaps.
- **Outsourcing Cost Advantage** — For early-stage concepts, outsourcing development work to third-party agencies is cheaper than maintaining staff, and studios often maintain networks of partner agencies that offer discounted rates.
- **Venture Capital Allocation** — Studios can fund outsourced services using either their operating budget (for pre-venture services) or the ventures' allocated invested capital, and some studios bill ventures back for these services once incorporated.
- **Leaner Studio Teams** — Several studios that initially had large engineering teams have chosen to transition to leaner teams focused primarily on ideation, recruiting, and administration (legal, HR, finance), viewing these as their unique value proposition.

## Quotes

> While studios initially kept all engineering, product, and design work in-house, we've started to see many studios outsource development work to third-party agencies because they are cheaper and easier to manage.

> Personnel costs to maintain these teams can be too large to be supported by management fees, or they may place significant strain on operating budgets. In addition, transitioning technical talent between studio and ventures can be difficult.

> This appears to be where a number of studios have determined their unique value proposition lies.
