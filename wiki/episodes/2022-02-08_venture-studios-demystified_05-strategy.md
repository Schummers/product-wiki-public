# Venture Studios Demystified: Strategy

## Summary

This chapter examines the strategic shift happening in venture studios as they confront financial limitations. The chapter contrasts the studio model's early-stage, cheap-equity approach with the venture capital model, which now focuses on larger check sizes and later-stage companies due to massive growth in assets under management. Studios initially capture substantial founding equity but see heavy dilution in subsequent rounds, reducing exit returns. In response, many studios are raising their own investment funds to participate in pro-rata follow-on rounds, maintain ownership stakes, and generate management fees to offset operating costs. This represents a convergence between traditional VC and the studio model.

## Key Takeaways

- **VC Model Growth Challenge** — Venture capital assets under management have grown faster than deal counts, resulting in larger check sizes and mega-deals capturing 44% of VC capital. This incentivizes VCs to invest in later-stage companies, moving downstream.
- **Studio's Early-Stage Advantage** — The original studio advantage was cheap founding equity (near zero valuation) enabling strong returns even on 50-100x exits. However, studios absorb front-loaded company building costs while seeking patient capital before exits materialize.
- **Dilution Problem** — Studios starting with 30-45% founding equity face heavy dilution in subsequent rounds as new investors and employees take equity. At exit, studio ownership can fall as low as 5%, limiting absolute returns despite cheap entry price.
- **Fund Raise Response** — 11 of 19 interviewed studios already raised investment funds and 4 more plan to do so. Fund management fees help offset studio operating expenses and extend runway before requiring portfolio company exits.
- **Pro-Rata Strategy** — Raising funds enables studios to maintain target ownership stakes (often 20%) by investing their pro-rata allocation in subsequent funding rounds. While paying higher valuations, this produces significantly higher absolute returns at exit (potentially $22M per exit).
- **Model Convergence** — Approximately 50% of studios now use their investment funds for external investments outside their own studios, moving closer to traditional VC economics and diversifying capital deployment beyond their internal portfolio.

## Quotes

> The venture studio model is premised on generating returns by receiving cheap initial ownership, not through writing large checks. However, studio ownership gets heavily diluted in subsequent rounds.

> Though a studio may start at a ~30-45% equity stake at founding, over time that stake is diluted as VCs and new employees take pieces of the pie. At exit, a studio's ownership stake could be as low as 5%.

> Studios and VC firms are similar in function, both spurring entrepreneurship in exchange for ownership. However, their approaches are quite different.
