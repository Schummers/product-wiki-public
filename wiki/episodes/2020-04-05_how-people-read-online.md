# How People Read Online: New and Old Findings

## Summary

This article reports on the second edition of Nielsen Norman Group's How People Read Online report, examining findings from five eyetracking studies spanning 13 years with over 500 participants and 750 hours of tracking time. While many fundamental reading behaviors from 2006 persist, new gaze patterns emerged in response to modern design trends. The research shows that basic human behavior remains stable even as technology changes, validating guidelines based on fundamental human nature.

## Key Takeaways

- **Scanning Remains Dominant** — people rarely read online word-for-word; they are far more likely to scan selectively, a behavior unchanged for 23 years and based on fundamental human information-seeking efficiency.
- **Lawn-Mower Pattern** — on pages with distinct cells of content (comparison tables, zigzag layouts), users often scan in a lawn-mower pattern: left-to-right across rows, then alternate direction on next row, continuing down the page.
- **Pinball Pattern** — users scan modern SERPs (search engine results pages) in nonlinear pinball patterns, bouncing between search results and SERP features like knowledge panels, likely due to their high visual weight and density.
- **Gaze Patterns Persist Across Cultures** — American users and Beijing users demonstrated nearly identical reading patterns despite differences in culture, character sets, and site design complexity, suggesting behaviors are universal human patterns.
- **Pull Quotes Disrupt Reading** — pull quotes and inline elements interrupt linear reading; users begin reading linearly but abandon careful reading upon encountering these elements, switching to light scanning.
- **Motivation Determines Depth** — the amount of time users spend reading depends on four factors: level of motivation, type of task, level of focus, and personal reading characteristics.
- **Content Design Guidelines** — use clear headings to break content, place information up front, employ formatting like bulleted lists and bold text, and use plain language to support scanning behavior.

## Quotes

> **People rarely read online**— they're far more likely to scan than read word for word. That's one fundamental truth of online information-seeking behavior **that hasn't changed in 23 years** and which has substantial implications for how we create digital content.

> Technology changes quickly, but humans don't.

> People are not likely to read your content completely or linearly. They just want to pick out the information that is most pertinent to their current needs.
