# UX-Maturity Stage 4: Structured

## Summary

Stage 4 organizations recognize UX's value and have one or more established UX teams. Leadership usually supports UX and sometimes incorporates it into high-level strategies. Design is widely understood across the organization and there is an established, iterative human-centered-design process. User research occurs throughout the product lifecycle. While most positives outweigh weaknesses at this stage, challenges include convincing remaining skeptics, managing specialized UX roles as teams scale, incorporating UX into company metrics, and avoiding resource allocation toward just a few customers. Stage 4 represents a structured but not yet fully systematic or optimized level of UX maturity.

## Key Takeaways

- **UX is established but not universal** — Most teams follow documented design processes and conduct discovery research, but some teams still struggle to apply methods correctly or may not prioritize UX work.
- **Strategy includes user-centered ideas** — UX contributes to organizational vision and there is a shared method for prioritizing UX work, though some decisions remain driven by business metrics rather than user needs.
- **Design systems are emerging** — Organizations start building design systems and gaining traction with development and business partners, though inconsistencies may still exist.
- **Some leaders remain resistant** — While UX is relatively respected, pockets of the organization lack UX support. A few leaders may still question UX's value or strategic importance.
- **Progression requires process flexibility** — Moving to stage 5 requires identifying why some teams struggle with UX methods, understanding if the overall process needs adjustment, and adapting approaches to fit different teams' unique needs.

## Quotes

> Stage-4 organizations recognize the value of UX and have one or more established UX teams. Leadership usually supports UX and sometimes even incorporates it into high-level strategies and initiatives.

> The positives often outweigh the weaknesses at this stage, but there are still some obstacles to overcome. These include convincing the few remaining unsupportive leaders, assigning responsibilities across specialized UX roles as teams scale, incorporating UX into company metrics, and excessively allocating UX resources to a few big customers instead of all teams.

> The important thing to remember at this stage is that UX is well-respected in the organization. That's a great thing! Moving up to the next stage requires small improvements rather than a complete overhaul.
