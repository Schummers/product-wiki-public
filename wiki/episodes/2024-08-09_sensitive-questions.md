# Handling Sensitive Questions in Surveys and Screeners

## Summary

This article guides researchers through handling survey and screener questions that respondents may find sensitive or invasive, including demographic questions and questions about socially undesirable behaviors. It explains why sensitive questions matter for data quality and outlines guidelines for asking them responsibly, including emphasizing confidentiality, providing context, using ranges instead of specific values, and employing indirect techniques. The article includes specific recommended wordings for common sensitive demographic questions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Sensitive questions reduce data quality** — respondents may abandon surveys or provide unreliable answers when uncomfortable; income questions are 10 times more likely to be left blank than other demographic questions.
- **Demographic questions can be offensive** — age, gender, race, and income questions must be carefully worded to be inclusive, avoid assumptions, and respect diverse identities.
- **Question placement matters** — placing sensitive questions after building rapport through nonsensitive questions increases completion rates; demographic questions belong at the end, not the beginning.
- **Ranges feel safer than specific values** — income ranges, age brackets, and frequency options reduce apprehension about exactness and comparison to others.
- **Question loading provides context** — adding explanatory context that normalizes potentially shameful behaviors increases honest responses, as does indirect techniques like the item-count method.
