# The 6 Types of Conversations with Generative AI

## Summary

Based on analysis of 425 interactions with generative AI bots like ChatGPT, Bing Chat, and Bard, researchers identified six distinct types of conversations that users have with these tools. Different conversation types serve different information needs and demand varied UI designs. Contrary to assumptions, conversation length is not a success indicator; both short and long conversations can be helpful depending on the user's goals and how well-articulated their initial prompt is.

## Key Takeaways

- **Search Query conversations** are one-prompt, simple queries with no refinements, where users transfer their search engine mental models to AI bots without providing sufficient context for effective responses.
- **Funneling conversations** start with vague, underspecified queries and require multiple back-and-forth exchanges as users narrow down requirements, though the underlying information need is usually specific.
- **Exploring conversations** occur when users have less-defined information needs and use AI responses to build knowledge through depth, learning terminology and asking follow-up questions based on bot answers.
- **Chiseling conversations** examine multiple facets of a single topic for breadth rather than depth, with users asking varied related questions without building on previous bot responses.
- **Pinpointing conversations** require significant user effort upfront with detailed, well-specified prompts that include all necessary context and format specifications, resulting in shorter exchanges.
- **Expanding conversations** start with narrow queries that users broaden after receiving unsatisfactory results, often removing criteria from original queries to get meaningful responses.

## Quotes

> Through analyzing 425 interactions with generative-AI bots like ChatGPT, Bing Chat, and Bard, we've discovered that conversations could involve many vague, underspecified prompts or few, razor-sharp ones. Why does this matter? First, different conversation types serve distinct information needs and demand varied UI designs. Second, there is no one optimal conversation length — both short and long conversations can be helpful, as they might support different user goals.

> In funneling conversations, the user's information need is usually specific and well-defined, but poorly articulated. In other words, the user will likely recognize a correct response, but will not be able (or sometimes will not be bothered) to say what that correct response should look like.

> For exploring and chiseling conversations, length is part of the conversation's nature and serves the user's less well-defined information need. Exploring conversations require a back-and-forth between the bot and the human, with the human learning from the bot and choosing to go deeper and build upon the bot's response.
