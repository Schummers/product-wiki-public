# Venture Studios Demystified: Part 1: Venture Studio Basics

## Summary

Part 1 of Venture Studios Demystified introduces the core concept and function of venture studios as entities that build startups at scale. Venture studios are fully-equipped teams of builders that develop venture ideas internally, test them, build early MVPs, recruit founding teams, and provide subsequent funding. This chapter establishes that studios have grown 625% since 2013, with 710 studios operating worldwide, and are significantly more hands-on than traditional startup support models like accelerators and incubators.

## Key Takeaways

- **Definition and Role** — Venture studios found startups by developing ideas internally, conducting testing, building MVPs, assembling founding teams, and providing initial capital. They operate as co-founders themselves, not merely advisors or investors.
- **Growth and Scale** — The venture studio model has experienced rapid adoption, growing 625% since 2013 with 710 studios now operating globally, though still smaller than the 1,965 venture capital firms in the United States.
- **Repeatable Process** — Studios develop standardized venture-building processes designed to reduce failure probability by evaluating product-market fit upfront through user research and implementing stage gates to prevent unsuccessful ideas from consuming resources.
- **Equity Structure** — In exchange for their substantial contribution to ideation, product development, team building, and initial funding, studios receive co-founding equity stakes priced at near zero at issuance, providing strong financial upside compared to traditional VC investment models.
- **Hands-On Involvement** — Unlike accelerators and incubators that provide time-bound, scalable program-based support, studios design and develop their own ventures with substantial, ongoing involvement beyond program timelines.
- **Ecosystem Benefits** — By funding venture ideas upfront, studios enable entrepreneurs who lack personal capital to participate in entrepreneurship and create a positive flywheel in entrepreneurial ecosystems as successful studio founders become venture capitalists, serial founders, or mentors.

## Quotes

> Studios develop venture ideas internally, conduct initial testing, build early MVPs, hire the founding team, and can even provide subsequent funding.

> Studios specifically design their processes to reduce the probability of failure. This includes evaluating product-market fit upfront via user research, and implementing stage gates to prevent bad ideas from advancing too far in the process.

> Studios take a founding equity stake in their launched companies in exchange for their efforts. Compared to venture capital, where investors get access to companies after their creation and some level of development, studio equity is priced at nearly $0 at issuance.

## Concepts

- [[Venture Studio]] — This chapter defines studios as entities that found startups, develop ideas internally, test them, build MVPs, recruit teams, and provide founding capital.
- [[Idea Validation]] — Studios validate ideas through user research and testing to evaluate product-market fit upfront before investing heavily in development.
- [[Founder Recruitment]] — Studios assemble founding teams for their portfolio companies as a core function, distinct from traditional VC investment.
- [[Stage Gate]] — Studios implement stage gates to prevent unsuccessful ideas from advancing and consuming further resources in the ideation and testing process.
- [[Minimum Viable Product]] — Studios build early MVPs as part of their internal development process to test and validate venture concepts.
- [[Equity Ownership]] — Studios take founding equity stakes priced at near zero at issuance, giving them substantial ownership upside compared to later-stage investors.
