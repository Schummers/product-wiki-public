# Catching Problem Participants in Remote Unmoderated Studies

## Summary

Problem participants (outliers, cheaters, and professional participants) are more common in remote unmoderated studies than in remote moderated or in-person testing. It is critical to identify and exclude nonrepresentative participants to maintain study validity. Three types of problem participants exist: outliers (nonrepresentative users due to background or circumstances), cheaters (participants interested only in payment who don't attempt tasks), and professional participants (frequent study participants too attuned to researcher goals). Detection methods differ based on whether studies are qualitative (watch recordings) or quantitative (use metrics first).

## Key Takeaways

- **Three problem types** — Outliers behave differently because they are different users, not because they are cheating. Cheaters click randomly without attempting tasks. Professional participants have seen too many studies and are too attuned to researcher goals.
- **Qualitative detection** — Watch for outlier signals (different experience level, background, or motivation), cheater signals (not attempting tasks, ignoring task instructions), and professional-participant signals (using terminology betraying too much knowledge like "kerning," "mental model," or "hamburger").
- **Quantitative detection** — Use frequency distributions of task times and task success metrics to identify suspicious participants. Low success rates combined with very fast times are strong cheater indicators. Open-ended text responses reveal lazy answers like nonsense keyboard mashing.
- **Not all outliers cheat** — Some users simply behave differently because they are different, not because they are trying to defraud. An unfavorable response to design alone is not sufficient reason to remove someone from data.
- **Removal decisions** — Remove outliers completely if truly unrepresentative. For cheaters, remove data from individual tasks they cheated on, or remove the entire session if they cheated throughout. Most remote testing tools offer free cheater replacement.
- **Professional-participant handling** — Generally should not remove professional participants if they showed up and participated. Screen future studies to exclude participants who participated within 0-3 or 0-6 months. If flagged in analysis, note the finding when drawing conclusions.

## Quotes

> Outliers are participants whose behavior or performance is very different from the rest of your user population, either because they are not part of your target user group or because they are in exceptional in some other way.

> Cheaters are **** participants interested only in getting paid and moving on to the next study. They may click randomly and not even attempt to perform the tasks.

> Professional participants are people who participate in too many studies too frequently. Often, these people are not representative of 'regular' users because they have seen too many UX-research studies and are too attuned to researchers' goals.

## Concepts

- [[Remote Research]] — Remote unmoderated studies are particularly susceptible to problem participants due to lack of direct facilitator oversight, requiring systematic screening methods to maintain data quality.
- [[Participant Recruitment]] — Proper screening involves watching qualitative recordings and analyzing quantitative metrics to identify participants whose behavior or performance is not representative of the target user population.
- [[Data Quality]] — Removing problem participants ensures that study findings remain valid and represent actual target-user behavior rather than outliers, cheaters, or professional study participants.
