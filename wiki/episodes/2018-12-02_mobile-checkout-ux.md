# The Mobile Checkout Experience

## Summary

This article outlines design guidelines for optimizing mobile checkout flows based on research from Nielsen Norman Group's Ecommerce User Experience report series. Mobile checkout requires special attention because the constraints and capabilities of mobile devices differ significantly from desktop. The guidelines address the entire checkout process from shopping cart management through payment, emphasizing efficiency, clarity, and reducing friction points that lead to cart abandonment.

## Key Takeaways

- **Minimize shopping cart friction** — Provide quick access to the shopping cart, make item removal explicit (not via quantity-to-zero), avoid unnecessary update buttons, and support cross-device continuity so users can seamlessly switch between devices while shopping.

- **Display order summary prominently** — Place order summaries at the top of mobile checkout pages rather than requiring scrolling to see final costs. Users often abandon carts when unexpected fees appear late in the process.

- **Use location data strategically** — Leverage device location detection to estimate taxes and delivery costs early, plus to display nearby store pickup options, reducing uncertainty about final pricing.

- **Optimize form fields for mobile interaction** — Use context-specific keyboards (numeric pad for card numbers, email keyboard for email), auto-calculate fields based on existing data (city and state from ZIP code), and ensure autofill functionality works across browsers.

- **Offer mobile payment alternatives** — Provide PayPal, Apple Pay, and similar services as frictionless payment options, but avoid overwhelming users with too many choices. Allow credit card photo capture via autofill to reduce manual entry burden.

## Quotes

> "Mobile-focused, user-centered UI elements can help move your users through the experience with speed and ease. Though often overlooked and easily forgotten, these seemingly small design details in your mobile checkout will create an experience that delights your users."

> "For each field, present the correct keyboard. Use a numeric pad for numeric data such as credit-card numbers or phone numbers."

> "Make guest checkout prominent and easy to find. Even people who have an account forget passwords; in many cases, it will be easier for them to check out as guests than to recover their password on their mobile device."
