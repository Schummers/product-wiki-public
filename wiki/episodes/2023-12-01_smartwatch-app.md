# Should You Build a Smartwatch App?

## Summary

Smartwatch apps present a dangerous opportunity for feature creep because it is easier to justify building a companion app than to justify building a new app from scratch. However, smartwatch apps only succeed if they provide value beyond convenience. From 200 submitted interactions over 5 days, over 80 percent were in native watch applications like messages and timers; third-party apps struggle to gain traction. Smartwatch value comes from context and the specific advantages of wearables (proximity to body, hands-free usage, quick access) rather than just functionality replication. Many companies should prioritize effective notifications over building standalone apps.

## Key Takeaways

- **Smartwatches enable being away from phones and collect unique biometric data** — but long, complex interactions are difficult on small screens; replicating phone functionality offers little value.
- **Native apps dominate over third-party apps** — over 80 percent of interactions submitted were with native apps like messages and timers, not third-party apps despite their availability.
- **Context determines value, not just functionality** — a smart-home door unlock app has long-term value for frequently-used home tasks when phones are unavailable, while a hotel app has no lasting value after checkout.
- **Third-party apps face adoption barriers** — users purchase smartwatches for specific purposes (notifications, activity tracking), native apps are built for these, and automatic app downloading creates unaware installations.
- **Prioritize notifications over building standalone apps** — notifications were most common interaction type; effective notifications from third-party apps serve users better than standalone apps with low usage.

## Quotes

> Smartwatches present a dangerous opportunity for feature creep. It's much easier to justify building a companion smartwatch app for an existing mobile application than to justify building a new mobile app from scratch. However, for a smartwatch app to get used, it needs to provide value beyond what is convenient and possible on other devices.

> Just because you can think of a function that a smartwatch application could perform, it does not mean users will find it valuable. Do adequate discovery work to determine if and how potential smartwatch applications would be valuable to users.

> Don't build a smartwatch app just because you can. Spend the time and resources to do so only if you are confident that it offers unique value. Replicating basic versions of tasks that users already complete on their phones will not always drive value.

## Concepts

- [[Smart Devices]] — the article argues smartwatch apps require special consideration; not all phone features translate to valuable watch experiences.
- [[Product Strategy]] — building smartwatch apps requires strategic thinking about whether the feature set justifies development effort or whether notifications suffice.
- [[Mobile Design]] — while smartwatches are small mobile devices, their design is distinct from phones; phone app features should not be assumed to work on watches.
- [[Context Design]] — the success of smartwatch apps depends on the specific contexts in which users need them; location, frequency, and hand availability matter.
- [[Prioritization]] — companies must prioritize high-value microinteractions over attempting to replicate the full functionality of phone apps.
