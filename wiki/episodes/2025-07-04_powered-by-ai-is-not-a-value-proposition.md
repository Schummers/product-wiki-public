# \"Powered By AI\" Is Not a Value Proposition

## Summary

A clear value proposition—a promise to users of the value they can expect—guides product teams to prioritize features aligned with a specific user goal and say no to nice-to-have features outside that journey. When teams begin with technology rather than user needs, they risk losing focus and creating products whose value is unclear to users. "Powered by AI" cannot serve as a value proposition because it is a technology, not a user benefit, and it leaves users responsible for identifying how the tool helps them.

Successful products start by solving one problem well. When product teams under pressure from stakeholders to integrate AI treat "it has AI" as a value proposition, they often end up with broad-scope chatbots or inappropriately applied AI features that fail to address user anxieties. Well-defined value propositions begin with clear user goals and determine which technologies, including AI, best enable those goals.

## Key Takeaways

- **Value propositions focus on user goals, not technology** — strong propositions emphasize what users achieve, not how it is built; "accelerate your research" or "never miss a bill" contrast sharply with "easier to use" or "powered by AI."
- **Technology comes after identifying value** — determining what users need to achieve must precede selecting tools; starting with technology often leads to features that don't address real problems.
- **AI presence can trigger fear** — users and designers are aware of LLM limitations and drawbacks; "powered by AI" can reduce conversion likelihood unless specific anxieties are addressed.
- **Broad AI features create focus loss** — when "it has AI" is the value proposition, the only guiding principle becomes making the experience "as AI as possible," often resulting in broad-scope chatbots with high adoption barriers.
- **Focused, specific value propositions sustain business** — products with clear value propositions beyond "powered by AI" land and keep customers longer than those marketed primarily on technology novelty.

## Quotes

> "This framing makes users wholly responsible for identifying opportunities to integrate the tool into their workflows."

> "We should think about how to deliver product value only after firmly establishing what that value is going to be."

> "Successful products start by doing just one thing and doing it well."

## Concepts

- [[Value Proposition]] — a clear promise to users of the specific value they will receive and the user goal they will accomplish by using a product.
- [[AI Product Design]] — approaching AI integration by starting with user needs and selecting AI as a tool when appropriate, not beginning with AI as a given.
- [[Product Focus]] — maintaining a clear, singular user problem and value proposition to guide feature prioritization and eliminate scope creep.
- [[User-Centered Design]] — grounding product decisions in user needs and goals rather than technology capabilities or business trends.
