# PDF: Still Unfit for Human Consumption, 20 Years Later

## Summary

Despite two decades of evidence since Jakob Nielsen's 1996 claim that PDFs should never be read online, PDFs remain problematic for users. The format is intended and optimized for print, is inherently inaccessible, and cumbersome to navigate online. Users strongly dislike PDFs and avoid them when possible. Organizations should either avoid PDFs altogether for digital content or use HTML gateway pages that summarize key information and offer PDFs for download, rather than opening them in browser windows where they create poor user experiences.

## Key Takeaways

- **Linear and limiting** — PDFs are typically converted from print-focused documents and rarely follow web-writing or accessibility guidelines. They present long, noninclusive masses of text and images that require substantial scrolling and are unsuitable for finding quick answers.
- **Jarring user experience** — PDFs look completely different from web pages, taking users out of familiar context into outdated and clunky interfaces. Even polished PDFs appear out of place in browsers, and keyboard shortcuts for searching do not work reliably for all users.
- **Slow to load** — PDFs load slowly on both desktop and mobile. In one study, a cafeteria menu in PDF took almost three minutes to download, causing users to lose interest or abandon the task entirely.
- **Stuffed with fluff** — PDFs lack the structuring and formatting techniques (chunking, bullets, subheadlines, accordions) that help users efficiently skim web content. Content creators favor quantity over quality, resulting in overwhelmingly long PDFs.
- **Cause disorientation** — PDFs lack standard website navigation, causing users to struggle staying oriented about their location. If PDFs open in new windows or tabs unexpectedly, users become confused about how to navigate back.
- **Unnavigable content masses** — Most PDFs have no internal navigation beyond hidden table-of-contents structures. Users must scan tables of contents and may find labels misleading, forcing them to backtrack through many pages.
- **Sized for paper, not screens** — PDF layouts optimized for printed pages misalign with browser windows and result in tiny, unreadable fonts. Mobile viewing requires pinching, zooming, and scrolling out of context.

## Concepts

- [[PDF Usability]] — Seven major usability problems (linear, jarring, slow, fluff-filled, disorienting, unnavigable, and screen-unfriendly) make PDFs fundamentally unsuitable for online reading, despite improvements in browser and system stability.
- [[Content Formats]] — Organizations must choose between HTML web pages or HTML gateway pages for digital content delivery, reserving PDFs only for print-oriented documents when necessary.
- [[UX Writing]] — Web content should be structured with web-specific techniques like chunking, bullets, and accessible formatting, not converted from print-focused documents into PDF format.
