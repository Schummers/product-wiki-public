# Solving Product Design Exercises: Chapter 3: Questions and Answers

## Summary

This chapter introduces a set of worked example exercises that demonstrate how the seven-step framework from Chapter 2 applies in practice. Dashinsky presents five design tasks drawn from real interview scenarios—ranging from hardware kiosks to healthcare systems to ATM redesigns—with the caveat that there is no single correct solution. Rather than providing high-fidelity implementations, the chapter emphasizes solution flow and product thinking, and notes that an appendix contains a framework canvas for working through exercises systematically. Dashinsky reassures candidates that different solutions are expected and acceptable; the value lies in demonstrating structured thinking applied to the problem.

## Key Takeaways

- **Framework is flexible** — There is no single right solution for design exercises; different candidates will arrive at different ideas and still perform well if they follow a structured process.
- **Focus on thinking, not polish** — The worked examples in the chapter emphasize solution flow and product thinking rather than high-fidelity design; candidates preparing for take-home exercises should prioritize demonstrating reasoning over production aesthetics.
- **Five example tasks span contexts** — The tasks cover consumer hardware (soap/shampoo refill kiosk), digital publishing (self-publishing on Amazon), freelancer tools (business dashboard), healthcare (primary care access), and infrastructure (ATM experience), showing that the framework applies broadly.
- **Framework canvas available** — The appendix includes a visual framework canvas (used in the "Improving primary health care" exercise) that candidates can use as a structured template for solving other exercises.

## Quotes

> I decided not to include high-fidelity implementation for take-home exercises, since there are already many resources that could teach you to do that. Instead, I have focused on the solution flow and product thinking described in the book.

> Obviously, there is no single right solution for these exercises, so if your solutions are different from those suggested here (which will most probably happen), don't feel that your solution is wrong.

> Most of them are relevant for whiteboarding or on-site interviews, and all of them can be used as a take-home challenge, if you are given enough time.
