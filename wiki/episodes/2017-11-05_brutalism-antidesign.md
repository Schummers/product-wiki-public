# Brutalism and Antidesign

## Summary

Web brutalism encompasses two distinct styles: brutalism (stripped-down, raw aesthetic inspired by architectural brutalism) and antidesign (intentionally ugly, complex, disorienting interfaces). While brutalism prioritizes function over form and honesty over artificiality, antidesign deliberately creates difficult experiences. Brutalism can work when applied to visual design while maintaining usable interaction, navigation, and hierarchy. Antidesign backfires for general users except in entertainment contexts or for designer audiences who understand the irony.

## Key Takeaways

- **Brutalism philosophy** — emphasizes honesty, rawness, and rejection of artificiality; digital brutalism echoes early 1990s websites with minimal styling, reflecting a reaction against cookie-cutter template designs.
- **Antidesign distinction** — different from brutalism; intentionally creates visual chaos, harsh colors, distracting animations, and poor hierarchy; motivated by humor, attention-seeking, or boredom rather than function.
- **When antidesign fails** — for products requiring task completion and content discovery, antidesign backfires; users need to accomplish goals, not solve puzzles; majority of users prefer clarity over novelty.
- **Brutalism can work** — limited to visual design only, maintaining strong navigation, hierarchy, and interaction patterns; appropriate for entertainment products and designer-audience contexts.
- **User needs dominate** — digital interfaces simplify because that's what users need; designers should not impose complexity for personal interest; the false tension between boring clarity and interesting difficulty misses that usability is the goal.

## Quotes

> Both in architecture and in digital design, brutalism is seen as a reaction against artificiality and lightness.

> We don't need to make things harder for our users just to stand out, or to keep them interested.

> People don't want to solve a puzzle when they use a product: looking around, paying attention, making inferences, reading, and thinking are all hard work. They don't want to be challenged. They just want to keep living their lives.
