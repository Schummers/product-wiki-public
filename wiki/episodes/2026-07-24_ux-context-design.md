# UX-Context Design: Using UX Knowledge to Inform AI-Generated Design

## Summary

This article introduces UX-context design, the practice of discovering and curating what an organization knows into context that guides AI-generated output. The article argues that as AI models produce output based on visible context, UX practitioners must shift from creating deliverables for human interpretation to creating context for AI consumption. Because everyone in an organization now uses AI tools to generate designs (not just designers), the responsibility is to ensure that everything AI generates is informed by research and design standards. The article describes how UX context leans AI output in a particular direction, presents Google's DESIGN.md as a concrete example, and proposes a broader UX.md format that includes research synthesis, interaction standards, glossaries, user and world models.

## Key Takeaways

- **Context is the new UX deliverable** — AI models produce output based on visible context. Without knowing your users, domain, standards, or what your team has learned from research, AI designs the average product. The difference between generic and contextual AI output is mostly context.
- **Everyone is designing now** — Product managers, engineers, and others use AI tools to generate designs before designers see them. Rather than gatekeeping, the practical goal is ensuring everything AI generates is informed by research and design standards.
- **Research deliverables must be AI-ready, not just human-readable** — Traditional artifacts (personas, journey maps) were designed for human empathy; AI needs the underlying reasoning. Research and design output must be made machine-readable, curated by skilled humans, and readily available.
- **DESIGN.md shows a concrete format for AI context** — Google Labs' DESIGN.md holds machine-readable exact values (colors, type sizes, spacing) alongside human-readable guidelines. It lives alongside code and is read by AI tools every time they generate something.
- **UX.md could extend context beyond visual identity** — A hypothetical broader format could include research synthesis as straightforward insights, interaction standards (when to confirm vs. undo), a glossary of domain terms, user models describing expertise and concerns, and world models capturing context of use.
- **Context is continuously curated, not handed off** — UX context lives with the product, changes when the product changes, and is read by every AI tool the organization uses. It is never finished; new research and watching what AI gets wrong both update it.

## Quotes

> "AI models produce output based on context. Context is everything the model can see when it does the work: your request, plus whatever instructions, standards, examples, and background information come along with it."

> "Context leans a model's output in a particular direction."

> "The difference between generic AI output and AI output that fits your users and matches your organization's standards is mostly a difference in context."
