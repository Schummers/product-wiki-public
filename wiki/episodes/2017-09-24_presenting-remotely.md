# 5 Strategies for Presenting UX Remotely

## Summary

Since 80% of corporate presentations are delivered remotely, mastering the skills to present UX work virtually is essential. Without physical presence to convey energy and read audience reactions, remote presenters must rely on environmental setup, humanized slides, clear focus, preparation, and storytelling to resonate with audiences. The article provides five strategies: creating a presentation-ready environment, being human through slides and visuals, reducing distractions, preparing thoroughly to prevent technical mishaps, and turning content into a narrative that engages viewers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Environment and audio quality matter more remotely** — stand while presenting, use upgraded microphones (around $100-250), and mimic in-person meeting setups to convey professionalism and energy.
- **Use slides to take your place** — include photos of presenters on slides, especially with multiple speakers, so audiences can connect faces to voices and feel a human presence.
- **One major idea per slide supports remote audiences** — remote viewers multitask more; clear slide purpose and simple content help them quickly regain context when attention lapses.
- **Reduce distractions by controlling your environment** — leave your desk for a conference room, delegate technical tasks, close distracting applications, and make eye contact with the camera.
- **Transform content into a story** — build presentations around user research, journey, and conflict; stories create relatable concepts that audiences remember after sign-off.

## Concepts

- [[Presentation Skills]] — remote presentations require different techniques than in-person talks, emphasizing slides, audio quality, and story structure rather than body language and eye contact.
- [[Remote Communication]] — asynchronous and distributed teams require clear presentation practices that compensate for lack of physical presence and nonverbal cues.
- [[Storytelling]] — effective UX presentations use user narratives and research findings to create engaging stories that resonate with audiences and drive design understanding.
