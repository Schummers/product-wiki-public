# Cognitive Maps, Mind Maps, and Concept Maps: Definitions

## Summary

Cognitive mapping, mind mapping, and concept mapping are three visual strategies for organizing and retaining knowledge. They visualize mental models but differ in structure and use. Cognitive maps are the umbrella term for all visual representations of mental models; they have no prescribed visual rules and can take any form (lists, flowcharts, affinity mappings). Mind maps have a clear tree structure with one central topic and nodes branching outward; each node (except root) has one parent. Concept maps are more complex: nodes can have multiple parents, and edges are labeled to show relationships between concepts. In UX, cognitive maps externalize knowledge, identify themes, and elicit user mental models in interviews. Mind maps organize information hierarchically within a single topic (e.g., website structure). Concept maps visualize complex interconcept relationships and can connect concepts across multiple perspectives. Selecting the right technique depends on the goal: mind maps for categorical organization, concept maps for relationship exploration, cognitive maps for capturing free-form processes or ecosystems.

## Key Takeaways

- **Cognitive Maps Are Freeform** — cognitive maps are umbrella term for any visual representation of a mental model with no prescribed structure; they include mind maps, concept maps, bulleted lists, flowcharts, and affinity mappings.
- **Mind Maps Have Clear Hierarchy** — mind maps center on a single topic with nodes branching outward in tree structure; each node has one parent; best for organizing information within a topic (e.g., website structure, page sections).
- **Concept Maps Emphasize Relationships** — concept maps allow nodes with multiple parents and labeled edges showing relationship types; they visualize complex interconcept relationships and support multiple perspectives on a problem.
- **Choose Technique by Goal** — use cognitive maps for free-form process capture, mind maps for single-topic hierarchical organization, and concept maps for exploring relationships among multiple related concepts.

## Quotes

> Cognitive map: Any visual representation of a person's (or a group's) mental model for a given process or concept.

> Mind map: A tree that represents a central topic and its subtopics.

> A concept map is a graph in which nodes represent concepts and are related through labeled, directed edges that illustrate relationships between them.

## Concepts

- [[Cognitive Maps]] — umbrella term for any visual representation of mental models; flexible, no prescribed structure; used to externalize knowledge, identify themes across concepts, and elicit user mental models in interviews.
- [[Mind Maps]] — tree-structured visualization with one central topic and hierarchically branching nodes; each node has one parent; suited for categorical organization and single-topic expansion.
- [[Concept Map]] — graph-structured visualization allowing multiple parents per node and labeled edges showing relationships; used to visualize complex interconcept relationships and support multiple perspectives.
