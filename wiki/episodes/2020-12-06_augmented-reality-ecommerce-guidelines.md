# UX Guidelines for Augmented-Reality Shopping Tools

## Summary

This article provides specific usability guidelines for designing AR shopping features based on a remote moderated study of 10 participants. The study examined both virtual try-on and view-in-room AR tools. The article covers AR discoverability (promotion, placement, labels), seamless channel transitions, calibration processes, and how focused attention requirements affect user interaction with the AR view.

## Key Takeaways

- **AR is not discoverable by default** — Most users don't look for AR features since the technology is new; AR tools must be promoted clearly, with descriptive labels and prominent placement near product images or prominent calls-to-action.
- **Use text labels with icons** — Text labels like "See it in AR" or "Try It On" work best; the AR cube icon is not universally recognized, so pair any icon with meaningful text to ensure users understand what will happen.
- **Promote on product-detail pages** — AR links work best near the product image with clear labels; hints and animations on first visit are well-received, but don't rely solely on hints that users might dismiss without reading.
- **Clear calibration instructions** — Calibration is unfamiliar and difficult; provide step-by-step contextual instructions that specify exactly what to show the camera (e.g., "scan the entire wall including where it meets floor and ceiling").
- **Support focused attention needs** — AR interaction requires intense focus on the main view and bottom controls; functions placed at the top are often overlooked, so put critical features in the bottom chrome or ensure visual feedback is obvious in the main view.
- **Seamless channel transitions** — If AR requires a separate app, seamlessly navigate users from the product page on the website to the same product in the app; generic links that only open the app store force users to relocate the product.

## Quotes

> Most users don't think to actively look for it when they browse products online.

> Specifying what to include in the camera view was immensely helpful, as users would otherwise not know what to show.

> The fact that you can play with the bottom part of the screen draws the eyes so much that I didn't really notice the icons at the top so much.

## Concepts

- [[Augmented Reality]] — specific UX guidelines for AR shopping tools including discoverability, labels, calibration instructions, and attention management for virtual try-on and view-in-room features.
- [[Discoverability]] — making AR features visible and understandable to users who don't expect to find them, through prominent placement, clear labels, and contextual promotions on product pages.
- [[Mobile Design]] — designing AR experiences for mobile devices where screen real estate is limited, attention is focused on primary content, and calibration must be simple enough for touch interaction.
