# Storytelling in Design: [ Preface ]

## Summary

Anna Dahlström's preface establishes the foundation for applying traditional storytelling principles to product and UX design. Drawing on her father's experience as a writer and her own work as a UX designer, Dahlström traces how stories have shaped human experience—from childhood reading sessions to building immersive product experiences. She argues that as product design becomes increasingly complex and crosses multiple devices and touchpoints, traditional storytelling methods offer practical tools and principles to create more effective, emotionally resonant experiences. The preface positions storytelling not as a novel concept but as a timeless skill now essential for designers, product owners, strategists, and anyone involved in shaping user experiences.

## Key Takeaways

- **Storytelling connects imagination with experience** — Dahlström opens on the books her father read to her, which sent the children on journeys to places they didn't know existed, created worlds in their minds and sparked their imaginations; that pull, wanting to know what happens next and how it ends, is what she sets out to connect to product design.
- **Great stories have proven structures and principles** — Dahlström holds up Walt Disney's ability to get both the bigger picture and the small details right as the standard product experiences should reach for. From character development and narrative structure to scene design, traditional storytelling offers tools and methods that complement and enhance existing product design practices rather than replace them.
- **Designers are storytellers of product narratives** — The question that started the book is one Dahlström asked her father, a writer, while preparing her first storytelling talk; the book addresses UX designers, product owners, strategists, founders and marketers, whose T-shaped skillset has to stretch wider as the world they design for becomes more complex and automated.
- **Storytelling is essential for modern product design** — As products increasingly exist across multiple devices, contexts, and touchpoints—both online and offline—applying storytelling principles helps designers build experiences that deliver to both the user and the business, while ensuring the people using the product remain the heroes of the story.
- **The book offers tools to enhance existing design processes** — Rather than introducing entirely new frameworks, the book takes methods, tools and principles from traditional storytelling to complement and enhance the tools designers and their clients already use: character development, narrative structure, main plots and subplots, and set, scene and shot design.

## Quotes

> The purpose of this book is not to come up with new tools and methods to replace those that we and our clients are used to working with, but instead to take methods, tools, and principles from traditional storytelling and use them to complement and enhance the tools we're already using.

Product designer Wilson Miner, quoted by Dahlström:

> We're not just making pretty interfaces; we're in the process of building an environment where we'll spend most of our time, for the rest of our lives. We're the designers, we're the builders—what do we want that environment to feel like? What do we want to feel like?

> Responding to and telling stories is part of what makes us human.

## Concepts

- [[Storytelling]] — Dahlström frames storytelling as a timeless skill that applies to product design; great stories capture imagination, build emotional connections, and resonate with audiences.
- [[Design Presentation]] — The preface emphasizes how storytelling shapes the way designers present their work to clients, stakeholders, and team members, as well as the importance of adapting stories to different audiences.
- [[Emotional Design]] — Dahlström emphasizes the emotional core of storytelling: great stories evoke emotions, move audiences, and hold them captive through anticipation and connection.
