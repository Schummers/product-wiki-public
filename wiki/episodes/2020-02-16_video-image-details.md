# How to Film and Photograph Online Content for Usability: UX Details for Videos and Images

## Summary

Small details in videos and images significantly impact how users perceive content and whether they succeed with instructional materials. Filming from the user's perspective rather than an observer's perspective eliminates mental rotation and makes instructions easier to follow. Videos should get to the point quickly, as user attention wanes around 30 seconds. For overview processes, keep videos under 30 seconds; for multistep instruction, breaking content into multiple short videos works better than one long video. Details like prop choice, background music tone, narrator intonation, and consistency in camera angle and props significantly influence perception and engagement.

## Key Takeaways

- **Film from doer's perspective** — Instructional videos should show what users see from their perspective, not from an observer watching them. This eliminates the cognitive load of mental rotation and makes steps much easier to follow.

- **Attention wanes quickly** — Users start checking video length around 30 seconds, especially if they don't perceive the video as immediately helpful. Long introductions and slow pacing frustrate users. For overview videos, aim for under 30 seconds.

- **Multiple short videos beat one long one** — For multistep processes, several short videos (3-4 minutes each) are better than one long video. Users can choose which videos to watch and feel less committed. They appreciate the modular format even if total time is longer.

- **Provide images or text alternatives** — For step-by-step processes, also provide images and text so users can follow at their own pace. Some users want videos, others prefer text; don't force video-only content.

- **Small details distract users** — Specks of dust in hair, unexpected props, inconsistent backgrounds, and cheesy background music all distract from instructional content. Scrutinize and control visible details.

- **Consistency helps focus** — Using the same camera angle, props, actors, and settings throughout maintains consistency that helps users focus on instructional content rather than noticing changes.

## Quotes

> For instructional content, you may need to literally show things from the user's perspective: what does this process look like through the user's eyes?

> It helped a lot because the video was from my perspective, rather than me having to mirror someone.

> Details such as continuity and consistency, the intonation of the narrator's voice, props, background music, and basically anything that is visible in the video or graphic must be scrutinized and designed thoughtfully, or they can easily distract users and change their perception of the content.
