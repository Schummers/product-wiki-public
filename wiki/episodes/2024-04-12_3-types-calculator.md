# 3 Types of Online Calculator and Quiz Tools

## Summary

Calculator and quiz tools fall into three distinct categories based on their outputs: conversion calculators translate information from one format to another (e.g., currency conversion, loan comparison), prediction calculators help users estimate future outcomes (e.g., fertility prediction, investment returns), and recommendation calculators provide personalized advice based on inputs (e.g., phone-plan recommendations). Understanding these types and their use cases helps designers create tools that meet user needs and expectations. The types can be combined to guide users from calculation through prediction to recommendation, supporting decision-making workflows.

## Key Takeaways

- **Conversion calculators** — translate inputs into comparable terms, facilitating comparison between options; users trust outputs if they trust the provider and don't question calculation accuracy.
- **Prediction calculators** — help users estimate future outcomes; users trust predictions from complex tools more than from simple ones because they don't understand how to question the algorithm.
- **Recommendation calculators** — provide personalized advice; users take outputs seriously and often trust recommendations as much as or more than their own judgment.
- **User trust varies** — users' willingness to question calculator outputs depends on complexity and their knowledge; designers are responsible for providing accurate information since users may base important decisions on outputs.
- **Combined types** — conversion and prediction can pave the way for recommendations, allowing tools to guide users from understanding their situation through predicting outcomes to suggesting actionable next steps.

## Quotes

> Calculators and quizzes are decision-making tools that accept information from users and generate personalized outputs based on their circumstances.

> Users turn to recommendation calculators for advice. They are aware that calculator recommendations are not entirely altruistic.

> Users with a lot of experience at a task will not need a calculator to help them with that task.
