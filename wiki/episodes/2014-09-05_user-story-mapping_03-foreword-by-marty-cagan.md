# User Story Mapping: Foreword by Marty Cagan

## Summary

In the third of the book's forewords, Marty Cagan frames story mapping as a tool for addressing a fundamental challenge in modern product teams: the gap between how the best product companies operate and how most organizations build software. Cagan argues that story mapping is not merely a technique but a gateway to the deeper transformations that distinguish winning product teams from struggling ones. With an explicit nod to Ben Horowitz's *Good Product Manager, Bad Product Manager*, Cagan sets out a list contrasting the practices of strong product teams with weak ones across multiple dimensions: product vision, discovery methods, stakeholder understanding, prototyping skills, cross-functional collaboration, and how they engage with learning and data.

## Key Takeaways

- **The good team / bad team list is modelled on Ben Horowitz** — Cagan credits "a grateful nod to Ben Horowitz's *Good Product Manager, Bad Product Manager*" for the form of the contrast list that makes up most of the foreword.
- **Strong product vision** — Good teams pursue a compelling product vision with missionary-like passion, whereas bad teams are merely mercenaries executing orders without genuine commitment.
- **Customer-driven discovery** — Good teams draw product ideas from KPIs, customer observation, and data analysis; bad teams passively gather requirements from sales and stakeholders.
- **Skilled prototyping and testing** — Good teams rapidly test product ideas to determine which are worth building; bad teams hold meetings to generate prioritized roadmaps instead of validating ideas.
- **Cross-functional collaboration** — Good teams have product, design, and engineering working side-by-side with mutual influence; bad teams work in silos and communicate through formal requests.
- **Empowered engineers in discovery** — Good teams give engineers time to review discovery prototypes daily so they can contribute to product improvement; bad teams show prototypes only during sprint planning for estimation.
- **Iterative refinement** — Good teams know many ideas will fail and expect multiple iterations to reach desired outcomes; bad teams build the roadmap as-is and stop once it ships.

## Quotes

> Good teams have a compelling product vision that they pursue with a missionary-like passion. Bad teams are mercenaries.

> Good teams engage directly with end users and customers every week to better understand their customers, and to see the customer's response to their latest ideas. Bad teams think they are the customer.

> Good teams know that many of their favorite ideas won't end up working for customers, and even the ones that could will need several iterations to get to the point where they provide the desired outcome. Bad teams just build what's on the roadmap and are satisfied with meeting dates and ensuring quality.

## Concepts

- [[Product Management]] — Cagan emphasizes that true product management differs fundamentally from gathering requirements; it requires a vision, empowerment, and continuous learning from customers.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — The foreword repeatedly stresses that the best teams have product, design, and engineering working tightly together, sharing both discovery and decision-making.
- [[Product Strategy]] — Cagan identifies a compelling product vision pursued with genuine passion as the defining characteristic of strong teams.
- [[Agile Development]] — While the book is about a technique used in Agile, Cagan situates story mapping within the broader context of how Agile principles can either enable or be undermined by team practices.
- [[Product Outcome]] — Cagan contrasts teams that focus on outcomes (KPIs, customer response, business impact) with teams that focus only on output (shipping on schedule).
