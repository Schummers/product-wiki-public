# 5 Common Ethical Dilemmas in User Research

## Summary

User research involving human participants must adhere to ethical principles that protect participant autonomy, safety, and dignity. Researchers face common ethical dilemmas including balancing honest disclosure with study validity, deciding when concealment or deception is justified, managing sensitive topics that may cause distress, studying vulnerable populations who cannot provide informed consent, handling accidental disclosure of sensitive information, and maintaining confidentiality in small participant pools. Best practices involve designing studies to minimize harm, obtaining informed consent, debriefing participants, and creating safeguards tailored to participant needs.

## Key Takeaways

- **Informed consent is foundational** — Participants must be told about all aspects of the study to make an informed decision about participation, balancing the need for transparency with research validity.
- **Deception is more problematic than concealment** — While both present ethical challenges, deception involves providing false information and is riskier; both require debriefing participants afterward.
- **Sensitive topics require special preparation** — Research on difficult subjects should include subject-matter expert guidance, clear informed participation, participant withdrawal options, and access to support resources.
- **Vulnerable populations need extra safeguards** — Children, people with dementia, and those who might face discrimination require additional protections and informed consent from legal representatives.
- **Participant care comes first** — When sensitive information is accidentally revealed in recordings, researchers must prioritize participant safety by removing and securing that information.
- **Small groups threaten confidentiality** — In small participant pools, anonymity may be impossible; researchers should inform participants they could be identified rather than falsely promising confidentiality.

## Quotes

> Informed consent means that participants are told about all aspects of the study so they can make an informed decision on whether to take part.

> UX studies rarely need to use concealment or deception.

> As UX researchers, our first duty is of care towards our participants: even though we might feel that the session is extremely valuable for our study goals, when information like this is captured on recordings or in our data-collection process, we must ensure that it is swiftly and securely removed, even though we might feel that it compromises the study goals or our ability to persuade stakeholders.

## Concepts

- [[Research Ethics]] — The principles and practices ensuring that user research protects participant autonomy, safety, and dignity through informed consent and safeguard measures, including ensuring participants understand what the study involves and receive disclosure of research goals and organization before agreeing to participate.
- [[Participant Protection]] — Strategies to safeguard participants from harm, including special considerations for vulnerable groups, management of sensitive information, and debriefing practices.
