# Good Customer Experience Demands Organizational Fluidity

## Summary

Good customer experiences (CX) across channels and over customer lifetimes require organizations to manage dependencies in processes, people, and technology. Customer journeys span three UX scopes (interaction, journey, relationship level), each with five experience components: consistent, optimized, seamless, orchestrated, and collaborative. Broader-scope experiences demand larger organizational changes; journey-level UX requires addressing siloed team structures that create fragmented experiences. Organizations must redesign how customer-facing experiences are created and delivered, not just the experiences themselves, to achieve omnichannel maturity.

## Key Takeaways

- **Five experience components build good customer journeys** — Consistency, optimization for each channel, seamlessness across transitions, orchestration with personalized interactions, and collaboration across channels each require progressively larger organizational changes.

- **Organizational dependencies grow with UX scope** — Interaction-level changes require minimal coordination; journey-level changes demand cross-department alignment around processes, technology, and people that are often siloed.

- **Siloed organizational structures create fragmented experiences** — When marketing, product, support, and other teams work independently on their channels, users experience disconnects (e.g., Facebook ads linking to generic sales pages instead of specific deals).

- **User journeys don't follow single-channel happy paths** — Users start, stop, and switch channels to complete goals; organizations must design horizontally around customer journeys, not vertically around channel solutions.

- **Root causes require structural solutions, not surface fixes** — Addressing individual pain points without restructuring teams and processes leads to repeated, similar problems; strategic organizational change positions companies to deliver better experiences across the board.

- **Digitally native companies possess omnichannel maturity by design** — Startups like Uber and Airbnb built integrated experiences from the ground up; longstanding companies must intentionally redesign legacy structures and technologies.

## Quotes

> It is difficult to solve today's problems with yesterday's solutions.

> To users, all these channels form one experience, and, for that experience to be good, it can't break down.

> If organizations are siloed and still focused on designing channel solutions, then this internal fragmentation will surface in the customer experience.

## Concepts

- [[Customer Journey]] — Customer journeys span multiple channels and touchpoints over time; delivering seamless, consistent experiences requires organizational alignment around journey-level goals.

- [[Omnichannel Experience]] — Omnichannel maturity requires consistent branding, optimized channel experiences, seamless transitions, personalized orchestration, and collaborative channel use; each level demands deeper organizational change.

- [[Change Management]] — Improving customer experience at journey and relationship levels requires structural change: cross-functional teams, integrated processes, shared technology platforms, and customer-focused culture.

- [[Service Design]] — Understanding and redesigning internal organizational systems, processes, and technology is as important as designing customer-facing experiences; service blueprints reveal internal dependencies.
