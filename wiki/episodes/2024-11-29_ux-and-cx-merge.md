# UX and CX Merge: The Shift from Products to Journeys

## Summary

Organizations have historically separated customer experience (CX) and user experience (UX) functions. CX teams have typically focused on the overall customer relationship and business strategy, while UX teams have focused on individual digital product interactions. However, many pain points in customer journeys transcend individual products and require solutions involving marketing, customer support, and other business functions. Progressive organizations are merging their CX and UX functions and establishing cross-functional journey teams to address the journey level — the middle level of experience between individual interactions and lifetime relationships.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three levels of experience** — Experiences occur at the interaction level (single product), journey level (multiple products or channels), and relationship level (lifetime customer connection); traditionally CX owned the relationship level and UX owned the interaction level.
- **Organizational silos deteriorate customer experience** — Siloed structures inherited from scientific management theory result in fragmented customer journeys where problems occur at touchpoint boundaries owned by different functional groups.
- **Journey pain points transcend products** — Many customer frustrations cannot be solved by product design alone; they require coordination across marketing, tech, support, business, and other functions.
- **Breaking silos requires connectivity, not destruction** — Successful organizations don't destroy silos but instead enable connectivity and collaboration across them to practice user-centered design at the journey level.
- **CX and UX must work together** — CX brings top-level insight and business connection while UX brings user-centered research and design skills; combining these under one functional group enables journey-level design.
- **Journey teams are cross-functional** — Journey-centric design brings together stakeholders from CX, UX, product, marketing, support, business, and technology to design end-to-end solutions.

## Quotes

> Siloed organizational structures deteriorate the customer experience. They result in a patchwork of channel experiences that don't work well together — the antithesis of omnichannel design.

> Designing good customer-journey experiences that maximize business value cannot be done by either group independently or while working separately.

> Journey-centric design offers an operational framework for efficiently and proactively optimizing journeys within existing company structures.

## Concepts

- [[Customer Journey]] — journey-centric design is an operational approach that facilitates cross-functional research and design of end-to-end customer journeys, managing and optimizing them as if they were products through ongoing research, measurement, and design iteration.
- [[Service Design]] — Explains service design as an approach that addresses cross-functional problems requiring solutions beyond individual product design.
- [[Organizational Silos]] — Analyzes how traditional organizational silos rooted in scientific management theory create fragmented customer experiences and prevent effective journey-level design.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Emphasizes the importance of connecting business strategy, CX strategy, and product design through shared strategic priorities and budgets to enable journey-level optimization.
