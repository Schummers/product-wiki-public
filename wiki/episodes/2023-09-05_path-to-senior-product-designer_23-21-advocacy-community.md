# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 21. Advocacy (Community)

## Summary

This chapter defines advocacy—promoting the company and design team's message externally—as one way to attract talent, improve brand perception, and critically, to build personal brand. While the chapter notes that advocacy is less critical than other competencies in the book, it emphasizes that personal brand can have outsized impact on career trajectory. The chapter situates this work within the broader industry reality that B2B roles are underrepresented in designer recruiting awareness, then covers five primary advocacy channels: public speaking, writing, dedicated design team websites, public mentorship programs, and design awards. It concludes with techniques for generating article and talk ideas and the concept of increasing "luck surface area" through consistent visibility efforts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Advocacy is a minor competency whose by-product is a personal brand** — the chapter calls advocacy one of the least critical competencies, but says a strong personal brand could have a bigger impact on your career by itself than excelling at advocacy inside any specific company, since writing and public speaking expose your own name as well as the company's.
- **B2B opportunities are systematically underrecognized by designers** — the author reports analysing 120 companies with open product designer positions in the San Francisco Bay Area and finding the majority of them to be B2B, and argues that designers who only target B2C are ignoring most of the relevant companies and should showcase more B2B work in their portfolio.
- **Speaking opportunities follow a progression, typically from internal events to small meetups to conferences** — the chapter distinguishes between being scouted (which it calls rare and dependent on an established public presence) and submitting via a "call for papers", which it presents as the more realistic route; it also relays the VP of design at Figma saying that publishing articles about their internal design work process brings design candidates to their door.
- **Writing about insider experience, patterns, and deep expertise generates traction** — the chapter lists idea-generation techniques: leveraging the time you have already spent on a topic, revealing insider information or unique access, naming an emerging industry pattern, examining a convention that has fallen out of favor, going deep on a small neglected topic, defining your own way of doing something, and making internal documentation public. It quotes Linus Pauling, "The way to get good ideas is to get lots of ideas", and warns that no technique guarantees an article will be popular.
- **Increasing luck surface area through consistent visibility compounds over time** — each article, speaking engagement, and open source contribution increases the chance that random opportunities (job offers, consulting gigs, book deals) land in your domain, a concept illustrated with the author's own experience of being approached by InVision leadership.

## Quotes

> Advocacy helps a company to become more attractive as an employer and perceived better by customers, investors, vendors, etc. So it partially intersects with the hiring competency.

> The reality is that you are much more likely to work on B2B products than B2C.

> The way to get good ideas is to get lots of ideas.
