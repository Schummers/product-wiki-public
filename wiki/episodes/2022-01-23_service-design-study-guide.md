# Service Design: Study Guide

## Summary

Service design is the activity of planning and organizing business resources (people, props, and processes) to improve both employee and customer experience. The discipline aligns internal business processes with external customer touchpoints and journeys. Nielsen Norman Group's study guide presents introductory resources on service design and its key visualization method: the service blueprint. The guide recommends a structured learning path starting with service design fundamentals before moving into service blueprinting, which visualizes relationships between service components (people, props, processes) and their connection to specific customer-journey touchpoints.

## Key Takeaways

- **Service design has three key components** — People (anyone who creates or uses the service or is indirectly affected), props (physical or digital artifacts and products needed for service delivery), and processes (workflows, procedures, and rituals performed by employees or users).
- **Service design distinguishes frontstage from backstage** — Key to service design is understanding which service components directly interact with customers (frontstage) versus internal operations (backstage).
- **Service blueprints are the primary service-design visualization** — A service blueprint is a diagram visualizing relationships between service components and their ties to touchpoints in a customer journey; it serves as part two to customer-journey mapping.
- **Service blueprints suit omnichannel, multi-touchpoint experiences** — The approach works well for experiences requiring coordination across multiple departments and touchpoints, particularly when service involves both digital and physical elements.
- **Service blueprinting is an ideal follow-up to journey mapping** — After creating a customer-journey map to understand the user experience, service blueprinting reveals how internal business processes support and enable those customer touchpoints.
- **Study guide provides structured learning resources** — NN/g's guide recommends resources in order from foundational service-design concepts through advanced service-blueprinting practices and common mistakes.

## Quotes

> "Service design is the activity of planning and organizing business resources (people, props, and processes) in order to improve the employees' and the customers' experience."

> "Service design aims to optimize the interplay between the customers' journey and the internal business processes that support it."

> "A service blueprint is a diagram that visualizes the relationships between different service components — people, props (physical or digital evidence), and processes — that are directly tied to touchpoints in a specific customer journey."

## Concepts

- [[Service Design]] — Service design plans and organizes business resources (people, props, processes) to improve customer and employee experience by aligning internal operations with external customer touchpoints.
- [[Service Blueprinting]] — The primary visualization for service design, showing relationships between service components and their ties to specific customer-journey touchpoints; ideal for omnichannel experiences requiring cross-functional coordination.
- [[Customer Journey]] — Service design focuses on understanding and supporting the customer journey through internal business processes; service blueprinting follows customer-journey mapping to reveal backstage operations.
- [[Business Processes]] — Service design distinguishes between frontstage processes visible to customers and backstage processes that enable customer touchpoints, requiring careful coordination to optimize both.
