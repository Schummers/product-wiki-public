# Stakeholder Interviews 101

## Summary

Stakeholder interviews are structured conversations with people who have vested interests in a project, conducted with the goal of gathering insights that drive project success. Unlike user interviews that explore end-user behaviors and needs, stakeholder interviews focus on understanding internal or external stakeholders' perspectives, business goals, project constraints, and expectations. Effective stakeholder interviews uncover contextual history and organizational politics, identify business objectives and key results, align teams on a shared vision, and increase stakeholder buy-in and communication.

This article provides practical guidance on conducting stakeholder interviews, including setting clear interview goals, preparing discussion guides covering success metrics, priorities, history/expertise, and process preferences, creating rapport through a welcoming introduction, conducting interviews one-on-one with active listening and open-ended probing, and following up with gratitude. Interviews are most valuable when conducted early in projects or when welcoming new stakeholders, as early engagement makes stakeholders feel heard and gathers insights when they're most useful for planning and design direction.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stakeholder interviews uncover context, business goals, and project landscape** — early interviews reveal organizational history, prior solutions, constraints, and politics before they become obstacles.
- **Set clear interview goals** — interviews focused on obtaining knowledge, securing buy-in, identifying concerns, or defining success metrics yield more actionable insights than unstructured conversations.
- **Use a discussion guide, not a script** — semi-structured interviews with prepared high-level topics (success metrics, priorities, history, process) allow natural conversation flow while ensuring coverage.
- **Conduct one-on-one with active listening** — neutral posture, eye contact, note-taking, and acknowledgments ("I see," "That's helpful") help stakeholders feel heard and encourage openness.
- **Segment data from multiple stakeholders** — with 5 or fewer stakeholders, informal analysis suffices; with more, use systematic thematic analysis to identify themes and build a shared understanding.

## Quotes

> "A stakeholder is anyone who has interest in your project or with whom you need to work with in some way to complete the project. Understanding your stakeholders and their perspectives is key to your project's success and is commonly done through stakeholder interviews."

> "I'll ask you a few questions today to get your feedback and input. There are no right or wrong answers; feel free to be open and honest; anything you share today will not be distributed or associated back to you."

> "Help the stakeholder feel heard by taking notes, making eye contact, and offering words of acknowledgment like I see, and That's helpful."
