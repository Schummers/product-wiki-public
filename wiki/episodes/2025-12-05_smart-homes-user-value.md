# What Users Value Most in Smart Homes and How to Design for It

## Summary

A diary study of 10 smart-home users revealed five core motivations that drive adoption and continued use of smart devices: convenience (time savings, cognitive load reduction, enabling multitasking); safety and peace of mind (remote monitoring, instant alerts, early issue detection); saving resources and money (energy efficiency, proactive maintenance); tracking home data (meaningful insights and anomaly detection); and mood and ambiance (emotional comfort through customization).

The research emphasizes that smart-home technology’s value depends not on technical novelty but on thoughtful design that removes friction, provides clear visibility, and delivers actionable insights. Users want devices that learn their preferences, adapt to their routines, and let them control multiple devices in tandem to achieve desired experiences (e.g., a "bedtime" scene) rather than performing device-specific actions one at a time.

## Key Takeaways

- **Five core motivations drive adoption** — Convenience (time and cognitive load savings), safety/peace of mind (remote monitoring and alerts), saving resources (energy efficiency), tracking home data (meaningful insights), and mood/ambiance (emotional comfort through customization).
- **Convenience requires reducing cognitive load, not just saving time** — For users with ADHD or busy lives, smart devices act as "scaffolding" for daily responsibilities; automations and reminders transfer cognitive burden, making devices essential, not just nice-to-have.
- **Raw data is insufficient; insights must be actionable** — Users need data presented in context with clear explanations, comparisons, and recommended actions; energy meters showing raw kilowatt numbers without guidance won't prompt behavior change, while a smart litter box flagging weight deviations with visual alerts and explanations drove immediate veterinary action.
- **Users think in experiences, not device-specific actions** — People want to achieve overarching goals like "relax after work," not perform individual device tasks; apps should support scenes (preset multi-device configurations) and automations (conditional workflows) that let users control multiple devices together.
- **Schedules must be flexible, forgiving, and learnable** — Users value templates and suggestions based on their routines and goals (e.g., energy-saving tips), but only if recommendations are realistic (respecting comfort needs), context-aware (based on actual usage patterns), and easy to adjust without starting over.
