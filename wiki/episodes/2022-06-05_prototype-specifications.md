# Use Good Prototype Specifications to Empower Team Collaboration

## Summary

Prototype specifications (also called redlines or annotations) are brief text descriptions written next to a prototype explaining ambiguous details. They reduce uncertainty about design intent and improve communication efficiency within teams. The article identifies three types of specifications (element, functionality, and content), describes what makes specifications good, and clarifies when they are necessary. Well-documented specifications serve as external memory for teams, help standardize designs, and are especially valuable for remote teams and complex interactions.

## Key Takeaways

- **Three types of specifications** — Element specifications cover visual details like font and color; functionality specifications explain behaviors and states; content specifications describe placeholder text and messages.
- **Remove uncertainty and save time** — Specifications prevent repeated explanations and misunderstandings, reducing the need for clarification meetings and improving efficiency.
- **Not always necessary** — Simple interfaces, small-scope projects, or designs using well-established design systems may not need detailed functionality or content specifications, though element specs are essential.
- **Clarity and concision matter** — Good specifications represent ideas clearly and briefly, describe the prototype rather than replace it, use consistent project vocabulary, and conform to design standards.
- **Combination is key** — Specifications alone are obscure; they work best combined with visual prototypes that show actual designs alongside text explanations.
- **Serve as external memory** — Documentation helps teams remember original design intent over time and supports new team members, reducing reliance on individual memory.
- **Enable standardization** — Specifications can be adopted into design systems and serve as reference for future designs involving similar UI elements.

## Quotes

> Delivering prototypes and wireframes along with prototype specifications is probably the most proactive and practical approach to address such issues.

> A text description of a design will be more difficult to understand than a wireframe.

> The context of your project and team determines whether you should include specification in your project.

## Concepts

- [[Prototyping]] — Creating prototype specifications alongside prototypes improves design clarity and team understanding of design intent.
- [[Design Handoff]] — The transition from designers to developers is where specifications reduce ambiguity about visual details, functionality, and behavior.
- [[Interaction Design]] — Specifications are especially important for complex interactions where behavior details cannot be expressed through wireframes or visual design alone.
