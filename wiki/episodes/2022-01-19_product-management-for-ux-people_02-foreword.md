# Product Management for UX People: Foreword

## Summary

Written by Matt LeMay (partner at Sudden Compass, author of *Product Management in Practice* and *Agile for Everybody*), this foreword describes the arc many UX professionals follow when transitioning to product management. LeMay frames the journey as testing a "redemption narrative" — the belief that becoming a PM will finally allow one to elevate UX and gain respect for its value — only to discover within a year that the realities of product management (deadlines, high-stakes decisions, competing stakeholders) complicate even the best intentions. The foreword positions the book as a guide to navigating these complexities while leveraging the UX superpowers UX professionals bring to the role.

## Key Takeaways

- **The redemption narrative collision** — Many UX professionals transition to PM with idealism about elevating UX and treating teams respectfully, but discover the pressure of product work quickly reveals why previous PMs made the decisions they did.
- **Real-world guidance required** — Good intentions are not enough: LeMay writes that "with the right real-world guidance" a UX person can navigate the complex realities of product management, and presents the book as that guidance because Crumlish lived the transition himself and shares other people's experiences as well as his own.
- **Day-to-day reality is ambiguous** — LeMay characterizes product management as a mix of "far-flung inspiration, somber realization, deep reflection, and anxious-yet-steely resolve," capturing the emotional and intellectual demands of the role.
- **UX superpowers transfer** — LeMay's phrase is that you navigate product management "while building on the user-centric superpowers you developed as a UX person": the UX foundation is what the PM role is built on, not something dropped at the door.
- **Building the PM you want to see** — LeMay frames the book's value as helping readers "be the kind of product manager you want to see in the world," turning frustration with past PMs into an opportunity for leadership.

## Quotes

> There's a particular sparkle I've often seen in the eyes of UX professionals who are considering a pivot to product management. In many cases, this is the sparkle of a redemption narrative in the making: "I'm sick of other product managers not understanding the value and importance of UX, and when I become a product manager, I'll treat my whole team with respect and elevate UX to a more strategic position!"

> The reality of product management—with its deadlines, estimates, high-stakes decisions, and high-pressure stakeholders—inevitably complicates your best-laid plans and purest ambitions. Slowly but surely, you start to realize why some of the product managers you've worked with were "like that."

> With the right real-world guidance, you are able to navigate the complex realities of product management while building on the user-centric superpowers you developed as a UX person.

## Concepts

- [[UX Career]] — LeMay's perspective here emphasizes that the UX-to-PM career path is real but requires acknowledging the differences between disciplines, not assuming one role is an extension of the other.
- [[Product Management]] — LeMay characterizes the role as one where good intentions collide with operational constraints, stakes, and stakeholder pressures; success means adapting UX skills rather than imposing UX values.
- [[Stakeholder Engagement]] — LeMay lists high-pressure stakeholders alongside deadlines, estimates and high-stakes decisions as the realities that complicate the redemption narrative; he does not rank them or single stakeholder management out as the core skill.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — LeMay emphasizes that treating teams with respect is a value UX people bring but that respect must coexist with difficult PM trade-offs.
