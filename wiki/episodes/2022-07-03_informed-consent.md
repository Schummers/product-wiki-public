# Obtaining Consent for User Research

## Summary

Informed consent is a fundamental ethical principle and practical requirement in user research. It requires researchers to inform participants about study involvement and consequences, while participants must understand the terms and voluntarily agree. A consent form documents this exchange. The article explains why informed consent matters, who cannot consent (minors, prisoners, cognitively impaired), what consent forms should contain, when they are necessary, and how to structure them. Modular consent forms allowing opt-in to specific study aspects improve participation rates.

## Key Takeaways

- **Informed consent has two requirements** — Researchers must inform participants about study involvement and potential consequences; participants must fully understand terms and make informed voluntary decisions.
- **Ethical foundation** — Informed consent protects participants and prevents research abuses documented in history (Nuremberg trials, Tuskegee syphilis study); most institutions have review boards ensuring compliance.
- **Respect and protect participants** — Even nonclinical UX research can cause temporary hardship or distress; vulnerable populations need extra precautions and communication about potential impact.
- **Vulnerable populations cannot self-consent** — Children, cognitively impaired adults, those with low literacy, and prisoners may not be capable of full understanding or voluntary participation; guardians must consent for them.
- **Written forms are critical** — Consent forms ensure every participant receives consistent information, hold researchers accountable, and empower participants to protect their own welfare and data.
- **Exceptions exist** — A/B testing and intercept studies may not require full consent forms, but even intercept studies need brief information pages about data use, storage, and deletion.
- **Modular consent** — Instead of all-or-nothing consent, offer checkboxes for consent to participate, audio recording, video recording, and data publication, increasing participation.

## Concepts

- [[Research Ethics]] — Informed consent is a core ethical principle ensuring researchers respect participant wellbeing and autonomy rather than exploiting their willingness to help.
- [[User Research]] — Proper consent processes protect participants and improve data quality by ensuring voluntary, understood participation rather than coerced or misled involvement.
- [[Participant Wellbeing]] — Consent forms identify potential harms, communicate protection measures, and empower participants to control their data and participation.
