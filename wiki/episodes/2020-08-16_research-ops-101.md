# ResearchOps 101

## Summary

ResearchOps (ReOps) is the orchestration and optimization of people, processes, and craft to amplify the value and impact of research at scale. It emerged from the exponential growth of the UX profession and the practical challenge of scaling research practices to meet increasing demand without proportionally increasing costs. ResearchOps comprises six common focus areas: participants, governance, knowledge, tools, competency, and advocacy. These areas are interconnected, with each affecting and being affected by the others.

## Key Takeaways

- **Definition and scope** — ResearchOps is broader than participant recruitment alone. It encompasses standardizing research methods, managing participants, ensuring research ethics, educating partners, managing insights, and socializing impact.
- **Scaling economics** — When organizations conduct 10 times more research, costs should not increase proportionally. Through ResearchOps practices, organizations can achieve cost scaling of 8 to 9 times the original budget instead of 11 times through economies of scale and reuse.
- **Six interconnected components** — (1) Participants: recruiting, screening, scheduling, and compensating; (2) Governance: processes for consent, privacy, and information storage; (3) Knowledge: collecting, synthesizing, and sharing insights; (4) Tools: enabling efficiencies through consistent toolsets; (5) Competency: enabling others to perform research; (6) Advocacy: defining and sharing research value.
- **Governance essentials** — Consent templates must comply with data-privacy regulations like GDPR and use plain language. Organizations must establish processes for proper maintenance and disposal of personally identifiable information and study artifacts.
- **Knowledge management value** — Compiled research insights help teams avoid repetitive studies and educate those outside research teams. A shared database of research insights across the organization creates a comprehensive source of knowledge.
- **Getting started** — Begin by identifying current pain points for researchers and research-team partners through internal research, then focus initial efforts on the most pressing area while maintaining balance across all components.
