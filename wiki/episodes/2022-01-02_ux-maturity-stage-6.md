# UX-Maturity Stage 6: User-Driven

## Summary

Stage 6 represents the ultimate maturity goal in Nielsen Norman Group's six-stage UX-maturity model. At this level, organizations are fully enlightened about the value of user-centered design, and user research drives everything from organizational strategy to individual design elements. Development operations embrace user-driven iterative design, and both individuals and teams plan for change and innovation based on unmet market needs. While this stage represents the ideal, it is difficult to achieve and challenging to maintain long-term.

UX maturity comprises four factors: strategy, culture, process, and outcomes. At stage 6, the organization has top-level leadership committed to UX, a specialized design-system team that continuously iterates, and adequate budgets consistently directed toward UX growth. The culture treats UX as an equal business-operations partner, with employee-training initiatives and development paths reinforcing UX values. The process is established yet malleable, applied throughout all teams and departments. Outcomes include industry-leading designs and success metrics aligned with user-centered strategy.

## Key Takeaways

- **User research drives all organizational decisions** — From highest-level strategy to smallest design details, user research and user-centered design are the driving force behind everything the organization does.
- **Culture is the linchpin for maintaining stage 6** — UX culture deteriorates when employees make decisions not aligned with user-centered design; maintaining stage 6 requires constant nurturing of shared user-focused values through leadership messaging, accountability, and celebration of UX successes.
- **Design systems require specialized teams** — Well-vetted design systems owned by specialized teams who continually iterate ensure consistent, high-quality output at scale across the organization.
- **Stage 6 organizations have different origin paths** — Newly established companies with UX from inception, small established companies with forward-thinking leadership, and larger organizations committed to design innovation can all achieve stage 6, though each faces distinct sustainability challenges.
- **Regression is possible and not failure** — Organizations that slip from stage 6 to stage 5 should not view this as total failure; they can use the memory of performing well to motivate renewed effort to return to the highest stage.
- **High-level management roles dedicated to UX are essential** — UX VPs, design directors, and research directors exist at stage 6, ensuring comprehensive, formalized operations subject to continuous improvement.

## Quotes

> "In stage-6 organizations, everyone is fully enlightened about the value of user-centered design. User research and user-centered design are the driving force behind everything these organizations do, from the highest level of organizational strategy down to the tiniest design element within a design system."

> "In order to maintain stage 6, you must focus on continuing to nurture a user-focused culture. Organizational culture is built by establishing and maintaining shared values among employees."

> "It is natural for organizations to change priorities or to adapt to changing market conditions in ways that may be suboptimal. After some time at stage 5, remind people how good it was to be on the top and motivate them to make a renewed effort to rectify the problem that caused the regression."

## Concepts
