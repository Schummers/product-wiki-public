# Empathy Mapping: The First Step in Design Thinking

## Summary

This article introduces empathy maps as a collaborative visualization tool for articulating and sharing understanding about users. An empathy map typically divides knowledge about a user into four quadrants: Says (what users vocalize), Thinks (what occupies their thoughts), Does (physical actions), and Feels (emotions). Unlike journey maps, empathy maps are not chronological; they capture a holistic snapshot of a user type. Empathy maps serve dual purposes: they help teams understand who users are and identify knowledge gaps, and they educate teammates about users by creating a shared, visual reference that can guide decisions. The article provides a step-by-step process for building empathy maps, emphasizing the importance of starting with research data (user interviews, field studies, diary studies) and involving the team in the mapping activity to build buy-in and shared understanding. Both individual empathy maps (based on one user) and aggregated maps (combining similar users) are valuable; aggregated maps can be a first step toward creating personas.

## Key Takeaways

- **Four Quadrants Capture Holistic User Understanding** — Says, Thinks, Does, and Feels create a multidimensional view of a user type; seeing the juxtaposition between quadrants (e.g., positive actions but negative emotions) can reveal hidden insights and opportunities.
- **Empathy Maps Are Not Chronological** — Unlike journey maps, empathy maps capture a snapshot of a user type at a particular moment; this non-sequential approach is appropriate for understanding overall user characteristics and needs.
- **Identify Knowledge Gaps** — Sparse empathy maps reveal areas where the team lacks understanding of users; this signals the need for additional research before proceeding with design decisions.
- **Team Collaboration Builds Alignment** — The empathy-mapping process itself, not just the finished artifact, provides value; involving team members in creating maps builds shared understanding and removes bias-based assumptions.
- **Aggregated Maps Synthesize Multiple Users** — Combining individual empathy maps from users who exhibit similar behaviors creates a map for a user segment; this synthesis is a first step toward developing personas.
- **Polish and Iterate** — Empathy maps should be treated as living documents that evolve as more research is gathered; version numbers and dates help track changes and signal that understanding is improving.

## Quotes

> "An empathy map is a collaborative visualization used to articulate what we know about a particular type of user. It externalizes knowledge about users in order to 1) create a shared understanding of user needs, and 2) aid in decision making."

> "Our users are complex humans. It is natural (and extremely beneficial) to see juxtaposition between quadrants."

> "Empathy maps should be used throughout any UX process to establish common ground among team members and to understand and prioritize user needs."

## Concepts

- [[Empathy]] — A four-quadrant visualization (Says, Thinks, Does, Feels) that captures holistic understanding of a user type; used collaboratively to build shared team understanding and identify research gaps.
- [[User Research]] — Methods for gathering information about user needs, behaviors, and emotions; provides the raw data that empathy maps synthesize and externalize for team alignment.
- [[Design Thinking]] — A problem-solving approach that centers on deep user understanding and empathy as the foundation for innovation; empathy mapping is a core tool for externalizing and sharing this understanding.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Creating shared understanding and goals across team members; empathy maps serve as a reference point for team decisions and protect against individual bias or unfounded assumptions.
- [[Persona]] — A fictional but research-based representation of a user type; empathy maps can be an intermediate step toward personas or an alternative representation depending on project needs.
