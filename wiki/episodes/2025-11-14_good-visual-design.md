# Good Visual Design, Explained

## Summary

Good visual design is intentional, built on four foundational principles applied consistently. Grids create cohesive, scannable layouts through columns and gutters; thin gutters suit airy content while wide gutters separate callouts. Typography establishes hierarchy through size and weight; limiting designs to three type sizes creates clear hierarchy without overwhelming users. Color palettes must be strategic; monochromatic or dual-color palettes create polish and enforce visual hierarchy. Imagery must serve purpose, providing product information or brand expression without visual clutter. These principles work together to create functional design where aesthetics affect how users perceive interface usability and trustworthiness.

## Key Takeaways

- **Grids enable consistent alignment** — columns and gutters create cohesive layouts across platforms; gutter sizes determine separation (thin for airy content, wide for separated callouts).
- **Three type sizes establish hierarchy** — limiting typography to three sizes (large primary, medium secondary, small captions) creates hierarchy without overwhelming; prioritize reading through appropriate leading and line length.
- **Strategic color palettes enforce hierarchy** — monochromatic or dual-color palettes create accessible, polished experiences; avoid neon colors, and let imagery take center stage with restrained palettes.
- **Imagery must be intentional** — provide valuable product information or support brand impression; use balanced, centered photos without visual clutter that distracts from content.
- **Consistency is foundational** — align elements to grids, apply typography hierarchy consistently, use color strategically, and maintain visual design principles across all pages.
- **Aesthetics affect usability perception** — how interfaces look affects perception of how well they function, making visual design principles essential to creating trustworthy, functional designs.

## Quotes

> Grids help designers create cohesive layouts, allowing end users to easily scan interfaces. A good grid adapts to various screen sizes and orientations, ensuring consistency across platforms.

> Limiting your designs to 3 type sizes will establish a strong hierarchy without overwhelming the design or user.

> Be intentional about decisions you make in your design by relying on well-established visual-design principles.
