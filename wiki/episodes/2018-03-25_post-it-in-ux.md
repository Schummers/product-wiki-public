# In Defense of Post-its

## Summary

Post-it notes are valuable tools in UX processes like ideation, affinity diagramming, and design thinking, despite criticism that they are trendy or overhyped. Post-its create visible, tangible evidence of collaborative work, maintain equality among contributors, force concise expression due to limited space, and encourage physical movement and team bonding. However, they should be used thoughtfully as tools to support a larger design process, not as replacements for designer expertise or as ends in themselves.

## Key Takeaways

- **Tangibility and visibility** — Post-its create physical evidence of group effort in different handwritings and colors, fostering team belonging and making ideas feel less abstract than digital documents.
- **Democratic participation** — all Post-its are the same size, creating visual equality among ideas and allowing both quiet and loud team members to contribute without tool proficiency barriers.
- **Concise expression** — the limited space forces teams to express ideas succinctly and at low-fidelity, preventing excessive refinement and making ideas easy to modify or reject.
- **Action and accountability** — Post-its drive action by requiring the group to physically organize ideas into a structure, preventing circular conversations and lost ideas.
- **Misuse warnings** — Post-its should not be treated as design themselves, should not replace strategic design thinking, and become problematic for remote teams or those with color blindness.

## Quotes

> Post-its are not the work of any one individual — they are the visible evidence of a group effort.

> By forcing people to write instead of talk, you move them into action faster. Once you have a set of Post-its, the group needs to act upon them.

> Design thinking is not Post-its, just as a photographer isn't Photoshop, and an accountant isn't Microsoft Excel.

## Concepts

- [[Customer Journey]] — using Post-its to map and visualize user journeys and customer touchpoints across the service experience.
- [[Design Process]] — employing Post-its as collaborative ideation and synthesis tools during design discovery and research phases.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — leveraging the physical and egalitarian nature of Post-its to foster inclusive teamwork and shared ownership of ideas.
