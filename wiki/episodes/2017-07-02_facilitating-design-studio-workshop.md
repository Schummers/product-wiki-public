# Facilitating an Effective Design Studio Workshop

## Summary

A design studio is a collaborative UX workshop combining divergent and convergent thinking to explore ideas and build shared vision rapidly. It incorporates brainstorming, critique, and prioritization in a single condensed session (typically hours). The workshop follows four segments: Sketch (individual ideation), Present and Critique (group feedback), Converge (combine ideas), and Prioritize (identify themes and value). Design studios benefit from diverse perspectives, fit lean/Agile workflows, and create team buy-in. The article describes specific facilitation methods including Crazy 8s for sketching, Six Thinking Hats for critique, SCAMPER for convergence, and the $100 Test for prioritization.

## Key Takeaways

- **Diversity strengthens ideation** — Invite participants from different teams, backgrounds, and expertise. Diverse perspectives generate more creative solutions and identify blind spots individual disciplines would miss.

- **Prioritize quantity in sketching** — During the sketch phase, emphasize generating many ideas quickly over producing polished work. Time constraints and quantity goals prevent overthinking and self-censoring.

- **Separate and structure critique** — Use frameworks like Six Thinking Hats to guide discussion through focused lenses: logic, emotions, strengths, weaknesses, creativity, and process. Structured critique is more objective and productive than free-form feedback.

- **Converge through combination, not consensus** — In the convergence phase, evolve and combine strong elements from separate ideas using methods like SCAMPER (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Magnify/Minimize, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse/Rearrange).

- **Monetize priorities** — Use the $100 Test: give groups a shared budget to allocate across features or concepts. Monetary framing triggers deeper investment in prioritization discussion than arbitrary point systems.

## Quotes

> A design studio is a type of UX workshop that combines divergent and convergent thinking: it allows UX professionals to explore a wide set of ideas and also create a shared vision to move forward within a short amount of time.

> Discourage talking during sketching. Individual concentration before group collaboration results in more ideas and more creativity.

> Don't just throw ideas out at this stage. Think about how they can be evolved or combined to become more relevant or stronger.
