# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 18. Mentorship

## Summary

The mentorship competency is the ability to actively help colleagues grow through knowledge sharing and feedback, either one-to-one or in group settings. The chapter distinguishes mentorship (sharing knowledge and experience) from coaching (a more advanced form that teaches someone to find solutions themselves, relevant for senior and management roles). Beyond benefiting the organization, mentorship benefits the mentor: it deepens understanding of the subject matter, improves communication skills, builds leadership capability, and increases visibility. The chapter provides concrete techniques for identifying mentorship opportunities, frameworks for teaching both hard skills (using the EDIP model: Explain, Demonstrate, Imitate, Practice) and soft skills (reverse-engineering one's own approach via inputs, outputs, and thinking process), and guidance on partnering with managers to support these efforts.

## Key Takeaways

- **Identify mentorship opportunities actively and passively** — Create a matrix rating teammates on your top competencies to find skill gaps, then proactively suggest mentoring. Passively, watch for recurring help requests (a teammate asking the same question repeatedly validates the mentorship need) or when other departments ask for something they could learn to do themselves, or when a teammate is struggling despite feedback.
- **Set outcome-focused goals for mentorship** — Instead of "teach David the Principle app," use SMART goals like "enable David to ship interactive prototypes independently within three weeks, reducing developer handoff time." Outcome-focused goals help secure manager approval, get the mentee to agree, and demonstrate impact for future performance reviews.
- **Use EDIP for hard skills** — The Explain-Demonstrate-Imitate-Practice framework, which the chapter says is used by extreme sports and military instructors, works for teaching tools and techniques: explain why it matters and what you'll do, demonstrate step by step, let them try it with correction, and have them practice until comfortable.
- **Reverse-engineer soft skills via inputs, outputs, and thinking process** — For soft skills like feedback processing or stakeholder management, reflect on scenarios where you use the skill, identify the inputs (context, who gave feedback, why), your outputs (what you did), and your reasoning (why you made that choice). This lets you teach the mental model rather than just examples.
- **Consider alternative mentorship formats** — Documentation (process guides that last forever and suit introverts), education hubs (curated links to learning resources, avoiding copyrighted redistributions), and formal mentorship programs (partnering with managers on a skill-mapping exercise, for which the chapter points to Nielsen Norman Group's free template, to identify team-wide skill gaps and allocate teaching resources).
- **Partner with your manager** — Managers benefit from mentorship as it helps them grow their team at scale, so they have incentive to support it logistically and help identify who needs which skills, but do not hesitate to ask them for help structuring the mentorship.

## Quotes

> Teaching a subject forces you to understand it really well yourself.

> Mentoring is the first step in building your leadership competency and it helps to put you on the path to management.

> Having a consistent mental model for processing feedback can save you the time and mental effort of making a decision on how to process each piece of feedback individually.

## Concepts

- [[Mentorship]] — This chapter defines mentorship as actively helping colleagues grow through knowledge and experience sharing, distinct from coaching, and outlines techniques for identifying opportunities (skill gaps, recurring help requests, struggling teammates), setting outcome-focused goals, and teaching both hard and soft skills.
- [[Design Leadership]] — The chapter frames mentorship as the first step toward leadership and management, benefiting the mentor through deepened expertise, improved communication, increased visibility, and developed leadership capability.
- [[Team Collaboration]] — Mentorship strengthens team collaboration by sharing expertise across the team, enabling teammates to solve problems independently, and building trust through knowledge exchange.
- [[Career Growth Plan]] — Structured mentorship with SMART goals and deliberate skill development (either individual or program-wide) directly supports teammate career growth and helps individuals progress in their roles.
- [[Design Competencies]] — The chapter emphasizes that mentorship develops specific design competencies across the team and that identifying which skills the team lacks is a prerequisite for effective mentorship.
