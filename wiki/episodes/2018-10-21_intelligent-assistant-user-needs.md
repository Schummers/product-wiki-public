# What Could an Intelligent Assistant Do for You? A Diary Study of User Needs

## Summary

This article presents findings from a diary study where 12 participants logged their ideal needs for an intelligent assistant over one week, then researchers tested whether current assistants like Siri, Google Assistant, and Alexa could address those needs. Participants logged 428 unique ideal needs, which researchers categorized by complexity and type. While 41% of ideal needs could theoretically be addressed by existing assistants and 21% could be partially addressed, participants only actually used assistants for 7% of needs. The research reveals a significant gap between what users want assistants to do and what they currently attempt to use them for.

## Key Takeaways

- **Significant gap exists between ideal needs and current usage** — Although 62% of logged needs could be fully or partially addressed by current assistants, users actually employed them only 7% of the time, suggesting low expectations and previous frustration.

- **Spoken commands are the strongly preferred interaction method** — 84% of ideal needs involved spoken commands as the trigger, with users expecting free-form voice input to be a core capability of intelligent assistants.

- **Simple actions dominate both ideal and actual use cases** — 58% of ideal needs were one-step tasks, but with current assistants only 26% of users engage with tasks more complex than one step, indicating unexploited potential for complex assistance.

- **Agency and proactive assistance are expected but limited** — 12% of ideal needs involved the assistant taking action without explicit command based on context, but current assistants rarely demonstrate this capability, disappointing users.

- **Most needs require personal data access** — 65% of ideal needs required personal information access (electronic data, physical information, or history), and 44% required web-based information, but current assistants have limited ability to leverage and integrate these data types.

- **Knowledge limitations severely restrict what assistants can accomplish** — Needs requiring access to third-party information, physical personal information, or leveraging prior interactions were unlikely to be addressable with current assistants, revealing fundamental capability gaps.

## Quotes

> We found that existing assistants could have addressed 41% (177) of the 'ideal' needs logged in the diary study,and another 21% of these needs could be partially addressed by the existing assistants.

> Diary-study participants attempted to use an existing digital assistant for only 7% of the unique needs they would want a 'perfect' assistant to help with.

> Both gaps must be closed (or at least narrowed substantially) for intelligent assistants to be truly useful.

## Concepts

- [[AI Agent]] — what users expect from intelligent assistants and the gap between ideal capabilities and current limitations in voice-activated systems.

- [[Voice Interfaces]] — user preference for spoken input as the primary interaction method and expectations for natural language processing and comprehension.

- [[User Research]] — the diverse range of tasks users want assistance with, from simple one-step actions to complex multitask activities requiring information synthesis.

- [[Artificial Intelligence]] — the critical need for assistants to understand varied, complex user utterances without requiring simplified or keyword-based language.

- [[Agency]] — users' expectation that intelligent assistants should proactively take actions based on context and infer goals from implicit signals rather than requiring explicit commands.
