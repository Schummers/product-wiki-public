# The Path to Senior Product Designer: 16. Independence (Ownership)

## Summary

This chapter teaches designers how to become more independent and increase their ownership across the organization. Instead of waiting for projects to be assigned, the chapter provides frameworks for identifying ownership opportunities, prioritizing work autonomously, proposing new initiatives to managers, and even outsourcing and managing vendor relationships. The underlying message is that taking control of your growth requires acting like an owner before being promoted to one.

## Key Takeaways

- **Find or Create Ownership** — Look for projects with no owner, ask PMs about responsibilities they'd delegate, watch for openings when people leave or during reorganizations, and expand your reach by owning multiple domains (design systems, documentation, onboarding) which increases job security.
- **Importance-Urgency Prioritization** — Rate each task one to ten on urgency and on importance, then place it on an urgency/importance matrix the author adapts from the Eisenhower matrix to get a priority from one to four; tasks landing in category four (neither urgent nor important) are candidates for elimination. Identify your Most Important Task (MIT) each morning and do it when you're most productive.
- **Building a Case to Initiate Work** — Frame work as solving a business problem, present alternatives with trade-offs, support with data and business context, explain how to measure success, justify timing, and suggest concrete next steps that don't add work to your manager.
- **Outsourcing as Leadership** — Identify work that can be outsourced (illustrations, video, development, content), make a business case for why it's needed, find or vet a vendor, and volunteer to own the vendor relationship, which increases your responsibility and leadership experience.

## Quotes

> If you want to be an owner of areas of work, act like one.

> It will also improve your productivity and your shipping competency.

> Replacing a designer who owns several domains (e.g., design systems, documentation, billing design, onboarding new designers, etc.) is much harder for organizations.

## Concepts

- [[Design Ownership]] — expanding the scope of what you take responsibility for by identifying and volunteering for projects, taking on others' responsibilities, prioritizing your own work rather than waiting for direction, and seeing yourself as accountable for outcomes rather than just task completion.
- [[Career Ladder]] — proactively seeking ownership opportunities and increasing your domain span (owning multiple areas of the product or organization) as a path to advancement and job security.
- [[Design Leadership]] — taking initiative to propose work, building a business case, managing external vendors, and creating accountability structures around your work rather than waiting for someone else to organize it.
- [[Design Strategy]] — prioritizing what to work on independently using importance and urgency, timing new initiatives to align with business context and available resources, and understanding business problems well enough to position design solutions as business solutions.
