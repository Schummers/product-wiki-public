# Page Composition and Hierarchy

## When to open this theme

Deciding what goes on a page, in what order, and what the eye should hit first.

The layout-level theme: hierarchy, scanning, disclosure, grouping. Pair it with [[Content and Interface Writing]] for what the sections actually say and [[Visual Design Craft]] for how they look.

## Concepts (8)

- [[Eye Tracking]] (18 sources) : what attention actually lands on, scanning patterns, and how stable those findings are
- [[Visual Hierarchy]] (17 sources) : the core techniques, colour as a hierarchy tool, and why prominence must be earned
- [[Web Usability]] (16 sources) : convention as default and deviation as cost, layout, links, trust, speed, forms
- [[Data Visualization]] (15 sources) : the 3 Cs, choosing a chart type, dashboards, tables, and the perceptual limits
- [[Progressive Disclosure]] (11 sources) : breaking long tasks into steps, layering depth, and signalling that more exists
- [[Aesthetic-Usability Effect]] (9 sources) : the instant judgement and what it decides, and countering it during research
- [[Information Design]] (8 sources) : signal-to-noise, deliberate encoding, redundant cues, grouping with containers
- [[Gestalt Principles]] (7 sources) : proximity, similarity, common region and closure, applied inside visual design
