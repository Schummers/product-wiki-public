# Psychology, Cognitive Load and Behaviour

## When to open this theme

Explaining why users behave as they do, reducing mental effort, or looking up a named law of UX (Fitts, Hick, Miller, Jakob, Tesler).

The 'why' underneath the guidance. Reach for it when you need the mechanism rather than the rule: attention, memory, mental models and bias, plus the ten psychological principles Laws of UX turns into design guidance.

## Concepts (20)

- [[Mental Model]] (31 sources) : conform to the model or change it, borrowing from the real world, surfacing models in research
- [[Decision Making]] (26 sources) : how users choose among alternatives, anchoring team decisions, auditing your own reasoning
- [[Cognitive Load]] (24 sources) : external memory, interruptions, what to remove, and whether hiding reduces or displaces load
- [[User Behavior]] (23 sources) : scanning patterns, learned conventions, filtering and avoidance, named behaviour patterns
- [[Cognitive Psychology]] (21 sources) : attention, working memory and chunking, automatic processing, perception-action mapping
- [[Interaction Cost]] (20 sources) : why counting clicks fails, how hiding raises cost, and cost across a whole journey
- [[Empathy]] (16 sources) : direct exposure over reports, and empathy maps: what they are, when and how to build them
- [[User Expectations]] (11 sources) : how design primes expectations, why perception lags performance, and sticky failures
- [[Cognitive Bias]] (10 sources) : biases that shape user decisions, biases that distort the team, and the limits of awareness
- [[Behavioral Economics]] (5 sources) : the named biases, choice architecture in copy, and reducing sludge rather than adding nudges
- [[Jakob's Law]] (3 sources) : starting from the convention, who sets the expectation, what breaking the pattern costs, turning it into team rules
- [[Human Factors Engineering]] (3 sources) : observing people where the work happens, measuring the human limit then designing to it, the lineage into HCI and UX
- [[Fitts's Law]] (2 sources) : size before proximity, spacing, screen edges as infinite targets, reach by device and hand, menu shape
- [[Hick's Law]] (2 sources) : cutting the choices shown at once, weighting options, the floor below which simplifying hurts
- [[Peak-End Rule]] (2 sources) : designing deliberate peaks, why the ending counts double, defusing negative peaks, locating them first
- [[Miller's Law]] (1 sources) : chunk rather than count, why the seven-item navigation rule is a myth, how to chunk visually
- [[Postel's Law]] (1 sources) : accepting input in the forms people give it, adapting to device capability, planning for language and typography
- [[Von Restorff Effect]] (1 sources) : the levers of contrast, signalling change on purpose, restraint, and contrast that excludes
- [[Tesler's Law]] (1 sources) : which side carries the irreducible complexity, complexity mistaken for competence, managing what must stay visible
- [[Doherty Threshold]] (1 sources) : the 400 ms mark, being perceived as fast, feedback during the wait, page weight, and delay added back on purpose
