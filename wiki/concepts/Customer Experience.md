# Customer Experience

## Definition

Customer experience is the broadest scope of experience: everything a person
encounters across their lifetime relationship with an organisation, not a single
screen. The clearest framing distinguishes three levels — the interaction level
(one user completing one task with a product), the journey level (an end-to-end
process spanning several channels to reach a goal), and the relationship level
(the whole lifetime of interactions) — and holds that the terms "UX" and "CX" are
essentially equivalent when properly interpreted, having emerged from different
eras with different default scopes [[2023-07-06_ux-vs-cx]]. What matters is not
which word a team uses but that they use it consistently and know which level
they are designing for [[2023-07-06_ux-vs-cx]].

In practice the concept covers everything outside the interface as well as
inside it: logistics, support, transactional emails, status updates, pricing
transparency and the internal operations that make those consistent
[[2017-02-19_3-user-experiences-reshaping-industries]],
[[2018-07-01_state-transactional-email]],
[[2019-02-03_status-tracker-progress-update]], [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]],
[[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]]. Delivering it consistently at scale
is described as an organisational problem: CX transformation is the
transformation of a company's values, structures, operations, technology and
culture so it can operate with a customer focus
[[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]].

## Practice

### Design across all three levels

- The three levels each require different expertise, and the sources explicitly
  reject any hierarchy between them [[2023-07-06_ux-vs-cx]].
- Interactions designed in isolation fail once woven into a real journey: a claims
  workflow can test perfectly and still conflict with the messaging of the
  confirmation email that follows it [[2023-07-06_ux-vs-cx]].
- The reverse also holds: a relationship-level vision needs flawless execution at
  the interaction level, because confusing UI copy or a rude support exchange
  destroys the relationship whatever the strategy says [[2023-07-06_ux-vs-cx]].
- Great experiences extend past the digital touchpoints. Rent the Runway's demand
  forecasting reduces how often anyone sees an out-of-stock indicator, and faster
  returns increase availability for the next customer — backend operations acting
  directly on the front-end experience
  [[2017-02-19_3-user-experiences-reshaping-industries]].
- Omnichannel consistency expands access: Airbnb across devices, Rent the Runway
  mobile browsing and ordering, Uber on-demand from anywhere
  [[2017-02-19_3-user-experiences-reshaping-industries]].
- Two-way rating systems create accountability and reduce perceived risk in
  peer-to-peer transactions, and real photos plus detailed information lower the
  friction of deciding [[2017-02-19_3-user-experiences-reshaping-industries]].

### Journey mapping as the shared view

- A journey map visualises the process a customer goes through to accomplish a
  goal, with goals, tasks, thoughts, emotions and touchpoints laid out across
  phases [[2020-05-31_journey-map-digital-template]]. It exposes where departments
  own service delivery and where friction or emotion runs high
  [[2019-04-07_journey-mapping-faq]].
- **Focus each map.** One persona, one scenario, tells a clear story; separate
  maps for separate personas or scenarios [[2019-04-07_journey-mapping-faq]].
- **The process outranks the artefact.** Building the map together forces the
  conversations and produces an aligned mental model across teams — both sources
  say this is the more valuable half [[2019-04-07_journey-mapping-faq]],
  [[2020-05-31_journey-map-digital-template]].
- **Base it on research.** Interviews, contextual inquiry and diary studies, not
  internal assumptions; marketing, customer service and sales supply the internal
  research [[2019-04-07_journey-mapping-faq]].
- **Involve stakeholders from the start** to secure buy-in and to focus the
  research [[2019-04-07_journey-mapping-faq]].
- **Map current and future states.** The gap between them reveals opportunities,
  and overlaying the emotional journey highlights what needs fixing
  [[2019-04-07_journey-mapping-faq]].
- **Treat it as a living document.** Version numbers and dates signal that it
  evolves [[2020-05-31_journey-map-digital-template]].
- **Lower the tooling barrier.** A spreadsheet template — phases as columns;
  actions, thoughts, emotions, insights and highs/lows as rows — needs no special
  software or accounts, passes most data-protection policies, requires no
  onboarding, and raises team ownership by using a familiar interface. It is a
  mid-fidelity first step toward higher-fidelity visualisations, and teams should
  feel free to add phases or expand swim lanes
  [[2020-05-31_journey-map-digital-template]].

### Keeping customers informed

- Status trackers are pull-based, checked at the user's convenience; progress
  updates are push notifications sent when the status changes. Offering both is
  recommended: a tracker alone induces anxiety and obsessive re-checking
  [[2019-02-03_status-tracker-progress-update]].
- Prioritise the latest update prominently, write in plain language rather than
  backend codes like "fulfilled" or "label created", and structure entries so they
  are scannable [[2019-02-03_status-tracker-progress-update]].
- Give updates even when a long process is inherently slow — lower-granularity
  reassurance prevents the assumption that something went wrong, which is what
  drives users to contact support and erodes trust in the tracker
  [[2019-02-03_status-tracker-progress-update]].
- Keep status identical across web, phone and email; inconsistency erodes trust
  [[2019-02-03_status-tracker-progress-update]].
- Let users change contact details and notification frequency from inside the
  tracker [[2019-02-03_status-tracker-progress-update]].

### Transactional email as a touchpoint

- Transactional emails (purchase confirmations, shipping and delivery
  notifications, registration updates) are touchpoints that strengthen the
  relationship when done well and undermine trust when not
  [[2018-07-01_state-transactional-email]].
- They have improved on average — spam fear has receded thanks to better
  filtering, HTML, images and emojis are now normal rather than text-only, and a
  diary study rated desirable characteristics 5.9 out of 7
  [[2018-07-01_state-transactional-email]].
- Utility comes first: communicate the information quickly, easily and completely,
  answering the user's questions without forcing them to click through or contact
  the company [[2018-07-01_state-transactional-email]].
- Aggressive marketing backfires; cramped ads and excessive frequency annoy users
  and waste the relationship-building opportunity
  [[2018-07-01_state-transactional-email]].
- Optimise for mobile reading, and use the preheader text after the subject line
  to deliver real information in the inbox rather than filler
  [[2018-07-01_state-transactional-email]].
- Keep it succinct and utilitarian; cleverness only if it does not obstruct the
  core message [[2018-07-01_state-transactional-email]].

### B2B is not exempt

- Jakob's Law applies: business buyers also use consumer sites and carry those
  expectations over, so the assumption that B2B is immune to e-commerce usability
  standards is a misconception [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]].
- Show prices. Hiding them raises interaction cost, produces sticker shock and
  reads as concealment; starting prices or ranges anchor expectations
  [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]].
- Explain upfront what can and cannot be customised, and display tiers in
  comparison tables with consistent feature ordering
  [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]].
- Address switching costs head-on — migration assistance, fee coverage — on FAQ or
  pricing pages [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]].
- Answer basic questions on How It Works pages, FAQs and forums before requiring
  sales contact or a login; giving value first makes people comfortable reaching
  out [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]].
- Equip the buyer to sell internally: case studies, testimonials, demos and free
  trials let them educate colleagues and defend the decision
  [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]].
- Clear copy is read as honest; convoluted writing draws scepticism
  [[2019-09-01_b2b-trust-from-b2c]].

### Operationalising CX at scale

Based on research with 148 UX practitioners and 22 CX leaders, the transformation
framework names four organisational areas that must change together — vision and
strategy, employees, operations, and technology — and four building blocks for
making the change [[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]]:

- **Leadership and governance.** A clear customer-focused vision statement,
  committed leadership, and defined governance to align the organisation
  [[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]].
- **Experience-design operations.** Shared standards, processes and playbooks so
  teams collaborate on journey design and apply user-centred practices
  consistently [[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]].
- **Customer insights and metrics.** Ongoing collection of behaviour data,
  feedback and sentiment so decisions stay grounded in real needs
  [[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]].
- **Culture management.** Training and change-management support, without which
  silos persist [[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]].
- **Technology.** Unified data models and CX management platforms enable seamless
  journeys; fragmented data systems perpetuate channel silos
  [[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]].

The diagnosis behind the framework is historical: most companies were built for
in-person and telephone business, and decades of investment created silos of
responsibility that now produce a patchwork of fragmented brand interactions
rather than a designed experience [[2021-03-14_framework-cx-transformation]].
