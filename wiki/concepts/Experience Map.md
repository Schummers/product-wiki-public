## Sources (4)

- [[2021-05-18_continuous-discovery-habits_07-chapter-four-visualizing-what-you-know]] — This chapter introduces experience maps as the foundational artifact: a visual representation of what teams currently know about how customers experience a process, revealing gaps in team understanding and serving as a guide for discovery interviews.
- [[2021-05-18_continuous-discovery-habits_08-chapter-five-continuous-interviewing]] — The chapter uses experience maps (introduced in Chapter 4) as a guide during interviews to track the customer's journey, identify nodes and steps in their story, and compare individual customer stories against the generalized experience map.
- [[2019-12-17_storytelling-in-design_08-chapter-7-defining-the-setting-and-context-of-your-product]] — The chapter recommends developing a setting and context chart, visualized as a customer experience map, to break the silos that come from teams not sharing a common understanding of where and when the experience takes place.
- [[2019-12-17_storytelling-in-design_09-chapter-8-storyboarding-for-product-design]] — The chapter emphasizes that experience maps should be living documents, continuously evaluated and improved; they are most valuable when printed large and visible to the team, showing how everything is intricately linked and how a change at one end impacts something elsewhere.
