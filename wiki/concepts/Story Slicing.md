# Story Slicing

## Definition

_Stub — to be written by the concept stage._

## Sources (4)

- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_09-4-plan-to-finish-on-time]] — The technique of dividing the feature into three crosscutting slices (opening, midgame, endgame) rather than horizontal layers; each slice runs the full width of the feature and builds toward a working end-to-end system.
- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_15-10-bake-stories-like-cake]] — The chapter defines the practice of breaking large stories into small, complete, end-to-end slices (cupcakes) that deliver value and allow early evaluation, rather than breaking by layer or subsystem.
- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_21-16-refine-define-and-build]] — Stories are split when workshops reveal they are too large to build in a single cycle; the Good-Better-Best game and acceptance criteria analysis reveal natural splits without creating the "asteroids" problem of too many tiny stories.
- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_22-17-stories-are-actually-like-asteroids]] — Stories should be broken progressively at four distinct stages (opportunity, discovery, release, cycle planning), each with different purposes. Breaking all stories small too early creates unmanageable backlogs; breaking on schedule keeps context and purpose aligned.
