## Sources (5)

- [[2019-02-17_startup-studio-playbook_08-building-a-100-co-owned-venture-builder]] — Lab.Coop's culture is built on foundational trust and radical transparency: all operations are disclosed, decisions are peer-driven through holacracy, and authority is distributed rather than hierarchical; this cultural foundation enables co-ownership and self-management to work without chaos; newcomers require education, but self-selection (attracted by the model) and peer pressure (tight-knit team) maintain culture as the organization scales from 7 to 35 people.
- [[2019-02-17_startup-studio-playbook_10-culture-is-a-verb]] — Backspace defines culture as an active practice built through six core practices: (1) defining core and aspirational values through individual reflection and team clustering, distinguishing between embedded values (already natural) and aspirational values (desired behaviors to cultivate); (2) visualizing mission and purpose through meditation and group envisioning; (3) designing rituals that embed values, including daily lunch, weekly celebrations, monthly off-sites, and quarterly governance reviews; (4) sharing personal stories to build trust across disciplines; (5) off-grid time for memory-making; (6) assigning one person responsibility for ongoing culture maintenance. The studio also fills three skill pillars (technology, design, traction) with no concessions, using a template to assess founder gaps and team composition; imbalance is a leading risk and multidisciplinary balance enables faster pivots and more balanced decision-making. Values must be actionable and revisited as the organization evolves, formulated as phrases that resonate emotionally and are easily recalled by the team. Culture-building is cyclical and never complete, requiring continuous attention.
- [[2019-02-17_startup-studio-playbook_12-changing-ecommerce-one-startup-at-a-time]] — The AWESOME core values (Always Keep Improving, Wow Your Customers, Experiment, Sweep Floor, Owner Mentality, Mutual Respect, Enjoy the Game) and human investment practices are positioned as foundational competitive advantage, supporting retention and performance of both core team and venture CEOs.
- [[2019-02-17_startup-studio-playbook_17-entrepreneurs-in-residence-and-ceos]] — EIR management requires regular one-on-ones, calibrated challenge levels (neither overload nor boredom), participation in fundraising, and facilitated knowledge sharing; these practices signal that the studio cares about individual success and motivates tenure despite eventual company releases.
