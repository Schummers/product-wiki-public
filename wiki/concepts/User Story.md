# User Story

## Definition

_Stub — to be written by the concept stage._

## Sources (5)

- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_04-preface]] — Patton introduces stories as a common but widely misunderstood practice in Agile, and positions the book as correcting misconceptions about their role and use.
- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_05-read-this-first]] — Patton redefines stories as a mechanism for conversation and building shared understanding about solving problems for customers and users, fundamentally different from a requirements writing format.
- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_06-1-the-big-picture]] — Patton traces the concept back to Kent Beck and Extreme Programming, emphasizing that stories are meant to be told as conversations, not documented comprehensively; the size of what one card represents (a couple of hours, days, weeks or a month of development) is unknowable until the team talks about it.
- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_10-5-you-already-know-how]] — Framed through the lens of tasks: each story describes something a user does, using a verb phrase; organizing these tasks reveals the structure of the user's journey and priorities.
- [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_15-10-bake-stories-like-cake]] — Patton reinforces that a user story is a conversation prompt anchored in who, what, and why—not just a technical requirement. The conversation explores not just the solution but the goal and outcome the user seeks.
