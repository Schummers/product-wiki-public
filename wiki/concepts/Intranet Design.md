# Intranet Design

## Definition

An intranet is the internal website that acts as the primary communication and
information hub for employees [[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].
Designing one is a distinct practice, not a variant of public web design: it
serves a captive audience whose productivity depends on it, it is usually built
by IT, HR or Communications rather than by designers
[[2018-12-23_branding-intranet]], and rules written for the public site do not
automatically transfer to it
[[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]], [[2018-12-23_branding-intranet]].

The sources converge on one diagnosis: intranet problems are organisational as
much as they are visual. The seven persistent mistakes catalogued in
[[2016-11-27_top-intranet-design-mistakes]] are rooted in staffing constraints
and politics, and fixing them means addressing those, not just applying design
technique. Correspondingly, the guidance is heavy on the work that happens
before design — leadership, research, vision, resources
[[2020-05-30_user-centered-intranet-redesign-steps]] — and on observing
employees in their real work environment rather than surveying them
[[2018-07-08_field-studies-intranet-redesign]]. Crises and mergers act as
stress tests of that groundwork [[2020-05-31_covid19-intranet-ia]],
[[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].

## Practice

### The persistent mistakes

[[2016-11-27_top-intranet-design-mistakes]] names seven that survive despite
being well understood:

- **Poor search results** — content not crawled, badly tagged or badly
  categorised stays hidden; employees waste time or act on inaccurate content.
- **Content in silos** — walled-off team spaces, collaboration platforms and
  subsites are hard to discover and isolate employees; politics drive the silos
  despite their cost.
- **Poor visual layout** — no page-layout strategy, no clear hierarchy, clutter;
  text styling, graphic placement and whitespace directly affect scannability.
- **Illegible text** — small light-grey text on white, low contrast, tiny fonts,
  flowery typefaces, made worse by glare on portable devices.
- **Announcements styled as ads** — internal promotions dressed in bright
  colours, animation and stock-art people trigger banner blindness and repel
  employees from critical messages.
- **Disappearing global navigation** — navigation vanishes on subsites and deep
  pages, stranding employees; progressive disclosure rarely justifies hiding it.
- **Portal pages that only promise** — bare links help less than a preview;
  showing a stock price, personalised values or post titles gives a reason to
  click.

### Design patterns seen on award-winning intranets

[[2016-06-05_top-intranet-trends]] reports the shared traits of the Intranet
Design Annual 2016 winners:

- Simple, sometimes minimalist aesthetics replacing the earlier flat-and-boxy
  look, with generous whitespace, elegant grey palettes with accent colours and
  legible typography.
- Helpful help and tutorials returning after years out of fashion, aiding
  discoverability and employee confidence.
- Authentic photography of employees doing real work, preferred over generic
  stock imagery, creating engagement and a sense of community.
- Search improved through user feedback, keyword adjustment, richer metadata and
  Best Bets — a critical differentiator in reducing information-seeking
  friction.
- Carousels still common but single hero images returning for stronger visual
  impact.
- Fat footers as a second chance to find content, repeating navigation,
  suggesting related items or highlighting popular links.
- Left-side global navigation, which translates from desktop to mobile better
  than megamenus.
- Targeted social features presented with clear context and guidelines rather
  than undescribed feed walls.
- Video normalised: production quality no longer expected to be high,
  self-shot videos welcomed, stored in dedicated sections with filtering and
  search.
- Leaders using the intranet to communicate organisational goals and status,
  which shows respect and builds engagement.

Note the tension between the two lists: [[2016-06-05_top-intranet-trends]]
records social feeds, carousels and imagery as strengths of the best intranets,
while [[2016-11-27_top-intranet-design-mistakes]] warns that promotional visual
treatment on internal content produces banner blindness. Both are about the same
surface; the difference is whether the visual energy carries information or
imitates advertising.

### Branding and naming for an internal audience

- Weak or missing intranet branding costs credibility, adoption and acceptance
  [[2018-12-23_branding-intranet]].
- Names should be memorable and pronounceable; clever acronyms and wordplay
  backfire, plain names (Portal, Center, Inside [Company], [Company] Hub) work,
  and a name is hard to change later because employees keep using the old one
  [[2018-12-23_branding-intranet]].
- Show the intranet name in the logo, optionally beside the company logo; the
  company logo alone leaves users unsure which site they are on
  [[2018-12-23_branding-intranet]].
- Colours and fonts can follow corporate guidelines while the tone of voice is
  tailored to employees [[2018-12-23_branding-intranet]].
- Do not overbrand the interface: no catchy feature names ("Upload Wizard"
  instead of "Upload a Document") and no third-party product names as labels
  ("Job Postings", not "SAS") [[2018-12-23_branding-intranet]].

### Setting up a redesign

[[2020-05-30_user-centered-intranet-redesign-steps]] draws on hundreds of
high-performing projects over 20 years and puts almost everything before the
first sketch, across eleven steps: appoint a leader, assemble the team, identify
the redesign catalyst, conduct user research and measurement, map the digital
ecosystem, involve stakeholders, align on vision, allocate resources, create
user-centered artifacts, plan systems and operations, advertise early.

- Appoint an official leader who owns vision, team coordination, stakeholder
  relationships and decisions; without one, responsibility diffuses.
- Research must precede vision-setting, not follow it, guided by four reference
  points: job roles and relationships, tasks tied to business objectives,
  current system performance, and improvement opportunities.
- Map every intranet, extranet and tool employees use, to find consolidation or
  replacement opportunities and avoid perpetuating silos.
- 80% of award-winning intranets hire an external agency; long-term partnerships
  that continue past launch beat one-off engagements.
- Keep distributed, part-time teams aligned with journey maps, personas, top
  tasks, service blueprints and jobs-to-be-done, and plan a design system
  covering brand, content, UI guidelines and pattern libraries.
- Advertise the redesign early to soften change aversion.
- The catalyst — merger, technology update, rebranding, or accumulated
  complaints — determines which steps will be easy or hard.

### Research: observe, don't survey

- Surveys and lab usability tests give incomplete or inaccurate pictures of real
  intranet workflows; field studies show how employees juggle multiple
  information sources, work around system limits, navigate organisational
  dynamics and mix digital with physical tools
  [[2018-07-08_field-studies-intranet-redesign]].
- Field observation corrects the team's theory of the problem, reveals how
  workflow issues hit roles beyond the primary user group, and shifts the team
  from blaming users to blaming system design
  [[2018-07-08_field-studies-intranet-redesign]].

### Mergers and acquisitions

- A merger is unusually favourable ground for an excellent intranet: several
  experienced teams collaborate, two existing designs offer lessons, management
  direction is clear and the intranet team gains autonomy
  [[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].
- Do not remove features or content without explaining why; unexplained removal
  confuses and demotivates and reads as high-handed
  [[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].
- Do not leave both intranets running by default, and do not hastily pick one:
  duplication, lost productivity and the message that one group was devalued all
  follow [[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].
- Use a two-stage strategy — a day-one interim solution for urgent needs, then
  research and design toward the long-term intranet — which relieves the time
  pressure [[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].
- Keep internal and external branding rules separate; employees may need
  deprecated brand names kept in parentheses for recognition and comfort
  [[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].
- Design for three coexisting employee emotional states: anxiety about job
  security, confusion about what changed, and excitement about new
  opportunities. Motivating employees is hard, demotivating them is easy, and
  everyone who publishes on an intranet holds that power
  [[2018-11-04_intranet-merger-or-acquisition]].

### Crisis information architecture

- Two strategies for rapidly changing crisis content: consolidate everything in
  one dedicated section linked from the homepage, which is fastest to build and
  maintain and suits urgent situations with limited capacity, or integrate it
  into the existing IA, which serves the long term but costs more upfront
  [[2020-05-31_covid19-intranet-ia]].
- Plan the conversion from temporary to permanent from the start: decide when an
  item becomes long-lasting and where it will live. The severe, common mistake
  is leaving a news item, policy or tool in a temporary section forever, never
  updated, moved or linked from related pages [[2020-05-31_covid19-intranet-ia]].
- When content lives in several places, crosslink related pages and improve
  metadata — keywords, tags, titles — so search works
  [[2020-05-31_covid19-intranet-ia]].
- Communicate phases explicitly (which is current, completed, planned), mark
  deprecated procedures as no longer valid, and show last-updated dates and
  version information so users can judge freshness
  [[2020-05-31_covid19-intranet-ia]].
