# Design Rationale

## Definition

_Stub — to be written by the concept stage._

## Sources (8)

- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_01-preface]] — Greever's journey reveals that designers must understand and articulate the reasoning behind each design choice to gain stakeholder support.
- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_02-chapter-1-great-designers-are-great-communicators]] — This chapter emphasizes that designers must write down and be consciously aware of why every design decision was made, including the problem being solved and how it affects users.
- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_05-chapter-4-listen-to-understand]] — Taking notes that explain not just what was decided but why (including the thinking and data behind it) creates a record that prevents rehashing old conversations.
- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_06-chapter-5-get-in-the-right-mindset]] — Explaining the thinking behind decisions, including constraints and trade-offs, is how designers help stakeholders understand and represent their work; vocabulary and story matter as much as logic.
- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_07-chapter-6-form-a-response]] — the chapter's three core questions (what problem does it solve, how does it affect the user, why is it better) form the strategic basis for any response; Greever frames these as the foundation for every communication with stakeholders.
- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_08-chapter-7-choose-a-message]] — the chapter's message categories provide strategic rationales for specific design moves; a designer must be able to articulate which category applies and use its language to justify their approach.
- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_09-chapter-8-lock-in-agreement]] — each case study in the chapter demonstrates how to articulate the reasoning behind a design (why it was chosen, what problem it solves, what trade-offs it makes).
- [[2020-08-03_articulating-design-decisions_10-chapter-9-follow-up-afterward]] — written follow-up notes serve as a record of reasoning, allowing the designer to avoid revisiting settled decisions and to reference the logic when changes are later questioned.
