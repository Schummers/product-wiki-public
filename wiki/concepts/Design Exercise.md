## Sources (21)

- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_03-why-should-you-read-this-book]] — Positions design exercises as a core mechanism for preparing candidates and evaluating whether they can solve product and business problems through design.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_04-chapter-1-introducing-the-design-exercise]] — Defines the design exercise as a structured problem-solving task used in hiring to evaluate how candidates approach product design challenges and justify their decisions.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_05-what-is-a-design-exercise]] — the chapter defines design exercises and establishes their three main formats, making this the foundational source for understanding the concept
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_06-chapter-2-how-to-solve-a-product-design-exercise]] — Presents the framework and mindset for approaching design exercises: process-driven problem-solving prioritizing understanding over aesthetics.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_07-answer-structure]] — establishes that a design exercise mimics real product-building by requiring candidates to understand the product vision, generate solutions, and measure success through a structured 5W1H framework; introduces the 5W1H as the foundational framework for solving design exercises, borrowed from journalism and product management disciplines.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_08-the-framework]] — establishes that the seven-step framework is the structured approach for solving design exercises, with the understanding that practice and adaptation are necessary; provides the overview of the seven-step framework, emphasizing that different steps require different skills and preparation levels.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_10-step-2-define-the-audience-who]] — Step 2 of the framework requires selecting and characterizing the primary target audience before proceeding to understanding context and generating solutions.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_11-step-3-understand-the-customer-s-context-and-needs-when-and]] — Step 3 of the framework requires understanding user context and needs before proceeding to ideation, building on the audience definition from Step 2.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_12-step-4-list-ideas-what]] — Step 4 of the framework requires listing three to four possible product solutions before narrowing down in later steps.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_13-step-5-prioritise-and-choose-an-idea]] — The interview format where candidates demonstrate their ability to articulate reasoning for design choices, including trade-offs in reach, value, and implementation effort.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_14-step-6-solve]] — The practical phase of a design interview exercise where candidates create wireframes, flows, and sketches to demonstrate their ability to translate a chosen idea into an interactive experience.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_15-step-7-measure-success-how]] — The closing step of an interview exercise where candidates articulate how they would validate and measure the impact of their proposed solution.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_16-how-to-present-your-solution]] — The communication and persuasion component where candidates articulate their entire thought process, framework application, and design decisions to interviewers or hiring committees.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_17-chapter-3-questions-and-answers]] — Real-world examples and worked solutions demonstrating how the seven-step framework applies across diverse problem domains (hardware, digital platforms, healthcare, finance).
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_18-3-1-designing-a-kiosk-interface]] — this chapter is a worked example showing how a designer approaches a complex design exercise combining physical and digital experiences, demonstrating the framework in practice
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_19-3-2-a-self-publishing-platform-for-amazon]] — A worked example of the framework on a large-scope brief, running goal, audience, context and needs, ideas, solve and measure; Step 5 is skipped outright, the chapter stating that the web app was already settled in Step 4 so there is nothing left to prioritise.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_20-3-3-a-dashboard-for-freelancers]] — The chapter walks through all seven steps of the design framework: understanding the goal of reducing admin burden, defining diverse freelancer personas, mapping context and needs, brainstorming web and mobile solutions, choosing web as the core, designing a master-detail dashboard, and defining completion and engagement metrics.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_21-3-4-improving-primary-health-care]] — The chapter demonstrates all seven steps: establishing the business goal (improve access, save costs), identifying patient and GP personas, mapping patient context (anxiety, diverse locations, urgent and preventive needs), brainstorming four distinct product approaches, using an impact/effort matrix to prioritize, designing the shared appointment flow, and defining both user engagement and long-term health outcome metrics.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_22-3-5-improving-the-atm-experience]] — A worked example demonstrating how a candidate can solve a realistic design problem by applying a structured problem-solving framework with explicit steps for research, ideation, and validation.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_23-chapter-4-how-to-use-a-design-exercise-when-interviewing]] — Guidelines for implementing design exercises in hiring: separate take-home (visual and deliverable skills) from on-site (product thinking) to surface candidate weaknesses early and test multiple competencies.
- [[2018-02-12_solving-design-exercises_24-chapter-5-tasks-list]] — A collated list of 30+ open-ended design prompts, the ones used earlier in the book plus additional ones, several carrying an explicit technology constraint or a business-metric target.
