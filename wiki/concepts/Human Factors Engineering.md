# Human Factors Engineering

## Definition

Human factors engineering is the discipline, established by psychologists, that
focuses on designing tools, machines and systems which take into account human
capabilities, limitations and characteristics
([[2024-01-23_laws-of-ux_02-a-brief-history-of-psychology-and-design]]). It
emerged during World War II, when psychologists such as Paul Fitts and Alphonse
Chapanis recognised that humans are error-prone, especially under stress, and
concluded that machines should be built to align with human capabilities rather
than requiring users to adapt to them.

That inversion is the founding move of the field and of what came after it.
[[2024-01-23_laws-of-ux_04-2-fittss-law]] traces it to wartime aviation crashes
caused by pilots confusing identical controls: Fitts and Chapanis showed the
causes were systematic rather than individual pilot error, and in doing so
established human factors as a design discipline. Its premise, stated in that
chapter, is that designing better technology means designing for humans, and
designing for humans means anticipating their emotions, limitations and
preconceptions.

## Practice

### Observe people where the work happens

Fitts and Chapanis interviewed the pilots involved in the crashes, an approach
the source describes as an early form of contextual inquiry — the ethnographic
field study that observes and interviews a small sample of users in their
natural environments. That method is what surfaced the systematic cause behind
the crashes; asking who erred would have found only pilots
([[2024-01-23_laws-of-ux_04-2-fittss-law]]). The same commitment runs through
the later discipline, where Jane Fulton Suri pioneered ethnographic research
and human-centered design at IDEO
([[2024-01-23_laws-of-ux_02-a-brief-history-of-psychology-and-design]]).

### Measure the human limit, then design to it

Human factors work turns an observed limitation into a quantity a designer can
build against. Fitts's 1954 study of human motor control produced
[[Fitts's Law]] and its index of difficulty, ID = log₂(2D/W), from which the
practical rules for [[Tap Area]] sizing, spacing and placement follow
([[2024-01-23_laws-of-ux_04-2-fittss-law]]).

### The lineage it sits in

[[2024-01-23_laws-of-ux_02-a-brief-history-of-psychology-and-design]] places
the discipline in a continuous line:

- [[Gestalt Principles]] before it, establishing that humans perceive organised
  patterns rather than isolated sensory elements.
- Human factors during World War II.
- The Cold War combination of psychology, systems analysis and computer science
  that became [[Interaction Design]], with Xerox PARC as its pivot and *The
  Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction* (1983) by Card, Moran and Newell
  placing [[Cognitive Psychology]] at the centre of personal computing design.
- [[User Experience]] as a named discipline, the term coined by Donald Norman
  to cover all aspects of a user's interaction with a system.

The record's summary of what carries across the whole line: psychology informs
how to design systems that align with how people actually are, rather than how
we want them to be.
