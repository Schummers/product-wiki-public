# Experience Shape

## Definition

_Stub — to be written by the concept stage._

## Sources (4)

- [[2019-12-17_storytelling-in-design_03-chapter-2-the-anatomy-of-a-great-story]] — Dahlström introduces the concept that stories have recognizable shapes—visual arcs that communicate emotional intensity over time; she references Nancy Duarte's observation that great talks follow a shape (What Is → What Could Be → What Is...New Bliss) that resonates emotionally with audiences.
- [[2019-12-17_storytelling-in-design_05-chapter-4-the-emotional-aspect-of-product-design]] — The chapter frames the emotional arc of a product experience as a story with shape: unhappy moments at barriers, happy moments at completions, neutral moments that allow flow, and reflective moments that pause for confirmation. The shape itself is explicitly deferred: Dahlström points to Chapter 5 for narrative structures and plot points and to Chapter 9 for visualizing the shape of an experience. The mapping she recommends here is running the "What if…?" exercise as a full end-to-end review over a customer experience or journey map.
- [[2019-12-17_storytelling-in-design_10-chapter-9-visualizing-the-shape-of-your-product-experience]] — Dahlström demonstrates that visualizing emotional shape helps designers and teams see the ebb and flow of the user experience; by borrowing story structures (Campbell, Vonnegut, Booker), the transfer Dahlström actually makes is The Quest, which applies well to a user needing to find information, book something or buy something; she notes other plots are less relevant to product design (hopefully not Tragedy).
- [[2019-12-17_storytelling-in-design_15-chapter-14-presenting-and-sharing-your-story]] — Dahlström emphasizes designing the emotional arc of a presentation, mapping desired emotional response (low, medium, high) to each slide or section to create an engaging experience that guides the audience through the narrative.
