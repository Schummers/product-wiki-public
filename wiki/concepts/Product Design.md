# Product Design

## Definition

Across these sources, Product Design names the role that UX and UI design have
been absorbed into: a porous, extended job that is no longer confined to the
visual interface but concerns itself with usages, problems worth solving, and
the company's own objectives [[2026-05-05_412_De_Product_Designer_à_CPO_(Chief_Product_Officer),_carrière_produit]].
The move from UX designer to Product Designer is described precisely as the
integration of business strategy into the designer's remit — financial goals
alongside user experience [[2024-12-31_358_Top_3_des_livres_pour_Designers_en_2025]],
[[2025-09-16_395_L_UX_Design_est_mort_L_impact_de_l_IA_&_du_business_sur_le_métier_de_designer]].
The disappearance of the pure UI role is partly attributed to design systems,
whose ready-made components made interface production far easier
[[2025-09-16_395_L_UX_Design_est_mort_L_impact_de_l_IA_&_du_business_sur_le_métier_de_designer]].

The corpus is written from inside a period of transformation, and the second
force reshaping the role is AI. The direction of travel is consistent: the
visual artefact is treated as a deliverable rather than as where the value sits,
so the designer's centre of gravity shifts towards identifying problems,
defining solutions, research and strategy, while the role simultaneously
hybridises outwards into code and data
[[2026-06-02_416_J_ai_testé_Claude_Design_-_Avis_&_impact_sur_le_métier_de_Designer]],
[[2026-03-31_407_L_avenir_du_rôle_de_Product_Designer_-_Avec_George_Turp]]. One
source goes as far as imagining a generation of designers five years from now
who never master visual creation tools, and judges that this might not be much
of a problem [[2026-06-02_416_J_ai_testé_Claude_Design_-_Avis_&_impact_sur_le_métier_de_Designer]].

## Practice

### Own the business, not only the user

- Companies are rationalising costs and want more from fewer resources, which
  puts the burden on UX to guarantee that what gets built succeeds and that the
  team is answering the right problems rather than spending on the wrong ones
  [[2025-09-16_395_L_UX_Design_est_mort_L_impact_de_l_IA_&_du_business_sur_le_métier_de_designer]].
- The designer can no longer act as the exclusive advocate of the end user: the
  job includes juggling buyers' needs and the company's own business constraints
  to reach a workable balance
  [[2025-09-16_395_L_UX_Design_est_mort_L_impact_de_l_IA_&_du_business_sur_le_métier_de_designer]].
- Business strategy is presented as methodologically close to design — diagnosis,
  guiding policy, action plan — which is why reading strategy literature is
  recommended to designers
  [[2024-12-31_358_Top_3_des_livres_pour_Designers_en_2025]].
- To move towards Head of Product or CPO, a designer must step outside the
  design perimeter: brand positioning, company decisions, the broader business
  stakes [[2026-05-05_412_De_Product_Designer_à_CPO_(Chief_Product_Officer),_carrière_produit]].
  In that account design was never the goal, only a lens and a tool serving the
  deployment of product strategy, with problem-solving and value creation as the
  actual end.

### From intuition to evidence

- The decisive shift for moving into product roles is leaving the intuition
  inherent to design for the factual: being data-oriented, measuring one's
  actions, reasoning in terms of return on investment
  [[2026-05-05_412_De_Product_Designer_à_CPO_(Chief_Product_Officer),_carrière_produit]].
- The rigid pursuit of perfection is no longer viable given demands for speed and
  flexibility; designers should calibrate their level of certainty to the level
  of risk, favouring fast prototypes and regular customer feedback
  [[2025-09-16_395_L_UX_Design_est_mort_L_impact_de_l_IA_&_du_business_sur_le_métier_de_designer]].

### AI as accelerator and as raw material

- AI speeds up prototyping, research and data analysis, freeing human time; it is
  also a new standard technology that designers must turn into genuine
  differentiating value for the customer
  [[2025-09-16_395_L_UX_Design_est_mort_L_impact_de_l_IA_&_du_business_sur_le_métier_de_designer]].
- The role hybridises: with AI's help designers build interfaces themselves and
  sometimes back-end logic, and use AI plus MCP to pull complex data out of tools
  like Metabase or Amplitude in seconds, potentially reducing the need for
  hyper-specialised profiles
  [[2026-03-31_407_L_avenir_du_rôle_de_Product_Designer_-_Avec_George_Turp]].
- Hiring is following: the same source reports that companies now look for people
  who are deep into AI, not only people who do product and design
  [[2026-03-31_407_L_avenir_du_rôle_de_Product_Designer_-_Avec_George_Turp]].
- Tooling advice from direct testing: an AI design tool that builds on a
  code-based design system is promising but slow, credit-hungry and prone to
  overloaded interfaces with alignment and hierarchy defects; for a designer's
  actual needs, generating working code directly is recommended instead, to test
  concepts fast, build advanced prototypes and ship proofs of concept
  [[2026-06-02_416_J_ai_testé_Claude_Design_-_Avis_&_impact_sur_le_métier_de_Designer]].

### Keep technical fundamentals

- Real innovation requires understanding and experimenting with the technology
  itself, notably AI APIs and LLMs; staying abstract and far from the technical
  layer limits the chance of discovering high-value new usages
  [[2025-09-16_395_L_UX_Design_est_mort_L_impact_de_l_IA_&_du_business_sur_le_métier_de_designer]].
- Solid foundations in code and data remain necessary to judge the quality of
  what AI generates; without them one falls into vibe coding and creates
  structural problems
  [[2026-03-31_407_L_avenir_du_rôle_de_Product_Designer_-_Avec_George_Turp]].
- Recruiters value pragmatic designers who can actually produce and collaborate
  with engineering, especially in the AI era
  [[2026-05-05_412_De_Product_Designer_à_CPO_(Chief_Product_Officer),_carrière_produit]].

### Human skills and career posture

- Despite the technology, the strongest productivity advice given is
  interpersonal communication: defuse conflicts early and solve problems by
  talking directly and honestly with the team
  [[2026-03-31_407_L_avenir_du_rôle_de_Product_Designer_-_Avec_George_Turp]].
- Rather than aiming at a title ten years out, listen to what motivates you day
  to day and favour doing over positioning
  [[2026-05-05_412_De_Product_Designer_à_CPO_(Chief_Product_Officer),_carrière_produit]].

### Design and development are distinct disciplines

- In his foreword to *User Story Mapping*, Alan Cooper argues that design and
  development are different disciplines: design is about finding a user's truth
  and narrating it, while development breaks that narrative into functional
  chunks; the two are performed by people with different skill sets and
  workflows [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_02-foreword-by-alan-cooper]].
- Cooper warns that building software one feature at a time is a legitimate
  development strategy but a poor design method: used as a way to decide a
  product's behaviour and scope, it yields a "Frankenstein monster" that loses
  the coherence and narrative structure good design depends on, so the user's
  truth risks being lost during feature-by-feature implementation unless it is
  explicitly preserved [[2014-09-05_user-story-mapping_02-foreword-by-alan-cooper]].

### Broadening one's reading

Reading pure design material is described as quickly becoming boring; stepping
sideways into adjacent subjects is what enriches the practice. The
recommendations are AI (to grasp how the models actually work rather than
treating them as magic black boxes), business strategy, and alternative growth
models that question the pursuit of infinite startup growth in favour of small,
stable, healthy teams — a model held to suit freelancers and autonomous teams.
A practical habit is offered with it: read at least two pages a day, every day,
about five minutes, so the thread of a book is never lost
[[2024-12-31_358_Top_3_des_livres_pour_Designers_en_2025]].
