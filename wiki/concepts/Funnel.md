# Funnel

## Definition

_Partially written: what follows covers only *Product Management for UX People*; the other sources listed below have not yet been folded into this prose._

A funnel is a multi-step process seen from the point of view of how many users drop off at each stage; analysing and optimising its conversion is, for Christian Crumlish, one of the core growth levers available to a product manager ([[2022-01-19_product-management-for-ux-people_09-chapter-6-product-analytics-growth-engagement-retention]]).

## Practice

### Reading drop-off, and what counts as anomalous (book)

[[2022-01-19_product-management-for-ux-people_09-chapter-6-product-analytics-growth-engagement-retention]] gives a rough rule of thumb — you are likely to lose 10% right off the top every time you add another step — while immediately hedging it: drop-off varies greatly, and some steps are trivial enough that everyone who completes the previous one completes the next. The useful work is therefore comparative, not absolute:

- Look for **anomalous** drop-offs at particular steps, as against normal attrition, to decide where to investigate and improve.
- **Monitor a funnel over time** to see whether experiments are actually improving conversion rates.
- Use **cohort analysis** to compare drop-off across time periods and user segments.
- Each AARRR growth stage (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue, with Awareness sometimes added on top) can be instrumented as its own funnel; Crumlish credits the mnemonic to Dave McClure and flags it as start-up-oriented.

See [[Analytics]] and [[Sustainable Growth]].
