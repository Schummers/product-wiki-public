## New Research to Drive Better Icon Design (and a New NN/g Book!)

In the [NN/g training](https://www.nngroup.com/training/virtual/)[Designing Complex Applications for Specialized Domains](https://www.nngroup.com/courses/complex-apps-specialized-domains/), we often discuss common "icon crimes" — UX-design transgressions such as redundant icons, icons that break established conventions, or absurd visual metaphors used to represent concepts within UIs.

This conversation naturally leads to a fun debate: Are there universal icons? Which ones (if any) are generally “safe” to use? And, most importantly, are there icons that users consistently understand?

Frustrated by the lack of data-driven answers to these questions, I embarked on a research journey that ultimately led to the first publication of NN/g Press: *Digital Icons That Work: A Comprehensive Guide to Enhancing User Experience with Effective Iconography .*

Digital Icons That Work *by Kate Kaplan (with a contributing chapter on icon design by Kelley Gordon) is the first book released from NN/g Press ( available for purchase on Amazon ).*

Through rigorous studies involving both [quantitative](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/quantitative-research-study-guide/)and [qualitative research](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/5-qualitative-research-methods/), I explored how people notice, recognize, and interpret icons. All the resulting data, insights, and guidelines are shared in the comprehensive book — plus a chapter on the visual design of icons by [Kelley Gordon](https://www.nngroup.com/people/kelley-gordon/).

This quiz draws from that research, offering a taste of the insights you’ll find in the book.

## Icon Quiz: Test Your Knowledge

1. **Which of the following icons do users associate most strongly with location?**

  1. Compass
  2. Map
  3. Location pin
  4. Globe

Reveal answer
2. **Why could it be problematic if a design used both the flame and trendline arrow icons?**

  1. The icons depict physically similar objects, so users might struggle to distinguish between their shapes.
  2. Both icons are associated with popularity, leading to ambiguous interpretations.
  3. The flame icon generally represents something good, while the trendline icon represents something bad.
  4. Both icons represent something intense, so users might not know which one is more important.

Reveal answer
3. **What is the primary challenge for users who encounter the gift icon?**

  1. It is often too complex to be recognized by users who cannot make out the small design details within the symbol.
  2. It suggests that the interface needs updating, because most users perceive the concept of physical gifts as outdated.
  3. It is often unclear whether the icon suggests that a gift is available for the user or that the user can give a gift to someone else.
  4. It is often mistaken for a shopping cart, which can lead to frustration when users cannot find items in their carts.

Reveal answer

8. **What is a potential risk of using the icon below to represent a menu?**

  1. It is likely to be interpreted as a way to adjust settings or preferences due to its visual structure.
