#### Aesthetic-Usability Effect

Users’ tendency to perceive attractive products as more usable. People believe that things that *look* better will *work* better — even if they aren’t actually more effective or efficient.

- Related article: [The Aesthetic-Usability Effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/)
- Related video: [The Aesthetic Usability Effect and Prioritizing Appearance vs. Functionality](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/aesthetic-usability-effect/)

#### Brightness

The proportion of light versus dark colors that an element has or the density of light colors within an image. Increasing the brightness of an image means that more light is perceived as being reflected by that image.

- Related article: [Dark Mode: How Users Think About It and Issues to Avoid](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode-users-issues/)
- See also: [dark mode](#DarkMode)

#### Change Blindness

Peoples’ tendency to ignore changes in a scene when they occur in a region that is far away from their focus of attention.

- Related article: [Change Blindness in UX: Definition](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/change-blindness-definition/)
- Related video: [Change Blindness in User Interfaces](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/change-blindness/)

#### Closure

A Gestalt principle that states that people will fill in blanks to perceive a complete object whenever an external stimulus partially matches that object.

![Graphic of four incomplete circles in a minimalist style, each missing a segment, arranged against a pink background.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/closure.png)

- Related article: [Principle of Closure in Visual Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principle-closure/)
- Related video: [Closure: Gestalt Principle for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/closure-gestalt/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Clutter

Visual elements that are unnecessary or excessive, making it difficult for the viewer to focus on important aspects of a design.

- Related article: [Clutter-Free: One of the 3 Cs for Better Charts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/clutter-charts/)
- Related video: [Clutter-Free Charts](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/chartjunk/)
- See also: [density](#Density), [minimalism](#Minimalism), [signal-to-noise ratio](#SignalToNoiseRatio)

#### Color Palette

A defined set of colors used in a design to maintain consistency and create harmony.

![A color palette with three rows of color squares. The colors range from teal to dark blue, vibrant red, and muted purples and pinks.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/color-palette.png)

- Related video: [Choosing a Color Palette](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/choosing-color-palette/)
- See also: [consistency](#Consistency), [design system](#DesignSystem)

#### Common Fate

A Gestalt principle stating that things that move in synch are perceived as belonging to the same group, while being different than other screen elements that stay put or move differently.

- Related video: [Common Fate: Gestalt Principle for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/common-fate-gestalt/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Common Region

A Gestalt principle stating that items within a boundary are perceived as a group and assumed to share some common characteristic or functionality.

![Illustration showing a grid of blue circles organized in three rows.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/common-region.png)

- Related article: [The Principle of Common Region: Containers Create Groupings](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/common-region/)
- Related video: [Common Region: Gestalt Principle for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/common-region-gestalt/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Connectedness

A Gestalt principle referring to the tendency to perceive elements that are connected by lines or other visual cues as belonging together. This principle is strong enough to overrule small differences between the items.

![A horizontal row of blue circles connected by lines, grouped into four labeled sections.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/connectedness.png)

- Related video: [Connectedness: Gestalt Principle for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/connectedness-gestalt/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Consistency

Using similar characteristics and patterns in design elements — in layout, typography, and color palettes — to provide coherence and predictability to users.

- Related article: [Maintain Consistency and Adhere to Standards (Usability Heuristic #4)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/consistency-and-standards/)
- Related video: [Usability Heuristic 4: Consistency and Standards](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/usability-heuristic-consistency-standards/)
- See also: [color palette](#ColorPalette), [design system](#DesignSystem), [external consistency](#ExternalConsistency), [internal consistency](#InternalConsistency)

#### Continuation

A Gestalt principle stating that the eye automatically follows lines and curves, continuing them. Continuation guides users along desired paths in a user interface.

![A series of dark red dots that transition into pink dots forming a diagonal line across a pink background](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/continuation.png)

- Related video: [Continuation: Gestalt Principle for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/continuation-gestalt/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Contrast

The difference in visual properties — including color, brightness, or size — that makes an object distinguishable from its surroundings.

![A grid of blue and light blue circles](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/contrast.png)

- Related article: [Ensure High Contrast for Text Over Images](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-over-images/)
- Related video: [The Visual Principle of Contrast in UI Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/visual-principle-contrast/)
- See also: [brightness](#Brightness), [visual hierarchy](#VisualHierarchy)

#### Dark Mode

The combination of light text on a dark background; also known as negative contrast polarity.

![A mobile phone interface with dark teal backgrounds and lighter teal screen elements](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/dark-mode.png)

- Related article: [Dark Mode: How Users Think About It and Issues to Avoid](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode-users-issues/)
- Related article: [Dark Mode vs. Light Mode: Which Is Better?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)

#### Data Visualization

The representation of data through visuals, such as charts, plots, infographics, and animations. These visualizations are used to communicate complex data relationships and data-driven insights in a way that is easy to understand.

![Bar chart with three horizontal bars in teal, pink, and blue, each with error bars](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/data-visualization.png)

- Related video: [Data Visualizations for Dashboards](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/data-visualizations-dashboards/)
- Related video: [Better Charts for Analytics & Quantitative UX Data](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/better-charts-analytics-quantitative-ux-data/)
- See also: [infographic](#Infographic)

#### Density

The number of visual elements in a given area or space. High-density designs are sometimes referred to as cluttered, busy, or crowded. Low-density designs use whitespace and few components. Low-density designs are sometimes referred to as “minimalist.”

- See also: [clutter](#Clutter), [minimalism](#Minimalism), [signal-to-noise ratio](#SignalToNoiseRatio)

Established design conventions in an industry or on the web at large, beyond one application or family of applications.

#### Figure/Ground

A Gestalt principle stating that people divide a scene into foreground and background and focus their perception on elements in the foreground.

![A bright pink message bubble in focus, with a lighter pink message bubble blurred](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/figure-ground.png)

- Related video: [Figure/Ground: Gestalt Principle for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/figure-ground-gestalt/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Fitts’s Law

Describes how long it takes a user to move a pointer (such as the mouse cursor or a finger) to a target in a graphical user interface (GUI) or other design, as a function of size and distance.

- Related article: [Fitts's Law and Its Applications in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/fitts-law/)
- Related video: [Fitts's Law](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/fittss-law/)

#### Flat Design

A design style characterized by minimalist use of visual elements such as gradients, shadows, and textures to create a clean and simple interface.

- Related article: [Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/)
- Related video: [Making Flat Design Usable](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/making-flat-design-usable/)
- See also: [minimalism](#Minimalism)

#### Gestalt Principles

Principles that explain how humans simplify and organize complex images that consist of many elements, by subconsciously arranging the parts into an organized system that creates a whole, rather than interpreting them as a series of disparate elements. Gestalt principles capture our tendency to perceive the whole as opposed to individual elements.

- Related video: [The Gestalt Principles for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/the-gestalt-principles-intro/)
- See also: [closure](#Closure), [common fate](#), [common region](#CommonRegion), [connectedness](#Connectedness), [continuation](#Continuation), [figure/ground](#FigureGround), [proximity](#Proximity), [similarity](#Similarity)

#### Golden Ratio

A mathematical ratio used in design to create organic, balanced, and aesthetically pleasing compositions, thought to be favored by the human eye. This ratio is found in design, nature, photography, and architecture. In UX, use the golden ratio to harmoniously adjust font sizes, highlight focus areas of images, and create balanced layouts.

- Related article: [The Golden Ratio and User-Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/golden-ratio-ui-design/)

#### Grid

A visual made up of columns, gutters, and margins that provide a structure for the layout of elements on a page. Common grid structures include column, modular, and hierarchical grids.

![A complex grid layout in varying shades of blue and white](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/grid.png)

#### Iconography

The use of symbols or icons to represent concepts, actions, or ideas.

![Row of common interface icons in light purple, including a gear, funnel, thumbs up, flag, user profile, light bulb, link chain, book, globe, and open book](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/iconography.png)

- Related article: [Icon Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)
- Related video: [Tips for Icon Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/icon-usability/)

#### Infographic

A multimedia graphic aiming to present complex information and data in a way that is easy to understand. Infographics often include data or (more rarely) information visualizations but must also provide context in the form of illustrations, facts, quotes, and captions. Thus, an infographic can stand on its own as a separate piece of content (though it can also be shown in the context of an article with additional text).

- Related article: [Designing Effective Infographics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-infographics/)
- See also: [data visualization](#DataVisualization)

#### Internal Consistency

Consistency within a product or family of products, either within a single application or across a family or suite of applications.

- Related article: [Maintain Consistency and Adhere to Standards (Usability Heuristic #4)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/consistency-and-standards/)

#### Minimalism

A low-density aesthetic style in which a design provides the bare minimum components needed to fulfill some purpose.

- Related article: [The Characteristics of Minimalism in Web Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/characteristics-minimalism/)
- Related article: [The Roots of Minimalism in Web Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/roots-minimalism-web-design/)

#### Monochrome

A color palette composed only of different shades of a single color, with no other colors present. Monochrome palettes can be centered around any color, as long as every color in that palette is a lighter or darker shade of that one color.

![Four squares in varying shades of pink from bright to dark](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/monochrome.png)

- See also: [color palette](#ColorPalette)

#### Prototype

#### Proximity

A Gestalt principle stating that design elements near each other are perceived as related, while elements spaced apart are perceived as belonging to separate groups.

![Two groups of blue geometric shapes (circles and triangles)](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/proximity.png)

- Related article: [Proximity Principle in Visual Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/)
- Related video: [Proximity: Gestalt Principle for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/proximity-gestalt/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Raster Image

An image whose size is static and is composed of pixels (as opposed to vectors). When a raster image is zoomed in on or stretched, the pixels are more defined or visible. Raster images are also known as bitmap images, including JPG, PNG, or GIF files.

![An illustration with a pixelated close up](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/raster-image.png)

- See also: [vector image](#VectorImage)

#### Responsive Design

An interface that adapts and responds to different screen sizes and devices, providing an optimal user experience across various platforms.

![Three variations of screen sizes: phone, tablet, and desktop](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/responsive-design.png)

- Related article: [Responsive Web Design (RWD) and User Experience](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/responsive-web-design-definition/)
- See also: [grid](#Grid)

#### Saturation

How intensely colors appear in any given element. Highly saturated elements use bright, bold colors, while less saturated elements use neutral tones or muted colors.

![Four pink squares with varying colors, from most saturated to least](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/saturation.png)

- See also: [brightness](#Brightness), [contrast](#Contrast)

#### Scale

A design principle that uses relative size to signal importance and rank in a composition.

![Three different sized blue circles](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/scale.png)

#### Signal-to-Noise Ratio

The ratio of relevant to irrelevant information in an interface or communication channel.

- Related article: [Signal–to–Noise Ratio](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/signal-noise-ratio/)
- See also: [clutter](#Clutter), [density](#Density)

#### Similarity

A Gestalt principle stating that user-interface elements that share one or more traits (such as shape, color, size, etc.) will be seen by users as being similar and belonging together.

![Alternating columns of circles and triangles](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/similarity.png)

- Related article: [Similarity Principle in Visual Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-similarity/)
- Related video: [Similarity: Gestalt Principle 1 for UI Design](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/similarity-gestalt-principle/)
- See also: [Gestalt principles](#GestaltPrinciples)

#### Skeuomorphism

A design practice incorporating real-world elements into digital interfaces to create a sense of familiarity with these interfaces.

- Related article: [Skeuomorphism](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeuomorphism/)

#### Squint Test

A technique used to evaluate visual designs by narrowing the eyes to reduce detail and focus on overall composition and visual hierarchy.

![A screenshot in focus next to a blurred screenshot](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/squint-test.png)

- Related video: [Squint Test](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/squint-test/)

#### Vector Image

An image that can be easily scaled or modified because the file contains mathematical calculations for every shape in the image. Vectors are typically used for images that are not photographs, such as logos, icons, diagrams, or other graphics.

![An illustration with a closeup of its contents in focus](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/vector-image.png)

- See also: [raster image](#RasterImage)

#### Visual Balance

A satisfying arrangement or proportion of design elements. Balance occurs when there is an equally distributed amount of visual signal on both sides of an imaginary axis going through the middle of the screen. This axis is often vertical but can also be horizontal. A design can have symmetrical, asymmetrical, or radial balance.

![A screenshot with a dotted line down the center](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/visual-balance.png)

- Related article: [5 Principles of Visual Design in UX](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/)
- Related video: [Balance in UX Design: Symmetry, Asymmetry, and Radial Balance](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/balance-in-ux-design/)
- See also: [visual hierarchy](#VisualHierarchy)

#### Visual Hierarchy

The organization of design elements on the page so that the eye is guided to consume each design element in the order of intended importance. Visual hierarchy can be created through color and contrast, scale, and grouping of elements.

- Related article: [Visual Hierarchy in UX: Definition](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)
- Related video: [Visual Hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/visual-hierarchy/)
- See also: [color palette](#ColorPalette), [contrast](#Contrast), [scale](#Scale), [visual balance](#VisualBalance)

#### Visual Weight

The perceived importance or prominence of a design element based on its size, color, contrast, or placement. Objects that are bigger in size, similar in shape, use dark colors, or have high contrast appear visually heavier.

- Related video: [What is Visual Weight?](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/visual-weight/)
- See also: [visual hierarchy](#VisualHierarchy)

#### Whitespace

The empty space between design elements used to achieve a balanced design, making it easier for users to scan and read.

- Related video: [What is Whitespace?](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/whitespace/)
- See also: [visual balance](#VisualBalance)

#### Wireframe

A skeletal outline of a design layout used to represent the structure and functionality of an interface before visual design is considered.

![Four phone screens with outlined elements](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/05/10/wireframe.png)
