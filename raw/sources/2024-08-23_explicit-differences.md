*This is an updated version of an article written by Jakob Nielsen in 2013.* Users are bombarded with choices on the internet. That’s why the best websites make it easy for users to analyze options and make informed, low-stress decisions.

One easy and effective way to improve decision making on your website is to distinguish between choice options clearly. **Emphasizing the attributes that differ** in choices with minimal distinction and **noting the key differences** in choices with many dissimilarities will greatly improve users’ confidence in making choices on your site.

For more information on improving the decision-making experience, check out our new report: [Helping Users Make Decisions: Reduce Choice Overload and Avoid Overwhelming Users.](https://www.nngroup.com/reports/make-decisions/)

## Explicitness Guidelines

![The title says "Explicit vs Implicit." Image one says "Explicit" and "Fully explained with no confusion between options." It shows a blue circle and pink square labeled as such. The second image says "Implicit" and "Little to no explanation, differences are assumed" and shows a blue circle and pink triangle but are labeled shape one and two.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/08/16/explicit-implicit.png)

Don’t assume that your users can figure out and prioritize options on your site by themselves. Without explicit differences, users are likely to assume all choices are the same or fret over the smallest distinctions. The decision-making process will be slowed down and needlessly complicated.

Instead, help users make the right decisions by following two key guidelines.

### Highlight the Differences

If options **differ on only a few attributes, highlight those features****** in a [comparison table](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/comparison-tables/) or move them to the top of the list. Sometimes, you can achieve additional clarity by merging cells across columns for attributes that are the same for all products.

![Screenshot of an Amazon product page. It shows two espresso machines, the Barista Express and the Infuser. Between them is a chart showing if they have the following features: Barista-Style, Microfoam, Auto MILQ, Built-in Grinder, 3 Second Heat Up, and Assisted Tamping. Next to the qualities they share, there is a check.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/07/26/breville.png)

✅*Amazon: The Breville espresso-machine line on Amazon features this visual to allow users to easily compare a whole line of products on six simple dimensions.*

### Highlight What’s Important

If there are **many differences but only a few are truly important**, highlight these key differences to focus users' attention on the things that matter. You can also use [progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) to give details on minor issues to the people who need them — and avoid overloading everyone else with extraneous information.

![This is a screenshot of a comparison table. It shows the two metal pens the company offers. It compares them along 11 different factors, however, they are only different on one: engraving vs. digital print.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/07/26/fastpens.png)

*❌ fastpens.com: This comparison table failed to make the differences in pens stand out, nor did it clarify why the differences were important. By not following the two principles, fastpens.com made it hard for users to determine which pen would suit their needs.*

![This screenshot shows two images of haircuts. The left is a blunt bob, a mid-length style that is straight and ends abruptly. The right is a layered bob, it is a wavy mid-length haircut that is wavy and ends in a more natural way. Under the blunt bob are the words "classic," "sleek," "versatile," and "check it out". Under the layered bob are the words "flexible," "fun," "timeless," and "check me out."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/07/26/greatclips.png)

✅ *Great Clips: Great Clips offered visuals to highlight the differences in their hairstyles. It also gave information on how each haircut is perceived, which is an important consideration when choosing a new look. In addition, clicking* Check it out *explained more key differences (such as hairstyle maintenance), fully informing customers before their decision.*

## Explicit Copy

Even if options have only a few simple distinctions, users might overlook or misunderstand their choices if the copy leaves exact differences to the imagination.**

![Under the listing for the "Signature Round Dutch Oven," Le Creuset allowed users to select colors and size. Under sizes, they provided the quart size and under that, they had the serving size. It reads as follows: "2 qt 1-2 Servings," "4.5 qt 4-5 Servings," "5.5 qt 5-6 Servings," "7.25 qt 7-8 Servings," "9 qt 9+ Servings," and "13.75 qt 9+ Servings."](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/07/26/lecreuset.png)

✅ *Le Creuset: Instead of assuming users knew the size of a quart, Le Creuset used servings to demonstrate the differences in pot size.*

For example, in past studies of [university-website](http://www.nngroup.com/courses/university-websites/) usability, NN/g tested different ways of explaining tuition rates for university students. Confusing wording, paired with [teenagers' developing research and attention skills](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/), made it unlikely that they could distinguish between *resident* (or *in-state*) and *non-resident* (or *out-of-state*) tuition.

Take this example from the University of West Virginia. Here, the difference between ***in-state***and***out-of-state***students was implied but not explained. It would be easy for someone unfamiliar with this term (like a teenager exploring colleges for the first time) to mistake its meaning.**

![This screenshot says "Tuition Rates. Looking for a quick ballpark figure? We have you covered.  Tuition Rates Cost Per Year: In-state tuition and fees $9,648, Out-of-state tuition and fees $27,360, Housing expenses $13,020." At the bottom it reads  "In addition to the rates above, your program will charge college tuition. Find your program's college tuition rate." This is all on a yellow background.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/07/26/uwv.png)

*University of West Virginia: The table showing the estimated cost of attendance assumed readers were familiar with the difference between the terms* in-state *and* out-of-state.

In addition,*Housing expenses* could also be confusing,**as some readers might have assumed that it covered any type of living arrangement**(including living with parents), even though this cost likely applied only to those who live on campus.

A better option would be *On-campus housing expenses* or *Dormitory expenses*, to explicitly state the charge and fully inform prospective students of the cost of attendance.

Finally, the table did not even state all the key differences. Users had to navigate to another page to determine the additional costs associated with specific majors. When users need to make an expensive decision like which university to enroll in, designers must explicitly lay out all the differences and fees to create a positive decision-making environment.

In contrast, the University of California (UC) San Diego explicitly showed the difference in options. By including the state before *resident,* the table reduced ambiguity around the term. The table also clearly broke down the differences in tuition by cost and even clarified potential housing expenses by listing the estimated cost for all housing options.

![The cost of attendance for California and non-California residents varies depending on living arrangements. For California residents, the total estimated costs are $33,969 if living with parents, $42,639 if living on campus, and $43,686 if living off campus. Non-California residents face higher costs, with totals estimated at $68,169, $76,839, and $77,886 respectively. These costs include tuition, fees, living expenses, books, miscellaneous personal expenses, and transportation. The annual UC San Diego Student Health Insurance Plan (SHIP) fee of $2,694 is also included. For more details, visit the UCSD FAQ page.](https://media.nngroup.com/media/editor/2024/07/26/ucsd.png)

*University of California San Diego: A detailed and clear table for estimating cost of attendance*

Best of all, UC San Diego (literally) highlighted the key differences, allowing users to quickly and clearly see the difference in tuition prices.

By being explicit about all the different tuition options, UC San Diego created trust with prospective students, allowing students to feel more confident in selecting it as their university.

## Explicit Search-Engine Results

Being explicit matters as much on search-engine results pages (SERPs) as on your website. Your users should be able to make the right choice, no matter where they make it.

SERP titles and descriptions should clearly state the product or service offered by the link. If your company offers two similar items, take special care to make them stand out from each other. There should be no ambiguity as to what the search result refers to. Clear copy is even more important on SERPs, as users have less detailed visuals to provide differentiation. The lack of visuals can become especially frustrating for the user if two options are not visually distinct. Good differentiation in copy can ease these frustrations and distinguish between the listings.

Finally, ensure you have strong SEO, as users rarely look beneath the first few options. The choice they’re looking for should appear early so they don’t spend needless time searching or choosing the wrong option.

❌ *The Furbo dog camera comes in two models: a cheaper one that works only with a subscription and a more expensive one that does not require one. When you search for*“Furbo no subscription *,” the top result immediately offers a subscription model. In addition, the majority of the sponsored cameras require subscriptions.*

## Why Does It Matter?

**Good UX ensures that users make the correct choice the first time** or, if they do make a mistake, that they don’t feel it’s the website’s fault. Presenting the difference in options explicitly is the first step in guaranteeing that users make the right choice.

When the differences between options remain implicit, you increase the likelihood that users will select the wrong option. When they do, you cost them time, frustration, and sometimes money. If they catch the mistake, they suffer the [interaction cost](http://www.nngroup.com/articles/interaction-cost-definition/) of returning and finding the right selection.

Each mistake made on your website costs your company goodwill, resulting in a negative [halo effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/halo-effect/).

## Guaranteeing Explicitness

As we always say — [you are not the user](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/false-consensus/). And you are likely not the person making these important decisions on your website.

The distinction between implicit and explicit differentiating criteria is one of the key usability determinants for any system that offers multiple options. Unfortunately, it's a design issue easily overlooked by people who know too much about the system — a.k.a. anybody on the design team.

In our tuition example, anyone in a university-admissions department probably thinks that *in-state**tuition* is a highly explicit description of that option. Such distinctions are obvious to people with well-formed [mental models](http://www.nngroup.com/articles/mental-models/) of the domain. However, new users approach your design with a fuzzy mental model.

So, anytime you hear design-meeting participants say *"X is obvious,"* remember that it **might not be so obvious to people outside your organization**.

The clearer and more explicit you can be — especially about distinctions — the more successful your site will be.
