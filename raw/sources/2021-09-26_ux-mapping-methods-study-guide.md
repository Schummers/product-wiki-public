## Journey Maps

**Definition:** A [journey map](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customer-journey-mapping/) is a visualization of the process that a person goes through in order to accomplish a goal.

In its most basic form, journey mapping starts by compiling a series of user actions into a timeline. Next, the timeline is fleshed out with user thoughts and emotions to create a narrative. This narrative is condensed and polished, ultimately leading to a visualization used to align stakeholders on the holistic experience and identify opportunities for optimizing and improving the journey.

## Service Blueprints

**Definition:** A [service blueprint](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/) is a diagram that visualizes the relationships between different service components — people, props (physical or digital evidence), and processes — that are directly tied to touchpoints in a specific customer journey.

Think of service blueprints as a part two to [creating customer-journey maps](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customer-journey-mapping-process/). Blueprinting is an ideal approach for experiences that are omnichannel, involve multiple touchpoints, or require a crossfunctional effort (that is, coordination of multiple departments).

## UX Roadmaps

**Definition**: A [UX roadmap](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-roadmaps/)is a strategic, living artifact that aligns, prioritizes, and communicates a UX team’s future work and problems to solve.

A UX roadmap should act as a single source of truth representing your UX team’s North Star. It helps your designers, researchers, developers, and stakeholders align around a single vision and set of priorities.

## Mapping Methods for Design Exploration

Some UX maps are useful for surfacing user [pain points](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/pain-points/) and [mental models](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mental-models/) that can be used as prompts for ideation. This section provides resources for UX mapping techniques that can used for design exploration and visualizing insights.

[Scenario mapping](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scenario-mapping-personas/) is a group exercise that helps your design team think about how your persona segments might approach an activity using your product or service and ideate around the type of experience you want to provide for them, producing candidate solutions for the future design.

[Cognitive mapping](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cognitive-mapping-user-research/) is a mapping method used to create a visual representation of a person’s (or a group’s) mental model for a process or concept. It can be a useful tool throughout user research, from gathering data to analyzing findings and articulating similarities and patterns.

## Mapping Techniques for Synthesizing Research and Ideas

Some UX mapping techniques help team members make sense out of lots of existing ideas or insights by organizing them into more manageable categories. Affinity diagrams and empathy maps are 2 such techniques.

An ****[affinity diagram](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/affinity-diagram/) is a technique used to organize related items into distinct clusters. This method helps teams collaboratively discuss and analyze research findings, as well as ideas from [ideation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/group-ideation/) sessions.

An [empathy map](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empathy-mapping/)is a collaborative visualization used to articulate what we know about a particular type of user. It externalizes knowledge about users in order to 1) create a shared understanding of user needs, and 2) aid in decision making.
